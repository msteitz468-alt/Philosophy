# Hadot vs Foucault — Care of the Self vs Cosmic Universalization
> They agree that ancient philosophy was an art of living that modernity forgot. They disagree about what the art was *for*: a culture of the self, or a going-beyond of the self into universal reason.

## The Positions

### Foucault: Aesthetics of Existence

In the late phase (c. 1980–1984), [[Thinkers/Michel Foucault]] rereads Greco-Roman *askesis* as **techniques of the self** — practices by which one constitutes oneself as an ethical subject. Ethics is not a moral code but an **aesthetics of existence**: giving style to one’s life as a work of art. The master term is care of the self (*souci de soi*, *epimeleia heautou*). *Hypomnemata* are “spiritual notebooks” in which one forges a self from selected past sayings. Joy in self-mastery can be described as another form of pleasure. The American gay scene is a laboratory for new relational forms. See [[Concepts/Ethics of the Self and Care of the Self (Foucault)]].

The vault’s Foucault page (from Macey’s biography) already has this phase. It does not yet have Hadot’s reply. Davidson reports, via Paul Veyne, that Foucault called logic and physics “enormous excrescences.” “Nothing could be further from Hadot’s own attitude.”

### Hadot: Interiorization Is Universalization

[[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]] wrote ch. 7 of *Philosophy as a Way of Life* as “Reflections on the Idea of the ‘Cultivation of the Self.’” Agreement first: ancient philosophy as art/way of life; modern philosophy forgot this; Christianity reused pagan techniques; Stoic “practices of the self” exist.

Then the disagreement, unsoftened:

1. Foucault’s “techniques of the self” are “focused far too much on the ‘self.’” Greco-Roman ethics is not pleasure-in-oneself. The Stoics insist on *gaudium* / “joy” precisely because they “refuse to introduce the principle of pleasure into moral life.” *Voluptas* ≠ *gaudium*.
2. “It seems difficult to accept that the philosophical practice of the Stoics and Platonists was nothing but a relationship to one’s self, a culture of the self, or a pleasure taken in oneself.” The goal is going beyond the self to universal reason / the cosmic whole (*Toti se inserens mundo*).
3. *Hypomnemata* do not constitute a self from eclectic past sayings. They actualize school dogmas as present reason and **universalize** the writer. “The point is not to forge oneself a spiritual identity by writing, but rather to liberate oneself from one’s individuality, in order to raise oneself up to universality. It is thus incorrect to speak of ‘writing of the self.’”
4. “By defining his ethical model as an aesthetics of existence — M. Foucault is propounding a culture of the self which is too aesthetic. In other words, this may be a new form of Dandyism, late twentieth-century style.”
5. “Interiorization is a going beyond oneself; it is universalization.”

See [[Concepts/Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)]], [[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]], [[Concepts/View from Above and Cosmic Perspective (Marcus Aurelius)]].

## Where They Disagree

| | Foucault | Hadot |
|---|---|---|
| Name of the practice | techniques of the self / *souci de soi* | spiritual exercises / *askēsis* |
| Aim | constitute a self; style a life | transform vision; raise the self to the Whole |
| Writing | writing *of* the self | writing that *liberates from* individuality |
| Affect | another form of pleasure | *gaudium*, not *voluptas* |
| Physics and logic | “enormous excrescences” (Veyne) | lived physics and lived logic; cosmic consciousness |
| Modern analogue | dandy / gay aesthetics of existence | Goethe, Marcus, Friedmann’s diary; not dandyism |

They are not talking past each other. Foucault read Hadot; Hadot answers Foucault by name. The disagreement is about the *telos* of the same archive (Epictetus, Marcus, Seneca, the *hypomnemata*).

## Open Questions

- The vault’s Foucault page already flags an internal tension: if power constitutes subjects, does a self-constituting ethics have purchase? Hadot’s cosmic universalization does not solve that; it changes the target.
- Whether late Foucault *could* absorb Hadot’s cosmic point (the 1982–84 lectures sometimes gesture at it) is not settled by this one essay.
- Holiday inherits Hadot’s *exercises* and Foucault’s *self-fashioning* without marking the fight. The vault should not repeat that merge.
