# Kierkegaard vs Kant - Absolute Duty vs Universal Law

> Can there be a duty to God that gives love of neighbour the opposite expression to universal moral law — or is that just failure of the maxim to be a law?

## The Conflict

### Position A: Kant — duty is the necessity of an action from respect for universal law
Moral worth lies in the maxim, not the aim. The first formulation: act only on that maxim you can will as a universal law. The Formula of Humanity: use humanity in yourself and in any other always at the same time as an end, never merely as a means. A private command to kill a son cannot be willed as universal law; it treats Isaac merely as a means to Abraham's (or God's) end. There is no extra-moral "absolute duty to God" that could license the act. What religion can add, in the critical system, is not a counter-duty but the postulates (God, freedom, immortality) that make the highest good thinkable. The *Groundwork* already treats divine command as needing the moral law as its criterion, not the reverse.

### Position B: Johannes *de silentio* — if the ethical is the universal, Abraham is done for
An absolute duty to God means the single individual relates himself absolutely, as the single individual, to the absolute. The ethical is reduced to the relative. It is not abolished; it receives a paradoxical expression. Love of God can cause the knight of faith to give love of neighbour the opposite expression to ethical duty. Abraham must love Isaac *more*, not less; only then is the act a sacrifice rather than Cain's. Luke 14.26 is to be heard as terror, not as "love less." Abraham cannot make the maxim public: speech would translate him into the universal and turn the trial into a temptation. The tragic hero (Agamemnon) is the one Kant can still admire — he has a universalizable *telos*. Abraham does not.

Johannes does not name Kant in Problema I the way he names Hegel. The catch is the same hoop: ethical = universal. Kant's universal is a law of reason, not Hegel's State; for Abraham the difference does not help. There is still no public maxim, still a son used as the means of a trial, still no mediation.

## What Is Not Settled

- Kant is a reported opponent in this contradiction: Johannes's target sentence is Hegel's, and the *Groundwork* is the vault's Kant. Kant never answers this book.
- Johannes insists the act is *sacrifice* only if Abraham loves Isaac more than himself. A Kantian can hear that as still treating Isaac as a means, however beloved.
- Sartre's pupil (mother vs Free French) already used the Formula of Humanity as a case it cannot decide. That is a different failure (underdetermination), not a suspension. Do not flatten them.

## Implications for the Vault

The vault's Kantian deontology vs Mill's consequentialism ([[Contradictions/Utilitarian Consequentialism vs. Kantian Deontology]]) now has a third corner: a religious ethics that refuses both the universal law and the calculation of outcomes ("do not judge by the outcome"). Abraham is not a utilitarian exception and not a Kantian duty. He is the named remainder.
