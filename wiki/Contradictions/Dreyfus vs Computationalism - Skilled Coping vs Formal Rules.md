# Dreyfus vs Computationalism — Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules
> Can intelligence be reproduced by formal operations on discrete, determinate, context-free elements? Computationalism says that is what intelligence *is*. Dreyfus says that is what Western metaphysics mistook it for.

## The Positions

### Dreyfus: Skilled Coping, Not Formal Rules

In *What Computers Still Can't Do* (1972/1979/1992), [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]] argues:

1. GOFAI — facts plus formal rules on a physical symbol system — is a Lakatosian degenerating research program, not a temporary engineering lag. See [[Concepts/GOFAI as Degenerating Research Program (Dreyfus)]].
2. The program rests on four optional assumptions (brain as digital computer; mind as rule-following processor; all knowledge formalizable; world as independent facts). Phenomenology and the research record undermine all four. See [[Concepts/Four Assumptions of Artificial Reason (Dreyfus)]].
3. Everyday intelligence is **know-how** in a situation, organized by a body and by needs. Experts do not apply the rules beginners follow. See [[Concepts/Skilled Coping and the Five Stages of Expertise (Dreyfus)]] and [[Concepts/Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)]].
4. Two questions: do humans follow formal rules? can behavior be formalized for a digital machine? The descriptive evidence says no; the *a priori* arguments *for* formalization invert into arguments against. Burden of proof is on the optimists.
5. This is not a proof that AI is impossible. It is a claim that the facts-and-rules program has reached a dead end, and that connectionist or Heideggerian successors still fail **relevance** and **human-like generalization** unless the device shares our body, architecture, and form of life.

Key pages: [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]], [[Concepts/Commonsense Knowledge Problem (Dreyfus)]], [[Concepts/Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)]], [[Concepts/Readiness-to-Hand and Presence-at-Hand (Heidegger)]]. Body-phenomenology now primary: [[Sources/Phenomenology of Perception - Maurice Merleau-Ponty (1945)]], [[Concepts/Motor Intentionality and the Intentional Arc (Merleau-Ponty)]]. "Maximum grasp" is Dreyfus's name for MP's hold.

### Computationalism: Intelligence as Formal Structure

The opposing pole in the vault is not one person. It is the family of views that take formal structure — symbols, programs, isomorphisms, or (later) learned weights — as sufficient for mind:

1. **[[Thinkers/Alan Turing]]** (1950): replace "Can machines think?" with the imitation game; digital computers are universal discrete-state machines; by ~2000 one will "speak of machines thinking without expecting to be contradicted."
2. **Newell, Simon, [[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]]**: physical symbol systems and heuristic programs *are* the theory of intelligence. Simon 1957: "there are now in the world machines that think." Minsky: "within a generation" AI will be "substantially solved"; humans are "meat machines"; frames store the stereotyped world.
3. **[[Thinkers/Douglas Hofstadter]]**: syntax acquires semantics via isomorphism and strange loops; sufficient self-referential complexity yields genuine mind. See [[Contradictions/Hofstadter vs Searle - Strange Loops vs Chinese Room]].
4. **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: heterophenomenology plus the multiple-drafts model — there is no further inner residue once the functional/narrative organization is described.
5. **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: [[Concepts/Strong AI and Computational Sufficiency (Chalmers)|implementing the right computation suffices]] (with natural necessity) for conscious experience; Dreyfus is named among the opponents.

They disagree among themselves (Hofstadter vs Searle, Dennett vs Nagel, Chalmers vs Dennett). They agree that Dreyfus's "something formalization misses" is either temporary or not a bar to machine intelligence.

## Where They Disagree

| Issue | Dreyfus | Computationalism (Turing / Minsky / Hofstadter / Chalmers) |
|---|---|---|
| What is intelligence? | Embodied, situational skilled coping | Formal structure (rules, symbols, or equivalent computation) |
| Status of the 1957–92 record | Degenerating program; first-step fallacy | Early success; remaining problems are engineering |
| Know-how | Cannot be converted into knowing-that | Expertise is compiled rules or stored frames |
| Commonsense | Holist background; relevance seen, not retrieved | Large data base + inference (Minsky, Lenat/CYC) |
| Body | Condition of intelligence | Implementation detail, or later add-on (robot reply) |
| Imitation Game | Builder’s goal, not a criterion of mind | Operational substitute for "Can machines think?" |
| Connectionism | Right *kind* of successor; still fails our generalizations | Vindication that the substrate can be non-symbolic |
| Possibility proof | No negative proof; program has no reason to succeed | In principle yes (universality + enough structure) |

## Third Voices

**[[Thinkers/John Searle]]** is not on Dreyfus's side and not on Minsky's. The Chinese Room grants the computationalist the formal structure and denies that it yields semantics. Dreyfus denies that the formal structure is even the right description of the competence. They can both be right. They can also fail independently: a lived body might still be a syntactic engine (Searle), and a non-formal analogue device might still lack biological causal powers.

**[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]** is the closest later attempt to *keep* Dreyfus's world-involving mind and *drop* his pessimism. The 1998 [[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)|extended mind]] paper (now primary) and later predictive processing say: you do not store the world; you couple to it. That is Chapman's slogan, which Dreyfus already quotes. Clark thinks a prediction machine is the right internal organ for that coupling. Dreyfus's 1992 dilemma remains: the learner must already share enough of our body and form of life to generalize *our* way. The 1998 paper addresses coupling, not learning.

**[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]** keeps the same prediction-error mechanism and *rebuilds* a sophisticated internalism: PEM is optimized "relative to just the nervous system," behind a veil of sensory input ([[Concepts/Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)]]). That is not GOFAI, but it is the opposite of Chapman's slogan. The Hohwy/Clark split ([[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]]) is the later predictive-processing form of this contradiction.

**[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]], [[Thinkers/Evan Thompson]], and [[Thinkers/Eleanor Rosch]]** (*The Embodied Mind*, 1991) take Dreyfus as the gadfly and offer the constructive program he only partly envisaged: [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]] plus [[Concepts/Natural Drift (Varela)|natural drift]] plus Brooks. Their fight is not "formal rules vs know-how" but **pregiven world vs enacted world** — see [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]]. Dreyfus's 1993 review rejected their mindfulness-as-inward-observation; Thompson 2016 accepts.

**[[Thinkers/Melanie Mitchell]]** documents brittleness, missing common sense, and hype cycles as a working AI scientist. She is an empirical witness for Dreyfus's record-claim and does not accept (or discuss) the Heideggerian metaphysics.

**[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]** is an ally only at one remove. Both reject functional analyses as complete. Nagel's remainder is what-it-is-like; Dreyfus's is coping. A perfectly coping zombie would refute Dreyfus's use of phenomenology as evidence about *intelligence* and would not touch Nagel.

## The Deepest Clash

Both sides can agree that current programs are limited. They disagree on the *kind* of limit.

- **Computationalism**: the limit is coverage — more facts, better search, more weights, more data. Intelligence is the same kind of thing at every scale (the continuum assumption Dreyfus attributes to Armer, Feigenbaum, Feldman).
- **Dreyfus**: the limit is ontological. Associationistic and simple-formal tasks are one kind of thing; adult nonformal intelligence is another, and is prior. You cannot approach the second by scaling the first. "The areas of intelligent behavior are *discontinuous* and … *the boundary is near*."

That is why the alchemy analogy matters. Distilling mercury from dirt (chess, logic, MYCIN) does not license the transmutation (general intelligence). By-products — compilers, arms, debug tools — can be real and still not be gold.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Deep learning after 2012 is the test neither the 1972 book nor the 1992 introduction could run. Mitchell's pages are the vault's empirical update; they have not settled the discontinuity claim.
- If Clark-style predictive processing is right, Dreyfus may have won against GOFAI and lost against a non-symbolic computationalism he only partly envisaged.
- Searle vs Dreyfus remains under-argued in the vault: same target, two diagnoses, almost no direct engagement in the ingested sources.
