# Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism

> Is the universe the best of all possible worlds, governed by divine reason, or is it the worst of all possible worlds, driven by a blind, insatiable Will?

## The Tension

This contradiction represents the deepest division in rationalist and post-Kantian metaphysics regarding the moral quality and rationality of the universe:
* **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz** argues for metaphysical **optimism**: the universe is governed by the Principle of Sufficient Reason and is the "best of all possible worlds," designed by an infinitely wise, good, and powerful God.
* **Arthur Schopenhauer** argues for metaphysical **pessimism**: the universe is the "worst of all possible worlds," driven by a blind, aimless, non-rational "Will" that guarantees perpetual suffering, conflict, and lack of ultimate purpose.

---

## The Positions

### 1. Leibnizian Optimism (The Best of All Possible Worlds)
In *Monadology* (1714) and *Theodicy*, [[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz]] argues that God, having infinite wisdom, knows all possible worlds; having infinite goodness, wills the best; and having infinite power, creates it.
* **Principle of Sufficient Reason**: Nothing happens without a reason why it should be so rather than otherwise. The ultimate reason for the universe is God.
* **Harmony**: The universe is structured in a divinely pre-established harmony where all individual monads are coordinated to produce the greatest possible variety with the greatest possible order.
* **The Problem of Evil**: Physical and moral evil are not defects in creation but necessary parts of a larger, harmonious whole, just as shadows enhance a painting.

### 2. Schopenhauerian Pessimism (The Worst of All Possible Worlds)
The *proof* is *WWR* Volume 2, Chapter XLVI (Payne), not the 1819 volume’s insult. Volume 1 already called Leibnizian optimism a “glaring absurdity”; XLVI defines **possible** as “not what we may picture in our imagination, but what can actually exist and last.” A little worse and the world could not continue; “since a worse world could not continue to exist, it is absolutely impossible; and so this world itself is the worst of all possible worlds.” Optimism is self-praise of the will-to-live and *pernicious* because it makes happiness the aim. See [[Concepts/Vanity and Suffering of Existence (Schopenhauer)]].
* **The Worst of All Possible Worlds**: possible = able to exist and last. Fossils are “somewhat worse than the worst.” Thus it is literally the worst *possible* world.
* **Sufficient reason, fourfold, then stopped**: *On the Fourfold Root* (1847, now primary) **affirms** the Principle of Sufficient Reason as the form of all representation — four a priori laws, Wolff's formula kept as abstraction. What he *denies* is applying it to the thing-in-itself or to the world as a totality ("no more a ground in general than there is a triangle in general"; unconditioned necessity is a contradiction). The noumenon is a blind **Will**, not a rational God. The earlier vault line "No Sufficient Reason" was a flattening of *WWR* reception. See [[Concepts/Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)]].
* **Evil is Positive**: Suffering is not a mere privation or shadow; it is the positive, default state of life, while pleasure is merely the temporary, negative cessation of pain.

---

## *Parerga* Volume 1 does not rename this

The 1851 volume is the book that made him famous, and reception (Oxenford, Wundt) glued “ultra-pessimism” to his name. He does not call his own philosophy pessimism in this volume. The Aphorisms are an **accommodation** to the error that we exist to be happy; the higher standpoint still answers that a happy existence is not really possible. The Sketch treats Leibniz’s pre-established harmony as a “monstrous absurdity” (duplicate worlds), which is the ideal/real problem, not this theodicy fight. Do not file the Aphorisms as a recantation of the worst-of-all-possible-worlds thesis. See [[Concepts/Eudaemonology as Accommodation (Schopenhauer)]], [[Sources/Parerga and Paralipomena Volume 1 - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2014)]].

## *WWR* II Ch. XLVI is the primary; he still does not name the system

The word *pessimism* appears in Ch. XLVIII as a classification of *religions* (optimism + theism vs pessimism + asceticism) and as a charge he answers (“melancholy and cheerless”). He does not baptize his philosophy with the word. Ch. XLIX names the inborn error that we exist to be happy — the system-side of the *Parerga* I accommodation.

## *Parerga* Volume 2 adds the popular theodicy fight, still without the self-name

Chs. XI–XII are additional remarks on vanity and suffering. Leibniz’s *Théodicée* §153 is a “palpable and pitiable sophism”; pain is positive, well-being negative; existence is “something that it were better never to have been.” He attacks “orthodox optimism.” He does not title his own view “pessimism” in these chapters (he applies “pessimistic” once to the *spirit of Christianity*). Ch. XIV: denial is *nolle*; “a happy life is impossible.” See [[Concepts/Vanity and Suffering of Existence (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]], [[Sources/Parerga and Paralipomena Volume 2 - Schopenhauer (Payne 2000)]].

## Implications for the Vault

This debate represents the collapse of the pre-modern rationalist belief in a meaningful, teleological cosmos:
* **The Fate of Reason**: For Leibniz, reason can trace the sufficient reasons of God's perfect plan, including why *this* world exists. For Schopenhauer, PSR is the intellect's own form: it organizes science inside the world and cannot explain the world. The Will is outside that form.
* **Existential Stance**: Meliorism and faith in progress (Leibniz, Mill, Marx) vs. resignation, aesthetic escape, and ascetic withdrawal (Schopenhauer).
