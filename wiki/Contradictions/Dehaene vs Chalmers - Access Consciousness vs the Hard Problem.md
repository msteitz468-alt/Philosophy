# Dehaene vs Chalmers — Access Consciousness vs the Hard Problem

> The vault's first primary from the global-workspace side of the reductive program Chalmers had already filed as a failure. [[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]]: explain [[Concepts/Conscious Access (Dehaene)|conscious access]] and the leftover "phenomenal" residue will go the way of vitalism; Chalmers swapped the labels — the easy problem is the hard one. [[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]: access is awareness, an easy function; [[Concepts/Hard Problem of Consciousness (Chalmers)|why the functions are accompanied by experience]] survives any complete GNW.

## The Positions

### Dehaene: start with access, the rest evaporates

From the introduction and Ch. 7 of *Consciousness and the Brain* (2014):

1. Narrow consciousness to **conscious access** — reportable entry of selected information. Vigilance, attention, self, and metacognition are neighboring phenomena.
2. A distinct **phenomenal consciousness** (the "exquisite sharpness of tooth pain," the "inimitable greenness of a fresh leaf") "is highly misleading and leads down a slippery slope to dualism." Once we know how information becomes reportable, "the insurmountable problem of our ineffable experiences will disappear."
3. The experimental program ([[Concepts/Minimal Contrast Method (Dehaene)]], [[Concepts/Signatures of Consciousness and Ignition (Dehaene)]], [[Concepts/Global Neuronal Workspace (Dehaene)]]) is not a preliminary to the hard problem. It *is* the problem, correctly posed.
4. Named in Ch. 7: Ned Block "concedes that the workspace machinery may explain conscious access, but he argues that it is inherently incapable of explaining our qualia." Chalmers "similarly maintains that even if workspace theory explains which operations may or may not be performed consciously, it will never explain the riddle of first-person subjectivity," and is "famous for introducing a distinction between the easy and the hard problems."
5. **The labels are swapped.** "It is the 'easy' problem that is hard, while the hard problem just seems hard because it engages ill-defined intuitions. Once our intuition is educated by cognitive neuroscience and computer simulations, Chalmers's hard problem will evaporate." Qualia, "pure mental experience detached from any information-processing role, will be viewed as a peculiar idea of the prescientific era, much like vitalism."
6. Current models already explain why *any* rational machine would suffer the same visual illusions. "The science of consciousness already explains significant chunks of our subjective experience, and I see no obvious limits to this approach."
7. A machine with flexible communication, plasticity, and autonomy would have the behavior "reminiscent of our own variety of consciousness." No logical bar.

### Chalmers: access is the easy problem

From *The Conscious Mind* (1996), already in the vault:

1. **Phenomenal vs psychological** concepts of mind. Access, report, integration, control are psychological. Experience is phenomenal. See [[Concepts/Phenomenal and Psychological Concepts of Mind (Chalmers)]].
2. Reductive programs — Baars's global workspace explicitly listed, with Dennett, Crick & Koch 40 Hz, Edelman reentry, Penrose–Hameroff, evolution — explain **awareness**, not experience. "From structure and dynamics, we can only get more structure and dynamics."
3. [[Arguments/Zombie Argument (Chalmers)|Zombies]] are conceivable: a physical/functional duplicate without experience. So consciousness does not logically supervene. Materialism is false. [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)]].
4. Charity in Ch. 6: Baars/Crick/Koch are rehabilitated as science *of awareness*. [[Concepts/Coherence Between Consciousness and Awareness (Chalmers)]] is a *psychophysical law*, not a reduction. The epistemic lever: "We can only have empirical evidence for a link between a process N and consciousness if we already have evidence for a link between N and awareness." Every Dehaene signature is, on this view, a signature of awareness.
5. Organizational invariance still lets Chalmers *defend* machine consciousness — the dualist is not the one who denies AI.

## The Core Disagreement

| Issue | Dehaene | Chalmers |
|---|---|---|
| What is the thing to explain? | Conscious access (reportable broadcasting) | Phenomenal experience ("what it is like") |
| Status of "phenomenal" leftover | Dualist slope; prescientific, like vitalism | The datum; taking consciousness seriously |
| What GNW / signatures explain | Consciousness itself | Awareness, the easy problem |
| Easy vs hard | Labels swapped; easy is the real work | Easy is function; hard is experience |
| Zombies / qualia detached from role | Ill-defined intuition; evaporates with models | Conceivable; shows no logical supervenience |
| Machine consciousness | Workspace + learning + autonomy suffices | Right organization suffices *and* experience is extra (natural necessity, not reduction) |
| Vitalism analogy | Qualia will go the way of vitalism | Vitalism concerned *functions* and dissolved; consciousness survives complete functional explanation |

## Where They Almost Agree

Both reject mystery-mongering that would stop experiment. Both think a computational architecture can be *conscious* in the access sense. Both treat reportability as the empirical handle. Chalmers's coherence principle is why Dehaene's clinical tests (P3b, Owen imagery) can be evidence of *anything*: we already tie those markers to awareness in healthy adults. Dehaene's method *uses* that lever; Chalmers thinks it cannot climb off it.

## The Fifth Voice on the Vault's Central Contradiction

This pair does not replace [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]]. It *localizes* the deflationary side onto a specific reductive program:

- **Dennett / Clark / Dehaene**: dissolve the residue (Type-A). Dehaene is the experimentalist of the trio — signatures, clinic, simulations — not just the diagnosis of a bad picture.
- **Nagel**: keep the residue; do not infer extra properties.
- **Jackson**: leftover *property*; Physicalism false; idle qualia livable.
- **Chalmers**: leftover + psychophysical laws.

Dehaene's vitalism analogy is the one Chalmers already rejected: vitalist doubts were about functions. If that rejection holds, educating intuition with better models of *access* is exactly what a Type-A theorist would do, and it cannot settle the issue.

## What Each Gets Right

**Dehaene gets right**: the C-word was a mess; access is a real, manipulable phenomenon; the unconscious is deep enough that "consciousness does everything" is false; the signatures are robust enough to change ICU practice; Block's leftover, if it never makes a difference to report or ignition, is methodologically idle in the laboratory. The two-tests problem Descartes actually posed — speech, flexibility — has a candidate mechanism.

**Chalmers gets right**: every contrast Dehaene can run is a contrast in *awareness*. The method cannot, by construction, exhibit a case of access without experience or experience without access. Calling the remainder "ill-defined" is a verdict, not a finding. The vitalism analogy fails if the leftover was never a function. Organizational invariance plus dualism is a coherent package Dehaene does not address: one can grant the machine the workspace and still ask why it feels like anything.
