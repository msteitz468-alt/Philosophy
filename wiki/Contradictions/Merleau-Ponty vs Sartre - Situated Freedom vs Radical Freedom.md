# Merleau-Ponty vs Sartre — Situated Freedom vs Radical Freedom
> Sartre: freedom is total or it is not at all; not to choose is to choose. Merleau-Ponty: there is never determinism and never an absolute choice; not-refusing is not a choice.

## The Two Positions

### Sartre (*Being and Nothingness*, Part Four)

Freedom is the being of the for-itself, not a faculty. "He is wholly and forever free or he is not free at all" (BN 441). "I am condemned to exist forever beyond my essence… I am condemned to be free" (BN 439). "Not to choose is, in fact, to choose not to choose" (BN 481). Situation is the "common product" of brute given and freedom (BN 489); one cannot split the contributions. Freedom is autonomy of *choice*, not of success: the prisoner is free to *try* to escape. The coefficient of adversity appears only after an end is posited. Even torture does not exempt us from constituting the impossibility. "There is freedom only in a situation, and there is a situation only through freedom."

The 1945/46 lecture popularizes the same structure: "man is condemned to be free"; "What is not possible is not to choose."

See [[Concepts/Radical Freedom and Situation (Sartre)]].

### Merleau-Ponty (*Phenomenology of Perception*, Part Three ch. III)

The Freedom chapter first reconstructs the total-or-none doctrine — "we cannot be partially free"; "All things are within our power, or none of them are"; "Our freedom, it is said, is either total or non-existent" — and then refuses it as "the dilemma of objective thought and its accomplice, reflective analysis." **"There is never determinism and never an absolute choice; I am never a mere thing and never a bare consciousness."** **"I can no longer pretend to be a nothingness and to choose myself continuously from nothing."** **"But not refusing is not a choice."**

Sartre is **named once**, locally: "As J.-P. Sartre himself shows, the dream excludes freedom because… there are no obstacles and there is nothing *to do*." The rest is an unnamed reconstruction of the position, then a rebuttal. Do not invent a monograph attack.

Supports of the rebuttal: autochthonous sense of the world prior to deliberate *Sinngebung*; sedimentation ("it's not *likely*" after twenty years of an inferiority complex); class lived before class consciousness; Husserl's "field of freedom" / "conditioned freedom"; "We choose our world and the world chooses us." **"Our freedom does not destroy our situation, but gears into it."**

See [[Concepts/Situated Freedom (Merleau-Ponty)]].

## What Is Actually in Dispute

Both deny a determinism that would make motives into causes. Both make situation internal to freedom. The dispute is whether freedom is **total in every situation** (Sartre: the prisoner is as free as the master *to break the chains*; MP: there is no freedom without some power) and whether **non-doing is a choice** (Sartre: yes, always; MP: no — not-refusing is not a choice; we are always already in the plenum).

A second, bodily dispute sits under the first. For Sartre the body is nihilated facticity; "we never have any sensation of our effort" (BN 324). For MP the body is an *I can* whose spontaneous valuations already allocate sense to the mountain as "to be climbed." Without those valuations there would be no obstacles and therefore, as Sartre's own dream example shows, no freedom.

A third dispute is the Other. Sartre: the Look turns me into an object. MP: "the other's gaze does not transform me into an object" unless both gazes withdraw into thinking nature. See [[Concepts/The Look and the Other (Sartre)]], [[Concepts/Coexistence and the Other (Merleau-Ponty)]].

## What Is Not in Dispute

- No Humean liberty of indifference.
- No Leibnizian Adam whose essence contains the act.
- History is not a foreign power that uses us; it is lived.
- The intellectual's "pure act" of joining a class is suspect for both (MP is explicit; Sartre's later *Critique* will make the social the problem).

## Open

Whether BN's "situation is the common product" already *is* MP's gearing-into, stated more rhetorically. The texts do not collapse: BN keeps "wholly and forever free"; PP denies that "not refusing is a choice." The friendship later broke; that biography is not an argument.
