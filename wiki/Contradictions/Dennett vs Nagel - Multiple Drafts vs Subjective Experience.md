# Dennett vs Nagel — Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience

> The vault's central phenomenology contradiction. [[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]] (1974): consciousness has an irreducibly **subjective** character — there is something it is like for an organism — that no third-person physical or functional description captures; physicalism is not thereby refuted, but we have no conception of how it could be true. [[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]] (1991): that residue is a philosophical intuition generated by the Cartesian Theater; [[Concepts/Heterophenomenology (Dennett)]] studies reports without remainder, and the Multiple Drafts model explains them. Neither has persuaded the other. Nagel is now in the vault at primary-source strength.

## The Positions

### Nagel: Subjective Character, Unintelligible Physicalism

From "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974), now read in the 2024 Oxford reprint:

1. An organism has conscious mental states iff **there is something it is like** to be that organism — something it is like *for* the organism ([[Concepts/Subjective Character of Experience (Nagel)]]).
2. Functional, causal, and intentional analyses do not capture this. They are logically compatible with its absence (robots that behave like people and experience nothing). Nagel does not deny that conscious states cause behavior; he denies that this exhausts them.
3. The "point of view" is a **type**, not a private inner object. Phenomenological facts can be ascribed by someone similar enough to adopt the subject's point of view; they remain subjective because even that ascription requires first-person uptake. Privacy and incorrigibility are the wrong glosses.
4. Bat sonar makes the gap vivid: we can ascribe general types of experience but not the specific subjective character. Imagination only yields what it would be like for *us* to behave as bats. The same structure holds between humans and would hold for Martians conceiving us. There are **facts beyond the reach of human concepts**.
5. Physical reduction elsewhere is a move toward greater objectivity — leaving a species-specific appearance behind. Experience *is* the appearance. "What would be left of what it was like to be a bat if one removed the viewpoint of the bat?"
6. **He does not conclude that physicalism is false.** "It would be a mistake to conclude that physicalism must be false. … It would be truer to say that physicalism is a position we cannot understand because we do not at present have any conception of how it might be true." The "is" in "mental events are physical events" is deceptively clear without a theoretical framework.
7. Next method: an [[Concepts/Objective Phenomenology (Nagel)]] that describes subjective character without requiring empathy.
8. The 2024 preface corrects a standard misreading: this is a call to *expand* science, not to place consciousness beyond it.

The 2023 lecture ([[Concepts/Psychophysical Nexus and Neutral Monism (Nagel)]]) adds: natural language is conceptually dualist; zombie conceivability is therefore genuine; every reductionist analysis fails. He still **refuses property dualism** and hopes for monism (Spinoza, dual-aspect, neutral monism). The view is hostage to consciousness doing causal work.

Dennett's *Content and Consciousness* (1969) is already a named target in the 1974 notes.

### Dennett: Heterophenomenology Dissolves the Gap

In *Consciousness Explained* (1991), Dennett argues:

1. Nagel's argument rests on a **first-person-plural presumption**: that there is a stable, theory-independent, fully determinate "what it's like" that third-person science systematically misses.
2. That presumption has not been established. It is a philosophical intuition, not a datum. The history of phenomenology (Wundt vs. Husserl) shows first-person reports systematically diverge; there is no privileged access to "raw phenomenology" that bypasses interpretation.
3. **Heterophenomenology** studies what subjects report without presupposing that their reports capture intrinsic phenomenal properties. The "heterophenomenological world" is a theorist's fiction with the status of Sherlock Holmes's London.
4. We *can* investigate bat vs human consciousness in third-person terms: neural architecture, discriminations, preferences, how echolocation compares to vision. This doesn't give bat experience "from the inside" — but Dennett denies that "from the inside" picks out anything beyond what the third-person investigation recovers.
5. The intuition that something always escapes is exactly what the Cartesian Theater fosters. Without a Theater, there is no inner show the scientific description must match.
6. "We can investigate such questions [about bat experience]" — he does not claim to have fully answered them, but he denies they are in principle unanswerable.

## The Core Disagreement

| Issue | Nagel | Dennett |
|---|---|---|
| Is there irreducibly subjective phenomenology? | Yes — the "what it's like" is necessarily tied to a point of view (a type, not a private atom) | No — or at least: the presumption has not been established |
| Can third-person science capture it? | Not by the physical sciences' shared-observation model; science must expand ([[Concepts/Objective Phenomenology (Nagel)]]) | Yes — heterophenomenology studies reports neutrally and without residue |
| What is the bat question? | A principled limit on forming a conception without occupying the bat's point of view | A tractable (if difficult) third-person research question |
| What does the Cartesian Theater have to do with it? | Nagel does not use this framing; he also rejects privacy/incorrigibility, so he is a poorer fit for Dennett's four-feature qualia package than Jackson is | The intuition that something is "left out" is generated by the Theater |
| Is physicalism false? | Not shown false; presently unintelligible. 2023: hope for monism, not dualism | Physicalism is true once the Theater picture is dropped |
| Is this an argument for dualism? | Conceptual dualism, yes; metaphysical dualism, no (2023) | The subjective/objective distinction is not irreducible |

## The "Explaining Away" Objection

Nagel (and Chalmers, who sharpens the point into the hard problem) would say Dennett is not *explaining* consciousness but *explaining it away*: substituting a functional/behavioral account for the real thing. If the residue is stipulated away, the explanation is hollow.

Dennett's response (Ch. 14 of *Consciousness Explained*, "Consciousness Explained or Explained Away?"): **all successful explanations "explain away"** the mysterious formulation they displace. Explaining fire as rapid oxidation "explained away" phlogiston — it didn't leave a residue of inexplicable "fireness." The intuition of inexplicability is not evidence of genuine mystery; it is evidence of an expectation formed under the wrong theoretical picture (the Cartesian Theater). When Multiple Drafts is fully assimilated, the sense of mystery should dissolve.

Whether this succeeds depends on whether the intuition of unexplained phenomenology is itself a product of the Cartesian Theater (Dennett) or a genuine datum (Nagel, and Chalmers). This is the deepest unsettled question in contemporary philosophy of mind.

Primary Nagel adds a refinement the 1991-only page missed: he already agrees that a purely objective description will not "capture" the experience, and he does not treat that as a proof of dualism. The live fight is whether that leftover is (a) a datum requiring new concepts and possibly a new metaphysics (Nagel), (b) a further fundamental property (Chalmers), or (c) a bad picture (Dennett).

## The Third Voice: Chalmers, and Nagel's Fork from Him

[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]] inherits Nagel's phrase and the robot-without-experience remark, then takes a step Nagel 2023 refuses: from conceivability of zombies to [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)|property dualism]]. Nagel uses the same conceivability argument and still hopes for [[Concepts/Psychophysical Nexus and Neutral Monism (Nagel)|monism]]. So the non-deflationist side of this contradiction is no longer a single position:

- **Dennett / Clark**: dissolve the residue (type-A).
- **Nagel**: keep the residue; do not infer extra properties; look for a more basic kind that is inner and outer at once.
- **Chalmers**: keep the residue; add fundamental phenomenal (or protophenomenal) properties and psychophysical laws.

[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] lands with Dennett: the "what it is like" puzzle is a conceptual artifact of self-opaque self-models ([[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]]).

[[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]] (2014, now primary) is the experimentalist of the same Type-A camp: leftover phenomenal consciousness is a "slippery slope to dualism"; Chalmers "swapped the labels"; qualia will go the way of vitalism. The dedicated page is [[Contradictions/Dehaene vs Chalmers - Access Consciousness vs the Hard Problem]] — same residue fight, now with signatures and a clinic instead of Multiple Drafts. Unmarked tension with Dennett: GNW keeps a finish line.

## The Fourth Voice: Jackson (now primary)

[[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]] (1982, [[Sources/Epiphenomenal Qualia - Frank Jackson (1982)]]) is the author of the Mary case this contradiction has been citing second-hand, and of the epiphenomenalism Dennett's parity argument and Chalmers's [[Concepts/Paradox of Phenomenal Judgment (Chalmers)]] are both answering. He is not a third Nagel and not a first Chalmers:

1. **A missing property, not a missing point of view.** 1982 §III is written to *separate* the [[Arguments/Knowledge Argument (Jackson)|knowledge argument]] from Nagel. Jackson is not complaining that we fail to know what it is like to *be* Fred. He is complaining that a property of Fred's experience is left out of complete physical information. Physicalism "makes no special claims about the imaginative or extrapolative powers of human beings."
2. **Physicalism is false**, not merely unintelligible. That is the conclusion Nagel refuses and Dennett denies.
3. **The leftover may be idle.** [[Concepts/Epiphenomenal Qualia (Jackson)]] — certain properties of mental states make no physical difference; they may still affect other mental states; the subject is aware of them. Obvious causation, Darwin, and other-minds do not refute this. Our failure to understand the how and why is what selection predicts.
4. **The modal/zombie argument is weaker.** Jackson accepts it as a *consequence* of the knowledge argument, not as its ground, and notes that the modal intuition is disputed (Kirk recanted). Chalmers later inverts the ranking.

Dennett's four-feature attack (intrinsic, ineffable, private, directly apprehensible) lands here rather than on Nagel — as the 1991-only page already said — but the four-feature list is Dennett's reconstruction, not Jackson's. The live Jackson–Dennett fights are: (a) whether Mary with genuinely all physical information still learns a *fact*; (b) whether a property that makes no physical difference, including to the utterance "I see red," can be what that utterance is about. Jackson's replies are the "just obvious" premise plus common-cause (*Times*/*Telegraph*). Dennett's replies are the blue banana and the gremlins.

The non-deflationist side now has three distinct leftover-stories:

- **Nagel**: point of view; Physicalism unintelligible; 2023 monism hostage to causal work.
- **Jackson**: property / fact; Physicalism false; idle properties livable.
- **Chalmers**: further facts + psychophysical laws; epiphenomenalism a *worry*, escaped if possible via Russellian intrinsicality.

## Relation to the Broader Vault

- **Hofstadter vs. Searle** ([[Contradictions/Hofstadter vs Searle - Strange Loops vs Chinese Room]]): that debate is about whether formal computation can produce genuine *understanding*. This one is about whether science can capture genuine *experience*. Searle's biological naturalism is partly motivated by the Nagel intuition; Hofstadter is allied with Dennett's deflation.
- **Husserl vs Naturalism** ([[Contradictions/Husserl vs Naturalism — Phenomenal Consciousness and the Hard Problem]]): Husserl supplies an ontological version of Nagel's claim (Consciousness and Nature as distinct regional essences). Nagel's [[Concepts/Objective Phenomenology (Nagel)]] is a different method from Husserl's first-personal reduction.
- **Wiener vs. Shannon** ([[Contradictions/Wiener vs Shannon - Information and Meaning]]): Wiener's insistence that information is irreducibly semantic parallels Nagel's insistence that experience is irreducibly subjective.
- **Spinoza**: Nagel's 2023 monism is an explicit retrieval of [[Concepts/Attributes and Parallelism (Spinoza)|dual-aspect parallelism]] for specific central states.

## What Each Gets Right

**Nagel gets right**: the phenomenological intuition is real and powerful, and he is more careful than the textbook summary. He does not claim privacy, incorrigibility, or a proof that physicalism is false. He does claim that a reduction which never mentions subjective character has changed the subject. That claim has not been eliminated by Dennett — it has been diagnosed.

**Dennett gets right**: traditional phenomenology failed to converge; introspective reports are unreliable; the assumption of a fully determinate "what it's like" prior to all interpretation may be unwarranted. Heterophenomenology is at least a more disciplined method than taking first-person reports as foundational. His four-feature attack on "qualia" lands harder on Jackson than on the 1974 Nagel — and Jackson is now in the vault to receive it. The parity argument still has to be retargeted: Jackson's subject *is* aware of the quale.

**Jackson gets right**: the knowledge argument is a different complaint from Nagel's, and treating them as one "what it's like" package blurs a distinction he drew in 1982. The 1986 clarifications (facts about *others*, not imagination, not Leibniz's Law) further restrict what a reply has to answer. Whether the "just obvious" premise survives Dennett's "all physical information" stipulation is the unsettled question.
