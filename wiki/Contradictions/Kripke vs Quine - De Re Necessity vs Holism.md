# Kripke vs Quine - De Re Necessity vs Holism

> Quine treats necessity as a confused shadow of analyticity and de re modality as unintelligible (the "Socratizer"; 9 / the number of planets). Kripke treats necessity as a metaphysical primitive the model theory made clear, detachable from the a priori, and exemplified by ordinary names and scientific identifications.

## The Two Positions

**[[Thinkers/W.V.O. Quine]]** ("Two Dogmas," 1951; already primary). There is no sharp analytic/synthetic line. Necessity, as the empiricist needed it, lived on that line. Once synonymy and analyticity go circular, no statement is immune to revision, and talk of an object's essential properties (as opposed to properties it has under a description) does not get off the ground. Lecture I files Quine's "Socratizer" as *not* a theory of reference, and the 9 / number of planets example as the stock objection to de re modality.

**[[Thinkers/Saul Kripke]]** (*Naming and Necessity*, 1970/1980). "'A priori' and 'necessary'" are not interchangeable: one is epistemological, one metaphysical (Lecture I, p. 34). Identity is an internal relation; if `a` and `b` are rigid, `a = b` is necessary if true. Nixon might have lost; that is a claim about *Nixon*, not about whoever satisfies "the winner of 1968." "Gold is the element with atomic number 79" and "Hesperus is Phosphorus" are necessary a posteriori. Addendum (g): much of what philosophy calls mere physical necessity is "necessary tout court."

## Why This Is Not a Synthesis

Quine can grant that we *talk* as if Nixon might have lost and still hold that the talk is description-relative or to be reconstructed holistically. Kripke's claim is that the talk is in good order and that the "dark doctrine" of contingent identity between *objects* is a confusion (Preface, pp. 3–5). Relocating necessity to metaphysics does not answer Quine's demand for a non-circular account of analyticity — Kripke barely uses analyticity except stipulatively. Dissolving analyticity does not, for Kripke, touch de re essence.

Chalmers's later two-dimensional semantics is a *third* position: it keeps Kripkean a posteriori necessity as a necessary secondary intension and then claims those cases never rule out a conceivable world. That is not Quine, and it is not Kripke.
