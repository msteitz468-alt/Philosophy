# Free Will as Controlled Hallucination (Seth)
> Experiences of volition do not reveal an immaterial self intervening in physics. They are distinctive self-related perceptions — another controlled, and perhaps *controlling*, hallucination.

## Summary

Chapter 11 of *Being You* treats the **volitional self** as one more layer of [[Concepts/Self as Controlled Hallucination (Seth)|self-as-perception]]. "Free will is not an intervention in the flow of physical events in the universe." That "spooky" picture "invokes Cartesian dualism" and is "an incoherent solution to a problem that doesn't exist." Once it is gone, the determinism debate "doesn't matter at all." Seth is "agnostic about whether the universe is deterministic" and "still… a compatibilist."

Voluntary actions "neither *feel* random, nor *are* random." Three phenomenological marks:

1. I do what I want, though I did not choose the wants (Schopenhauer: "Man can do what he wills, but he cannot will what he wills").
2. It *feels* as if I could have done otherwise, though given the exact state of universe, body, and brain "I could not."
3. The cause seems to come from within.

The *feeling* of alternatives is for **future** similar-but-not-identical cases, not for replaying this one. Voluntary behaviour is competence to control many **degrees of freedom**, implemented as Haggard's what/when/whether network (including intentional inhibition). "This kind of freedom is both a freedom *from* and a freedom *to*. … It is not, however, freedom from the laws of nature."

Libet's readiness potential precedes reported intention: "bad news for spooky free will." Libet's "free won't" "doesn't work" — "a cute trick." Schurger: RPs are threshold-crossing fluctuations, "neural fingerprints of a special kind of controlled hallucination." Wegner's *Illusion of Conscious Will* is the named foil: the experience is a real, useful, self-fulfilling inference, not an illusion of conscious will.

"So is free will an illusion? … The correct answer… is, of course, 'It depends.'" Spooky free will is unreal (and "may not even qualify as being illusory"). The *experience* of volition is as real as colour perception. The *capacity* to execute and inhibit voluntary action is "not illusory at all."

On responsibility Seth is "attracted to" a competence-threshold view (with Dennett, vs Waller): past a threshold we *can* be held responsible. Whitman and tumour cases are posed, not solved.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Compatibilist, not illusionist, degrees-of-freedom.
- Determinism vs indeterminism is orthogonal once spooky intervention is rejected.
- Anarchic hand and akinetic mutism are clinical dissociations of the volitional layer.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: explicit ally. Degrees of freedom cite *Elbow Room* (1984); responsibility cites *Freedom Evolves* (2003) and Dennett & Caruso (2021). Same neighbourhood as Clark's later deflation: the puzzling quale (here: "I could have done otherwise") is a useful label, not a metaphysical extra.
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]**: **not named**. Closest: given the precise state of the universe I could not have done otherwise, plus Schopenhauer on unchosen wants. Sits nearer Spinoza's "freedom as understood necessity" than Kant's autonomy — but as a *perception of control*, not as intellectual love of God. See [[Contradictions/Free Will - Spinozan Necessity vs Kantian Autonomy]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: appears in ch. 10 only via Hohwy's FEP/conditions-of-existence analogy, **not** as autonomy. No two-standpoints discussion.
- **Libet**: data stand; they refute only spooky causation.
- **Wegner**: foil — illusionism about conscious will.
