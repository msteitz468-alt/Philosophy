# The Immoralist (Nietzsche)

> "I am the first immoralist: and by this I am the destroyer *par excellence*." *Ecce Homo*, "Why I am a Destiny" §2. The title is claimed, not conceded — and §4 defines it with unusual precision, which is why it belongs on its own page rather than inside the master/slave material.

## The Definition: Two Denials

"In reality, my title immoralist involves **two denials**" (§4):

1. **A denial of the "good man" as the highest type.** Not a denial that anyone is good, but of the ranking. This is the less decisive of the two by his own account.
2. **A denial of Christian, i.e. decadence, morality.** "Far more decisive."

The word does *not* mean "one who acts badly," and Nietzsche is at pains to block that reading. The immoralist is one who has stopped taking morality as the frame in which such questions are settled — which is why the position cannot be refuted by the observation that Nietzsche was personally gentle, and why "immoralist" is a *methodological* title. It stands to morality roughly as atheism stands to theology: a refusal of the terms, not a bad score within them.

## 1881 work, 1886 name (*Dawn*)

The title is 1888. The work is 1881. *The Dawn of Day* §103 already states **two denials of morality**: (1) La Rochefoucauld — moral motives are words; (2) Nietzsche — judgments *do* motive, but rest on *errors*. "I deny morality in the same way as I deny alchemy, *i.e.* I deny its hypotheses." He also denies *immorality* as a true reason to feel immoral: "We must learn anew… feel anew." §9: the free man who will depend on himself is *immoral* to the [[Concepts/Morality of Custom (Nietzsche)]]. §148: "When men no longer believe themselves to be evil, they cease to be so." The 1886 Preface then says "we immoralists and atheists" and names the formula [[Concepts/Faith in Morals (Nietzsche)|autosuppression of morals]]. Do not flatten the 1888 two denials (good man / Christian decadence) into the 1881 two denials (hypocrisy of motives / false hypotheses). Same campaign, different cuts. See [[Sources/The Dawn of Day - Nietzsche (Kennedy, Collection 2013)]].

The argument for the ranking-denial (§§4–5) is the sharpest thing in the chapter:

> "**The condition of the existence of the good is the lie.**"

And: "The good — they cannot create; they are always **the beginning of the end**. They crucify him who writes new values on new law tables." The good man requires that reality be other than it is in order to remain good; his goodness is therefore parasitic on a falsification, and his social function is to prevent the revaluation. §5 gives the mechanism: raising the herd animal requires devaluing the exceptional man as "wicked," so truth ends up housed among the despised. This is the *Genealogy*'s slave-revolt account restated as a psychology of the contemporary good citizen — see [[Concepts/Master Morality and Slave Morality (Nietzsche)]], though note that the terms *master* and *slave* do not appear in this chapter.

## Why Zarathustra

§3 supplies the strangest and best argument in the book, and it is the reason the Persian is the mouthpiece at all:

> "**Zarathustra created this most fateful of all errors — morality; therefore he must be the first to recognise it.**"

The historical Zarathustra invented the moral reading of the world (good and evil as the cosmic forces, translated into metaphysics as force, cause, end). But his own highest virtue was **truthfulness**. So the truthfulness he installed at the summit of morality is what eventually turns on morality and destroys it. Nietzsche's name for this:

> "The self-overcoming of morality through truthfulness, the moralist's self-overcoming of himself into his opposite — into me — that is what the name Zarathustra means to me."

This is the same engine the *Genealogy*'s third essay runs on — Christian truthfulness kills the Christian God, and then must ask what the will to truth itself means ([[Concepts/Ascetic Ideal (Nietzsche)]], [[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]]). Here it is given a mythic author and made the reason for a literary choice. Morality is not defeated from outside; it completes itself into its own negation. The structure is Hegelian in shape, which Nietzsche would have hated to be told — compare [[Concepts/Aufhebung (Sublation)]].

## What the Immoralist Is Not

§1 is unusually careful to fence off the religious reading, and *Ecce Homo* exists partly to prevent it:

- "**I am not a man, I am dynamite.**"
- "Religions belong to the rabble; after coming into contact with religious people I always feel that I must wash my hands."
- "**I refuse to be a saint; I would rather be a clown. Maybe I am a clown.**"
- "for nothing more false has ever existed than a saint."

He also grounds the discovery in instinct rather than argument: "I was the first to discover the truth and for the simple reason that I was the first who became conscious, to sense the lie as a lie. **My genius lies in my nostrils.**" And he absorbs politics into it: "The concept 'politics' then becomes entirely absorbed into the realm of spiritual warfare."

The indictment itself, §7 — "That which defines me, that which makes me stand apart from the whole of the rest of humanity is the fact that **I unmasked Christian morality**" — is expanded as: anti-nature raised to law, contempt for the instincts, the invention of "soul," the slander of sexuality and of selfishness, the elevation of "selflessness." Its agent is the priest, not humanity: morality is "the idiosyncrasy of decadents having the hidden desire to revenge themselves upon life — and being successful." §8 compresses it to a phrase: "**Morality conceived as Vampirism.**"

## How This Sits in the Vault

- **[[Concepts/Morality as Anti-Nature (Nietzsche)]]** — *Twilight*'s treatment; §7 here is the compressed 1888 restatement and the best single sentence for that page.
- **[[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]]** — the immoralist is the genealogist who has drawn the practical conclusion. Genealogy explains how the valuations arose; immoralism is the refusal to keep standing inside them afterwards.
- **[[Concepts/Death of God (Nietzsche)]]** — related but distinct, and the vault should not merge them. §8's "The concept 'God' was invented as the opposite of the concept 'life'" is a claim about the *function* of the concept, not about the historical event GS §125 announces.
- **[[Concepts/Sovereign Individual (Nietzsche)]]** — the *Genealogy* II figure described as "**beyond morality**" and entitled to promise is the immoralist's constructive counterpart; where "Why I am a Destiny" is entirely destructive, the sovereign individual is what the destruction is supposed to make room for.
- **[[Thinkers/Michel Foucault]]** — the closest twentieth-century heir of the *method* (unmask the moral as the historically produced), while declining the heroic first-person that carries it here.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

> [!warning] The §3 argument proves less than it claims, and the vault should say so. That truthfulness undermines morality shows morality to be *unstable*, not *false* — and the demonstration relies on truthfulness retaining its authority through the collapse of the system that installed it. Nietzsche needs truthfulness to survive as a value while denying that values of that kind have standing. This is the same problem [[Concepts/Perspectivism (Nietzsche)]] carries, and *Ecce Homo* does not address it.

> [!warning] Against [[Thinkers/Kant]] head-on: §7 names the Categorical Imperative (without naming Kant) among the instruments of anti-nature. Where Kant makes the moral law the one thing whose authority survives every critique, Nietzsche makes precisely the *self-legislating* character of that law the deepest form of the sickness — the decadent's revenge internalized until it feels like reason. See [[Concepts/Autonomy and Heteronomy of the Will (Kant)]], [[Concepts/Categorical Imperative - Universal Law Formulation (Kant)]].

> [!warning] Against [[Thinkers/Bertrand Russell]]'s verdict in the *History*: Russell concedes that no appeal to facts refutes Nietzsche and that his own case rests "not in an appeal to facts, but in an appeal to the emotions." §4's "the condition of the existence of the good is the lie" is the claim that argument is being asked to adjudicate. Recorded on [[Thinkers/Nietzsche]] already; this is where the disputed sentence actually lives.
