# Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)
> Greco-Roman *philosophia* is a choice of existence and a set of spiritual exercises, not a body of theory. Discourse *about* philosophy is not philosophy.

## Summary

[[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]’s governing claim, stated most cleanly in the title essay of *Philosophy as a Way of Life* (ch. 11): ancient philosophy is “a mode of existing-in-the-world, which had to be practiced at each instant, and the goal of which was to transform the whole of the individual’s life.” Wisdom remains an inaccessible ideal; the *philo-sopher* is a lover of wisdom, not a sage. See [[Thinkers/Socrates]].

The Stoics distinguish the parts of philosophy — logic, physics, ethics — as parts of *philosophical discourse*, required by teaching, not as parts of philosophy itself. Philosophy is “a unitary act, which consists in living logic, physics, and ethics.” Polemon: exercise yourself with realities, not dialectical speculations. Epictetus: a carpenter does not discourse about carpentry; he builds a house. “If one contents oneself with this [abstract] discourse, one does not do philosophy.”

Each school is a **form of life** defined by an ideal of the sage. To philosophize is a rupture with *bios* (everyday life); the philosopher is *atopos*, a stranger in the world. Theory is “never considered an end in itself; it is clearly and decidedly put in the service of practice.” Dogmas stay fixed; advanced work debates demonstrations, not first principles. The lived content of the three parts is the three disciplines of desire, action, and assent. See [[Concepts/The Three Disciplines - Perception, Action, and Will (Hadot)]] and [[Concepts/Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)]].

## How the Reduction Happened

Hadot names three causes of the modern theoretical reduction:

1. The philosopher’s “perpetual danger” of shutting himself in concepts.
2. The medieval split: spiritual exercises absorbed into Christian spirituality (see ch. 4); philosophy becomes *ancilla theologiae*. Hadot dates the first appearance of *systematic* philosophy to Suárez.
3. The university: professors training professors. “In modern university philosophy, philosophy is obviously no longer a way of life or form of life — unless it be the form of life of a professor of philosophy.”

**Adjacent, not the same cut — Schopenhauer 1851.** *Parerga* I “On university philosophy” is an earlier, angrier refusal of the professor’s trade: philosophy for hire versus love of wisdom; Socrates versus paid sophists; private means as almost a condition. Hadot is historical (ancient *askēsis*; schools as communal practice). Schopenhauer is polemic (genius versus rabble; teach only logic + Thales-to-Kant). Do not flatten. See [[Concepts/University Philosophy (Schopenhauer)]].

Early Christianity could present itself as a philosophy *because* philosophy was already a way of being. The Apologist–Origenist line and later monasticism (*philosophia*) imported the pagan exercises and thereby acquired “a specific style of life… which had been absent from primitive Christianity.” The final synthesis is “essentially Christian” (grace, humility, obedience). See [[Thinkers/Augustine]].

## Still Available

Hadot does not say academic philosophy *cannot* be a way of life. He says it usually is not. Descartes, Spinoza, Nietzsche, Bergson, Husserl “rediscovered” existential aspects. Detached from outmoded cosmology, the ancient schools remain usable **models of life**: Socratism, Platonism, Aristotelianism, Epicureanism, Stoicism, Cynicism, Skepticism. One need not choose exclusively; Stoicism and Epicureanism are “two opposite but inseparable poles” — tension vs relaxation, willing the instant vs enjoying it. See [[Concepts/The Present Instant (Hadot)]].

Wisdom as Hadot restates it: *ataraxia* + *autarkeia* + cosmic consciousness (the dilation of the self through universal nature). See [[Concepts/Ataraxia]], [[Concepts/View from Above and Cosmic Perspective (Marcus Aurelius)]].

## Adjacent: Confucius (Chin 2014), not a Hadot school

The *Analects* is a way of life that refuses theory (9.4: no conjectures, does not think he must be right) and trains speech, balance, and office through *Odes*, rites, and music (8.8, 16.13). That is practice, not a body of doctrine — close to Hadot's governing claim. It is **not** one of Hadot's Greco-Roman models. Confucius wants to be "put to use" in government; withdrawal is a live option he refuses (18.6–18.8). He "dare[s] not call [himself] a sage" (7.34), which tracks Hadot's *philo-sopher* who is not a sage, but the test is the city of Lu, not the Athenian marketplace. Do not enroll [[Thinkers/Confucius]] in [[Schools]] Hadot lists. See [[Schools/Confucianism]], [[Concepts/Li - Ritual Propriety (Confucius)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Socrates** — prototype: no system; irony and dialogue as exercise; justice must be lived. See [[Thinkers/Socrates]].
- **Stoics / Epicureans / Platonists** — rival conversions, shared structure (discourse vs life; present instant; view from above).
- **Ryan Holiday** — popularizes the thesis (“never what happened in the classroom”) via Hadot without the scholarly apparatus. See [[Thinkers/Ryan Holiday]].
- **Foucault** — agrees that antiquity is an art of living and that modernity forgot it; disagrees that the aim is a culture of the self. See [[Contradictions/Hadot vs Foucault - Care of the Self vs Cosmic Universalization]].
- **Wittgenstein** — later philosophy as therapy, not theory; Hadot reads language-games as forms of life and closes the interview on *Tractatus* 6.522. Contrast: Wittgenstein “leaves everything as it is”; Hadot’s exercises aim to *transform* vision and being. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as Therapy (Wittgenstein)]].
- **Seneca** — philosophy as medicine of the soul is the same claim in a clinical metaphor. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as Medicine (Seneca)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Clash with Foucault’s late ethics — see [[Contradictions/Hadot vs Foucault - Care of the Self vs Cosmic Universalization]].
- Holiday’s instrumental framing (obstacles as fuel for success) softens the claim that virtue is the only good. The way-of-life thesis does not license that softening.
- Whether university philosophy *can* be more than a professor’s form of life is left open; Hadot treats it as a standing danger, not an impossibility.
