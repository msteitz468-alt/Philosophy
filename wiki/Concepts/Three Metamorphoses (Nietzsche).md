# Three Metamorphoses (Nietzsche)
> How the spirit becomes a camel, the camel a lion, and the lion at last a child.

## Summary

The first discourse of *Zarathustra* Part I (Common 1909) is the book's psychology of the spirit. Three forms, in order.

**Camel.** The reverent load-bearer. It kneels and takes the heaviest: to humble oneself, to love those who despise us, to feed on the acorns of knowledge. Laden, it hastens into the wilderness.

**Lion.** In the loneliest wilderness the spirit becomes a lion. It will capture **freedom** and be lord in its own desert. Its last lord is the great dragon **"Thou-shalt"** — scales glittering with a thousand years of created values: "All values have already been created." The lion says **"I will."** It can utter a **holy Nay** and make room for new creating. It **cannot yet create new values**.

**Child.** "Innocence is the child, and forgetfulness, a new beginning, a game, a self-rolling wheel, a first movement, a holy Yea." For the game of creating, a holy Yea is needed. The spirit now wills its own will; the world-lost wins its world.

This is not a biography of Zarathustra and not a moral progress from humility to pride. The camel's reverence is a strength (it can bear). The lion's Nay is necessary and insufficient. The child's Yea is the form that can still create — the same "holy Yea" that Part III will have to sing after the thought of return almost chokes him. See [[Concepts/Eternal Recurrence (Nietzsche)]], [[Concepts/The Superman (Nietzsche)]].

## Key Claims

- Order is load-bearing: camel (bear) → lion (Nay / I will) → child (Yea / create).
- The dragon is inherited "Thou-shalt," not a person. A thousand years of values already created.
- The lion wins freedom *for* creating, not the values themselves.
- The child is forgetfulness and a first movement, not regression to immaturity.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — the opening table of Part I. Later discourses fill it: backworldsmen still need a beyond (failed camel piety); the creating one must be "free FOR WHAT?" (lion's question); the bestowing virtue is overflow (child's Yea as gift).
- **[[Concepts/Three Metamorphoses]] vs Hegelian [[Concepts/Aufhebung (Sublation)]]** — not a dialectic that preserves the earlier stages as cancelled-and-kept truth. The lion slays the dragon; the child forgets. Do not Hegelianize the sequence.
- **[[Concepts/Eternal Return as Selective Ontology (Deleuze-Nietzsche)]]** — Deleuze will read affirmation as transmutation of negation. The child's holy Yea is the nearest *published* image. It is still a metamorphosis of *spirit*, not an ontology of forces.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The lion cannot create. Reception that stops at "I will" (heroic No) misses the discourse's point.
- > [!warning] The child's forgetfulness is not [[Concepts/Amor Fati (Love of Fate)]]. The phrase is absent from this book. The Yea is formal kin, not the GS §276 vow.
