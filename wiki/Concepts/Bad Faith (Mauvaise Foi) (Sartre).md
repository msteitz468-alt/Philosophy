# Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi) (Sartre)
> A lie to oneself within the unity of a single consciousness: the metastable vacillation between facticity and transcendence that refuses to recognize either for what it is.

## Summary

### From *Being and Nothingness*, Part One ch. 2 (primary)

Consciousness can take negative attitudes toward itself; the essential case is **bad faith** (BN 47–48). The liar "is in complete possession of the truth" and hides it from the Other. "Bad faith then has in appearance the structure of falsehood. Only what changes everything is the fact that in bad faith it is from myself that I am hiding the truth" (BN 49). Deceiver and deceived are one consciousness. Total **translucency** makes cynical self-lying collapse; yet bad faith is "metastable," evanescent, and can be "the normal aspect of life" (BN 49–50).

**Against Freud.** Psychoanalysis "substitutes for the notion of bad faith, the idea of a lie without a liar" (BN 51). The censor that would split deceiver from deceived must *know* what it represses in order to repress it. "What does this mean if not that the censor is in bad faith?" (BN 53). Steckel's "frigid" cases are conscious distraction, not buried complexes. See [[Contradictions/Sartre vs Freud - Lucidity vs the Unconscious]].

**Patterns.** The **woman on a date** freezes the man's conduct as in-itself, enjoys his desire only as transcendence, and leaves her hand as a thing while she is "all intellect" (BN 55–56). Instruments of bad faith: facticity/transcendence, for-itself/for-others, the temporal ekstases. Human reality is "a being which is what it is not and which is not what it is" (BN 56–58).

The **waiter** "is playing at *being* a waiter in a café" (BN 59). He cannot *be* a waiter as the glass is a glass. "I am a waiter in the mode of being what I am not" (BN 60). Society's ceremony of trades (grocer, tailor, soldier) is the same structure. This is **not** a moral verdict on the waiter. It is the ontology of a role.

**Sincerity** ("be what one is") is the ideal of the in-itself. "What then is sincerity except precisely a phenomenon of bad faith?" (BN 63). The homosexual who says "I am not a paederast" *as a table is not an inkwell*, and the "champion of sincerity" who demands he constitute himself as a thing, are **both** in bad faith. "The essential structure of sincerity does not differ from that of bad faith since the sincere man constitutes himself as what he is in order not to be it" (BN 65).

**The faith of bad faith.** It is faith, not cynical lie: it decides not to demand too much evidence. "One puts oneself in bad faith as one goes to sleep and one is in bad faith as one dreams" (BN 68). To believe is already not-to-believe. Good faith flees disintegration *toward* the in-itself; bad faith flees the in-itself *by* disintegration (BN 69–70). "If bad faith is possible, it is because … consciousness conceals in its being a permanent risk of bad faith" (BN 70).

**Authenticity** is named once, in a footnote, as possible "self-recovery," "the description of which has no place here" (BN 70 n.). The treatise does not describe it. Welten's stronger claim — authenticity is *impossible as a state* — overreads the chapter: Sartre withholds the description; he does not demonstrate impossibility. The lecture will then *use* authenticity as the plane of moral judgment.

### From Welten (2020)

Welten prefers "against better knowledge" to "bad faith" because the phenomenon presupposes lucidity. He treats the waiter as non-moral and authenticity as never achieved. The first is BN; the second is Welten's gloss on the withheld footnote. Bourgeois morality as social ally of bad faith is Welten's emphasis; BN's nearest equivalent is the **spirit of seriousness** in the Conclusion (values written in things). See [[Concepts/Value as Lack and the Impossible Synthesis (Sartre)]].

## From the 1945/46 lecture (2026-08-12)

The lecture does not use *mauvaise foi* as a technical title. It talks of **self-deception**, **error**, **good faith**, **cowards**, and **scum** — and it is more willing to *judge* than Welten's BN reading allows.

**Self-deception as error (logical judgment).** "Any man who takes refuge behind the excuse of his passions, or by inventing some deterministic doctrine, is a self-deceiver." "It is also a self-deception if I choose to declare that certain values are incumbent upon me; I am in contradiction with myself if I will these values and at the same time say that they impose themselves upon me." The shrug "everyone will not do it" is the same figure: the lie implies the universal it denies. Sartre refuses a *moral* verdict at this level ("it is not for me to judge him morally") and still "define[s] his self-deception as an error." "The attitude of strict consistency alone is that of good faith."

**Moral judgment: cowards and scum.** Once freedom is seen as the foundation of all values, he will freedom for freedom's sake, and therefore the freedom of others. "Those who hide from this total freedom, in a guise of solemnity or with deterministic excuses, I shall call cowards. Others, who try to show that their existence is necessary, when it is merely an accident of the appearance of the human race on earth — I shall call scum." Neither can be identified except "upon the plane of strict authenticity." Content of morality is variable; a certain *form* is universal.

This is a real tension with the BN/Welten claim that authenticity is impossible as a state and that the waiter is not morally condemned. The lecture names authenticity as the plane of judgment and uses "good faith" as the name of consistency, not as a second-order bad faith. Keep both: the ontology of the waiter is still BN; the public ethics of cowards and scum is the lecture.

**Unused potentialities as the popular form.** The lecture's other emblem is the person who consoles himself: circumstances were against me; I was worthy of a great love, a great book, children I never had. "In reality and for the existentialist, there is no love apart from the deeds of love." Dreams, hopes, and expectations define a man "negatively, not positively." The coward of *Les Chemins de la liberté* horrifies because he is *guilty* of his cowardice, not because he was born with poor blood.

## Key Claims / Positions
- Bad faith = a lie to oneself in one consciousness; metastable; can be a style of life.
- The Freudian censor is itself in bad faith; there is no lie without a liar.
- Waiter, coquette, homosexual, champion of sincerity: patterns, not a vice-list.
- Sincerity and (BN) good faith are structurally bad faith (flight toward the in-itself).
- Authenticity is named and withheld; not demonstrated impossible.
- Lecture: hiding behind passions, determinism, or incumbent values is self-deception (error); cowards and scum are the moral names; good faith = strict consistency in willing freedom.

### How Sartre Differs From Prior Thinkers in the Wiki
- **vs Freud**: the censor argument is BN's own, not only Welten's — [[Contradictions/Sartre vs Freud - Lucidity vs the Unconscious]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]'s** authentic/inauthentic (*Eigentlichkeit*): BN withholds authenticity rather than describing resoluteness; cf. [[Concepts/Being-toward-Death and Authenticity (Heidegger)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- > [!warning] Conflicts with **Freud** on whether self-deception requires an unconscious — [[Contradictions/Sartre vs Freud - Lucidity vs the Unconscious]].
- > [!warning] The lecture's "good faith" / "strict authenticity" sits badly with BN's analysis of sincerity as itself bad faith and with the withheld description of authenticity. Two registers: ontological (waiter) and public-ethical (cowards, scum). Do not collapse them.
