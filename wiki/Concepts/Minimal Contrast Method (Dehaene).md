# Minimal Contrast Method (Dehaene)

> Make two trials differ only in whether the subject saw the stimulus. Consciousness becomes an experimental variable; the brain difference is the thing to explain.

## Summary

The "key idea that cracked wide open the doors to the supposedly inaccessible sanctuary of consciousness" is Bernard Baars's **minimal contrast**: "a pair of experimental situations that are minimally different but only one of which is consciously perceived." Combined with treating introspection as **raw data** (not as a research *method* for how the mind works), this turns the C-word from a late-1980s taboo into a sorting of trials.

Three ingredients, stated in the introduction and executed in Ch. 1:

1. Narrow the target to [[Concepts/Conscious Access (Dehaene)|conscious access]].
2. A "panoply of tricks" that render a stimulus visible or invisible at will.
3. Trust the subject's "I saw it / I didn't" as the *definiens* of the phenomenon — even when the experimenter can still see the image.

Behaviorism (Watson) is rejected for conflating two issues: introspection as a *method* (untrustworthy — Nisbett/Wilson stockings, choice blindness) versus introspection as *data* (indispensable). Out-of-body reports are not evidence of leaving the body; they are evidence of a genuine feeling whose neural source (Blanke: right TPJ) can be found.

## The Toolbox (Ch. 1)

| Paradigm | Contrast |
|---|---|
| **Troxler fading** | Fixed twelve dots around a cross pop in and out. "The objective stimulus is constant, but its subjective interpretation keeps changing." |
| **Binocular rivalry** (Wheatstone 1838; Leopold & Logothetis) | Two images, one per eye; perception alternates. Early V1/V2 encodes both; IT/STS tracks the report. Consciousness is a late winner-take-all. Continuous flash suppression holds one image unseen for long stretches. |
| **Attentional blink** | Encoding letter 1 makes letter 2 (~<500 ms) often unseen; the letter still enters early visual stages. |
| **Inattentional / change / choice blindness** | Gorilla, door-swap, swapped faces: attention as a contrast tool. Dehaene notes a forgetting/comparison alternative for change blindness. |
| **Masking** | Target + mask; threshold ~40–60 ms in adults. Even informed, trying viewers report nothing. Objective costs: naming, compare-to-5, no recognition after twenty masked repetitions. |

At threshold the stimulus is held constant and the brain does the switching — the cleanest contrast. The program that follows: search for [[Concepts/Signatures of Consciousness and Ignition (Dehaene)|signatures]] that index only the seen trials.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Baars (1989)**: named source of "minimal contrast."
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: [[Concepts/Heterophenomenology (Dennett)]] is the philosophical sibling — reports as data, not as incorrigible transcriptions. Dehaene adds that the *same* reports must be paired with neural events; folk causal stories (why I chose that stocking) can be "delusional" and still measure *what it felt like*.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: NCC research, including this method, only ever correlates process N with *awareness*. That is his [[Concepts/Coherence Between Consciousness and Awareness (Chalmers)|epistemic lever]] — and why he thinks the method cannot touch the hard problem. Dehaene thinks once access is explained, the leftover evaporates.
