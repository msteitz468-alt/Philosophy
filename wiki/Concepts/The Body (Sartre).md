# The Body (Sartre)
> "Being-for-itself must be wholly body and it must be wholly consciousness; it can not be united with a body." Three incommunicable dimensions: body-for-itself, body-for-others, body known by the Other.

## Summary

Part Three, Chapter Two of *Being and Nothingness* refuses the project of uniting "my" consciousness with the dissected organism. That organism is already *the Other's body*. "Either it is a thing among other things, or else it is that by which things are revealed to me. But it can not be both at the same time" (BN 304).

**First dimension: body-for-itself = facticity.** "We could define the body as the contingent form which is assumed by the necessity of my contingency" (BN 309). To be is to-be-there, an engaged point of view; "pure knowledge" without a point of view is contradictory. Lived space is Lewin's **hodological** space. Sensation is "a pure daydream of the psychologist" (BN 315). A sense is the objective orientation of a field; I cannot "see the seeing." I do not *use* my acting body as a tool (that yields infinite regress or a soul handling a machine). "I am my hand"; "The body is lived and not known" (BN 324). Unreflective consciousness **exists** its body (transitive); body-consciousness is non-thetic, "lateral and retrospective," the surpassed Past. Pain is the existed texture of an act; accessory reflection makes **illness**; **nausea** is "the dull and inescapable" perpetual revelation of facticity (BN 338). "To say that I have entered into the world … or that I have a body is one and the same thing" (BN 318).

**Second dimension: body-for-others.** The Other is first the one for whom I am object. His body is transcendence-transcended, a tool among tools, **flesh** as "pure contingency of presence," always *in situation*. Expression *is* the emotion: "These frowns, this redness … do not *express* anger; they *are* the anger" (BN 346–347). Anatomy and physiology start from the corpse. Character exists only for-others and is not distinct from the body.

**Third dimension.** I exist for myself as a body known by the Other. "My body's depth of being is for me this perpetual 'outside' of my most intimate 'inside'" (BN 352). The Look extends the lived outside; shyness, blushing, language, and **disease** (*maladie* — illness aimed at in its being-for-others) are this alienation. Seeing or touching my own limbs is a late, contingent adoption of the Other's standpoint and cannot found a theory of the body.

## Key Claims / Positions

- The for-itself *is* body; it is not united *with* a body.
- Three incommunicable dimensions; "double sensation" is two orders, not a bridge.
- Nausea is the non-thetic taste of facticity.
- Flesh is the Other's facticity as tasted; disease is illness for the Other.

### How Sartre Differs From Prior Thinkers in the Wiki

- **vs [[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: rejects two substances and "the soul is easier to know than the body." Union is not contingent; "the very nature of the for-itself demands that it be body" (BN 309).
- **vs [[Concepts/Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)]]**: agrees the body is not a known object and that action/perception are one in hodological complexes. Dreyfus (via Heidegger/Merleau-Ponty) makes the body absorbed skill and the hidden condition of intelligence. Sartre's body is *nihilated* facticity, nausea, and (under the Look) alienated object — "we never have any sensation of our effort … We perceive the resistance of things" (BN 324). Skillful fluency is not his theme.
- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]** is not named in this chapter. *Phenomenology of Perception* (now primary) gives the missing skillful body: habitual/actual layers, phantom as ambivalent presence, body schema, *I can*. Touching-touched is non-simultaneous, "a sort of reflexion," not Sartre's two incommunicable orders. See [[Concepts/One's Own Body and the Body Schema (Merleau-Ponty)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- The three dimensions are "incommunicable," yet the third is precisely their haunting of one another. The Look is the only passage.
