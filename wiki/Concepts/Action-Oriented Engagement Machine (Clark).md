# Action-Oriented Engagement Machine (Clark)
> The predictive brain is not an insulated inner inference engine. It is an action-oriented engagement machine: a node in dense reciprocal exchange that harvests its own sensory stream and locks onto affordances.

## Summary

This is the distinctive moral of *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016). The preface already refuses the secluded picture: "the predictive brain will stand revealed not as an insulated inner 'inference engine' but an action-oriented engagement machine." The Introduction makes that the book's thesis: predictive processing is "the perfect neuro-computational partner for recent work on the embodied mind." Chapter 10 repeats it as the result: transient neural assemblies recruit, and are recruited by, shifting webs of bodily and extra-bodily structure; "the resulting transient wholes are the true (frugal, efficient) vehicles of adaptive and behavioural success."

The claim is not that brains do not infer. It is that the *point* of prediction-error minimization is "the guidance of world-engaging action, not the production of 'accurate' internal representations" (6.1). Learned generative models deliver a grip on **affordances** — "the possibilities for action and intervention that the environment makes available to a given agent" (6.2) — not an action-neutral inner image. Perception and action are "two sides of a single computational coin" (Ch 10): both minimize sensory prediction error inside a circular causal flow in which action selects the next stimulation.

Clark's name for the specific architecture is **action-oriented predictive processing**. Motor cortex is not the inverse of visual cortex; both send top-down predictions (proprioceptive vs retinotopic). Motor commands are proprioceptive predictions cashed by spinal reflex arcs. Broader than that implementation: "perception, cognition, and action are manifestations of a single adaptive regime geared to the reduction of organism-salient prediction error" (4.11).

This is the 2016 reply to [[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]'s seclusion thesis. Hohwy treats PEM as forcing a skull-bound mind behind an "evidentiary boundary." Clark treats that as an **ambiguous appeal to inference**: PP inference need not yield reconstructive contents or an inner stand-in that "throw[s] away the world." It can pick frugal, action-involving procedures (the outfielder; optical acceleration cancellation). The Cartesian/Matrix demon is a "red herring" (6.8). See [[Concepts/Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)]] and [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].

## Key Claims / Positions

- **Not a representational veil.** "It is only the structured probabilistic know-how distilled from prediction-driven learning that enables us to see through the veil of surface statistics to the world of distal interacting causes itself" (6.2).
- **Not-indirect perception.** If a label is needed, Clark prefers this to "controlled hallucination" as a metaphysics of contact (6.9). Inner representations "function within us and are not encountered by us."
- **Affordances, not pictures.** Percepts are engagement parses. Many "joints" in nature are interaction-based. Frugal models are a *prerequisite* for contact, not a defect.
- **Circular causation.** Dewey-style loops: action continuously selects new sensory stimulations, folding environmental structure in. Precision-weighting sculpts which inner–outer coalition runs next.
- **Representations kept, but action-oriented.** Clark refuses a fully non-representational rewrite (9.11). What he keeps are "probabilistic and action-oriented through and through" — incomplete predictive codes, not action-neutral mirrors of a pregiven world.
- **Two stories.** A broad vision of multilevel probabilistic prediction could survive even if many details of hierarchical predictive coding fail (Introduction).

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]**: same Helmholtz/Friston mechanism; opposite geography. Hohwy 2013 already named Clark 2012/2013 as taking the framework "in different directions." This book is that direction, argued at length in Ch 6.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: PP is offered as the neurocomputational partner of world-involving, skillful coping — "not another 'new science of the mind'" but a meeting point for connectionism, Bayesian neuroscience, robotics, and the embodied mind. The 2016 text does not name Dreyfus; the structural debt is to that tradition (Gibson, Cisek, Pfeifer, Varela).
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: the Introduction's SLICE story is Dennett's — commanding a generative model *is* understanding hidden causes. 2016 uses Dennett for the mid-level model, not yet for 2023's strange-inversion deflation of qualia.
- **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: later names this book as the champion of *action-oriented* PP, then biologizes the same machinery (consciousness as bodily control). 2016 already uses Seth on presence and interoceptive inference (Ch 7).
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]] / Helmholtz**: hierarchical models can learn even "Kantian" hyperpriors from data (6.3). Experience is structured by the agent's own model, but the model is for engagement, not for a secluded inner theater.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Hohwy treats the sensory boundary as what identifies the agent. Collapse it and, on his account, PEM loses its subject. Clark treats that boundary as a "magical membrane" (Hurley, later) and the demon as a red herring. See [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].
- The 2016 slogan "controlled hallucination" is discussed and partly *rejected* as a metaphysics of what we perceive (6.2, 6.10). Do not collapse this page into [[Concepts/Controlled Hallucination (Clark)]], which was built from the 2023 popularization.
