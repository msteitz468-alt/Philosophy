# The Superman (Nietzsche)
> Man is a rope over an abyss. The meaning of the earth is not a better man. It is what surpasses him.

## Summary

*Also sprach Zarathustra* is the vault's primary. Thomas Common (1909) renders *Übermensch* as **Superman**. Kaufmann later used **overman**. Deleuze writes **Overman** and then **superfold**. None of those English words is Nietzsche's. Quote Common; keep the German in view.

The Prologue is the first teaching. After ten years on the mountain Zarathustra *goes under* (*Untergang*) to bestow overflowing wisdom. In the market: "I TEACH YOU THE SUPERMAN. Man is something that is to be surpassed." "The Superman is the meaning of the earth. Let your will say: The Superman SHALL BE the meaning of the earth!" Remain true to the earth; after God died, blasphemy of the earth replaces blasphemy of God. Man is "a rope stretched between the animal and the Superman—a rope over an abyss." What is lovable in man is that he is an **over-going** and a **down-going**. The market laughs and asks for the [[Concepts/The Last Man (Nietzsche)|last man]] instead.

He is not yet a specimen. Part II, "The Priests": "Never yet hath there been a Superman." Part I, "The Despisers of the Body": those who can no longer "create beyond" themselves are "no bridges… to the Superman." The friend is a "foretaste"; marriage is the will of two to create "the one that is more." The close of Part I: out of chosen solitaries a people, "and out of it the Superman." "DEAD ARE ALL THE GODS: NOW DO WE DESIRE THE SUPERMAN TO LIVE."

The motion toward him is **self-surpassing**, not moral improvement. Part II, "Self-Surpassing": life is "that WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF." That speech names the motion [[Concepts/Will to Power (Nietzsche)]]. The [[Concepts/Three Metamorphoses (Nietzsche)|child]] is the form that can still create. Part IV's [[Concepts/The Higher Man (Nietzsche)|higher men]] are explicitly *not* this figure: "The Superman, I have at heart; THAT is the first and only thing to me—and NOT man." They are bridges and failures. See that page.

## Key Claims

- *Übermensch* is a task and a meaning of the earth, not a biological type already on hand.
- Man is bridge, not goal; lovable as over-going and down-going.
- Remain true to the earth — against backworlds and superearthly hopes.
- Creating beyond oneself, even if one succumbs, is the motion; despisers of the body cannot do it.
- Never yet has there been a Superman (Part II). Higher men are not him (Part IV).
- Common's "Superman" is a translation. Do not write Kaufmann's "overman" or Deleuze's "superfold" back into 1883.
- *The Antichrist* §3’s “sort of superman” is a lucky accident in the past, never deliberately willed. Not this figure. See [[Concepts/Formula of Values (Nietzsche)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — here as the teaching that replaces God once the saint has not heard and the market wants the last man. Later *Ecce Homo* will call *Zarathustra* the greatest gift; that is reception relative to this text.
- **[[Concepts/Eternal Return as Selective Ontology (Deleuze-Nietzsche)]]** — Deleuze's Overman is a *superfold*: a new form when human forces meet "unlimited finity" (silicon, genetic code). Productive, not an exegesis of the Prologue. The Prologue's Superman is still a meaning *of the earth*, a bridge-end for man, not a post-human plane.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]** — reads Nietzsche as last metaphysician and the Overman as the last subject of will-to-power. The primary speech is "remain true to the earth," not a treatise on Being.
- **[[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]]** — groups Nietzsche with Romantic *Kraftmenschen*. The Prologue's figure is not a Carlyle hero; the market's joke is that they will *take* the last man and *give* Zarathustra the Superman.
- **[[Concepts/Junzi - The Gentleman (Confucius)]]** — a contrasting ideal of cultivated humanity *within* rites. Zarathustra's figure is what *surpasses* present man.

## The 1888 Corrections (*Ecce Homo*)

Nietzsche returns to the word twice, and both passages are corrective.

**Against the misreadings** ("Why I Write Such Excellent Books" §1). He lists the readings his own audience had already produced — the Superman as an *idealist* type, as a saint or genius, as **Darwinism**, as **Carlyle's hero-cult** — and treats each as an instance of the reader adjusting the text to his own image. The vault's existing warnings against the Deleuzian and Heideggerian back-formations therefore have Nietzsche's own precedent: the earliest distortions were contemporaneous. See [[Concepts/Style as Communication of a State (Nietzsche)]].

**Against the moral reading** ("Why I am a Destiny" §5). The sharpest sentence in the corpus on this figure, addressed to the good:

> "I think that you would call my Superman — **a devil!**… the Superman would be terrible in your eyes with his goodness."

This is the type's definition by its reception rather than its properties, and it belongs with [[Concepts/The Immoralist (Nietzsche)]]'s two denials: since the good man is not the highest type, what exceeds him must appear to him as wickedness.

**And the *Zarathustra* chapter §6** gives the figure a location rather than a description: "Here at every moment man is overcome and the concept 'Superman' becomes the greatest reality" — i.e. the Superman is what happens *in* the book's language, not a specimen the book predicts. Note that "amor fati" and "will to power" are both absent from that chapter. See [[Sources/Ecce Homo - Nietzsche (Collection 2013)]].
