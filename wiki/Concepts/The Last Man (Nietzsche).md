# The Last Man (Nietzsche)
> The most contemptible thing: a small earth, discovered happiness, and a blink. The market asks for *this* and will make Zarathustra a present of the Superman.

## Summary

The *locus classicus* is *Zarathustra*'s Prologue §5 (Common 1909). After the market laughs at the [[Concepts/The Superman (Nietzsche)|Superman]], Zarathustra changes tactic: "I will speak unto them of the most contemptible thing: that, however, is THE LAST MAN!"

The last men have no chaos left out of which a star could be born. The earth has become small, and they hop on it. They no longer despise themselves. They are clever and know everything that has happened. They still work, for work is a pastime — but they take care that the pastime does not exhaust. They have their little pleasure for the day and their little pleasure for the night: **"We have discovered happiness"—say the last men, and blink thereby.** "No shepherd, and one herd! Every one wanteth the same; every one is equal." Whoever feels otherwise goes voluntarily into the madhouse.

The market's response is the load-bearing joke of the Prologue: "Give us this last man, O Zarathustra,—make us into these last men! Then will we make thee a present of the Superman!" They understand the diagnosis as an offer. Zarathustra is "not the mouth for these ears." He turns from the people to **companions** — fellow-creators, not the herd's herdsman.

The type is not named again as a set speech. Part III, "Old and New Tables," recalls the last man against the good and just. Part IV's kings mention "the last men" in passing. The standing contrast in this book is last man vs Superman, then (Part IV) both vs the [[Concepts/The Higher Man (Nietzsche)|higher men]], who are failures on the way and still not the last men.

## Key Claims

- The last man is the *most contemptible*, not the most evil. He has no longing beyond man and no hour of great contempt.
- Herd-equality without a shepherd; discovered happiness; the blink.
- The market *prefers* him to the Superman and treats the Superman as a tip.
- This is not yet the soothsayer's "All is empty, all is alike, all hath been" (Part II) — that is exhausted sameness. The last man is satisfied smallness.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — the failed popular teaching of the Prologue. The later free-spirit and *Genealogy* "herd" vocabulary is kin; do not flatten the last man into slave morality. Slave morality still *creates* values (good/evil). The last man has stopped creating.
- **[[Concepts/Death of God (Nietzsche)]]** — GS §125's marketplace unbelievers laugh at the madman. Here the market laughs at Zarathustra and *asks* to be last men. Same square, different joke.
- **The soothsayer** (Part II) — "All is empty, all is alike, all hath been!" is the other face of the same exhaustion. The last man has found a comfort the soothsayer has not.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Do not write twentieth-century "last man" reception (Kojève, Fukuyama) into this speech. The primary is a small blinking herd that has discovered happiness.
- > [!warning] Higher men are not last men. They still suffer, still pray, still fail upward. See [[Concepts/The Higher Man (Nietzsche)]].
