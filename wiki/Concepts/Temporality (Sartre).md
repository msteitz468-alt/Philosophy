# Temporality (Sartre)
> The for-itself does not *have* a past, present, and future; it *temporalizes itself* by existing. Sartre accents the present ekstasis, "not … the future ekstasis as Heidegger does."

## Summary

Part Two, Chapter Two of *Being and Nothingness* treats time as an organized structure, not a series of nows. "The past is no longer; the future is not yet; as for the instantaneous present … this does not exist at all" (BN 107). Past, present, and future are *Unselbständig* moments of one synthesis.

**Past.** Only the for-itself *is* its past; it does not "have" one. The in-itself cannot have a past (hysteresis is present molecular structure). "It is through the for-itself that the past arrives in the world." "Was" is an ontological leap: I am my past (responsibility; no dissociation except as secondary) *and* I am not it (I *was* it). The past is in-itself that I am *as surpassed*. "'Facticity' and 'Past' are two words to indicate one and the same thing" (BN 118). "The Cartesian *cogito* ought to be formulated rather: 'I think; therefore I was'" (BN 119). Death "changes life into Destiny": the for-itself slips wholly into the past as in-itself.

**Present.** Present is *presence to* being-in-itself, not an instant. Presence is an internal negative bond: the for-itself as witness of itself as *not* that being. "The Present is not"; it is flight from the being it was toward the being it will be (BN 123).

**Future.** The future arrives only through a being that *is* its own future. It is not a now-not-yet and not a representation. It is lack — the possible I have to be and can not-be. "The Future is not, it is possibilized" (BN 129). Freedom gnaws it; anguish is being the future only problematically. "To be free is to be condemned to be free" (BN 129).

**Ontology.** Static order (before–after) cannot be rebuilt from in-itselfs plus God or a Kantian *Ich denke*, nor dissolved into Bergsonian/Leibnizian given continuity. "Temporality is not, but the For-itself temporalizes itself by existing" (BN 136). It is a diasporatic quasi-multiplicity: (1) not-be what it is, (2) be what it is not, (3) both in one referring. Sartre *accents the present ekstasis*, "not … the future ekstasis as Heidegger does" (BN 142). Dynamic "change" is one phenomenon: the upsurge of a new present *making-past* the present it was. Death is "final arrest of Temporality," recapture of human totality by the in-itself (BN 147).

**Original vs psychic temporality.** Original temporality is non-thetic enduring. Psychic duration (joy after sadness; states as tapestry motifs) is the object of *impure* reflection: a virtual in-itself shadow (Ego, qualities, states, acts), magic action at a distance — Bergson's duration as hypostasis. Pure reflection is a katharsis. "Reflection is a second effort by the for-itself to found itself" (BN 154). Impure reflection is "in bad faith" as "intuition of the for-itself in in-itself"; the psychic fact is "the shadow of the reflected-on" (BN 161). The *cogito* is not instantaneous.

Part Two, Chapter Three then derives **universal time** as the reflection of this ekstatic structure onto a-temporal being: "UNIVERSAL time comes into the world through the For-itself" (BN 204). Motion is contingent "exteriority-to-self."

## Key Claims / Positions

- Time is the intra-structure of a being that is its own nihilation, not a container of nows.
- Past = facticity I have to be; Present is not; Future is possibilized.
- Accent on the *present* ekstasis, against Heidegger's privilege of the future.
- Impure reflection constitutes the psyche as a virtual in-itself (and is already bad faith).

### How Sartre Differs From Prior Thinkers in the Wiki

- **vs [[Concepts/Temporality (Heidegger)]]**: uses ekstasis and quotes Dasein as "always infinitely more than … its pure present" (BN 125), then inverts the accent. Time is not the meaning of Being but the intra-structure of nihilation. Past is not authentic having-been so much as in-itself/facticity.
- **vs [[Thinkers/Aristotle]] / vulgar now-time**: the series of nows is derivative; Sartre agrees with Heidegger that far.
- **vs Bergson**: duration as given continuity is the hypostasis of impure reflection.
- **vs [[Concepts/Cogito (Descartes)]]**: "I think; therefore I was."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Which ekstasis is primary — Heidegger's future or Sartre's present — is a live fork inside the vault's two phenomenological ontologies.
- Death as "final arrest" and "Destiny" sits next to [[Concepts/Being-toward-Death and Authenticity (Heidegger)]]; Sartre's death is the in-itself's victory, not Dasein's ownmost possibility.
