# Psychophysical Nexus and Neutral Monism (Nagel)

> Nagel's 2023 constructive guess: natural language is conceptually dualist, but the metaphysics is probably monist — conscious mental properties and their neurophysiological conditions are inseparable aspects of a more basic kind we do not yet have a concept for.

## Summary

The 2023 lecture "Further Thoughts: The Psychophysical Nexus" (ASS C; fuller version in *Concealment and Exposure*, 2002) is Nagel's attempt to imagine a *positive* solution that takes [[Concepts/Subjective Character of Experience (Nagel)|subjectivity]] seriously. It is avowedly speculative.

**Conceptual dualism.** Ordinary language distinguishes mental and physical events and implies they are different in kind. From that surface, Descartes's conceivability argument follows: we can clearly conceive a physical organism behaviorally and chemically indistinguishable from us with no inner life — a zombie. If conceivability is evidence of possibility, the mental is something in addition to the physical.

**Reductionist analyses fail.** Ryle, Smart, Putnam, and functional-role or criterial analyses try to show that the surface of mental concepts is misleading. Nagel's reply is the 1974 point restated: there is "no contradiction between an organism's satisfying any behavioral, functional, physiological, or chemical description, however elaborate, and its having no inner consciousness at all."

**Resist metaphysical dualism anyway.** He rejects Cartesian substance dualism *and* property dualism (mental properties distinct from all physical properties). The empirical pattern of systematic mind–brain correlation makes monism attractive. The conceivability argument would then fail the way other incomplete-concept arguments fail: the mind–brain relation is necessary, but our split concepts make it *seem* contingent.

**What the monistic concept would have to be.** It cannot be introduced by definition ("an X that is both mental and physical"). It would have to emerge as part of an explanatory theory of an expanded natural order, by inference to the best explanation of the mental, the physical, and the correlations. Physical micro-reduction (temperature and pressure as kinetic energy) is not the model. Both the mental and the physical would be identified with something that is neither, in the usual sense, but that necessarily has both as essential aspects. Historical names: Spinoza, **neutral monism**, **dual-aspect theory**.

**The concrete guess.** Familiar contingent links between consciousness and peripheral input/output (sense organs, muscles, tendons) are a clue to deeper, *unmediated* links in the CNS. Conscious experiences have something analogous to essential behavioral manifestations there: they *are* what the immediate causes of those manifestations feel like from the inside. Pain's typical causes and avoidance-effects are mediated and contingent; the central state at the meeting point of those pathways is not merely physical — it has a subjective inside unique to that constellation of dispositions. Same structure for thirst. Experience and physical state are necessary and sufficient for each other; neither causes the other. They have the same causes and effects because they are the inner and outer aspects of one state, incompletely described by exclusively mental or exclusively physical concepts.

**Relation to functionalism.** The view *uses* functionalism's causal-role facts — they are why we can have public mental concepts — but refuses to treat them as an analysis of those concepts. The intrinsic nature is mental from the inside and physiological from the outside, and that nature is what ultimately explains the functional roles.

**Hostages and limits.** The states would be identified only by theoretical inference, not defined as a conjunction of independently given mental and physical components. Composition would need a new theory of psychophysical parts and wholes (not just smaller brain chunks). If the monistic properties go all the way down, the fundamental constituents are neither physical nor mental. "This position is not equivalent to panpsychism. Panpsychism is, in effect, dualism all the way down. This is monism all the way down."

The view is **hostage** to a radical empirical condition: if the entire physical course from cause to behavior can be explained with only physical concepts and laws, there is not sufficient reason to prefer monism to property dualism plus epiphenomenal psychophysical laws. So consciousness, or something that entails it, must be essential to the explanation of behavior. The most promising place to look is **affect** — bodily pleasure and pain, hunger and thirst — where the link with behavior is most direct (citing Mark Solms, *The Hidden Spring*, 2021: affect is sufficient for consciousness; other functions are conscious when enlisted in its service).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]** ([[Concepts/Attributes and Parallelism (Spinoza)]]): the named ancestor. Thought and extension as attributes of one substance, not two things in interaction. Nagel wants a *scientific* version of that structure for specific central states, not a finished metaphysics of Substance.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: nearest neighbor and clearest fork. Chalmers also starts from zombie conceivability and the failure of functional analysis. He accepts [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)|property dualism]] and extra psychophysical laws; Nagel treats that as the option monism is supposed to beat. Chalmers's [[Concepts/Double-Aspect Theory of Information (Chalmers)]] (experience as information from the inside) is the 1996 construct closest to Nagel 2023; Chalmers is less confident of it than of dualism, Nagel less confident of dualism than of the monist *direction*.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: would read the whole program as Theater metaphysics. Nagel's "subjective inside of a causal constellation" is, for Dennett, exactly the inner show [[Concepts/Qualia Disqualified (Dennett)|Ch. 12]] is written to eliminate. The hostage clause — monism dies if a closed physical story of behavior is available — is the point Dennett thinks is already in.
- **[[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]]**: the hostage clause names Jackson's combination — property dualism plus [[Concepts/Epiphenomenal Qualia (Jackson)|epiphenomenal psychophysical laws]]. Jackson's 1982 §IV is written to make that combination livable (common cause, polar-bear coats, sea-slugists). Nagel will not take it.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: Clark's interoceptive, affect-first picture of sentience ([[Concepts/Interoception and Constructed Emotion (Clark)]], Solms-adjacent) overlaps Nagel's recommended starting point, then draws the opposite moral (deflate the residue).
- **Davidson**: already flagged in the 1974 essay as arguing that mental events must have physical descriptions without a general psychophysical theory. Nagel takes the gesture and denies that we yet have a conception corresponding to it.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Nagel vs Chalmers on the inference from conceivability: both accept conceptual dualism; only Chalmers treats it as settling the metaphysics. See also [[Arguments/Zombie Argument (Chalmers)]].
- > [!warning] Nagel vs Dennett on whether a "subjective inside" of a central state is a coherent posit — [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]].
- The hostage clause is the same pressure as Chalmers's [[Concepts/Paradox of Phenomenal Judgment (Chalmers)]] and Dennett's parity argument. Nagel is unusually explicit that if physics already explains the behavior, monism has no empirical edge.
