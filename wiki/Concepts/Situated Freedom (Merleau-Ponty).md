# Situated Freedom (Merleau-Ponty)
> "There is never determinism and never an absolute choice; I am never a mere thing and never a bare consciousness." Freedom does not destroy the situation; it gears into it.

## Summary

Part Three, Chapter III first reconstructs a doctrine of **total freedom** and then refuses it. The reconstruction: "If I am free, even once… I must be free continuously"; "we cannot be partially free"; "All things are within our power, or none of them are." "Our freedom, it is said, is either total or non-existent." That is "the dilemma of objective thought and its accomplice, reflective analysis."

Such a freedom **cancels doing**. Merleau-Ponty names [[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]] once: "As J.-P. Sartre himself shows, the dream excludes freedom because… there are no obstacles and there is nothing *to do*." Without spontaneous valuations of the sensible world there would be no obstacles, only representations of a universe. "There is an autochthonous sense of the world that is constituted in the exchange between the world and our embodied existence and that forms the ground of every deliberate *Sinngebung*."

**Sedimentation.** Pain and fatigue are not causes that act on freedom; they express an attitude toward the world. After twenty years of an inferiority complex, "it is not *likely* that I would change." The "likely" is not a reified fiction; it is a phenomenon of a being who is situated. **"Our freedom does not destroy our situation, but gears into it":** the situation "calls forth privileged modes of resolution" and "by itself, lacks the power to procure any of them."

**Class prior to class consciousness.** Neither objective thought (class from economic position) nor idealist reflection (class from the decision to see history as class war) reaches the phenomenon. "'I exist as a worker' or 'I exist as a bourgeois' first," and that lived mode "motivates both my revolutionary or conservative projects and my explicit judgments." "I do not choose to experience it this way." Revolution is born "day to day, from the interlocking of immediate ends"; it is an **existential project**, not an intellectual positing of ends. "We give history its sense, but not without history offering us that sense."

**Not-refusing is not a choice.** "I can no longer pretend to be a nothingness and to choose myself continuously from nothing." "But not refusing is not a choice." That sentence is the unnamed reply to Sartre's "not to choose is… to choose not to choose." We are always in the plenum; silence is still a modality of the sonorous world. "We choose our world and the world chooses us." Husserl is cited for a **"field of freedom"** and a **"conditioned freedom"** — not because freedom is absolute inside a line and zero outside, but because, like the perceptual field, it has immediate and more distant possibilities. "There is no freedom without some power."

The book ends in presence, not in a Hegelian State: the synthesis of in-itself and for-itself "is accomplished at each moment before our eyes in the phenomenon of presence, only it must be immediately started over." "I am a psychological and historical structure." "I am free, not in spite of or beneath these motivations, but rather by their means." Last words (Saint-Exupéry, named only in the note): "Man is a knot of relations, and relations alone count for man."

The Preface's formula **"condemned to sense"** is not repeated in this chapter. The Freedom chapter's formulas are gearing-into, conditioned freedom, and "never determinism and never an absolute choice."

## Key Claims / Positions

- Total-or-none freedom is the same error as objective thought.
- Freedom gears into a sedimented situation; "it's unlikely" is a phenomenon.
- Class is lived before it is judged.
- Not-refusing is not a choice.
- Conditioned freedom / field of freedom (Husserl).

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: freedom is the last of the three Part Three studies (cogito, time, freedom) that rewrite being-for-itself as being-in-the-world.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: "He is wholly and forever free or he is not free at all" (BN 441) is the reconstructed target. Situation in BN is the common product of given and freedom, but freedom remains total. See [[Concepts/Radical Freedom and Situation (Sartre)]], [[Contradictions/Merleau-Ponty vs Sartre - Situated Freedom vs Radical Freedom]].
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: "field of freedom" / "conditioned freedom" is the named ally.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: history does not walk on its head *or* its feet; class consciousness is neither deduced from the economy nor decreed by a will.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: the prisoner who will not speak still lives under a gaze, "a certain mode of *Mit-Sein*."
