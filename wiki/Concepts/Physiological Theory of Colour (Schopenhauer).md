# Physiological Theory of Colour (Schopenhauer)
> Colour is the qualitatively divided activity of the retina. Goethe delivered the data; this theory says what colour *is*. Newton looked in the light for what belongs in the eye.

## Summary

The vault's primary is the 1854 second edition of *On Vision and Colours* (composed 1815, printed Easter 1816; Latin *Theoria colorum physiologica*, 1830). See [[Sources/On Vision and Colours - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2012)]].

Brightness, darkness, and colour, taken strictly, are **states of the eye**, immediately sensed. A theory of colour must start with this *effect* and only then ask after the external cause. Newton's "fundamental mistake" was hurrying to the cause without knowing the effect's inner relations. Goethe's *Zur Farbenlehre* (1810) broke Newton's *Irrlehre* and supplied "data for a theory of colour" — not the theory. He postulated colour as *Erscheinung* and showed how it comes about, not what it is.

Sensibility is never pure passivity but reaction. The eye's unique reaction is the **activity of the retina**. Light (or white, treated as the same effect) puts the retina in **full activity**; darkness or black is inactivity. That activity can be divided three ways:

1. **Intensive** — degrees toward darkness: half-shade / gray.
2. **Extensive** — different spots of the retina at once (Goethe's black-cross after-image is exhaustion, not spontaneity).
3. **Qualitative** — colour. A colour and its physiological spectrum are complementary halves of the full activity. After the stimulus is removed the unused half follows of itself, because the retina tends to complete itself.

> "Colour is the qualitatively divided activity of the retina. The difference of colours is the result of the difference of the qualitative halves into which this activity can be separated and of their ratio to one another." (§5)

Hypothetical but (he says) intuitively forced fractions of full activity:

| Colour | Fraction | Complement | Fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| yellow | ¾ | violet | ¼ |
| orange | ⅔ | blue | ⅓ |
| true red (Goethe's purple / carmine, not Newton's prismatic yellowish-red) | ½ | perfect green | ½ |

Red and green are "colours *par excellence*." The six named colours are the simplest rational bipartitions, as the scale marks the rational frequency ratios; the number of colours is infinite. Black and white are **not** colours (no fractions). Colour is always a **duality** — one may not speak of a single colour, only of pairs. An odd number of primitive colours (Newton's seven, or five, or three) is a "double absurdity."

This qualitative bipartition is a **polarity** of the retina: two phenomena opposed at the species level, identical at the genus; neither posited nor abolished without the other; their union is the disappearance of both. Unlike magnetism, electricity, and galvanism, retinal polarity is **successive** (in time), and the indifference-point is alterable. Notation: **+** red, orange, yellow; **−** green, blue, violet. Red tires the eyes; green rests them. Do not relabel this "opponent-process theory." That name is not his.

From polarity he derives *a priori* Goethe's **skieron**: colour is always brighter than black and dimmer than white, because one half's appearance is conditioned by the other's inactivity (darkness). Gray is a "mechanical mixture" of light and darkness; colour is a "chemical fusion." The *skieron* in a present colour *is* what later appears as its spectrum.

**§9.** Energy vs paleness/dimness is leftover **undivided remainder**. Maximum energy (equator of Runge's colour sphere) = pure qualitative division, no remainder. Remainder fully active → pale toward white; at rest → dim toward black.

**§10.** Complementary colours reunited **on the same retinal spot** restore full activity = white. `red + green = white`. Only a **physiological pair**, stimulated separately (not mixed as matter). Mixing chemical colours leaves a material *skieron* (gray "death's head"). Prismatic colours can yield white because their *skieron* is pale enough to vanish.

Here he differs from Goethe in two points only, "for which latter, Goethe never forgave me" (§14): (1) polarity belongs to the **retina**, not to light; (2) white *is* produced from colours. Goethe "went too far" in denying it, to save the *skieron* against Newton. Newton taught white-from-colours only in the false sense (seven homogeneous lights recomposed). "To Newton's credit one can say no more than that by accident he had come near to expressing the truth." The fact "was asserted by Newton on incorrect grounds and in service of a false theory, but was denied by Goethe in connection with an otherwise correct system of facts." Production from **two** proves impossibility from **seven**. The achromatic refractor is Newton's factual refutation; its proximate cause is two complementary pairs superimposed.

**§13.** Colour in the strictest sense is an affection of the eye — the first and essential part of colour theory. External causes are subordinate stimuli (friction : electricity). **Physical colours** (law-governed, any turbid medium) vs **chemical colours** (incomprehensible surface modification). Newton inverted the order: reduced physical to chemical. The true **urphenomenon** is not Goethe's turbidity but "the retina's organic capacity to divide the activity of its nerves into two qualitatively opposed halves."

This is representation-side physiology, not noumenal Will. See [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]]. Chapter I is the forerunner of [[Concepts/Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)]].

## Key Claims

- Colour is a function of the retina, not a part of the ray of light and not a real property packed into bodies.
- Method: know the effect (sensation) before the cause. Physiology first; physical and chemical colours are subordinate.
- Full activity (light/white) vs inactivity (darkness/black). White and black are not colours.
- Three divisions of retinal activity: intensive, extensive, qualitative.
- Complements and fractions as above. Colour always a pair; infinite colours; six named colours as simplest rational bipartitions.
- Polarity is successive and retinal, not a polarity of light.
- *Skieron* is the unused half at rest. Gray ≠ colour.
- White from two physiological complements on one retinal spot. Goethe denied too much; Newton was right only in wording.
- True urphenomenon = the retina's capacity to split. Goethe remains the champion of the facts.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — his own colour theory, 1816/1854. Completes Goethe's observations with a retinal mechanics. Self-position: student and champion, not epigone. The 1851 *Parerga* II colour chapter was a storehouse folded back into this edition.
- **Goethe** — named interlocutor, not a vault thinker page. Broke Newton; delivered data and the *skieron*; stopped at the urphenomenon. Corrected on polarity and on white-from-colours. Do not make him co-author of the fraction/polarity theory.
- **Newton** — named opponent, not a vault thinker page. Sought in light what belongs in the eye; seven occult rays from preference for the scale; prismatic spectrum has four colours, not seven. Distant presentiment only (divided ray → divided retinal activity).
- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]** — "most thoroughly" taught that colours clothing objects are in the eyes. Presentiment only. Schopenhauer does not stop at a power in the body: colour *is* a polar, fractionable function of the retina. See [[Concepts/Primary and Secondary Qualities (Locke)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — 1854 preface: the subjective essence of colour is a primer for the a priori subjective forms of all cognition. Copernicus and Kant are the models for the object→subject turn (§8: "Know thyself").
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]** — *Dioptrics* ch. 1 cited, with Locke and Sextus, for colours being in the eyes alone.
- Do not weld later opponent-process physiology, qualia talk, or Wittgenstein's colour-language (Stahl's introduction, not this text).
