# Tacit Cogito (Merleau-Ponty)
> The Cartesian *Cogito* is spoken and second-hand. Beneath it there is a tacit cogito — and it is only a *cogito* when it has expressed itself.

## Summary

Part Three, Chapter I takes up Descartes as both an argument and a cultural object. "I am thinking of the Cartesian *Cogito*, wanting to finish this work, sensing the coolness of the paper under my hand, and perceiving the trees of the boulevard through the window." An idea is a field of latent intentions, not a transparent thing.

The *Cogito* one reads in the *Meditations* is **"a second-hand *Cogito*"**: "I have only grasped my thought and my existence through the medium of language," so the true formula of that spoken act would be "'One thinks, one is' [*On pense, on est*]." It "fails to reach its goal." The split of doubtful things vs. indubitable *pensée de voir* "is untenable." "In whichever way we understand the 'thought that one is seeing,' it is only certain if actual vision is certain as well." If I have an absolute consciousness of myself, "the plurality of consciousnesses is impossible."

**Beyond the spoken cogito there is a tacit cogito** — an experience of myself by myself, prior to every philosophy. "But this indeclinable subjectivity has but a fleeting hold upon itself and upon the world." **"The tacit *Cogito* is only a *Cogito* when it has expressed itself."** Expression never exhausts it, any more than reflection exhausts the unreflected. The primordial 'I' is not a thing for not being transparent; what is absent is the illusory transparency of objective thought. "I am a field, I am an experience." "The world is entirely on the inside, and I am entirely outside of myself."

The 1946 lecture slogan **"all consciousness is perceptual" is not a sentence of this chapter.** Closest: "Consciousness is entirely transcendence"; "In the present and in perception, my being and my consciousness are one."

This is not Sartre's [[Concepts/Pre-reflective Cogito (Sartre)|pre-reflective cogito]]. Sartre's is the non-positional self-consciousness that is the *condition* of the Cartesian cogito and is not fleeting: every positional consciousness of an object *is* consciousness (of) itself. MP's tacit cogito is pre-personal, indeclinable, needs speech to *be* a cogito, and has only a fleeting hold. Do not equate them.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Spoken / cultural *Cogito* ≠ tacit cogito.
- Tacit cogito is only a cogito in expression; the hold is fleeting.
- Certainty of seeing is not detachable from the seen.
- Subjectivity is a field, not an inner man.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: dissolves the Cartesian problem of union by making the perceiving mind an embodied mind. He later criticizes the tacit cogito (*The Visible and the Invisible*); that recantation is not in this book.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the *Meditations* remain a second-hand *cogito* that points toward the tacit one. See [[Concepts/Cogito (Descartes)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: the meditating Ego is kept as beginner, not as absolute density. Reduction cannot be complete because reflections *sich einströmen*.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: pre-reflective cogito is lucid and total; MP's tacit cogito is anonymous and incomplete.
- **[[Thinkers/Augustine]]**: the Preface already refused "truth dwells in the inner man." There is no inner man; "man is in and toward the world."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- If the tacit cogito is only a cogito when expressed, is there a pre-linguistic subject at all, or only a field that speech retroactively names?
- Merleau-Ponty's own later dissatisfaction is a hole this ingest cannot close.
