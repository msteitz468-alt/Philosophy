# Parity Principle (Clark and Chalmers)
> If a process would count as cognitive were it in the head, it counts as cognitive when it is in the world.

## Summary

The **parity principle** is the load-bearing criterion of [[Sources/The Extended Mind - Andy Clark and David Chalmers (1998)]]. Stated in §2 after the Tetris cases:

> If, as we confront some task, a part of the world functions as a process which, were it done in the head, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is (so we claim) part of the cognitive process.

(Clark's later popular wording in *The Experience Machine* is almost identical, adding "for that time.") The slogan that follows is "Cognitive processes ain't (all) in the head!"

Parity is a **functional** test, not a metaphysical inflation of "the world." It does not say every worldly process is cognitive. It says: do not use *location* as a primitive reason to withhold the label. Skin and skull "cannot simply [be] point[ed] to… as justification, since the legitimacy of that boundary is precisely what is at issue."

## How It Is Applied

**Tetris / implant.** Mental rotation (case 1) is cognitive. A neural implant that does the same rotation (case 3) is "clearly" on a par. A rotate-button that implements the same computational structure outside the skull (case 2) therefore is too, unless some *further* difference besides location can be produced.

**Otto / Inga.** Inga's standing memory that MoMA is on 53rd Street is a belief. Otto's notebook entry plays "just the sort of role that beliefs play." Differences of reliability, bandwidth, and perceptual phenomenology are run through and judged **shallow**. To refuse Otto solely because the information is not in the head "would be to beg the question." If the difference is relevant, "it is surely not primitively relevant."

**What parity is not.** It is not a claim that consciousness extends (experiences "may be determined internally"). It is not a claim that every coupled resource qualifies — the Filofax, the unread page, and the 1998 Internet are graded by further conditions (constancy, availability, automatic endorsement). Parity gets a candidate in the door; those conditions decide how clearly the mental *term* (belief, memory) applies.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] / [[Thinkers/David Chalmers]] (1998)**: the original statement.
- **Chalmers, *Reality+* ch. 16**: restates parity as the Ishi/Omar Opera House case and calls denying it "biological chauvinism, or maybe skin and skull chauvinism." AR is the superior extender because of seamlessness.
- **Clark, *The Experience Machine* ch. 6**: same principle, now with a mechanism — predictive brains "do not care" whether information is stored internally or in notebooks and GPS.
- **Functionalism generally**: parity is extended-mind functionalism. If role individuates a mental state, the realizer may straddle the skull. This is why Twin Otto can differ from Otto in **notional** content even though they are skin-in duplicates (1998, n. 4).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Parity assumes we already know what we would "have no hesitation" counting as cognitive *inside* the head. Opponents can reject the inner analogue (no standing beliefs at all; only occurrent ones) rather than the location step. Clark and Chalmers treat that as "extreme" because it also evicts Inga.
- The principle is silent on *consciousness*. Chalmers later insists the 1998 thesis never claimed phenomenal extension; the vault should not collapse parity into a claim about qualia.
