# European Nihilism (Nietzsche)
> The highest values devaluate themselves. The aim is lacking; "why?" finds no answer. This is not a mood. It is the next two centuries.

## Summary

The vault's primary for *nihilism as a European event* is not a published book. It is Book I of the posthumous Kaufmann compilation [[Sources/The Will to Power - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]] (notes 1–134). Nietzsche never issued this as a treatise. *Genealogy* III.27 announced a forthcoming *The Will to Power: Attempt at a Revaluation of All Values* / *The History of European Nihilism*; he then dropped the plan. Treat these notes as the workshop, not as a fifth published doctrine.

The Preface (Nov. 1887–March 1888) states the spine. "What I relate is the history of the next two centuries. I describe what is coming, what can no longer come differently: the advent of nihilism." The speaker is "the first perfect nihilist of Europe who, however, has even now lived through the whole of nihilism, to the end, leaving it behind, outside himself." The title names a **countermovement** that can come only *after and out of* perfect nihilism: "because the values we have had hitherto thus draw their final consequence." New values require that experience.

**n.2** is the definition: "What does nihilism mean? That the highest values devaluate themselves. The aim is lacking; 'why?' finds no answer."

**n.1** refuses the usual causes. Not social distress, not physiological degeneration, not corruption. "Ours is the most decent and compassionate age." Distress always permits interpretations. Nihilism is "the radical repudiation of value, meaning, and desirability," and it is rooted in "**one particular interpretation, the Christian-moral one**." Christianity ends by its own morality: cultivated truthfulness turns against the Christian God — rebound from "God is truth" to "All is false." See [[Concepts/Death of God (Nietzsche)]] (*Gay Science* §357 / §344 is the published form of this mechanism).

**n.4:** the Christian moral hypothesis was "the great antidote against practical and theoretical nihilism" (it gave man absolute value, made suffering meaningful, granted knowledge of the highest things, prevented self-contempt). **n.5–8:** the same truthfulness then discovers morality's teleology and "partial perspective." **n.6** is the antinomy: "Insofar as we believe in morality we pass sentence on existence." **n.7:** once the "shabby origin" of the values is clear, the universe seems meaningless — "but that is only a transitional stage."

## Three cosmological forms (n.12)

Nihilism as a psychological state has three last forms:

1. One sought a **meaning** / aim in becoming (moral world-order, harmony, happiness, even universal annihilation as a goal). Becoming aims at nothing.
2. One posited a **totality**, unity, monism, a whole in which the individual could immerse. There is no such universal. Man loses faith in his own value when no infinitely valuable whole works through him.
3. One then invents a **true world** beyond becoming. Once that world is seen as fabricated from psychological needs, the last form arrives: disbelief in any metaphysical world, yet one "cannot endure this world though one does not want to deny it."

The categories pulled out are "**aim**," "**unity**," "**being**" (truth). "The faith in the categories of reason is the cause of nihilism." This is the notebook version of [[Concepts/How the True World Became a Fable (Nietzsche)]] and [[Concepts/Belief in Antitheses of Values (Nietzsche)]].

## Active and passive; complete and incomplete

**n.22–23** (the words are in the notes; do not rewrite as Deleuze):

- **Active nihilism** — increased power of the spirit; violent force of destruction; does not stop at the No of judgment. **n.24:** "their nature demands the No of the deed. The reduction to nothing by judgment is seconded by the reduction to nothing by hand."
- **Passive nihilism** — decline and recession of the power of the spirit; "the weary nihilism that no longer attacks; its most famous form, Buddhism."

**n.28:** complete nihilism is the necessary consequence of prior ideals. **Incomplete nihilism** tries to escape without revaluation and "produce[s] the opposite, make[s] the problem more acute." **n.30** lists the escapes: this-worldly socialism as triumph of truth/love/justice; leftover unegoistic moralism; an antilogical "x" still used for metaphysical comfort; divine governance in events; English good-and-evil (Mill); contempt for the natural; the "Christian state," "Christian marriage."

**n.43.4:** "Nihilism is no cause but merely the logical result of decadence." See [[Concepts/Decadence (Nietzsche)]]. Do not reverse the arrow.

## History of European nihilism (notes 57–134)

Not a published narrative. Domain-by-domain traits (**n.69**): science as meaninglessness / mechanism; politics without faith in one's right; economics without a redeeming class; history as fatalism and Darwinism; art as romanticism still without an opposite valuation. **n.90:** "'Mankind' does not advance, it does not even exist" — a laboratory of scattered successes. **n.112:** "every fruitful and powerful movement of humanity has also created at the same time a nihilistic movement." **n.114:** "we have no longer such need of an antidote to the first nihilism"; "'God' is far too extreme a hypothesis."

**n.125:** socialism hides a "will to negate life" and yet delays "peace on earth" and "the total mollification of the democratic herd animal." **n.132:** "we are atheists and immoralists, but for the present we support the religions and moralities of the herd instinct." **n.134:** "This is the time of the great noon, of the most terrible clearing up."

The phrase **last man** is **not** in these notes. Do not back-read [[Concepts/The Last Man (Nietzsche)]].

## Recurrence as extreme nihilism (n.55)

Do **not** weld this to *Gay Science* §341.

**n.55:** once the moral interpretation collapses, it seems as if there were no meaning at all. Duration "in vain," without end or aim, is the most paralyzing idea. "Let us think this thought in its most terrible form: existence as it is, without meaning or aim, yet recurring inevitably without any finale of nothingness: 'the eternal recurrence.' This is the most extreme form of nihilism: the nothing (the 'meaningless'), eternally!" Kaufmann's "most scientific of all possible hypotheses" is **this** note: we deny end goals; if existence had one it would have to have been reached. European Buddhism. The underprivileged experience recurrence as a curse.

*GS* §341 is a demon's *if* that can also be a god. *Zarathustra* is the bite and the marriage to Eternity. **n.55 is recurrence as the meaningless eternally.** See [[Concepts/Eternal Recurrence (Nietzsche)]]. **n.617** (Book III.I) then tries the other stamp: imposing being on becoming as supreme will to power. That is not this note.

## Key Claims

- Nihilism is the self-devaluation of the highest values, not misery and not atheism as a slogan.
- The Christian-moral interpretation is the root; Christian truthfulness is the mechanism.
- Three last psychological forms: no aim, no unity, no true world.
- Active ≠ passive; complete ≠ incomplete. Incomplete nihilism (socialism, leftover morality, Christian institutions) sharpens the problem.
- Nihilism is the logical result of decadence, not its cause.
- Recurrence *in n.55* is the most extreme nihilism, not the published Yes.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — published books give death of God (*GS*), will to nothingness (*Genealogy* III), decadence (*Case of Wagner*, *Twilight*). These notes are the **history and typology** those books assume. Do not let the compilation replace them.
- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — **n.9–11, 17:** pessimism is a preliminary form; his nihilism "still follows from the same ideal that created Christian theism." Pity as supreme danger (**n.54**).
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — **n.17:** "intelligible freedom" as a bit of logic to acquit the *ens perfectum*. Categories of reason as the cause of nihilism (**n.12**).
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]** — **n.1.4** "Critique of Spinozism"; **n.55** Spinoza's logical-necessity affirmation vs affirmation of will to power.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]** — reads Nietzsche as last metaphysician, being = will to power. These notebooks are the dossier that reading used. **n.17**'s "last metaphysicians" in the notes = those who keep the theistic ideal as thing-in-itself. Do not import *Kehre*.
- **[[Thinkers/Gilles Deleuze]]** — active/reactive forces are **not** n.22–23's active/passive nihilism. Flag, do not merge.
- **[[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)]]** — objectivism and nihilism as one grasping. These notes give the *European* sequence Varela inherits as a problem; they do not give a middle way.
