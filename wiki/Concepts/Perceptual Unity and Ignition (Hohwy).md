# Perceptual Unity and Ignition (Hohwy)
> Conscious perception is the winning first-order inference. It is unified because a creature like us can act on only one hypothesis at a time. That is not a solution to the hard problem.

## Summary

Chapters 9–10 of *The Predictive Mind* turn PEM inward onto attention, consciousness, and unity.

**Attention is precision optimization** — already stated in ch. 3 (p. 71) and developed in ch. 9 after Friston: "attention is just optimization of precisions in hierarchical prediction error minimization" (p. 194). It is not a searchlight, a bandwidth limit, or intrinsic salience. Expected precisions are learned, state-dependent, second-order statistics. **Endogenous** attention (Posner cueing) predicts a high-precision target and raises gain. **Exogenous** capture: stronger/abrupt signals have higher SNR, so the system expects high precision and up-weights that PE. **Volitional** attention is [[Concepts/Active Inference and Action (Clark)|active inference]] under the hypothesis that "something with high precision will occur in that region" (pp. 197–98). **Inattentional blindness** is down-weighted gain plus low prior, plus over-long active inference that holds the model fixed (gorillas, fights) (pp. 199–201).

**Conscious perception** is the winning first-order inference; attention is second-order. "Conscious perception is the upshot of unconscious perceptual inference. … What is conscious is the result of the inference — the conclusion" (p. 201). In a world without state-dependent noise, consciousness without attention is possible (p. 202).

**The hard problem is declined.** Hohwy is "not intending to touch upon the metaphysical mind-body problem — the 'hard problem' of consciousness (Chalmers 1996)" (p. 202). A 206 note is zombie-style conceivability: a PEM creature matching us in neuronal detail need not be phenomenally conscious; "nothing in the total physical story entails that it will be." "If the question is the straightforward one whether prediction error minimization is the mechanism… that explains why some perceptual content is conscious rather than not conscious, then the answer is 'no'" (p. 208). He offers empirically enhanced commonsense functionalism (first-person perspective, richness, binding, penetrability, illusions/reality-testing, attention, plus unity).

**Unity is contingent, not a priori.** He accepts Bayne's unity thesis for *perceptual* states: they "occur as the components of a single subsuming total phenomenal perceptual state" (p. 210). It is "a contingent truth about our consciousness."

**Ignition.** Hohwy takes Dehaene's global neuronal workspace: conscious access = crossing an evidence-accumulation threshold, late sensory amplification, long-distance synchrony, "ignition" of a prefronto-parietal network. He *reinterprets* ignition as the switch from perceptual to active inference: a hypothesis good enough (given expected noise) to hold fixed and generate descending proprioceptive predictions (pp. 213–15). Unity follows because you can act on only one hypothesis: one bodily trajectory forces one sampling policy (pp. 216–17). Rivalry is the same constraint.

Action does not buy embodiment. Unity needs agency, but PEM remains an "internal mirror of nature" ([[Concepts/Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)]]).

## Key Claims / Positions

- Attention = expected precision (Friston 2009; Hohwy 2013 is earlier than Clark 2023).
- Consciousness = currently best hierarchical predictions, not PEM as such.
- Hard problem left open; conceivability gap kept.
- GNW ignition = perceptual → active-inference switch.
- Unity because one body, one policy.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]] / Baars**: GNW evidence accepted; ignition given a PEM job. Dehaene's primary is now in: [[Sources/Consciousness and the Brain - Stanislas Dehaene (2014)]]. Ignition there is a late, all-or-none parietal–prefrontal phase transition whose job is broadcasting, not the perceptual→active-inference switch Hohwy assigns it. See [[Concepts/Signatures of Consciousness and Ignition (Dehaene)]], [[Concepts/Global Neuronal Workspace (Dehaene)]].
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: cited to *decline* the hard problem, not to deflate it. Opposite of [[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**, Levine: named in the 206 note as the conceivability/gap literature Hohwy will not close.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: not used in these chapters (index points to ch. 12 on the self). Hohwy is not a Multiple-Drafts ally here.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: same attention = precision identification; opposite stance on the hard problem and on whether action implies a world-involving mind.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
