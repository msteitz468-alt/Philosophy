# Skilled Coping and the Five Stages of Expertise (Dreyfus)
> Beginners follow context-free rules. Experts see the situation and respond. The rules that help you acquire a skill need not be operating when you have it — so programming the rules cannot produce the expert.

## Summary

The positive account in [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]] is **skilled coping**: absorbed, bodily, situational know-how in which the world shows up as a field of attractions and repulsions rather than as a set of facts plus a decision procedure. The 1992 introduction states the skill phenomenology he developed with his brother Stuart (*Mind over Machine*, 1986):

1. **Novice** — context-free features and strict rules (the training wheels).
2. **Advanced beginner** — situational aspects begin to be recognized ("that looks like the last time the engine overheated").
3. **Competent** — the learner adopts a goal and plans; relevance is chosen, not seen; performance is exhausting.
4. **Proficient** — the situation is *seen* as a whole with a salient deviation; the response is still reasoned.
5. **Expert** — immediate response. The chess grandmaster "just sees" the right move; the everyday master of rooms just sits, pours, talks. No detached representation of the domain is consulted.

"There is no reason to think that the rules that play a role in the *acquisition* of a skill play a role in its later *application*." Recalling the rules and acting on them again produces beginner clumsiness (1992, n.4). Feigenbaum's expert-systems bet — interview the expert, extract the rules, run them faster — therefore extracts the wrong thing. What the expert can *say* is what a competent performer would say. Japan's Fifth Generation and the Rockefeller/Cornell hematology project failed on this analysis: physicians "are unable to spell out in detail how they do it; hence they cannot transfer the ability to a computer."

The 1972 book has the proto-version without the five labels. One first "slowly, awkwardly, and consciously follow[s] the rules," then a "muscular gestalt" gives "new flexibility and smoothness" — this is not "dropping these same rigid rules into unconsciousness." Coping's criterion is what Dreyfus calls Merleau-Ponty's *maximum grasp* (MP's wording: the **hold** / **optimum equilibrium**), not trait-checking. Language itself is a motor skill: one *dwells* in it (Polanyi). A gestalt *defines* its parts; a plan or rule only organizes independently defined elements.

This is the AI application of [[Concepts/Readiness-to-Hand and Presence-at-Hand (Heidegger)|ready-to-hand]]. The expert's world is equipment that withdraws. Breakdown (the novice's explicit rules, the expert system, the malfunctioning hammer) makes the domain present-at-hand. GOFAI models the present-at-hand and wonders why expertise is missing.

Bourdieu on gift-exchange is Dreyfus's social illustration: fluent practice is not the application of a theory of gifts. Reading the anthropologist's rules back into the practice is the same error as reading the beginner-rules back into the grandmaster.

## Key Claims / Positions

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: expertise is holistic discrimination and immediate response, not faster rule-application.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: the ontological ground — *Zuhandenheit*, circumspective taking-care, the workshop totality. Dreyfus is applying Division I, not inventing a new distinction.
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: [[Concepts/Practical Wisdom - Phronesis (Aristotle)|*phronēsis*]] already knew that application is perception of the case, not a formula. Dreyfus credits the insight and rejects the practical syllogism as a formalization of it.
- **[[Thinkers/Wittgenstein]]**: [[Concepts/Rule-Following (Wittgenstein)|rule-following]] — a rule does not contain its application; interpretation comes to an end in a practice, not in a further rule.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: skilled tool-use and the "sense of virtuality" as equipment-withdrawal (already noted on the Heidegger page). Predictive processing tries to give a mechanism for proficient salience (precision-weighting as what lights up).
- **[[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]]**: frames as the expert's stereotyped situations. Dreyfus: a frame is what a competent performer would draw if asked to make the background explicit — i.e., not expertise.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Kuhn]]**: a paradigm, like expertise, structures which facts are relevant; "without a paradigm all facts are equally relevant." Minsky misreads Kuhnian exemplars as frames.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] If an expert cannot state the rules, a sufficiently large net trained on expert behavior might still *instantiate* the skill without representing it. That is the connectionist hope Dreyfus already doubts on generalization grounds — see [[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]].
- The five-stage model is phenomenological, not a controlled developmental theory. It can be right about experts and still be a poor training curriculum.
