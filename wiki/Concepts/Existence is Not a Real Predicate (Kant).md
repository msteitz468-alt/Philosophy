# Existence is Not a Real Predicate (Kant)

> "Being is obviously not a real predicate, i.e., a concept of something that could add to the concept of a thing. It is merely the positing of a thing…" (A 598 / B 626)

## Summary

The **Ideal of Pure Reason** is reason's one genuine ideal: the thoroughly determined individual thought through the idea of the All of reality (*omnitudo realitatis*) — the **ens realissimum** / *ens originarium* / *ens summum* / *ens entium*. Finite things are defective copies (*ectypa*) of this *prototypon*. Reason may use the *idea* as a condition of determination; **hypostatizing** it as an existing God is a "mere fiction" (A 567–580 / B 595–608).

The natural course of reason: if anything exists, something exists necessarily; the concept that best fits unconditioned necessity is unlimited reality; hence the highest being is inferred as absolutely necessary (A 584–587 / B 612–615). There are **only three** speculative proofs — physicotheological, cosmological, ontological — "and there also cannot be any more" (A 590–591 / B 618–619).

**Ontological proof fails.** Absolute necessity of a *judgment* is not necessity of a *thing*. Cancel subject and predicate together and no contradiction remains ("God is not" cancels omnipotence with the subject). Existence is not a real (determining) predicate; "being" only posits. "A hundred actual dollars do not contain the least bit more than a hundred possible ones" — though "in my financial condition there is more with a hundred actual dollars than with the mere concept of them" (A 599 / B 627). Guyer/Wood render *Taler* as "dollars." "The famous ontological (Cartesian) proof… is only so much trouble and labor lost" (A 602 / B 630).

**Cosmological proof** (*a contingentia mundi*) illicitly leans on the ontological: it uses experience only for one step, then infers properties from mere concepts. "It is really only the ontological proof from mere concepts that contains all the force of proof in the so-called cosmological proof" (A 607 / B 635). Nest of errors: causality used beyond the sensible world; leap from impossibility of an infinite causal series to a first cause; confusion of logical with transcendental possibility. "The transcendental principle of inferring from the contingent to a cause… has significance only in the world of sense" (A 609 / B 637).

**Physicotheological (design) proof** is oldest and most respectable, but at most yields a **highest architect**, limited by given material, **not a creator** (A 627 / B 655). To reach an all-sufficient being it "jumps" to the cosmological, hence to the concealed ontological (A 629 / B 657).

**Result.** "All attempts of a merely speculative use of reason in regard to theology are entirely fruitless and… null and nugatory" (A 636 / B 664). Transcendental theology keeps a *negative* use (censor of anthropomorphism, atheism, deism). Moral theology is reserved for the [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)|Canon]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Anselm / Descartes.** Direct target of the ontological critique. Anselm is not named; Kant calls it the "Cartesian" proof. See [[Arguments/Ontological Argument]].
- **Aquinas / cosmological tradition.** The *a contingentia mundi* proof is granted one experiential step and then charged with smuggling the ontological inference and using causality beyond experience.
- **Hume.** The Fork already said existence is never part of an essence and the contrary of any existence-claim is conceivable. Kant's "not a real predicate" is the German-school sharpening of that point, plus the systematic dependence of the other two proofs on the first.
- **The vault's earlier Ontological Argument page** already stated this critique second-hand. This page is the primary-source home.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Kant forbids *knowing* God speculatively and then *requires* assuming God for the highest good. That is not a contradiction in his system (think vs cognize; knowledge vs moral belief), but it is the hinge later readers (Hegel, Nietzsche) will press.
