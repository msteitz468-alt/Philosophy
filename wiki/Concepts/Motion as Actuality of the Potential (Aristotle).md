# Motion as Actuality of the Potential (Aristotle)
> Motion (*kinēsis*) is "the fulfilment of what exists potentially, in so far as it exists potentially" — an incomplete actuality, neither mere capacity nor finished work.

## Summary

*Physics* III.1: nature is a principle of motion; if motion is unknown, nature is unknown. There is no motion "over and above the things." Change is always of substance, quantity, quality, or place.

The definition: "The fulfilment of what exists potentially, in so far as it exists potentially, is motion." Bronze *as bronze* is not in motion; bronze *as potentially a statue* is, while it is being shaped. "The actuality of the buildable as buildable is the process of building. … when there is a house, the buildable is no longer buildable." Motion is therefore an **incomplete actuality**: the potential whose actuality it is is itself incomplete. That is why motion looks indefinite and why *Metaphysics* IX later distinguishes complete *energeiai* (seeing, thinking — "we are seeing and have seen") from incomplete *kinēseis* (building, walking).

Mover and moved have **one** actuality, two in definition — as the road Thebes–Athens is one. Physical movers that can move are themselves moved by contact. Something can cause motion while remaining unmoved; that possibility is cashed in Books VII–VIII.

Book V narrows **motion** against **change** (*metabolē*). Only change from subject to subject is motion. Coming-to-be and perishing (contradictories) are change, not motion. The three motions are qualitative (alteration), quantitative (increase/decrease), and local (locomotion). There is no motion of motion (regress). Motion is in the movable, "the movable in activity," not in the form. Contrary motions run between contraries; rest is privation of motion in what *can* move.

Book VI: the continuum of magnitude, time, and motion stand or fall together; everything continuous is "divisible into divisibles that are infinitely divisible." There is **no first part** of a process of change. Nothing is in motion *or* at rest in the indivisible now. This is the machinery of the reply to [[Concepts/Zeno's Paradoxes of Motion (Zeno)]].

Book VIII restates: "Motion… is the fulfilment of the movable in so far as it is movable." Light and heavy things do not move themselves ("a characteristic of life"); they contain a source of *suffering* motion. The only single, continuous, eternal motion is **rotatory locomotion**.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Motion = actuality of the potential *qua* potential.
- Incomplete vs complete actuality (*kinēsis* vs *energeia*).
- Three motions; becoming/perishing are change, not motion.
- Continuum: no first stage; no rest or motion in the now.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: the definition that lets change be real without being from non-being ([[Concepts/Potentiality and Actuality (Aristotle)]], Book I's reply to Parmenides).
- **[[Thinkers/Parmenides]]** / **[[Thinkers/Zeno of Elea]]**: the target. Becoming is from *potential* being; infinite halves are potential, not a completed actual infinite.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Aquinas]]**: *motus est actus entis in potentia inquantum huiusmodi* — the *prima via* quotes this definition.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: motion reduced to local displacement of *res extensa*; alteration and growth drop out as real kinds.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: inverts the priority of actuality for Dasein; but the *Physics* sense of being-underway is closer to care than to presence-at-hand.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- The III.1 definition and the V.1 restriction (motion ≠ becoming) must be read together. Substantial change is *metabolē* explained by form/privation/substratum, not a fourth *kinēsis*.
- Book VIII's "whatever is moved is moved by another" sits in tension with II.1's internal principle — resolved by distinguishing a source of *suffering* motion from a self-mover.
