# Cartesian Anxiety (Varela)
> Either knowledge has a fixed foundation, or there is darkness and chaos. That either/or — not Descartes's dualism alone — is the anxiety that keeps cognitive science oscillating between objectivism and nihilism.

## Summary

Chapter 7 of [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)|*The Embodied Mind*]], after Richard Bernstein: the anxiety is a dilemma.

> "Either we have a fixed and stable foundation for knowledge, a point where knowledge starts, is grounded, and rests, or we cannot escape some sort of darkness, chaos, and confusion. Either there is an absolute ground or everything falls apart."

> "By treating mind and world as opposed subjective and objective poles, the Cartesian anxiety oscillates endlessly between the two in search of a ground."

**Representationism** then hunts an *outer* ground (a pregiven world recovered by cognition) or an *inner* ground (mind, representations, a self). Both hunts are the same craving. The 1991 introduction already sets the extremes that ignoring circularity produces: replace self-understanding with mature cognitive science (Stich, the Churchlands) or deny a science of the life-world ([[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]], Taylor).

Part V completes the diagnosis. Nihilism is first a *response* to the collapse of objectivism. The grasping mind reifies the absence of a ground into an "objective groundlessness" that can still serve as an ultimate reference point. "They ultimately share a common basis in the grasping mind." Nietzschean "positive nihilism" (Vattimo) and theoretical no-self without a path (Minsky forced to believe what he knows is false) are still halfhearted. See [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)]].

The name is Cartesian because Descartes formulates the problem as a thinking thing over against an extended world, and because Husserl's epoché is treated in 1991 as "twentieth-century Cartesianism." The anxiety is not identical with substance dualism. Damasio's [[Concepts/Descartes' Error (Damasio)|Descartes' error]] (reason split from the organism) and Clark's rejection of the sealed inner picture are neighbors, not the same thesis.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] et al.**: the either/or is optional. [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|Enaction]] and mindfulness are a middle way, not a third foundation.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the historical name. The *cogito* "stops short of examining the 'I'" (ch. 4). Dualism is a formulation of the problem, not a solution.
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: 1991 — epoché as inner ground. 2016 Thompson — *Crisis* life-world as the always-present background of science, which is the *exit* from the anxiety, not another Cartesianism.
- **[[Thinkers/Richard Rorty]]**: also anti-foundational, but "continuing the conversation of the West" is refused as provincial. Heidegger's planetary thinking is preferred.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]**: nihilism after the death of God. Nishitani: the will / eternal return still clings.
- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]**: later secluded inference rebuilds an *inner* ground (the nervous system behind a sensory veil). That is one pole of the oscillation in predictive-processing dress. See [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The anxiety is the structure of [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]].
- Whether predictive processing (Clark's action-oriented version vs Hohwy's secluded version) escapes the either/or or restages it is not a 1991 question.
