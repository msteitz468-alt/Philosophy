# Divine Names (Dionysius)

> The scriptural titles of God — Good, Being, Life, Wisdom, Power, Peace, One — do not describe the Super-Essence in itself. They name the powers that stream from it into the world, each applying to the *whole* Godhead except the Trinitarian distinctions.

## Summary

*The Divine Names* is addressed to Timothy after the (lost) *Outlines of Divinity*. Rule of speech: only what Scripture reveals, "not with enticing words of man's wisdom" (DN I.1). Revelation is "unto each created spirit in proportion to its powers" (DN I.1). The treatise deals with **intelligible** names; anthropomorphic images are deferred to *Symbolic Divinity* (DN I.8; XIII.4).

**Nameless and many-named.** The Sacred Writers "celebrate It by every Name while yet they call It Nameless" (DN I.5). As Super-Essence it eludes every name; as "Subsistence of goodness" and Cause it "must both be nameless and also possess the names of all things" (DN I.7).

**Unified vs distinguished.** "All the Names proper to God are always applied in Scripture not partially but to the whole, entire, full, complete Godhead" (DN II.1). **Undifferenced** titles (Good, I Am, Life, Lord, Fair, Wise, Cause) belong indivisibly to Father, Son, and Spirit. **Differentiated** titles "are the Super-Essential names and connotations of 'Father,' 'Son,' and 'Spirit.' In these cases the titles cannot be interchanged, nor are they held in common" (DN II.3). "The Father alone is the Source of the Super-Essential Godhead, and the Father is not a Son, nor is the Son a Father" (DN II.5). The Incarnation is a differentiated act: "the Super-Essential Word should wholly and completely take Human Substance… In these acts the Father and the Spirit have no share, except… the loving generosity of the Divine counsels" (DN II.6). Hierotheus on the Super-Essential Jesus: even in the human lot He "possesses His Supernatural and Super-Essential Existence… without change or confusion of Attributes" (DN II.10).

**What the names name.** "If we call the Super-Essential Mystery by the Name of 'God,' or 'Life,' or 'Being,' or 'Light,' or 'Word,' we conceive of nothing else than the powers that stream Therefrom to us" (DN II.7). One God, not stacked godheads: "We do not regard the Good as one thing, the Existent as another, and Life or Wisdom as another… one God is the universal Source of the emanations" (DN V.2).

**Itinerary of the treatise.** Good (first name, with Light, Beauty, Love, Ecstasy, and the treatise on Evil) → Being → Life → Wisdom / Reason / Truth → Power, Righteousness, Salvation, Redemption → Great/Small, Same/Other, Like/Unlike, Rest/Motion → Almighty, Ancient of Days, Eternity and Time → Peace → Holy of Holies / King of kings / Lord of lords / God of gods → Perfect and One, "the most important Title of all" (DN XIII.1). Reduplicated titles mark "overflowing causality" and the excess of the Unparticipated Creator over both participants and "Very Qualities" (DN XII.4).

Prayer begins every theological endeavour. It does not "pull down to ourselves that Power which is nigh both everywhere and nowhere" but unites us to it (DN III.1). Hierotheus's *Elements* are treated as "second Scriptures"; DN unpacks them for lesser capacities (DN III.2–3).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Proclus]]**: henads and participated unities between the Unparticipated One and everything below. Dionysius's "gods" in "God of gods" are "the higher Ranks in each Kind" (DN XII.4), not a pagan pantheon.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Aquinas]]**: commentary on this treatise; analogical predication as the Latin stabilization of "names from effects, not of the Essence."
- **Erigena**: the names become pastoral symbols for an ineffable Absolute (Sparrow-Simpson).
