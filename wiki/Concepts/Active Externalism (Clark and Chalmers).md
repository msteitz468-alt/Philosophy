# Active Externalism (Clark and Chalmers)
> The environment is not a historical backdrop to content; it is in the loop, driving cognition here and now.

## Summary

[[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)|The extended mind]] is the headline; **active externalism** is the position that makes it a third option between skin/skull internalism and Putnam/Burge content externalism. Clark and Chalmers introduce it in §1–3 of the 1998 paper: "a very different sort of externalism… based on the active role of the environment in driving cognitive processes."

A human organism linked with an external entity in **two-way interaction** is a **coupled system** that "can be seen as a cognitive system in its own right." All components play an active causal role and "jointly govern behaviour in the same sort of way that cognition usually does." Remove the external component and competence drops "just as it would if we removed part of its brain."

## Against Putnam and Burge

Standard externalism (Putnam 1975, "The meaning of 'meaning'"; Burge 1979, "Individualism and the mental") makes the meaning of "water" depend on the watery stuff of one's linguistic community and history. When I believe that water is wet and my twin believes that twin-water is wet, the responsible features are **distal and historical**, "at the other end of a lengthy causal chain." Teleport me to Twin Earth and my beliefs still concern H₂O, because of my history. "Features of the present are not relevant." Twins produce physically indistinguishable behaviour. Those external features are **passive**: they do not drive the cognitive process in the here-and-now.

In the Tetris, Scrabble, and Otto cases, the external features are **active**. They are "in the loop, not dangling at the other end of a long causal chain." Hold internal structure fixed, change the external features, and behaviour may change completely (Otto walks to 53rd; Twin Otto to 51st). The standard complaint against Putnam/Burge — that external content is explanatorily idle because counterfactual twins behave the same — "is not threatened" here.

Footnote 2 is the historiographical claim: much of the *intuitive appeal* of content externalism may actually be the appeal of active externalism. Externalists "often make analogies involving external features in coupled systems" and "appeal to the arbitrariness of boundaries between brain and environment," but "these intuitions sit uneasily with the letter of standard externalism," where "only the historical environment counts." Debate has asked whether mind must be in the head; "a more relevant question… might be: is mind in the present?"

## What Active Coupling Requires

Not every environmental contact counts. A one-off guidebook, an unread page, the 1998 Internet typically fail. What is needed is **reliable coupling**: the resource is generally there when required, trusted, and used as a matter of course. Occasional decoupling (shower, darkness, sleep, intoxication) is allowed, because the biological brain already suffers the same. Evolution, they add, has built onboard capacities "especially geared to parasitizing the local environment," so coupling is not an optional extra.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] and [[Thinkers/David Chalmers]] (1998)**: the named thesis. Clark later supplies a mechanism ([[Concepts/Predictive Processing (Clark)]]); Chalmers later updates the examples (smartphone, AR) without retracting the active/passive cut (*Reality+* ch. 16).
- **[[Thinkers/Hilary Putnam]] / Burge** (Putnam now has a reception page, not a 1975/1981 primary): the contrast class. See [[Concepts/Semantic Externalism (Putnam)]]. Clark and Chalmers do not reject Twin-Earth content; they refuse to *identify* mind-externalism with it. Putnam's BIV use of the same causal constraint is **semantic** (what can be thought), not a claim that the vat is in the cognitive loop.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the sealed inner mind is what active coupling dissolves.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]] / [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: world-involving mind by a different route. Ready-to-hand equipment is already "in the loop"; Dreyfus would not automatically call the loop *mental*.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Active externalism is compatible with keeping **experience** inside the head. The 1998 paper says so. It is *not* a general "everything mental is extended" thesis.
- Open: how much of later 4E cognition (embodied, embedded, enactive, extended) is this thesis and how much is a neighbouring family. The paper's named kin are Suchman, Beer, Thelen and Smith, Hutchins, Haugeland, Varela/Thompson/Rosch, and Wilson's "wide computationalism." [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)]] is now primary: **enaction** is enactment of a perceiver-dependent *world*, not extension of *belief* into a notebook. Neighbouring family, not the same thesis. See [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)]], [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]].
