# Ability Analysis of Phenomenal Knowledge (Lewis-Nemirow)

> What Mary gains on release is a representational or imaginative *ability* — knowing **how**, not knowing **that** — so a physicalist can grant that she acquires something momentous without granting that her earlier factual knowledge was incomplete.

## Summary

The ability analysis, due to **David Lewis** ("Postscript to 'Mad Pain and Martian Pain'," *Philosophical Papers* vol. I, 1983) and **Laurence Nemirow** (review of Nagel's *Mortal Questions*, 1980), is the physicalist reply Jackson takes most seriously. It does not deny that something important happens when Mary sees red; it denies that what happens is the acquisition of a *fact*. The knowledge argument needs premise (2)′ — that Mary *learns something* about other people — and learning, in the relevant sense, means adding to what one knows *that*. If the gain is instead an ability (to imagine, to recognize, to remember), the physicalist's inventory of facts was complete all along and the argument's inference to (3)′ never starts. Jackson states the position without softening it: "She knew all *that* there was to know about the experiences of others beforehand, but lacked an ability until after her release."

**Jackson's reply** concedes the abilities and denies they exhaust the gain. Mary certainly acquires abilities — to imagine what seeing red is like, to remember it, and "to understand why her friends regarded her as so deprived (something which, until her release, had always mystified her)." The counter-case is the **other-minds skeptic**: suppose Mary heard a lecture on skepticism about other minds while incarcerated. On release she first judges that she now knows more about the experiences others have when looking at ripe tomatoes; then she remembers the lecture and worries — is she really knowing more, or wildly generalizing from a single case?; then she decides she does know and that skepticism is mistaken, even without knowing how to demonstrate its errors. Jackson's question is what the deliberation was *about*: "What was she to-ing and fro-ing about — her abilities? Surely not; her representational abilities were a known constant throughout. What else then was she agonizing about than whether or not she had gained factual knowledge of others? There would be nothing to agonize about if ability was all she acquired on her release."

The structure of the reply is worth noting: it is not a direct argument that a phenomenal fact exists, but an argument from the **content of a rational deliberation**. Whatever Mary is uncertain about must be something that could be true or false; abilities she manifestly has are not candidates.

Jackson also disentangles the ability analysis from Churchland's first objection. Churchland mentions both Nemirow and Lewis, and may have intended this objection; but he says explicitly that his objection "does not need an 'ability' analysis of the relevant knowledge." So the description/acquaintance equivocation charge and the knowledge-how reply are two different attacks, and only the second engages premise (2)′.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Lewis and Nemirow**: the analysis is a way to be a physicalist without calling Mary's reaction an illusion. It concedes the phenomenology and reinterprets the epistemology.
- **[[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]]**: treats it as the only serious reply and answers it with the deliberation case above. Notably, Lewis is thanked in the paper's acknowledgment note — this is a dispute between allies in method.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: reaches the same verdict from the other end. [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)]] records that Jackson's Mary "gains factual knowledge; the only coherent materialist reply, the Lewis–Nemirow ability analysis, is 'deeply implausible.'" Chalmers's reason is systematic (an ability could not explain why the *primary intension* of a phenomenal concept resists a priori entailment); Jackson's is a single well-chosen case.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: does not need the ability analysis, because he denies the setup earlier — a fully informed Mary is not surprised in the first place ([[Concepts/Qualia Disqualified (Dennett)]]). The ability analysis grants Dennett's opponents more than Dennett does: it accepts that Mary's release is a genuine transition and only reclassifies it.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: the vault's other major know-how/knowing-that page, [[Concepts/Skilled Coping and the Five Stages of Expertise (Dreyfus)]], runs the same distinction in the opposite direction — Dreyfus uses the irreducibility of know-how to *deflate* the claims of propositional, rule-based AI, while Lewis and Nemirow use it to *protect* a complete propositional physics. Both take the distinction to be real; they disagree about which side of it is the impoverished one.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The dispute turns on a question neither side settles here: can a rational subject be uncertain about *nothing propositional*? Jackson assumes not. A defender of the analysis could reply that Mary's agonizing concerns the general proposition that others' experiences resemble hers — a proposition she could have entertained before release — rather than a newly available phenomenal fact.
