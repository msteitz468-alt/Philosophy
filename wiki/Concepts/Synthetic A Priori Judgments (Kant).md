# Synthetic A Priori Judgments (Kant)

> "The real problem of pure reason is now contained in the question: How are synthetic judgments a priori possible?" (B 19)

## Summary

Kant crosses two distinctions that earlier moderns had run together.

**A priori / a posteriori.** A priori cognitions occur "absolutely independently of all experience"; empirical cognitions are "possible only a posteriori, i.e., through experience" (B 2–3). Marks of the a priori: *necessity* and *strict universality* (B 4). All mathematical propositions, and "every alteration must have a cause," carry these marks. If causal connection were only Humean frequent association plus habit, the necessity in "cause" would be lost (B 4–5).

**Analytic / synthetic.** In subject–predicate judgments: if the predicate B is "(covertly) contained" in the subject A, the judgment is analytic (clarification, connection thought through identity); if B "lies entirely outside the concept A, though to be sure it stands in connection with it," the judgment is synthetic (amplification) (A 6–7 / B 10–11). "All bodies are extended" is analytic; "All bodies are heavy" is synthetic. Judgments of experience are all synthetic (B 11).

The two axes are *not* coextensive. Analytic judgments are a priori (their denial contradicts the subject-concept). Empirical judgments are synthetic a posteriori (experience supplies the missing X that joins predicate to subject). The remaining cell is the problem: **synthetic a priori** — ampliative, yet necessary and strictly universal. Experience cannot be the X, because the principle claims more generality and necessity than any experience can give (A 9 / B 13).

**Where they occur (B §V).** Mathematics: 7+5=12 is synthetic — "The concept of twelve is by no means already thought merely by my thinking of that unification of seven and five" (B 15); one must go out to intuition (fingers, points). "The straight line between two points is the shortest" is synthetic (straight is quality, shortest is quantity) (B 16). Pure natural science: quantity of matter unaltered; action = reaction (B 17–18). Metaphysics "consists of purely synthetic a priori propositions" (B 18) — e.g. "The world must have a first beginning."

**The general problem** therefore splits: How is pure mathematics possible? How is pure natural science possible? How is metaphysics as a natural predisposition possible? How is metaphysics possible as science? (B 20–22). Hume came closest but stopped at causality and inferred that metaphysics is a delusion of habit; in full generality that inference would abolish pure mathematics too (B 19–20).

The *Critique*'s answer is the [[Concepts/Copernican Turn (Kant)|Copernican turn]]: we know a priori what we ourselves put into objects — [[Concepts/Forms of Intuition - Space and Time (Kant)|space and time]] as pure intuitions (Aesthetic) and the [[Concepts/Categories of the Understanding (Kant)|categories]] as concepts of an object in general (Analytic). Such judgments never extend beyond objects of possible experience (B 73).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Hume's Fork.** Hume divides all objects of reason into relations of ideas (a priori, denial is contradiction) and matters of fact (contingent, contrary conceivable). Kant keeps the two marks but denies that they line up: mathematics is a priori *and* synthetic, so not a mere relation of ideas; causation is synthetic a priori, so not a mere matter of fact. See [[Concepts/Hume's Fork (Relations of Ideas vs Matters of Fact)]].
- **Leibniz.** Truths of reason vs truths of fact is the ancestor of both Fork and Kant's axes. Kant refuses to treat mathematical and metaphysical amplification as disguised analysis of concepts.
- **Ayer.** Logical positivism collapses Kant's synthetic a priori: analytic propositions are tautologies; everything else is empirical. See [[Concepts/Analytic Propositions as Tautologies (Ayer)]].
- **Quine.** "Two Dogmas" undercuts the *presupposition* of Kant's question: the analytic/synthetic distinction cannot be made sharp without circularity. See [[Concepts/Analytic-Synthetic Distinction (Quine)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Saul Kripke]].** Keeps Kant's wording of a priori ("known independently of any experience") and denies that necessity is its mark. Necessary a posteriori and contingent a priori both exist. "Gold is a yellow metal" is a bad analytic example — it can turn out false. Addendum (b) retracts ascribing to Kant the characterization of a priori *truth* as truth that *can* be known independently of experience. See [[Concepts/Necessary A Posteriori and Contingent A Priori (Kripke)]].
- **Popper.** "Kant's problem" of demarcation is downstream: once synthetic a priori metaphysics is possible as *predisposition* but not as *science* without critique, the question becomes what marks science off. Popper rejects Kant's solution and substitutes falsifiability. See [[Concepts/Problem of Demarcation (Kant and Popper)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The entire critical system stands or falls with this cell of the table. If Quine is right that analytic/synthetic cannot be drawn, the "general problem of pure reason" is ill-posed.
- > [!warning] Hume's Fork treats the contrary of any matter of fact as conceivable. Kant's Second Analogy will claim that the contrary of a causal law is *not* a possible experience. See [[Concepts/Schematism and Principles of Pure Understanding (Kant)]].
