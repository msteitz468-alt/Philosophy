# Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)
> Enacted worlds can be investigated in detail and still have no fixed substrate. The middle way is not a third foundation: it is the refusal to reify either presence of ground or absence of ground.

## Summary

The working title of [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)|*The Embodied Mind*]] was *Worlds Without Ground*. Thompson 2016: in Buddhist philosophy groundlessness means phenomena are empty of "own being"; in Western philosophy it means knowledge and meaning lack an absolute foundation. "Cognition as the enaction of a world means that cognition has no ground or foundation beyond its own history, which amounts to a kind of 'groundless ground.'"

Part V states the scientific half: "The worlds enacted by various histories of structural coupling are amenable to detailed scientific investigation, yet have no fixed, permanent substrate or foundation and so are ultimately groundless." Putnam: "Science takes away foundations without providing a replacement." Living without foundations is the historical task.

The philosophical half is Madhyamaka. Nāgārjuna refutes an independent subject, relation, *and* object; he extends (does not reject) Abhidharma. "Nothing is found that is not dependently arisen. For that reason, nothing is found that is not empty." Emptiness (*śūnyatā*) is not the void and not nihilism. Dependent arising blocks annihilation; "do not inherently exist" blocks permanence. Two truths: *saṃvṛti* = the phenomenal world as it appears (lawful, not a linguistic decentering); *paramārtha* = emptiness of that same world. True and false still hold conventionally.

**Enaction is not Madhyamaka.** Grasping "enaction" as a real third term vitiates the dialectic (pragmatism likewise).

Objectivism and nihilism "ultimately share a common basis in the grasping mind." Nihilism first arises as a response to the collapse of objectivism; the grasping reflex reifies absence into an "objective abyss." Western discovery of groundlessness is treated as breakdown, not path — that is one of three gaps they name versus contemporary anti-foundationalism (the others: no *entre-deux* of self *and* world; interpretationism ungrounds objects but not minds or schemes).

Ch. 11's circulation-circle: the scientist conceives a mind without a self → theory → mindful discovery that there is no actual self → emergence / societies of mind → insight into codependent lack of foundations → the mechanism of [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enactive cognition]] → loosening of both objectivism and subjectivism.

Against [[Thinkers/Richard Rorty]]'s "continuing the conversation of the West" they take Heidegger's "planetary thinking." Nishitani: Nietzsche still clings (will / eternal return); Western nihilism is "halfhearted" for lack of a pragmatic transformation of experience. They do **not** say: adopt Buddhism's cultural trappings. Work from scientific premises via enaction + mindfulness.

**Ethics.** When groundlessness is embraced, "the outcome is an unconditional sense of intrinsic goodness that manifests itself… as spontaneous compassion." *Śūnyatā* is inseparable from *karuṇā*. Nonegocentric compassion is situational *upāya*, not axiomatic ethics. Insight without compassion is worse than ordinary foundationalism. Social-science "economic man" / Tragedy of the Commons is unexamined ego-territory.

Rosch 2016: phase 2 of enaction *is* this "other mode of knowing not based on an observer and observed."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Nāgārjuna / Madhyamaka**: the named resource. Not ingested as a primary.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]**: nihilism after foundations fail. They take Nishitani's verdict that the will still grasps. The Western scene of that collapse is now primary: GS §§108–125 (shadows of God; de-deified nature; no land; the madman) and §374 (infinite interpretations, no looking around our own corner). Nishitani's charge that the will / eternal return still clings should be read against [[Concepts/Eternal Recurrence (Nietzsche)]] (§341 as test, not a new ground) and [[Concepts/Death of God (Nietzsche)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Richard Rorty]]**: anti-foundational ally whose Western conversation is too small.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: planetary thinking; Vattimo's *Verwindung*.
- **[[Thinkers/Evan Thompson]]** 2016: keeps the spine; recants the claim that Nāgārjuna simply "derives from meditation."
- **[[Schools/Daoism]]**: Saraha's epigraph (substantiality *and* emptiness are errors) is the nearest rhyme in the vault. Ziporyn's unhewn course is not this argument. Do not identify *śūnyatā* with *dao*.
