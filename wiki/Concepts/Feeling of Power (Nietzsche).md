# Feeling of Power (Nietzsche)
> Dawn's psychology: not "will to power," but the *feeling*, *desire*, *love*, and *demon* of power.

## Summary

*The Dawn of Day* never says **will to power**. It does, repeatedly, treat the **feeling of power** as mankind's strongest propensity and as the demon that remains when need is satisfied. File this page as the 1881 psychology. File the later slogan on [[Concepts/Will to Power (Nietzsche)]]. The vault already marks GS §13 as "feeling of power, not yet will to power." Dawn is the book that makes that feeling a *campaign*, not a seed.

Load-bearing Kennedy moments:

- **§23.** Animism of things inflates impotence; the **feeling of power** becomes man's strongest propensity.
- **§113.** Desire for distinction = desire to *subject* the neighbour (ladder: torture → admiration → ascetic self-torture). Happiness as "feeling of power developed to the utmost." Digression: "desire for power" / "voluptuousness of power."
- **§189.** High politics' sharpest spur is the **need for the feeling of power** (masses as well as princes). When man feels power he calls himself **good**; those who endure it call him **evil**.
- **§204.** Gold-lust is the same **fanaticism of the desire for power** once named "love of God."
- **§262.** "Neither necessity nor desire, but the love of power, is the demon of mankind." Give men health, food, shelter, enjoyment and they stay unhappy until the demon is fed.
- **§348.** The seeker of the *feeling* of power uses any means; the possessor grows fastidious.
- **§356.** Happiness's first effect *is* that feeling, discharged as gifts, derision, or destruction.

Nearby rights-psychology, not a second page: **§112** duties = others' claims; rights = "recognised and guaranteed degrees of power." When power shifts, rights vanish.

Do **not** quote note 1067 or BGE §13 from this book. They are not here.

## Key Claims

- The feeling of power is the strongest propensity and, in §262, the demon *instead of* need.
- Good/evil tracking: the one who *has* the feeling calls himself good; the one who undergoes it calls him evil (§189).
- Distinction, politics, gold, and leftover "love of God" are one fanaticism under different names.
- Rights are guaranteed degrees of power, not moral properties.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — 1881: feeling / desire / love of power. 1882 GS §13: feeling of power (still not the slogan). 1883 *Zarathustra*: **Will to Power** first published. 1886 BGE: analytic of the slogan. Keep the dates.
- **[[Concepts/Will to Power (Nietzsche)]]** — later name and later claim (life must surpass itself; psychology as morphology). This page is the workshop, not the doctrine.
- **[[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]]** — 1887 II.12 will make purposes signs that a will to power has become master. Dawn has the feeling, not that sentence.
- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — Will to Life is the named opponent of *Zarathustra*'s Will to Power. Dawn does not run that argument. Do not import it.
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]** — *conatus* as striving to persevere. Feeling of power is expansion and subjection, not perseverance. Kinship only at a distance.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] **Do not cite Dawn for the will to power.** The phrase is absent. Near-misses ("desire for power," "love of power," "feeling of power") stay on this page.
