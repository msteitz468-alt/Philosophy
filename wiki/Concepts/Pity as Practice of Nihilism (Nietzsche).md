# Pity as Practice of Nihilism (Nietzsche)
> Pity is not a side-vice of Christianity. It is the technic of nihilism.

## Summary

*The Antichrist* §7 makes pity (*Mitleid*; Mencken: pity / “practical sympathy”) the *practice* of the religion §2 has just defined as harmful. Christianity is “called the religion of pity.” Pity is a depressant: it multiplies misery, conserves what is ripe for destruction, and is “the chief tool for the advancement of *décadence*.” It thwarts the law of selection. It preserves the botched. “Schopenhauer was right in this: that by means of pity life is denied, and made worthy of denial—pity is the technic of nihilism.” Aristotle is cited on the contrary: a morbid and dangerous state against which one does well to use a purgative (tragedy). The case of the objector who still praises pity as a virtue is diagnosed as *décadence* of instinct.

This is not *Zarathustra*’s “God is dead: of his pity for man hath God died” (Part II, “The Pitiful”; Part IV, last pope). That is a *scene*: the old God suffocates of all-too-great pity; great love is *above* pity; pity for the higher men is Zarathustra’s last sin. *The Antichrist* is the *technic*: how a morality of pity works on a population — as selection inverted, as a multiplier of suffering, as the everyday practice of the will to nothingness. Do not weld the scene to the technic.

Nor is it GS §13, where pity is “the most agreeable feeling” of those with little pride — a feeling-of-power psychology, not yet a diagnosis of Christianity as such.

***Dawn* (1881) is the earlier campaign**, not the 1888 technic. Kennedy: being pitied was an insult in the morality of custom (§18); Christian compassion is also suspicion of the neighbour's *joy* (§80); pity is *our* pain, a polyphonous mix, and “increases suffering throughout the world” — a day's rule would “bring humanity to utter ruin” (§§133–134); higher than neighbour-utility is knowledge even if it plunges others into doubt — “victory over ourselves beyond our pity” (§146). Book IV: compassionate visitors gloat (§224); charity gratifies *his* need (§334); “The Comedy of Pity” (§383). Do not weld this psychology to A §7's selection-inverted *technic*. See [[Sources/The Dawn of Day - Nietzsche (Kennedy, Collection 2013)]].

*Genealogy* already feared the union of “great loathing” and “great pity” as the “ultimate will” of man, his will to nothingness (Johnston III.14). A §7 names the second term as Christianity’s *modus operandi*.

## Key Claims

- Pity multiplies suffering and preserves what selection would remove.
- It is the practice, not an accidental tone, of Christian morality.
- Schopenhauer’s metaphysics of pity as denial of the will to life is accepted as *description* and rejected as *prescription*.
- The happiness-formula of §1 (Yea / Nay / straight line / goal) and the value-table of §2 are the contrary climate: ice, not sirocco.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — 1888: the ethical centre of Book I of the Revaluation. *Zarathustra* already made pity the last sin of the teacher and the death of God. This page is the *population* argument.
- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — named and used: pity denies life. In *CW* he is the philosopher of decadence who allowed Wagner to find himself. Here he is right about the mechanism and wrong to call it wisdom. See [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]** — tragedy as purge of a morbid state, not as school of pity. Do not rewrite the *Poetics* from this sentence.
- **[[Concepts/The Higher Man (Nietzsche)]]** — Part IV: pity for the cave-guests is the last temptation. Individual, dramatic. A §7 is civilizational.
- **[[Concepts/Death of God (Nietzsche)]]** — *Zarathustra*’s God dies *of* pity. A §18’s God is nothingness deified. Two uses of the same word; keep them apart.
- **[[Thinkers/William James]]** — *Varieties* already answers Nietzsche on saintliness by fruits. James’s sick soul *asks* for help; Nietzsche’s pity-technic is what a church does with that request. Do not flatten.
- **[[Concepts/The Present Instant (Hadot)]]** — Hadot’s Nietzsche is yes-to-one-moment, not this polemic. Do not import.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Mencken’s “practical sympathy” and “charity” in §2 are this page’s object, not Victorian philanthropy as a separate topic.
- > [!warning] Do not file Rousseau’s natural pity ([[Concepts/State of Nature (Rousseau)]]) as the ancestor. Nietzsche is not writing a history of *pitié*.
