# Real Problem of Consciousness (Seth)
> Accept that consciousness exists, then *explain, predict, and control* its phenomenological properties in terms of embodied, embedded brains — dissolving, not solving, the hard problem.

## Summary

[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]'s methodological alternative to [[Concepts/Hard Problem of Consciousness (Chalmers)]]. Chalmers splits consciousness into **easy problems** (functions: discrimination, report, integration) and the **hard problem** ("Why should physical processing give rise to a rich inner life at all?"). The NCC programme (Crick and Koch: "the minimal neuronal mechanisms jointly sufficient for any one specific conscious percept") pairs naturally with that split: find the correlates, then still face the *why*.

Seth refuses both horns of the resulting dilemma. Do not try to solve the metaphysical *why-at-all* first. Do not "sweep the mystery of consciousness away under the carpet" while explaining the brain. The **real problem** is to account for *properties* of experience — how conscious (level), of what (content), and of being you (self) — so that the relations drawn between mechanism and phenomenology "are not arbitrary but make sense." What "make sense" means: explain, predict, and control.

The historical model is **vitalism**. Life looked irreducible until its properties (metabolism, reproduction, development) were individuated and mechanized. "The fatal flaw of vitalism was to interpret a failure of imagination as an insight into necessity. This is the same flaw that lies at the heart of the zombie argument." Philosophical zombies become less conceivable as we know more about brains (A380-flying-backwards analogy). The hard-problem intuition should "vanish in a puff of metaphysical smoke."

Seth's preferred metaphysics is a "functionally agnostic flavour of **physicalism**." Functionalism is left open (is consciousness more like Go, or like weather?). Panpsychism is untestable; mysterianism (McGinn) "unjustifiably pessimistic"; illusionism (Frankish) is treated sympathetically only in the weak form that experiences exist but are not what we think.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Consciousness is first-person phenomenology, defined with [[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]: "there is something that it is like to *be* that creature." Any phenomenology counts; language and intelligence are not required.
- Divide the explanandum: **level**, **content**, **self**. Consciousness is not one big scary mystery in search of one big scary solution.
- NCCs are a research recipe, not an explanation. "Correlations are not explanations." Bridging mechanism to phenomenology should shrink the residual *why*.
- Brains are **wetware** — chemical, living, environmentally embedded — "not computers made of meat."
- The real-problem strategy still "goes after" the hard problem, but indirectly. Chalmers's mapping problem, structural coherence, and meta-problem are noted; they are not adopted as the agenda.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: the named foil and the source of the hard/easy vocabulary. Seth quotes the 1995 formulation at length and refuses it as the first question. Now a dedicated contradiction: [[Contradictions/Seth vs Chalmers - Real Problem vs Hard Problem]].
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: ally on dissolving rather than solving. Clark attacks the *meta-problem* (why we posit a gap) via self-opacity; Seth attacks the *properties* via measurement and mechanism. Same deflationary neighbourhood, different route. See [[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]**: declines the hard problem outright ("not intending to touch upon" it). A PEM creature matching us neuron for neuron need not be phenomenally conscious. Seth is more ambitious: the real problem is supposed to make the residual *why* evaporate.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: *Consciousness Explained* is named as a turning point. Ally on treating mystery as a failure of imagination. Seth keeps experience as the thing to be explained (not eliminated).
- **[[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]]**: *Consciousness and the Brain* is an **in-progress** ingest. Dehaene also uses the vitalism analogy and files the hard problem as mislabeled. Seth set GWT/Dehaene aside in ch. 1; later treats Dehaene-style global-availability criteria as *liberal functionalism* he will not accept as sufficient for machine consciousness.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- > [!warning] Real-problem dissolution vs. hard-problem residual *why* — see [[Contradictions/Seth vs Chalmers - Real Problem vs Hard Problem]]. A Chalmers reply: explaining structure, unity, and report still leaves *why the functions are accompanied by experience* — the very distinction the real problem tries to work around.
