# Ren — Humaneness (Confucius)

> In Annping Chin's *Analects*, *ren* is **humaneness** / **humanity** — not "benevolence" as the default English. It is the beginning and the end of the journey. "Restrain the self and return to the rites" is "the way to be humane." "Humaneness rests with the self" (12.1). Confucius will not call himself humane (7.34) and will not certify most disciples.

## Load-bearing claims in the text

- **Roots (1.2).** Youzi: "being filial to one's parents and being respectful to one's elders are the roots of one's humanity [*ren*]." A gentleman looks after the roots; then "a moral way will grow."
- **Without it, rites and music fail (3.3).** "Being human and yet lacking humaneness — what can such a man do with the rites? … with music?" Chin: "the rites have no relation to a person who lacks moral impulse."
- **Home, not calculation (4.1–4.6).** *Ren* is the beautiful neighborhood one *can* choose. "A humane person feels at home in humaneness. A wise person [practices it because he] sees benefits in humaneness" (4.2). Set the mind on it and you are "not morally culpable" (4.4). "If the gentleman forsakes humaneness, how can he be worthy of the name of gentleman? The gentleman does not abandon humaneness, not even for the duration of a meal" (4.5). Confucius has never seen one who truly loves it, yet "never seen a person who lacks the strength" to practice it for a day (4.6).
- **Yan Hui (6.7, 6.11).** Hui "lets his heart abide in humaneness for three months at a time"; others "only now and then." Joy on "a bowlful of millet and a ladleful of water."
- **Method, not philanthropy (6.30).** Zigong's wish to relieve all is "no longer a matter of humaneness" but sage-work even Yao and Shun found hard. "A humane person wishes to steady himself, and so he helps others to steady themselves. … The ability to make an analogy from what is close at hand is the method and the way of realizing humaneness."
- **Keji fuli (12.1).** Yan Hui asks. "Restrain the self and return to the rites. This is the way to be humane. If for one day you are able to restrain the self and return to the rites, this means that your capacity to be humane will open up to the world. Humaneness rests with the self. How could it come from others?" Steps: do not look, listen, speak, or act contrary to ritual propriety. Chin: not Song "universal principle"; Han = keep the self on the measure of *li*; Qian Mu = one day's effort starts self-cultivation, "not… effecting a change in other people."
- **In office (12.2).** Guest/sacrifice demeanor plus "Do not impose on others what you do not desire for yourself."
- **Competence is not it (5.8, 5.19).** Military, administrative, and loyal service can be real and still not *ren*. Guan Zhong is later called humane by large political benefit, not by "petty loyalty" suicide (14.16–14.17) — a judgment Chin flags as "difficult to defend."
- **Five marks (17.6).** Respectful, large-minded, trustworthy, quick, generous. Without love of learning, noble tendencies become foolishness, unmoored cleverness, harm, derision, unruliness, wildness (17.8).
- **He will not speak of it as a theory (9.1)** and "dare[s] not call [himself] a sage or a humane man" (7.34). Disciples recorded the talk anyway.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. Ziporyn's "humankindness" in the *Daodejing***: same graph, opposite valuation. Chs. 18–19, 38: when the great course is abandoned, humankindness and duty arise; ritual is "the beginning of disorder." Confucius: when humaneness is lacking, rites and music are empty (3.3); returning *to* the rites is the way *to* humaneness (12.1). See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Eudaimonia]]**: Aristotle's highest good is excellent activity of the rational soul, completed in *theōria*. *Ren* is not a function-argument product and is not crowned by contemplation; it is fulfilled in relationships and office, and it "rests with the self" through *li*.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Duty (Kant)]]**: Kantian moral worth requires acting *from* duty, i.e. from a motive of respect for law. 4.2 distinguishes feeling at home in *ren* from practicing it because one "sees benefits." The higher Confucian mark is the first, not the dutiful second — almost the reverse of Kant's ranking of inclination vs duty.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/De - Virtuosity and Potency (Laozi)]]**: Laozi's highest *de* "harbors no motives" and treats humankindness as a descent-step. Confucian *ren* is effortful (4.6: no one lacks the strength for a day) and is the name of the goal, not a decay-marker.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Vs. [[Schools/Daoism]]: *ren* as remedy vs *ren* as symptom. Dedicated page: [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- He seldom spoke of *ren* (9.1) yet the book is full of it. Chin/Liu: reluctance because he had "not achieved it."
- 12.1 Qian Mu: one day does not convert the world — yet 12.19 still claims the ruler's character bends the grass. Flag, do not flatten.
- 14.16–17 Guan Zhong as humane: large benefit vs the "petty loyalty" of dying with one's lord. Chin: nonpartisan and hard to defend.
