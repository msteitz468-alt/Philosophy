# Transcendent Fatalism (Schopenhauer)
> A life can look like a well-conceived epic: moral necessity wearing the stamp of complete randomness. The will is the secret impresario — of the dream, and by analogy of the life.

## Summary

“Transcendent speculation on the apparent deliberateness in the fate of the individual” is framed as “mere metaphysical fantasy.” Not only the solution but the problem is doubtful; a dogmatic tone is only to avoid “formulas of doubt.”

Two fatalisms must be kept apart.

**Demonstrable fatalism:** “everything that happens, without exception, takes place with *strict necessity*.” A priori, from the *Prize Essay on the Freedom of the Will*; a posteriori via somnambulists, second sight, and some dreams. Attempts to foil a vision *bring it about*. Accidents are “encompassed by a deeply hidden necessity, fate, whose mere tool is chance itself.”

**Transcendent fatalism** is “fatalism of a higher order.” The necessity of what happens is “*not blind*.” It “cannot be demonstrated.” It is “deposited by experiences” that bear “the stamp of a moral, or inner, necessity” *and* “the distinct mark of external, complete randomness.” A life then looks like “a whole, consistent within itself… just as the most well-conceived epic.” Design belongs to the *individual* life, not world history (“Peoples only exist in abstraction”).

Deeds are “the necessary product of two factors”: immutable *character* (known only a posteriori) and external *motives* that determine it “with a necessity that equals a mechanical one.” The judging I is the *subject of cognition*, “a stranger to these two actors.”

The hidden power “can ultimately have its root only in our own mysterious inner being.” Dream analogy: dream-accidents obey “our own *will*… from a point of view that does not belong to our dreaming consciousness,” appearing as “inexorable fate.” “Just as we all are the secret impresarios of our dreams, so too by analogy the fate that governs the actual course of our lives ultimately springs somehow from the *will*” — our own, but “far beyond our representing, individual consciousness,” which supplies motives for the empirical will that “has often to fight hard with the will that presents itself as fate.”

**Two constructions of one event:** (1) “objective, causal connection of the course of nature”; (2) “subjective connection… as subjective as his own dreams,” succession “in the manner of the succession of scenes determined according to the poet’s design.” One *will to life* is “the subject of the great dream of life.” Natural necessity does *not* cancel the ominous, nor the reverse.

The ultimate aim is not “temporary, provisional welfare” but “eternal existence”: a “metaphysically most expedient impression on the *will*.” Given that “the will’s turning away from life is the ultimate aim of temporal existence,” guidance is “in that direction,” often by “misfortune and suffering.” Death is a *Krisis* that determines rebirth. See [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]].

Ancient *fatum* / *heimarmenê* is “blind,” Christian *pronoia* “seeing”; the difference “disappears” at “the innermost, metaphysical essence.” *Pronoia* starts from *nous*, “superficial and false.” Genius/daimon lore is “only figurative, allegorical.”

## Key Claims

- Demonstrable fatalism is ordinary and a priori. Transcendent fatalism is undemonstrable, experiential, “not blind.”
- Unity of randomness and necessity at the root of things. Phenomenal chance is ruled by an intelligible world.
- Two constructions of a life: causal net / dramatic design. Both hold at once.
- The impresario is the will beyond representing consciousness, not a theistic author.
- Guidance aims at *denial* of the will, not at worldly welfare.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — essay as avowed fantasy; three “compatibilities” via Kant’s thing-in-itself/appearance + will/representation: (1) freedom of will *in itself* vs universal necessity of actions; (2) efficient vs final causality; (3) contingency of events vs moral necessity. Insight is weakest in (3).
- **[[Concepts/Amor Fati (Love of Fate)]]** — **do not weld**. Seneca’s *ducunt volentem fata, nolentem trahunt* is quoted twice. The aim is *turning away from life*, not affirming or loving fate. Nietzsche’s New Year’s vow is a later, opposite use of necessity.
- **[[Concepts/Compatibilism - Liberty and Necessity (Hume)]]** — **flag only**. Demonstrable fatalism is not Humean liberty-as-acting-from-character amid leftover empirical contingency. Character + motives = “mechanical” necessity; a somnambulist could predict a life “down to the smallest details.”
- **[[Concepts/Dichotomy of Control (Epictetus)]]** — **flag only**. “At every moment we [believe we] are masters of our deeds”; retrospectively “a foreign power.” Character is immutable, known a posteriori — not *eph’ hēmin*.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Do not take *pronoia*/providence *sensu proprio*. It is “mediate, allegorical… as good as true.” Do not put metaphysical design in world history.
- > [!warning] Do not treat genius/daimon, Platonic lot-choice, or Erigena’s God as Schopenhauer’s literal ontology; they are similes.
- > [!warning] Do not read the essay as dogma: he says the following “is not to be taken seriously.”
