# The Small Things — Nutrition, Place, Climate, Recreation (Nietzsche)

> "Why I am So Clever" §§1–3 and §10: the four questions on which "the salvation of humanity" actually depends are diet, locality, climate, and choice of recreation — and every one of them was left to philosophy's contempt while it argued about God.

## Summary

This is the most literal statement anywhere in the corpus of what [[Concepts/Philosophy as Involuntary and Unconscious Autobiography (Nietzsche)]] asserts in the abstract. If a philosophy is the confession of its originator, then the conditions of that originator's body are not biographical trivia surrounding the thought — they are its content. So Nietzsche opens his account of his own cleverness not with an argument but with a menu.

The chapter's move is a substitution. He begins by dismissing the theological questions as ones he never had ("Of religious difficulties for instance I have no experience"), grounding his atheism not in argument but in constitution — "I have not come to know atheism as a result of logical reasoning and still less as an event in my life: in me it is a matter of instinct." God is "a too palpably clumsy answer... even a crude prohibition to us: you shall not think!" Having cleared the ground, he installs the replacement: "I am much more interested in another question... I refer to nutrition."

The three questions, in his order:

1. **Nutrition** (§1). The German spirit is a digestive complaint — "German sprit is indigestion; it can digest nothing" [OCR: *spirit*] — and German cookery is causally implicated in it. His own rule against slow poisoning: "A sedentary life... is the real sin against the holy spirit."
2. **Locality and climate** (§2). Genius is conditioned by dry air and quick metabolism; the wrong climate can withhold a man's task from him for years. His indictment of his own past is not moral but physiological: what ruined years of his life was "Idealism" — the ignorance that let him take physiological questions for spiritual ones. Hence the reversal that makes the chapter: "It was only illness that brought me to reason."
3. **Recreation** (§3). Reading is rest *from* himself and is therefore forbidden during productive work: "Shall I allow a strange thought to steal secretly over the wall? For that is what reading would mean." What he does read is almost entirely French — Montaigne, Pascal, Stendhal, Molière, Maupassant — the taste that governs his judgement of Wagner two sections later.

§10 gives the summary and the polemical point. These are "the small things" — and they are "more important than everything hitherto esteemed." The chapter calls the discipline they require "the whole casuistry of selfishness," which is a joke with a knife in it: casuistry was the priest's science of applying moral law to cases, and Nietzsche is annexing the word for the art of eating and walking well.

## The Two Consequences

**Idealism becomes a physiological verdict, not a philosophical error.** Elsewhere Nietzsche argues against idealism; here he *diagnoses* it, as a way of not knowing where one's states come from — which is why the argument cannot be answered by argument. This is the same structure as the genealogical unmasking in [[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]], turned on himself.

**Not-reacting becomes a virtue.** §8's supporting doctrine: the instinct of self-preservation shows itself as an instinct of *defence*, and "the popular word for this defensive instinct is taste." Habitual saying-of-no is ruinous expenditure. From which the line the vault should hold onto: "**The scholar is a decadent**" — a pure reacting medium, spending itself on other people's stimuli. See [[Concepts/Decadence (Nietzsche)]].

## How This Sits Against the Rest of the Vault

- **Against the Stoics.** [[Concepts/Dichotomy of Control (Epictetus)]] sorts the world into what is and is not up to us and then trains the *assent*. Nietzsche relocates the whole operation into the body: what is up to us is climate, diet, and what we read, and the training of assent is a symptom to be explained rather than a technique to be practised. Both are regimens; only one of them is about food.
- **With [[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]].** Hadot's thesis that ancient philosophy was a *way of life* with spiritual exercises finds an unexpected nineteenth-century successor here — except that Nietzsche's exercises are dietetic rather than spiritual, and he would call Hadot's "spiritual" the idealism he is diagnosing. See [[Concepts/Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)]].
- **With Epicurus.** The one classical figure whose regimen this resembles: garden, diet, avoidance of the wrong stimuli. Nietzsche's GS §45 already read Epicurus's afternoon happiness as the invention of a man who suffered continually. See [[Thinkers/Epicurus]].
- **Wagner as recreation** (§§5–6). Wagner is filed under this heading, which is itself the judgement. "What is it that I have never forgiven Wagner? The fact that he condescended to the Germans—that he became a German Imperialist." And yet: "Wagner is the antidote to everything essentially German—the fact that he is a poison too I do not deny." See [[Concepts/Wagner as Cagliostro of Modernity (Nietzsche)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

> [!warning] The strongest objection is that this chapter proves too much. If philosophy is diet, Nietzsche's own philosophy is his diet, and there is no standpoint from which to prefer his physiology to anyone else's — the difficulty [[Concepts/Perspectivism (Nietzsche)]] carries in general form. He does not answer it here; he asserts a criterion instead ("the sharpest discernment for every sign of healthy instincts"), which is the very move he refuses to grant his opponents.

> [!warning] Against [[Concepts/Space of Reasons (Sellars)]] and the vault's epistemology cluster: this is the pure case of explaining a claim by its causes rather than placing it in the space of giving and asking for reasons. [[Thinkers/Quentin Skinner]]'s [[Concepts/Explaining Beliefs Without Truth (Skinner)]] accepts Bloor's *impartiality* but rejects *symmetry* — true and false beliefs need different sorts of explanation. Nietzsche here is a symmetry theorist about his own thought, which is the boldest and most vulnerable version of the position.
