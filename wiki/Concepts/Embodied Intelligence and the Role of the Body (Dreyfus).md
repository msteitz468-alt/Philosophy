# Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)
> From Plato to Descartes the body gets in the way of intelligence. Dreyfus inverts the claim: if the body is indispensable and cannot be simulated on a heuristically programmed digital computer, then the project of artificial intelligence is "doomed from the start."

## Summary

Adherents of the psychological and epistemological assumptions must develop a theory of intelligence that "makes no appeal to the fact that a man has a body, since at this stage at least the computer clearly hasn't one." [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]'s Part III argues that this is not a temporary hardware gap.

**Descartes inverted.** Descartes was the first to conceive robots and the first to see that a finite-state machine fails as a *universal* instrument: "reason is a universal instrument which can be used in all sorts of situations, the organs of a machine have to be arranged in a particular way for each particular action" (*Discourse*). Descartes concludes to an immaterial soul. Dreyfus: a huge state-space blunts that argument. What actually distinguishes persons from machines is "not a detached, universal, immaterial soul but an involved, situated, material body."

**"Higher" intelligence is derived.** Detached, logical, determinate forms of intelligence are guided by global, involved "lower" forms — pattern recognition, skilled movement. Those are what resist simulation. Pattern recognition is gestalt: parts get their value from an underdetermined whole (Husserl's outer and inner horizons). A computer must take every bit as determinate or not at all. A gestalt *defines* its parts; a plan only organizes independently defined elements.

**Three bodily functions programs lack:**

1. An **inner horizon** — the object is given as having more to it than is currently determinate.
2. **Global anticipation** that both determines and is determined by details (Dreyfus: "maximum grasp"; MP's wording is the **hold** / **optimum equilibrium**).
3. **Cross-modal transfer** / synergy — a skill learned with one organ transfers (writing with the foot; the cane that, in use, is not an external object).

Skills are motor schemata ("Do that again"), not physics calculations. The MIT arm already shows the mismatch: motor space is not objective space; more degrees of freedom make calculation worse; Ping-Pong is a real-time constraint no heuristic search meets. Digital robots are the philosophically interesting restriction; a wet/analogue agent is not in principle ruled out.

**Situation, not inner model.** The body is what lets humans be **in a situation** — the halt to the rule-regress of relevance. When we are at home, things "are the world itself," not a stored model. [[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]]'s world-model / self-model hierarchy is computer-Cartesianism. Chapman, quoted in 1992: "You don't need to maintain a world model; the world is its own best representation." That is Heideggerian AI (Winograd, Agre–Chapman), still failing relevance.

**Needs, not utility functions.** Watanabe: "a man has values while a machine has objectives." Dreyfus: even "values" already objectifies the field. Concern organizes which facts *appear*. A utility function is one more determinate object and cannot generate the creative discovery of what one needed (Todes). Nietzsche/Sartre-style tables of chosen values miss that the field is not a list.

**1992 body as condition of common sense.** Deixis, optimal grip, somatic primitives; Oliver Sacks's Madeleine as a rebuttal to the idea that a catalog of facts could replace a body. Mark Johnson and Lakoff on imagination and bodily metaphor, against Lenat's hope that analogy is just another inference rule. A network "must share our commonsense understanding of the world if it is to share our sense of appropriate generalization" — and that understanding is bodily. "It seems at least as unlikely that one could build a device sufficiently like us to act and learn in our world."

**Part III conclusion.** "Artificial intelligence must begin at the level of objectivity and rationality where the facts have already been produced." Extracted facts are an unwieldy mass of neutral data. There are no fixed facts, and indeterminate needs cannot be a series of determinate machine states. "Our risk is not the advent of superintelligent computers, but of subintelligent human beings."

## Key Claims / Positions

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: the body is a transcendental condition of relevance, not a sensor package.
- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: figure/ground, synergy, tool-use that "does not run through the objective world." Primary now: [[Sources/Phenomenology of Perception - Maurice Merleau-Ponty (1945)]]. "Maximum grasp" is Dreyfus's name for MP's **hold** / **optimum equilibrium** / **scope of this hold** — those phrases are in Part One ch. III, not "maximum grasp." See [[Concepts/Motor Intentionality and the Intentional Arc (Merleau-Ponty)]], [[Concepts/One's Own Body and the Body Schema (Merleau-Ponty)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: [[Concepts/Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)|being-in-the-world]] and [[Concepts/Readiness-to-Hand and Presence-at-Hand (Heidegger)|ready-to-hand]] are the ontological frame; Dreyfus adds the motor and gestalt detail.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the robot passage of the *Discourse* and the [[Concepts/The Two Tests and the Animal-Machine (Descartes)|two tests / bête-machine]] are the ancestor. Dreyfus keeps the inadequacy claim and swaps the soul for the body.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: the most developed later ally. The 1998 [[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)|extended mind]] paper (now primary) and later [[Concepts/Active Inference and Action (Clark)|active inference]] are attempts to *mechanize* the involved body. Clark and Chalmers will count a notebook as part of the *mind*; Dreyfus's 1992 worry is whether the *learner* is enough like us. The 1998 paper does not address learning, only coupling.
- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] et al.**: same Merleau-Ponty ancestor; they define [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]] as perceptually guided action whose world is *enacted*, not recovered. Brooks's "use the world as its own model" (their ch. 9) is Chapman's slogan in another bibliography. They add operational closure, enacted color, and a mindfulness method Dreyfus rejected as introspection. See [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)]].
- **[[Thinkers/John Searle]]**: both reject disembodied programs as minds. Searle's robot reply to the Chinese Room still fails (for Searle) because syntax is syntax even in a robot. Dreyfus would say a real robot body might matter — if it is a *lived* body, not a sensor suite.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: the bat's body is what makes its point of view unreachable. Dreyfus cares about the body's role in *coping*, not in what-it-is-like.
- **[[Thinkers/Antonio Damasio]]**: same anti-disembodied-reason target, lesion register rather than phenomenological. Frontal patients *have* bodies and still lose advantageous choice — the body must be *mapped and felt*, not merely possessed. See [[Concepts/Somatic Marker Hypothesis (Damasio)]], [[Concepts/Body-Minded Brain and Neural Self (Damasio)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Embodied vs formal intelligence: [[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]].
- Dreyfus allows a non-digital, situated automaton in the long run. That concession is easy to miss and is the opening Clark and later 4E cognition walk through.
- Whether current robots + foundation models are "lived bodies" or "sensor suites" is the 2026 form of the question.
