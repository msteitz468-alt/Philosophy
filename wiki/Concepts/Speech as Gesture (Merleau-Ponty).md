# Speech as Gesture (Merleau-Ponty)
> "Speech does not translate a ready-made thought; rather, speech accomplishes thought." The word is a gesture, and its signification is a world.

## Summary

Chapter VI of Part One ("The Body as Expression, and Speech") is offered as the place "to leave behind, once and for all, the classical subject–object dichotomy." Empiricism and intellectualism both make the word *have* no sense: either it is a third-person acoustic event associated with a thought, or it is the empty envelope of a thought already complete in the inner man.

Against both: **"For the speaker, then, speech does not translate a ready-made thought; rather, speech accomplishes thought."** "The orator does not think prior to speaking… his speech is his thought." The body "converts a certain motor essence into a vocalization… because the body is a natural power of expression." **"Speech is a genuine gesture and, just like all gestures, speech too contains its own sense."** **"Speech is a gesture, and its signification is a world."** Communication is bodily reciprocity: I do not decode a representation behind the other's words; I take up a style of being-in-the-world the way I take up an angry gesture. Anger *is* the gesture, as Sartre will also say of expression — but here the point is the *accomplishment* of thought, not the Look.

**Speaking speech** (*parole parlante*) vs **spoken speech** (*parole parlée*): languages sediment speech. Constituted vocabulary and syntax are the available significations; authentic acts of expression (writer, artist, philosopher) are the nascent intention that exceeds them. Speech "is the excess of our existence beyond natural being." There are no merely natural signs set over against purely conventional ones; the linguistic gesture sketches its sense the way the body sketches a world.

Pathological confirmation is the same Schneider, now with **congealed** language (no improvisation), and color-name amnesia as loss of a categorial *attitude*, not of verbal images. The young woman's **aphonia** is a refusal of coexistence, not a voluntary silence and not a missing verbal image.

The sexuality chapter already uses the analogy. Existence "accomplishes itself in the body" the way speech accomplishes thought. Sexuality is "continuously present in human life as an atmosphere"; it is neither a reflex cycle nor a *cogitatio* aiming at a *cogitatum*. "Erotic perception is not a *cogitatio* that intends a *cogitatum*; through one body it aims at another body." Libido "is not an instinct… it is the subject's general power of adhering to different milieus… the libido is what ensures that a man has a history." Existence is "the equivocal milieu" of communication between sexuality and the rest of life; neither reduces to the other. "Man is an historical idea, not a natural species."

## Key Claims / Positions

- Thought is not prior to speech; speech accomplishes it.
- The body is a natural power of expression.
- Speaking vs spoken speech: sedimentation of authentic expression into available significations.
- Sexuality is an atmosphere / original intentionality, not a function or a representation.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: the linguistic half of motor intentionality. Same *I can*.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: language as a motor skill one *dwells* in (with Polanyi). The five-stage model is Dreyfus's; the cane/organist logic is here.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]**: meaning as use / forms of life is a cousin, not a source. MP's route is the body as gesture, not the public language-game as such.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: "These frowns… *are* the anger" (BN) agrees that expression is not a sign of an inner state. Sartre does not make speech the accomplishment of thought or the exit from the subject–object split.
- **[[Thinkers/Wilfrid Sellars]]**: the myth of the given is an intellectualist foil MP would recognize; Sellars's linguistic holism is not this gesture theory.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Whether "speech accomplishes thought" can survive inner rehearsal, aphasia with intact thought, and the later structuralist objection that *langue* precedes *parole*.
- *The Visible and the Invisible* will rework expression; that recantation is not in this chapter.
