# Semantic Externalism (Putnam)
> Meaning and thought-content are not determined by what is in the head. For Putnam, the missing determinants are causal and communal: without the right relations to trees (or to a community that talks about trees), one cannot think about trees.

## Summary

Pritchard and Ranalli (2016) state the thesis Putnam needs for the BIV argument this way: "in order to be able to even think thoughts with a particular content, then certain external conditions — in particular, causal conditions — need to be met." Their example is a Martian who has never encountered trees and is not part of a linguistic community with an established practice of referring to trees. That Martian "cannot even think thoughts about trees. One’s thoughts would instead be about something altogether different, insofar as they had content at all."

The same constraint is applied to a lifelong [[Concepts/Brain in a Vat|BIV]] stimulated by supercomputers. The BIV has no causal route, direct or indirect, to trees. So the BIV's word "tree" does not refer to trees, and the BIV cannot think the thought that it is a brain in a vat. Vat-English is **not disquotational**: "I'm a BIV" in vat-English is true iff the speaker is a BIV-in-the-image (a BIV*), not iff the speaker is a BIV.

This is **content** externalism used as a semantic constraint on what can be thought, not a theory of how cognition is dynamically driven. It is the position Clark and Chalmers (1998) called **passive** / historical and refused to identify with mind-externalism. See [[Concepts/Active Externalism (Clark and Chalmers)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Hilary Putnam]]** (1981b, reception only): the load-bearing premise of [[Arguments/Putnam Brain in a Vat Argument]]. Putnam himself (1981b: 16) describes the investigation as transcendental — preconditions of reference "built in to the nature of our minds themselves," though not wholly independent of empirical assumptions. Twin Earth (1975) is the other famous application; this chapter does not reconstruct it.
- **Tyler Burge** (1979, named by Clark and Chalmers, not by this chapter): social externalism about the mental. The vault's 1998 paper treats "Putnam/Burge" as one contrast class.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] and [[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]** (1998, primary): a **third** option. Twin-Earth features are "distal and historical," "dangling at the other end of a long causal chain"; twins behave indistinguishably. Otto's notebook is *in the loop*. They do not reject Twin-Earth content; they refuse to identify mind-externalism with it. Footnote 2: much of the *appeal* of content externalism may actually be the appeal of active externalism.
- **Crispin Wright** (1992, as Pritchard and Ranalli report him): externalism **problematizes the self-knowledge of content** the BIV argument needs. Homophony between two speakers does not yield sameness of meaning; the assessor cannot, from the armchair, know that "BIV" in Putnam's language means what it does in the assessor's. This is the same family of worries as McKinsey (1991), Boghossian (1989), Davies (1998), Wright (2000).
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]** (1986, reported): the causal theory of reference is "refuted by the evident possibility and intelligibility of skepticism." By "tree" Nagel does not mean "just anything that is causally responsible for my impressions of trees." See [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]].
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]** (*Reality+* ch. 20): Kripke/Putnam externalism can anchor a lifelong sim's "hurricane" to virtual hurricanes, but "ultimately, it's structuralism, rather than externalism, that drives the simulation realism." Externalism fails for structural words like "computer" and "simulation."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Whether causal constraints on reference refute, or are refuted by, the intelligibility of skepticism — see [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]].
- Open (flagged in the chapter, not resolved): compatibility of content externalism with a priori self-knowledge of one's own thought contents (the McKinsey problem).
- This page is written from a 2016 reconstruction plus the 1998 Clark/Chalmers contrast. Putnam 1975 and 1981 remain outstanding.
