# Primacy of the Will in Self-Consciousness (Schopenhauer)
> The will is the strong blind man; the intellect is the sighted lame man on his shoulders.

## Summary

Chapters XVIII–XXII of *WWR* Volume 2 are the vault’s primary for *how* the will is known, and for the claim that it has **primacy** in self-consciousness. They do not replace [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]]; they specify the inner path Volume 1 opened.

**XVIII.** On the path of *objective* knowledge “we shall never get beyond the representation.” Locke barred the senses from the thing-in-itself; Kant barred the perceiving understanding as well. The only other path: “*we ourselves are the thing-in-itself*.” Willing is the one event known immediately from within. Nature is to be read from us, not we from nature.

This is **not** adequate noumenal knowledge. Inner observation still splits knower (intellect) and known (will). Space and causality fall away; **time** remains. The will is “the nearest and clearest *phenomenon* of the thing-in-itself”; it has “cast off its veils, but still does not appear quite naked.” After Book IV’s self-abolition of will, further determinations may be absolutely unknowable.

**XIX** is the *locus classicus*: a battery of proofs that will is man’s inner, true, **unconscious** nature and intellect a brain-function, “mere accident,” tool, lantern, parasite of the organism. Will has no degrees of *essence*, only of excitement; intellect has degrees from insect to genius. Intellect presents motives; will decides, often hiding the real motive from the intellect. Will is untiring (heart, vital force); intellect tires and must sleep. Character is unalterable; teaching changes only the application of motives. Personal identity is the identical will, not memory. The will-to-live is a *prius* of intellect (*horror mortis*).

**XX.** What is subjectively intellect appears objectively as **brain**; what is subjectively will appears objectively as the **entire organism**. Act of will and bodily movement are **identical**, not cause and effect — the same claim *Fourfold Root* §21 made, now as metaphysics of the body. Motives (representations in the cerebrum) reach muscle only as *stimuli*. Blood and heart are the most immediate objectification.

**XXII.** Knowledge arises from individuation; “where the being-in-itself of things begins, knowledge ceases.” Intellect is *medium of motives*, “light of nature,” not a world-interpreter. Genius is surplus intellect detached from will — the bridge to Book III.

## Key Claims

- Objective knowledge cannot reach the thing-in-itself; the inner path can approach it.
- The approach is not naked: time still veils; after denial, more may remain unknown.
- Will is substance; intellect is accident and tool.
- Body and will are one under two modes of knowledge, not a causal pair.
- Primacy is not “I know what I will” as immediate certainty — Nietzsche later attacks that slogan. Here primacy is metaphysical and physiological.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — the 1844 specification of the “narrow gateway” *Parerga* I names: the body as twofold.
- **[[Concepts/Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)]]** — motivation remains causality from within; will is now *not* a last causal link but the substratum of irritability.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — the empty-concept rule is admitted for everything *except* knowledge of one’s own willing, and even that is not exhaustive.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — *BGE* §16–19: “I will” is a superstition of immediate certainty; the will is “a unity only in name.” Reception of this chapter’s *tone*, not a reading of its inadequacy-clause.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] “Will = thing-in-itself” on the thinker page must carry XVIII’s qualification: nearest phenomenon, not quite naked, not the last residue after abolition.
- > [!warning] Do not weld to [[Concepts/Free Will as Controlled Hallucination (Seth)]] or to Kantian autonomy. Freedom here is *aseity* of the *esse*, not of the *operari* (Ch. XXV).
