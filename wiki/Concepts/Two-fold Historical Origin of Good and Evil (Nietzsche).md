# Two-fold Historical Origin of Good and Evil (Nietzsche)
> 1878: good and bad begin as requital among the powerful and as hostility among the subjugated — not yet ressentiment, not yet master and slave *morality*.

## Summary

Chapter II of *Human, All Too Human* ("History of the Moral Feelings," 1878) gives the account *On the Genealogy of Morals* (1887) later calls a clumsy first expression. The 1878 wording must be kept. "The notion of good and bad has a two-fold historical origin": (1) in ruling races and castes, who can requite — for a period "noble and servile, master and slave"; (2) in the subjugated, for whom every other is hostile and "goodness" looks like refined badness. Existing morality developed on the foundation laid by ruling races and castes. The chapter then dissolves responsibility itself: discretionary freedom is a fable; to be moral is to obey ancient usage; "Everything is necessity… All is guiltlessness." Do not rewrite this as the 1887 slave revolt or as [[Concepts/Ressentiment (Nietzsche)]].

## Key Claims (1878, §§35–107)

**Psychological observation (§§35–38).** The "human, all-too-human" is a neglected art (La Rochefoucauld). Science can no longer spare "psychological dissection." False analyses of "disinterested actions" rear a false ethic propped by "religion and like mythological monstrosities."

**§37 (the unnamed Rée book).** The author of *Concerning the Origin of the Moral Feelings*: "The moral man… stands no nearer the knowable (metaphysical) world than the physical man" — an axe to "metaphysical necessities." Paul Rée is not named in this chapter; *Genealogy*'s Preface will name him later.

**Fable of discretionary freedom (§39).** Estimates run acts → conduct → motives → nature; nature is a necessary synthesis, so "man is to be made responsible for nothing." The history of moral estimates is "the history of error, of the error of responsibility." Schopenhauer infers *intelligibeln Freiheit* (esse free, operari necessary) from guilt-depression. Nietzsche: "only because man deems himself free, but not because he is free, does he experience remorse and the stings of conscience." "No one is responsible for his acts, no one for his nature; to judge is tantamount to being unjust." Plato and Kant are "equally responsible" for the fable.

**Two-fold origin (§45).**
> "The notion of good and bad has a two-fold historical origin: namely, first, in the spirit of ruling races and castes."

Who can requite is "good," the powerless "bad." "Good and bad is, for a considerable period, tantamount to noble and servile, master and slave." Enemies who can requite are not bad (Homer). Second origin: among the subjugated, every other is hostile; "goodness" looks like refined badness. "Our existing morality has developed upon the foundation laid by ruling races and castes."

This uses "master and slave" as a *period synonym* for noble/servile and power to requite. It does **not** name [[Concepts/Master Morality and Slave Morality (Nietzsche)]], priestly inversion, or ressentiment.

**Justice among equals (§§92–93).** Justice originates among "approximate equals in power" (Thucydides): compromise, "reprisal and exchange." Revenge and gratitude belong here. High "unegotistical" esteem rests on forgetfulness of the egoistic origin. "Each has as much law on his side as his power extends."

**Three phases; custom vs. emancipation (§§94, 96–97).** Morality hitherto: permanently useful; honor and others' opinion; one "becomes a law giver to opinion," lives as "collective individuality." "To be moral, virtuous, praiseworthy means to yield obedience to ancient law and hereditary usage." The fundamental opposition is **custom vs. emancipation**, not egoistic vs. non-egoistic.

**Necessity and guiltlessness (§§99, 102, 106–107).** "Bad" acts are self-preservation/pleasure. The waterfall: all acts "compulsory," "mathematically calculated"; assumed free will is part of the mechanism. "The absolute irresponsibility of man for his acts and his nature is the bitterest drop in the cup of him who has knowledge." "Between good and bad actions there is no difference in kind but, at most, in degree. Good acts are sublimated evil. Bad acts are degraded, imbruted good." "Everything is necessity… All is guiltlessness, and knowledge is the way to insight into this guiltlessness." The goal is the "wise, guiltless man (conscious of guiltlessness)."

## How This Relates to the 1887 Genealogy

[[Sources/The Genealogy of Morals - Nietzsche (1887)]] Preface: the origin of good and evil was "first expressed clumsily" in *Human, All Too Human*. What 1887 adds, and 1878 does not have:

| 1878 (this chapter) | 1887 (*Genealogy*) |
|---|---|
| Two-fold origin: ruling requital vs. subjugated hostility | Master *morality* (good/bad from pathos of distance) vs. slave *morality* (good/evil from ressentiment) |
| "master and slave" = noble/servile, power to requite | Priestly inversion; Rome vs. Judaea; "evil enemy" |
| Custom vs. emancipation | Morality of custom → [[Concepts/Sovereign Individual (Nietzsche)]] |
| Irresponsibility / guiltlessness | [[Concepts/Bad Conscience (Nietzsche)]] as internalization |
| No "ressentiment" | Ressentiment as creative engine |
| Question is still origin, plus dissolution of responsibility | Question is the *value* of morality |

Do not file 1878 as an early draft of the slave revolt.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] "Master and slave" in §45 is not [[Concepts/Master Morality and Slave Morality (Nietzsche)]]. The words overlap; the doctrine does not.
- The vault's BGE "beyond good and evil" is not this chapter's conclusion. 1878 ends in *guiltlessness*, not revaluation.
