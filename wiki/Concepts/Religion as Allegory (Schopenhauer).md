# Religion as Allegory (Schopenhauer)
> There may be a true philosophy. There is no true religion *sensu proprio*. Religion is true at best *sensu allegorico*.

## Summary

The systematic cut is already in *WWR* Volume 2, Chapter XVII: philosophy is obliged to be true *sensu stricto et proprio*; religion only *sensu allegorico*. “Before the people truth cannot appear naked.” See [[Concepts/Man's Need for Metaphysics (Schopenhauer)]].

Chapter XV of *Parerga* II is the full popular essay. Volume 1 already called moral theology a “sop” and an allegory, and pantheism a euphemism for atheism.

**§174 is a dialogue, not a verdict.** **Demopheles** (religion as necessary popular allegory) vs **Philalethes** (truth *sensu proprio*). Do not treat either speaker as Schopenhauer. They part agreeing each has stared at only one face of Janus. Shared premise, restated later in the author’s voice: religion is true at best *sensu allegorico* / *sensu mythico*. Philosophy wants the unvarnished, *sensu proprio*. “Mystery” is the theological name for that allegory. Rationalists and supernaturalists both err by demanding literal truth.

Man is an *animal metaphysicum*. The metaphysical need will be met by authority if not by argument. Even a true philosophy would be *faith* for nine-tenths. Demopheles therefore treats religion as Kantian “hypotheses for a practical purpose” / regulative schemes. Philalethes refuses the *pia fraus*.

**Authorial §§175–182.** Philosophy concerns what can be *known*, not what should be *believed*. No non-human “revelation”; only sages’ thoughts, often in allegory, then called religions. Priesthoods live by pretending to possess the riddle’s word.

Christianity is Indian-ethical allegory grafted on Jewish theism. NT is pessimism (world not an end; devil “prince of this world”); OT is optimism/theism (“all things very good”). Augustinism (original sin, grace, small number of the elect) is “the real Christianity”; Pelagianism/rationalism reduces it to “crude and shallow” optimism. Taken *sensu proprio*, Augustinian election + eternal hell + creation from nothing is a theodicy disaster. Taken *sensu allegorico*, the few elect = few who deny the will / reach Nirvana; “eternal damnation” = *this* world as Samsara. See [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]].

**The fundamental difference in religions is optimism vs pessimism**, “certainly not whether they are monotheism, polytheism, Trimurti, Trinity, pantheism, or atheism (like Buddhism).” A personal being invoked and thanked is idolatry, whether the idol is wood or abstract concepts.

Physics and metaphysics are religion’s “natural foes.” Civilization is high among Christian nations *because* Christianity’s influence has declined. “All religion is antagonistic to culture.” The chapter’s name for the end of that fight is the **euthanasia of religion**.

## Key Claims

- Religion = popular metaphysics, true only as allegory.
- Philosophy = what can be known. Faith that taught what we could know would be ridiculous.
- Dialogue ≠ author. Shared premises may be used when §§175–182 restate them.
- Optimism/pessimism is the real cut among religions, not the number of gods.
- Genuine moral motive is sympathy, “independent of all religions.” See [[Concepts/Compassion and Justice (Schopenhauer)]].
