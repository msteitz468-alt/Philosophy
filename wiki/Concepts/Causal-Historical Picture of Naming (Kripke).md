# Causal-Historical Picture of Naming (Kripke)

> Not a theory of necessary and sufficient conditions. "I want to present just a better picture than the picture presented by the received views." A baby is baptized; the name is "spread from link to link as if by a chain"; the receiver must intend the same reference. What is relevant is "the actual chain of communication," not the descriptions the speaker could recite.

## Summary

[[Sources/Naming and Necessity - Saul Kripke (1980)]] Lecture II replaces the Frege–Russell description theory, and its cluster weakening, with a **picture** of how names refer. Kripke does not call this "the causal theory of reference." Closest phrases: "chain of communication," "initial baptism," "causal or historical connection" of "the required type" (Lecture II, pp. 91–96 and n. 43).

**Received view.** A name is a disguised definite description (Frege, Russell) or a **cluster** / family of descriptions (Wittgenstein on Moses, Searle, Strawson, Ziff). Lecture I splits this into a theory of **meaning** and a theory of **reference**; only the meaning reading yields analyses of "Moses does not exist" and of informative identity by substitution. Lecture II restates six theses plus noncircularity **(C)**:

1. To every name `'X'` there corresponds a cluster: the φ's such that the speaker believes `φX`.
2–4. The speaker believes the φ's uniquely identify; if a weighted most is uniquely satisfied, that is the referent; if the vote yields no unique object, `'X'` does not refer.
5. "If X exists, then X has most of the φ's" is known a priori.
6. That same sentence "expresses a necessary truth (in the idiolect of the speaker)."
**(C)** "For any successful theory, the account must not be circular" (Lecture I, p. 67; Lecture II, p. 71).

**(6) is false** because names are rigid: "It just is not, in any intuitive sense of necessity, a necessary truth that Aristotle had the properties commonly attributed to him" (Lecture II, p. 74). Searle's necessary "logical sum" of Aristotle's achievements is "clearly" false. Kneale's "'Socrates' means the individual called 'Socrates'" either isn't trifling or violates (C) ("Glunk").

**(2)–(4)** fail on a large class of cases. Ordinary speakers of "Feynman," "Cicero," "Einstein" need not believe uniquely identifying properties; an encyclopedia-circle still violates (C). **Gödel/Schmidt**: even if the man we call Gödel stole the incompleteness theorem from Schmidt, "when our ordinary man uses the name 'Gödel', he really means to refer to Schmidt… But it seems to me that we are not. We simply are not" (Lecture II, p. 84). Peano/Dedekind, Columbus, Jonah: the vote does not fix the referent; a name may refer though nothing satisfies the φ's. Strawson's "borrowing credentials" still treats the source as an identifying description; the speaker may forget or misremember.

**The picture.** "An initial 'baptism' takes place. Here the object may be named by ostension, or the reference of the name may be fixed by a description. When the name is 'passed from link to link', the receiver of the name must… intend… to use it with the same reference as the man from whom he heard it" (Lecture II, p. 96). The baptismal description **fixes reference**; it is **not synonymous** with the name (n. 42). Only in "a rare class of cases, usually initial baptisms," do (2)–(4) all hold.

**Addenda.** Baptism is oversimplified (d). Reference can *shift*: Madagascar (Evans), native use to island via Marco Polo; "a present intention to refer… overrides the original intention to preserve reference in the historical chain" (e, p. 163). Buck-passing is allowed if it does not loop (c). Donnellan independently similar; Kaplan's `Dthat` can formalize much of the argument (f).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Bertrand Russell]]**: principal target. Ordinary names are descriptions; genuine names are perhaps only "this"/"that" under acquaintance. See [[Concepts/Knowledge by Acquaintance and Knowledge by Description (Russell)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]**: *PI* §79 Moses cluster is filed as a source of the cluster theory (Lecture I). The metre argument is a different collision — [[Concepts/The Standard Metre Bar (Wittgenstein)]].
- **Searle / Strawson** (no pages): cluster as meaning (Searle's logical sum) or as a democratic vote (Strawson). Both rejected.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: *Reality+* later loans "Kripke/Putnam externalism" to simulation semantics. That is a reception. This picture is about *names*, not about virtual hurricanes.
- **Putnam** (no page): addendum/Lecture III ally on natural kinds, not the source of this naming picture.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- The picture is not necessary-and-sufficient conditions. Madagascar-style shifts are named and left "for further work."
- > [!warning] Do not call this "the causal theory of reference" as if that were Kripke's title. He refused the word *theory* in the N&S sense.
