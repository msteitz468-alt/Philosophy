# Active Inference and Action (Clark)
> Action is not the execution of motor commands but a self-fulfilling prophecy: the brain predicts the bodily sensations that *would* occur if a movement were already done, then moves the body to make those predictions come true.

## Summary

Under [[Concepts/Predictive Processing (Clark)]], action is built the same way as perception. *Surfing Uncertainty* Ch 4 is the technical source. Two error-reducing moves: change predictions, or act so the world fits them. "Such predictions act as self-fulfilling prophecies" (4.1). Motor cortex is not the inverse of visual cortex; both send top-down predictions (proprioceptive vs retinotopic). Motor commands are proprioceptive predictions cashed by spinal reflex arcs. Adams, Shipp & Friston, quoted: "the perceptual and motor systems should not be regarded as separate but instead as a single active inference machine that tries to predict its sensory input in all domains." Direction of fit differs (Anscombe's shopping list); the computation does not.

Relative to classical optimal feedback control, active inference drops a distinct inverse model, a distinct efference copy, and explicit cost/value functions: costs, rewards, even "desired consequences" are folded into priors over trajectories. "Reward is a perceptual (hedonic) consequence of behavior, not a cause" (4.8). No free lunch: the burden shifts onto acquiring the generative model. Pickering & Clark: Integral vs Auxiliary Forward Model.

The 2023 popularization compresses this: "Ordinary daily actions are caused by predictions of the flow of bodily sensations that would occur if that very action were to be performed" — "the first fully unified treatment of perception and action."

To move, "the brain — when I want to move my hand — [must] gently lie to itself, forcibly downgrading genuine sensory information associated with the currently immobile state of my arm" and upgrading the prediction of the moved state. That downgrading/upgrading *is* [[Concepts/Precision-Weighting (Clark)]] in the motor domain. This resolves a long-standing anomaly (motor and sensory cortex sharing the same wiring): "one wiring diagram to rule them all."

## Key Claims / Positions

- **Ideomotor theory revived** (Hermann Lotze, William James): "the idea of the completed action is what brings the actual action about" — reversing commonsense causality. The mental representation of the finished effect is the cause.
- **Passive-motion paradigm / marionette metaphor**: the brain solves for the *endpoint*, and the lower joints and degrees of freedom fall into place automatically, as if the body were pulled toward the goal.
- **Self-tickling fails** (Blakemore–Wolpert–Frith) because a forward model plus predictive coding attenuates well-predicted reafference. 2016 diagnoses this as *incomplete*: mere veridical prediction does not yet explain attenuation (a familiar saccade does not blind you); full attenuation needs [[Concepts/Precision-Weighting (Clark)|precision]] (Ch 7). Schizophrenia *reduces* that attenuation and invites agency delusions. The older "efference copy" / corollary-discharge story is absorbed, then dropped as a distinct extra mechanism.
- **Active sensing / embodied expertise**: perception is itself intelligent action. The outfielder "run[s] with their eye on the ball, so that their own movement cancels out any apparent changes in the acceleration of that ball" — controlling action by predicted sensory consequences rather than computing a trajectory.
- **Temporal depth**: long-term, goal-directed behavior uses counterfactual predictions nested above short-term ones, requiring "realistic optimism" (Clark quotes Muhammad Ali: "If my mind can conceive it and my heart can believe it, then I can achieve it").
- This is the **active inference** side of Karl Friston's framework (named in Clark's notes): predictions play the role usually assigned to motor commands. [[Thinkers/Anil Seth]] credits Friston with the coinage and the slogan "actions *are* perceptions": self-fulfilling proprioceptive predictions, plus "disattention" to the body so that it can move. Same mechanism; Seth then claims the *primary* function of the whole machinery is physiological control, not worldly action ([[Concepts/Beast Machine Theory (Seth)]]).

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: strong convergence with [[Concepts/Readiness-to-Hand and Presence-at-Hand (Heidegger)]] and [[Concepts/Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)]] — the skilled, absorbed agent who copes without explicit representation. Active inference is a mechanistic model of Heidegger's non-thematic, world-engaged coping: the hammer's readiness-to-hand as a low-error, prediction-guided coupling.
- **[[Concepts/The Three Disciplines - Perception, Action, and Will (Hadot)]]**: Clark naturalizes the perception→action link the Stoics treated normatively; for Clark it is one continuous error-minimizing loop.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the unified perception–action loop dissolves the Cartesian sensory-input/motor-output separation.
- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]** (2013, earlier): the same two directions of fit — change the model or change the input — plus two Hohwy-specific stresses. Perception alone cannot reduce surprisal ("sinking with weights on the feet"); only action can, and only if phenotype priors bind the creature to low-surprisal states. They must *alternate* or the bound loosens. Dark Room is answered by "we are not defined by dark-room phenotypes" (p. 87), not mainly by Clark's later "we predict foraging." Agency reduces to expected proprioceptive precision (p. 84). Action does **not**, for Hohwy, buy a world-involving mind — see [[Concepts/Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)]].
