# Wu Wei — Nondoing (Laozi)

> Ziporyn's rendering of *wuwei*: **nondoing**, not "non-action" as idleness. The sage "abides in the work of nondoing" (ch. 2). The constancy of the course is "doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone" (ch. 37). The work of the course is daily subtraction until that nondoing is reached (ch. 48). To do something with a thing is to ruin it; the sage "helps each thing be as it is of its own accord" (ch. 64).

## What nondoing is in this text

Not a blank refusal to move. The same chapters that command nondoing also command: empty hearts and fill bellies (ch. 3); handle the difficult while easy, the vast while minute (ch. 63); start the thousand-mile journey underfoot (ch. 64); take no action so the people transform themselves (ch. 57). Nondoing is the refusal of *purposive intervention* — of taking a course *as the right course* — so that the B-background can do what A-forcing wrecks.

Paired throughout with **noninstruction** (*wuyan* teaching): "the teaching of noninstruction, the benefits of nondoing — few things in the world extend as far as these" (ch. 43). Highest rulers give instructions "so sparingly" that when the work is done the people say they did it "of ourselves, all on our own" (ch. 17) — Ziporyn's hook for *ziran*.

## Load-bearing claims

- **Ch. 2.** Because opposites already generate each other, the sage "abides in the work of nondoing, practicing the teaching of noninstruction." Through this the ten thousand things arise, none possessing or possessed; achievements complete, no credit; "never dwelling… never gone."
- **Ch. 3.** "Doing nondoing, all is ordered, everything heals."
- **Ch. 10.** "In seeing clear through all the four directions, can you keep on doing nothing at all?"
- **Ch. 37.** "The constancy of the course lies in doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone. If lords and kings could hold fast to it, all things would transform of their own accord." Desires that arise are settled with "the nameless unhewn."
- **Ch. 38.** Highest virtuosity "*does* nothing, for it harbors no motives or purposes." Lower virtuosity is always something done with a motive.
- **Ch. 43.** "The softest things in the world run roughshod over the firmest… What has no presence enters where there is no gap. This is how I know that nondoing has its benefits."
- **Ch. 47–48.** The sage "knows without traveling… achieve[s] without doing." "'The work of learning is daily addition.' But the work of the course is daily subtraction, subtracting and further subtracting until nondoing is reached — a nondoing that leaves nothing undone. It is the constancy of having no task to do that allows one to gain the world. Wherever there is anything to do, no amount of doing is ever sufficient to gain the world."
- **Ch. 57.** "It is by doing nothing at all about it that the whole world is gained." More taboos, poorer people; more laws, more thieves. "I take no action and the people transform themselves. I love staying still and the people rectify themselves. I do nothing and the people enrich themselves. I am free of desire and the people, of their own accord, revert to their unhewn simplicity."
- **Ch. 63–64.** "Do nondoing. Serve what imposes no task. Taste what has no flavor." Sages "achieve their greatness by never doing anything great." "To do something with it is to ruin it. To hold it is to lose it. And so a sage does nothing and thus ruins nothing… This is how he helps each thing be as it is of its own accord — never venturing any doing at all."
- **Ch. 81.** The sage's course is "to take action for some one purpose yet without striving against any other."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. [[Concepts/Shih - Momentum, Formlessness, and Striking Weakness (Sun Tzu)]]**: Sun Tzu's water-like formlessness is still *for* victory — impose your will, do not let the enemy impose his. Laozi's nondoing is not a tactic inside a campaign; "to do something with it is to ruin it." Ch. 68 ("the most skillful victory… is never to engage them at all") is the nearest overlap; even there the point is the "power that comes from not contending," not calculation of the five factors.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Dichotomy of Control (Epictetus)]]**: Stoic practice *uses* impressions and assents only to what is up to us. Nondoing does not assay impressions; it subtracts the project of governing by knowing. Closer in feel to not forcing externals; different machinery.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Epoché (Suspension of Judgment)]]**: both stop a certain kind of doing (judging / intervening). *Epoché* is epistemic; nondoing is practical-political-ontological. Ataraxia follows epoché "by chance"; the *Daodejing* promises that the world "will settle itself" (ch. 37).
- **Vs. Confucian *wuwei* (now primary, Chin 15.5)**: "The one ruler who was able to order the world without taking any [deliberate] action [*wuwei*] was Shun. So what did he do? He held himself respectfully and faced south—that was all." Chin: Confucian scholars insist this is not "the sage acts by doing nothing, and everything is in order"; both still make the ruler's cultivation the key. Shun's facing south is ritual stillness after *de* is complete, not daily subtraction until there is no task (ch. 48). See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Duty (Kant)]] / [[Concepts/Radical Freedom and Situation (Sartre)]]**: both modern figures make agency the site of value (the good will; the condemned-to-be-free legislator). Nondoing relocates value to what happens when agency steps down.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Skilled Coping and the Five Stages of Expertise (Dreyfus)]]**: Dreyfus's expert no longer follows rules; he copes. That is still *skill*, a high *de* in the pre-ironic sense. Laozi's nondoing includes a suspicion of skill itself ("cut off skill and discard profit," ch. 19).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Chs. 3 and 65 (empty minds; befuddle the people; wisdom in government is pillage) can be read as nondoing *or* as a technique of rule. The authoritarian reading treats wu-wei as the ruler's monopoly on knowing. Flag, do not close.
- "Leaving nothing undone" is easy to hear as a hidden consequentialism (nondoing as the best means to order). Ch. 38 blocks that: highest virtuosity harbors *no* motives. The paradox is load-bearing.
