# Socratism and the Theoretical Man (Nietzsche)
> Whatever is to be beautiful must also be sensible. That law wrecks the old tragedy.

## Summary

*The Birth of Tragedy* §§11–15 (1872; Kaufmann) give the vault’s first *primary* Socrates from Nietzsche — a cultural-aesthetic type, not the later physiological case.

Greek tragedy “died by suicide, in consequence of an insoluble conflict” (§11). The death-struggle is fought by **Euripides**. The heir is New Attic Comedy, “a monument to its painful and laborious death.” Euripides transports *the spectator* onto the stage: the common man, “bourgeois mediocrity,” life “as it really is.” The older hero’s incommensurable depth is rejected because **reason** is now “the fountainhead of all doing and enjoying.” His aim is “to eliminate from tragedy the primitive and pervasive Dionysian element” and rebuild on “non-Dionysian art, custom and philosophy” (§12). The result is not epic-Apollinian drama but inartistic naturalism: “cold paradoxical *ideas*” plus “fiery *emotions*” instead of Apollinian contemplation and Dionysian transport.

Euripides is a **mask**. The daemon speaking through him is **Socrates**. The new antagonism is Dionysian vs **Socratic**; “tragedy was to perish in the conflict.” The wrecking-law is **aesthetic Socratism**: “Whatever is to be beautiful must also be sensible” — later, “whatever is to be beautiful must be conscious” // “whatever is to be good must be conscious.” Parallel to “knowledge alone makes men virtuous.” “If the old tragedy was wrecked, aesthetic Socratism is to blame… we may call Socrates the god’s chief opponent.”

Socratic posture: everything great works “only from instinct”; that condemns prevailing art and ethics. In Socrates the roles reverse: “instinct is the critic, consciousness the creator—truly a monstrosity **per defectum**!” He is the pattern of the *non-mystic*. **Plato** burns his own tragedies; the Platonic dialogue is the “lifeboat” of shipwrecked poetry (image of the novel; poetry as *ancilla* of dialectic). The Apollinian becomes **logical schematism**.

Three maxims — virtue is knowledge; all sins arise from ignorance; only the virtuous are happy — are “basic formulations of **optimism**” and “**spell the death of tragedy**.” “Optimistic dialectics took up the whip of its syllogisms and drove *music* out of tragedy.”

§15 names the new type: the **theoretical man**, the **theoretical optimist**. Science lives on a “sublime metaphysical *illusion*”: thought, using the thread of logic, “can penetrate the deepest abysses of being” and even *correct* it. The **dying Socrates** is that science’s emblem. At the periphery, logic “bites its own tail” and turns into **tragic insight**, which needs art. The open symbol for a rebirth is the dream-command “Practice music, Socrates!” — the *artistic Socrates* / *music-practicing Socrates*. §§16–17 repeat: rebirth of tragedy is hoped for only after science is pursued to its limits (Kant and Schopenhauer as that limit).

Do not import 1888 *décadence*, “fight the instincts,” or *ressentiment* into this 1872 type. Those are *Twilight*’s diagnosis of the same last-words motif.

## Key Claims

- Tragedy dies by suicide, not by accident; Euripides is the struggle, Socrates the daemon.
- Aesthetic Socratism: beauty = intelligibility / consciousness.
- Instinct criticises, consciousness creates: a defect, the non-mystic.
- Three Socratic maxims = optimism = death of tragedy = music driven out.
- Theoretical man: science as the faith that thought can know *and correct* being.
- The music-practicing Socrates is an open question, not a character already achieved.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]] (1872)** — Socrates as anti-Dionysian cultural type. First revaluation’s antagonist, before “revaluation” is a slogan.
- **[[Thinkers/Socrates]]** — add as a *dated layer*. This page is the 1872 voice. Do not overwrite [[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]’s figure, *Gay Science* §340 (“life is a disease”), or *Twilight* “Problem of Socrates” (shared physiology of the sages; dialectics as last weapon; he forced the hemlock).
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]** — burns tragedies; dialogue as mixed-genre lifeboat; poetry made *ancilla*. Reception of Plato as Socratism’s literary form, not a reading of the *Republic*.
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]** — not the target of §§11–15. Catharsis is rejected later (§24), not here.
- **Later Nietzsche.** *Twilight* Problem of Socrates 2: “I realized that Socrates and Plato were symptoms of degeneration… anti-Greek (*Birth of Tragedy*, 1872).” The late book *cites* this one, then adds physiology. Keep the citation as reception.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] 1872 Socrates is a type of *optimism and science*. 1888 Socrates is a type of *decadence and instinct-war*. Same last-words motif; different diagnosis. Layers, not a merge.
- > [!warning] The “artistic Socrates” hoped in §14–15 is not Kaufmann’s later gloss on *The Gay Science* as an “artistic Socrates.” Do not back-fill the 1882 title from this 1872 question.
