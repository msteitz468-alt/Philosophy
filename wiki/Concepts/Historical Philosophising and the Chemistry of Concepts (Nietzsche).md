# Historical Philosophising and the Chemistry of Concepts (Nietzsche)
> There is no antithesis except in popular or metaphysical exaggeration; the magnificent is made of despised ingredients.

## Summary

The opening programme of *Human, All Too Human* (1878), Volume I, chapter I. Metaphysical philosophy assigns a miraculous origin — from the "thing-in-itself" — to whatever it deems highest. Historical philosophy, which "can no longer be viewed apart from physical science," finds experimentally that the usual antitheses (reasonable/non-reasonable, altruism/egoism, truth/error) are errors of reason. What is needed is a **chemistry** of moral, religious, and aesthetic conceptions and feelings. Philosophers' traditional defect is lack of the **historical sense**: they take contemporary man as *aeterna veritas*. "There are no eternal facts as there are no absolute truths." This is the 1878 method. It is the precursor of, not the same claim as, the 1886 [[Concepts/Belief in Antitheses of Values (Nietzsche)]] or the 1887 genealogical "grey" history. *Dawn* (1881) continues the method on **morality as such**: enduring things get so soaked with reason that an origin in unreason looks sacrilegious (§1); moral feelings are ape-imitated likes whose “justifications” come later (§§34–35); insight into origins *cheapens* origins (§44). See [[Sources/The Dawn of Day - Nietzsche (Kennedy, Collection 2013)]], [[Concepts/Faith in Morals (Nietzsche)]].

## Key Claims (1878, §§1–34)

**Chemistry (§1).** "How can a thing develop out of its antithesis?" Metaphysic "repudiate[s] the evolution of one thing from another" and assigns a miraculous origin "due to the very nature and being of the 'thing-in-itself.'" Historical philosophy finds "there is no antithesis whatever, except in the usual exaggerations of popular or metaphysical comprehension." Unselfish conduct and the disinterested point of view are "simply sublimations" in which the basic element has almost evaporated. "A chemistry of the moral, religious, aesthetic conceptions and feeling" is all that present science can give. If that chemistry showed that "the most magnificent results were attained with the basest and most despised ingredients," many would not continue. "Must one not be almost inhuman in order to follow the opposite course?"

**No eternal facts (§2).** "Lack of the historical sense is the traditional defect in all philosophers." Everything a philosopher says about man is "testimony concerning man during a very limited period of time." Man has *geworden*; the intellectual faculty itself is an evolution. "There are no eternal facts as there are no absolute truths. Accordingly, historical philosophising is henceforth indispensable, and with it honesty of judgment."

**Little truths over magnificent errors (§§3–4).** Advanced civilization values "little, simple truths, ascertained by scientific method" over "pleasing and magnificent errors." Religious, moral, aesthetic, and logical notions concern "the superficialities of things" — the same conceit as astrology (heavenly bodies regulated for mortals).

**Dream as source of metaphysic (§§5, 12–13).** "In the dream, mankind… thought they were introduced to a second, substantial world: here we have the source of all metaphysic." Soul/body, the embodied soul, belief in god: "The dead still live: for they appear to the living in dreams."

**Thing-in-itself as empty (§§9–10, 16).** A metaphysical world is barely disputable as abstract possibility, but only an inaccessible *elsewhere*; knowledge of it would be of least consequence. Once religion, art, and ethics are understood without metaphysical postulates, interest in "thing in itself" / "phenomenon" ceases. Phenomena are a gradual evolution painted by intellect, need, passion; "the thing-in-itself is a meet subject for Homeric laughter: that it seemed so much, everything, indeed, and is really a void—void, that is to say, of meaning."

**Language, logic, number (§§11, 18–19).** Language was taken as *aeternae veritates*. "Logic itself rests upon assumptions to which nothing in the world of reality corresponds." Number-laws hold only in the world of mankind.

**Age of comparison; art not metaphysic as substitute (§§23–24, 27).** After leaving religion one must still overcome metaphysic. This is the age of comparison. Progress is possible as a *new conscious civilization*, not a revival of the old *Cultur*. Art, not metaphysic, is the safer substitute for religion.

**Illogical and injustice as conditions (§§31–33).** "The illogical is necessary to mankind and that from the illogical springs much that is good." "We are primordially illogical and hence unjust beings *and can recognise this fact*." Belief in life's value rests on defective thinking.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. 1873 [[Concepts/Truth as Worn-Out Metaphor (Nietzsche)]]:** five years earlier, unpublished, the thing-in-itself is already “not in the least worth striving for,” language is already two metaphors, and “there are no absolute truths” is already the coin-without-embossing formula. 1878 answers with *chemistry* and *historical sense*, not with metaphor-genesis. Do not collapse.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Belief in Antitheses of Values (Nietzsche)]] (BGE 1886 §2):** same problem — highest things assigned a pure origin — but 1878 answers with *chemistry* and *historical sense*, not yet with "dangerous Perhapses," "beyond good and evil," or value-creation. File BGE as the later sharpening.
- **Vs. [[Sources/The Genealogy of Morals - Nietzsche (1887)]]:** GM wants "grey" authentic history of the *value* of morality. 1878 still writes a chemistry that can dissolve "ought" into necessity and guiltlessness ([[Concepts/Two-fold Historical Origin of Good and Evil (Nietzsche)]]). The method is continuous; the question (origin vs. value) is not yet GM's.
- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Kant]]:** the thing-in-itself is the miraculous origin assigned to the highest (§1) and then emptied (§16). Kant and Plato are "equally responsible" for fabulous *intelligibeln Freiheit* in chapter II §39 — a different page.
- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]:** named as reaction: a Christian *Weltbetrachtung* resurrected, useful for justice, then to be left for the enlightenment line (Petrarch, Erasmus, Voltaire) (§26). Annihilation-preaching and world-negation appear in §§16, 29.
- **Vs. [[Thinkers/William James]]:** James's fruits test asks what a belief *does*; 1878 chemistry asks what despised *ingredients* a sublime feeling is made of. Do not flatten.

The words **perspectivism**, **will to power**, and **death of God** do not appear in this chapter.
