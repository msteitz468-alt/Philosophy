# Concrete Relations with Others (Sartre)
> "Conflict is the original meaning of being-for-others." Love, desire, sadism, hate, and the We are two circling attitudes — assimilate a freedom, or make the Other an object — neither of which founds *Mitsein*.

## Summary

Part Three, Chapter Three of *Being and Nothingness* treats concrete relations as new modes of the for-itself in a world with Others, always on the ground of facticity and the body (BN 361). Two primitive, mutually lethal attitudes form a **circle**, not a dialectic: assimilate the Other's freedom while leaving it free, or make the Other an object (BN 363–364). "Conflict is the original meaning of being-for-others" (BN 364). Reciprocal enslavement, not unilateral object-relations. "My project of recovering myself is fundamentally a project of absorbing the Other" (BN 364).

**First attitude: Love, language, masochism.** Love is the project of being loved: possess a freedom *as* freedom, exist as the a priori limit of the beloved's transcendence, be "the whole World" and the foundation of values (BN 366–370). "He wants to possess a freedom as freedom" and wants that freedom "to will its own captivity" (BN 367). The ideal is fusion that still preserves otherness (God / in-itself-for-itself). Unrealizable. Love is essentially **seduction** plus **language**. "Language is not a phenomenon added on to being-for-others. It is originally being-for-others … I am language" (BN 372). Triple failure: infinite reference (to love = to want to be loved); the Other can always objectify me; a Third relativizes the couple. **Masochism** inverts the project (be absorbed as object) and fails because I remain the one who offers myself.

**Second attitude: Indifference, desire, sadism, hate.** Looking at the look collapses the Other into object. **Indifference** is chosen "factual solipsism," a bad-faith blindness (BN 380–381). **Desire** is a primary ontological attitude, not a sex-instinct: I make myself flesh to incarnate the Other; the caress is desire's language; the ideal is possession of transcendence *as* body (BN 382–396). "Desire is an invitation to desire." "Possession truly appears as a double reciprocal incarnation" (BN 391, 396). Pleasure and "taking" kill it. **Sadism** forces incarnation (the obscene) without my own incarnation; the victim's Look collapses it. **Hate** is the despair-project of a world without Others; even successful killing leaves being-for-others irremediable. Ethics of respect and tolerance still violate freedom. "Thus original sin is my upsurge in a world where there are others" (BN 410). A footnote: an ethics of salvation would require a "radical conversion" not discussed here (BN 412).

**Mitsein and the We.** **Us-object** is real alienated solidarity under a Third (shame, class as looked-at). **We-subject** is a contingent psychological *Erlebnis* (rhythm, tools, theater); it presupposes the Other and **realizes no Mitsein**. "The being-for-others precedes and founds the being-with-others" (BN 414). "The experience of the We-subject is a pure psychological, subjective event … and does not realize any Mitsein" (BN 425). "It is therefore useless for human-reality to seek to get out of this dilemma: one must either transcend the Other or allow oneself to be transcended by him. The essence of the relations between consciousnesses is not the Mitsein; it is conflict" (BN 429).

## Key Claims / Positions

- Conflict is original; the two attitudes circle without *Aufhebung*.
- Love wants a freedom to will its own captivity — the same impossible synthesis as God.
- Language *is* being-for-others.
- Desire is incarnation, not libido.
- We-subject does not realize *Mitsein*; humanity-as-We is "only a dream."

### How Sartre Differs From Prior Thinkers in the Wiki

- **vs [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: *Mitsein* (crew, we) is not the structure of being-for-others; at best a derived, unstable experience. See [[Concepts/Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Hegel]]**: master/slave recognition is kept as dependence and refused as reconciliation.
- **vs the 1945/46 lecture**: the lecture needs inter-subjectivity as the condition of a human community. This chapter cannot underwrite a primary we-subject or fraternity. Any later group praxis (*Critique*) is a shift of register, or the undescribed "radical conversion."
- **vs Freud**: desire is ontological incarnation, not libido — [[Contradictions/Sartre vs Freud - Lucidity vs the Unconscious]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Conflict as the *essence* of relations between consciousnesses sits against the lecture's warmer "inter-subjectivity" and against the *Critique*'s group. The conversion-footnote is the only explicit crack in BN.
- Us-object is ontologically real but is *alienation* (the Third), not co-subjectivity.
