# Divine Darkness and Unknowing (Dionysius)

> The term of *The Mystical Theology*: the initiate leaves sense and intellect, plunges into the "dazzling obscurity" of Divine Darkness, and by "a rejection of all knowledge" possesses "a knowledge that exceeds his understanding" (MT I).

## Summary

The treatise opens with a prayer to the Trinity "which exceedeth all Being, Deity, and Goodness" and a counsel to Timothy: "in the earnest exercise of mystic contemplation, thou leave the senses and the activities of the intellect and all things that the senses or the intellect can perceive, and all things in this world of nothingness, or in that world of being, and that, thine understanding being laid to rest, thou strain (so far as thou mayest) towards an union with Him whom neither being nor understanding can contain" (MT I). By "unceasing and absolute renunciation of thyself and all things" one is "led upwards to the Ray of that divine Darkness which exceedeth all existence."

The mysteries "lie hidden in the dazzling obscurity of the secret Silence, outshining all brilliance with the intensity of their darkness." This is not ordinary ignorance. Rolt's note 514: the Greek marks a "transcendent or spiritual Unknowing (as distinguished from mere ignorance)."

**Moses' ascent** (MT I) is the governing image. Purification; separation from the unpurified; many-voiced trumpets and diverse-streaming lights; the chosen priests press to the topmost pinnacle. "Nevertheless he meets not with God Himself, yet he beholds—not Him indeed (for He is invisible)—but the place wherein He dwells." The highest things perceived by eye or mind are "but the symbolic language of things subordinate to Him." Then the presence "plunges the true initiate unto the Darkness of Unknowing wherein he renounces all the apprehensions of his understanding and is enwrapped in that which is wholly intangible and invisible… being through the passive stillness of all his reasoning powers united by his highest faculty to Him that is wholly Unknowable, of whom thus by a rejection of all knowledge he possesses a knowledge that exceeds his understanding."

**Method.** "In ceasing thus to see or to know we may learn to know that which is beyond all perception and understanding (for this emptying of our faculties is true sight and knowledge)" (MT II). Negation is like carving a statue: remove impediments to display the hidden image. Affirmations descend universal → particular; negations ascend particular → universal, to "naked knowledge of that Unknowing" and the "super-essential Darkness" (MT II).

**The last denials** (MT IV–V). The Cause of sensibles is none of the things sensed (not body, shape, quantity, place, passion, change). The Cause of intelligibles is none of them: "nor is It one, nor is It unity, nor is It Godhead or Goodness… nor does It belong to the category of non-existence or to that of existence… nor can any affirmation or negation apply to it" (MT V).

The uninitiated are those "who cling to the objects of human thought, and imagine there is no super-essential reality beyond" (MT I). Worse are those who describe the Cause from the lowest order of being.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Plotinus]]**: *henosis* by contemplation; the One beyond intellect. Dionysius keeps the union and adds Moses, Scripture, and an explicit denial even of "one" and "Godhead."
- **[[Concepts/Return of the Soul (Epistrophe)]]**: MT is the Christian *epistrophē* as stripping rather than as Plotinian awakening of an undescended summit.
- **Later mystics** (Rolt I; not detailed in Sparrow-Simpson's essay): "The Mystical Divinity ran across England like deer"; Ruysbroeck "slaked his thirst" here. The later triad Purgation / Illumination / Union is Rolt's reading of Moses (n. 521), not a heading in MT.
- **[[Concepts/Beatific Vision - Last End (Aquinas)]]**: Aquinas's last end is *vision* of the essence; Dionysius's last word is Darkness that exceeds vision. The vault now has both poles of Latin mystical theology's debt to this text.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Vision vs Darkness as last end: Aquinas's beatific *visio* vs MT's Unknowing. Not a formal contradiction in Dionysius (the Darkness "outshines all brilliance"), but a live tension in the reception.
- > [!warning] Rolt n. 523: the via negativa is "in the truest sense, positive" and the personal God "enshrines, but is not, the Ultimate Reality" — Bradley again, not MT.
