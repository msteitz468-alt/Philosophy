# Binding as Inference (Hohwy)
> The brain does not glue separately detected colours and shapes together. It *assumes* bound objects and treats the separate sensory bits as feedback to that unified hypothesis.

## Summary

Chapter 5 of *The Predictive Mind* takes the classical **binding problem** — attributes (colour, shape, motion) processed in different regions at different speeds, yet perceived as belonging to the right objects — and recasts it as [[Concepts/Prediction Error Minimization (Hohwy)|PEM]]. Hohwy says the mechanism "dissolves this problem" by the chapter 2 "profound reversal": the brain "assumes bound attributes on the basis of sparse input and prior learning, and queries the sensory input" (p. 101). "Instead of binding disparate sensory attributes, the brain assumes bound attributes… the disparate parts of sensory input are feedback to the brain's unified hypothesis" (p. 101).

Binding is **inference to a common cause**, "located at the shallow end of the causal hierarchy" (p. 101). An independence hypothesis (redness and bouncing caused separately) competes with a binding hypothesis (one red bouncing ball). If the world matches the binding prediction, PE is minimized "and they will be perceived as bound" (p. 103). Deep common causes (barometer and storm; subprime crisis) are not *perceptual* binding; perceptual binding is shallow, first-person, variant (pp. 110–11).

The rubber-hand illusion and binocular rivalry are the tests. Synchrony favours a common-cause hypothesis even when it "radically misconstrues reality" (pp. 107–8). Rivalry analogously cannot ignore spatial overlap. Attention enters as precision: "attention is precision optimization of prediction errors" and, on Hohwy's reading of Treisman, is needed for all binding (pp. 112–13). Everyday binding "glosses over" expected imprecision so priors "sharpen" attributes; extra unexpected uncertainty yields illusory conjunctions (p. 113). "Binding is essentially a statistically based inner fantasy, shaped by the brain's generative model" (p. 115).

Hohwy's verdict is *recast / dissolved in principle*, not empirically closed. He calls it a "speculative" PEM test that "turns the binding problem inside out" and still needs research (pp. 101, 115).

## Key Claims / Positions

- Binding is not extra machinery on top of feature detection.
- Common-cause vs independence is the hypothesis competition.
- Shallow (object-surface) vs deep (scientific) common cause marks perception off from cognition.
- Precision/attention is how binding survives noise; unexpected imprecision yields illusory conjunctions.
- "Perceptual inference is biased towards efficient suppression of prediction error over complying with conceptual prior beliefs" (p. 107) — the rubber hand is believed false and still felt.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **Treisman / von der Malsburg**: the problem Hohwy inherits. Treisman is vindicated only if attention = expected-precision optimization for *all* binding.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: *The Experience Machine* is light on binding as a classic cognitive-science puzzle. Hohwy does that philosophy-of-perception work here.
- **[[Concepts/Controlled Hallucination (Clark)]]**: "inner fantasy" is Hohwy's phrase for the same constructive percept, applied to the binding case.
