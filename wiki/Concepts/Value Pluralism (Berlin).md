# Value Pluralism (Berlin)

> Ultimate human goods are many, genuine, and clash. There is no sovereign principle that ranks them without remainder. The “perfect whole” is not merely unattainable but **conceptually incoherent**. Primary statement: *The Crooked Timber of Humanity*. The 1958 lecture (as quoted in 2013 reception) is the political slogan of the same view.

## Summary

[[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]]’s own essays now hold this page. “The Pursuit of the Ideal” (1988) and “The Decline of Utopian Ideas in the West” (1978) give the doctrine; “Alleged Relativism…” (1980) keeps it from collapsing into taste. See [[Concepts/Pluralism versus Relativism (Berlin)]].

The West’s *philosophia perennis* assumed “there could be no conflict between true ends.” Berlin’s discoverers:

- **Machiavelli** — pagan *virtù* and Christian humility are “incompatible”; he “does not recognise an overarching criterion.”
- **[[Thinkers/Giambattista Vico]]** — successive cultures with incommensurable visions; *fantasia* lets us enter them.
- **[[Thinkers/Johann Gottfried Herder]]** — each society has its own “centre of gravity.”

Values clash across cultures, within a culture, and “within the breast of a single individual” (truth vs kindness; justice vs mercy; liberty vs equality). “Some among the Great Goods cannot live together. That is a conceptual truth. We are doomed to choose, and every choice may entail an irreparable loss.”

Politics, therefore, is not technique. Where ends are agreed, questions of means are technical — that slogan is how the 2013 reception quoted “Two Concepts.” Here the same point is historical: monism generates [[Concepts/Utopianism and the Pursuit of the Ideal (Berlin)|utopia]] and then omelette politics.

**Kant’s sentence** is the motto: “Out of the crooked timber of humanity no straight thing was ever made.” Used as argument at pp. 19 and 50, not only as the book’s title.

## Berlin’s Claims (primary)

- **Incommensurability of objective ends.** Many ends can be sought by men still “fully rational, fully men.” Communication is possible because “what makes men human is common.” Objective values are ends pursued for their own sakes, finite, “within the human horizon.”
- **Not relativism.** Relativism is “I prefer coffee, you prefer champagne.” Momigliano “was mistaken.” See [[Concepts/Pluralism versus Relativism (Berlin)]].
- **Anti-monism.** The three-legged stool (one true answer; a path; compatibility of all truths) is false of values. Hegel and Marx historicise conflict but keep a “happy ending.”
- **Equilibrium, not a final ranking.** Collisions can be “softened.” “The first public obligation is to avoid extremes of suffering.” A “precarious” / “uneasy equilibrium” is “the first requirement for a decent society.” There is at least “a minimum without which societies could scarcely survive.”
- **Negative liberty is not overriding.** That claim is from the 1969 Introduction as the 2013 volume quotes it. This book does not re-argue the two concepts. Do not flatten the 1988 essay into the 1958 lecture.

## How the 2013 Reception Used This (kept)

Baum and Nichols said value pluralism is what distinguishes Berlin’s liberalism, and that it has drawn the most scholarly attention (Gray 1997 named). George Crowder: pluralism, not the two concepts, is “the master-theme of ‘Two Concepts.’” That reception is still on [[Sources/Isaiah Berlin and the Politics of Freedom - Baum and Nichols (2013)]]. This book is the history-of-ideas primary Crowder’s claim was pointing at.

Crowder vs Tully, Gould’s “bad faith,” Nichols’s ontological monism — those are debates *about* the lecture. They are not resolved here.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur O. Lovejoy]]:** Lovejoy tracks **unit-ideas**. Berlin tracks **value-collision** and the political cost of monism. Both belong to the History of Ideas leg; they are not the same method. This book is the method Outstanding Sources named.
- **[[Thinkers/William James]] / [[Concepts/The One and the Many (James)]]:** *Pragmatism* already has “the one and the many” as the central problem and sides with pluralism against the Absolute. Metaphysical cousin; Berlin’s clash is of *moral/political ends*.
- **[[Thinkers/Paul Feyerabend]]:** [[Concepts/Proliferation and Tenacity (Feyerabend)]] is Millian theoretical pluralism in science. Kinship of anti-monism, different domain.
- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]:** experiments of living are a utilitarian argument for *diversity*. Berlin is harsher: even with full information, goods still clash.
- **[[Thinkers/Robert Nozick]]:** side constraints pick *one* value (rights) as inviolable. That is a ranking Berlin’s pluralism would call a choice. [[Concepts/Framework for Utopia (Nozick)]] is many lives under one side-constraint frame — not Berlin’s “Utopia.”
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]:** the CI is a monism of moral law. In *this* book Kant is also the crooked-timber witness against perfection. Do not flatten the two Kants.
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]:** ancestor of the one-answer politics the stool restates.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Does pluralism *entail* liberalism? Crowder (career answer, outside both sources) says yes; John Gray (named in 2013, not ingested) says pluralism undercuts any unique liberal ranking. This book states equilibrium; it does not close that debate.
- > [!warning] Romantic “values are created” (Decline) vs “world of objective values” (Pursuit). Historical diagnosis vs Berlin’s own view. See [[Concepts/Utopianism and the Pursuit of the Ideal (Berlin)]].
- > [!warning] The 1958 lecture remains outstanding. Do not cite TC/IN page numbers from this book; they are from Hardy via Baum/Nichols.
