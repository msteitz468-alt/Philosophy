# Biopolitics and Governmentality (Foucault)
> Since the classical age the West replaced the sovereign's right to take life or let live with a power to foster life or disallow it to the point of death — anatomo-politics of the body plus bio-politics of the population.

## Summary

**Bio-power** is named and defined in Part Five of *La Volonté de savoir* (1976; Hurley). The Macey biography and the Collège lectures already had the outline; this is the 1976 primary. **Governmentality** is *not* named in this book — it remains the 1978–79 lectures.

**From the sword to the administration of life.** "For a long time, one of the characteristic privileges of sovereign power was the right to decide life and death" (p. 135) — a diminished form of Roman *patria potestas*, conditioned on defense of the sovereign. Foucault asks whether, with [[Thinkers/Thomas Hobbes]], this is "the transfer to the prince of the natural right possessed by every individual to defend his life even if this meant the death of others," or a right proper to the new juridical being of the sovereign (Pufendorf). In either form it is dissymmetrical: the sovereign "evidenced his power over life only through the death he was capable of requiring." The right formulated as "power of life and death" was "the right to *take* life or *let* live" (p. 136). Its symbol was the sword. Power here is *deduction* (*prélèvement*): seizure of things, time, bodies, ultimately life.

Since the classical age those mechanisms transform. Deduction becomes one element among others in a power "bent on generating forces, making them grow, and ordering them, rather than one dedicated to impeding them, making them submit, or destroying them" (p. 136). The old right is replaced by "a power to *foster* life or *disallow* it to the point of death" (p. 138). Death becomes power's limit, the most private aspect of existence; suicide, once a usurpation of the sovereign's (or the Lord's) right, enters sociological analysis as a right at the interstices of a life-administering power.

**The underside is more death, not less.** "Wars are no longer waged in the name of a sovereign who must be defended; they are waged on behalf of the existence of everyone… massacres have become vital" (p. 137). "If genocide is indeed the dream of modern powers, this is not because of a recent return of the ancient right to kill; it is because power is situated and exercised at the level of life, the species, the race, and the large-scale phenomena of population" (p. 137). The death penalty recedes — "not the awakening of humanitarian feelings" — because execution contradicts a power whose role is to "ensure, sustain, and multiply life"; killing is then justified by the criminal as "biological danger" (p. 138).

**Two poles, one technology** (pp. 139–40). From the seventeenth century, power over life evolves in two linked forms:

1. **Anatomo-politics of the human body** — the first pole, centered on the body as a machine: disciplining, optimization of capabilities, extortion of forces, usefulness *and* docility, integration into efficient controls. The procedures of **[[Concepts/Disciplinary Society and the Panopticon (Foucault)|the disciplines]]**.
2. **Bio-politics of the population** — formed somewhat later, focused on the species body: propagation, births and mortality, health, life expectancy, longevity. Supervision through interventions and **regulatory controls**.

"The disciplines of the body and the regulations of the population constituted the two poles around which the organization of power over life was deployed." Their setting-up in the classical age "characterized a power whose highest function was perhaps no longer to kill, but to invest life through and through." In the eighteenth century the two still appear separate (army and schools vs demography: Quesnay, Moheau, Süssmilch). They join in nineteenth-century *agencements concrets*; "the [[Concepts/Deployment of Sexuality (Foucault)|deployment of sexuality]] would be one of them, and one of the most important" (p. 140). Sex is "a means of access both to the life of the body and the life of the species" (p. 146). Passage "from a *symbolics of blood* to an *analytics of sexuality*" (p. 148). Racism (Nazism) reactivates blood inside sexual administration.

**Capitalism and the threshold of modernity.** "This bio-power was without question an indispensable element in the development of capitalism; the latter would not have been possible without the controlled insertion of bodies into the machinery of production and the adjustment of the phenomena of population to economic processes" (pp. 140–41). What occurred was not (or not only) Weberian ascetic morality but "the entry of life into history" (p. 141) — phenomena peculiar to the life of the species entering the order of knowledge and power. "If one can apply the term *bio-history* to the pressures through which the movements of life and the processes of history interfere with one another, one would have to speak of *bio-power* to designate what brought life and its mechanisms into the realm of explicit calculations and made knowledge-power an agent of transformation of human life" (p. 143). "A society's 'threshold of modernity' has been reached when the life of the species is wagered on its own political strategies." Aristotle inverted: "For millennia, man remained what he was for Aristotle: a living animal with the additional capacity for a political existence; modern man is an animal whose politics places his existence as a living being in question" (p. 143). Law recedes into the *norm*; a "normalizing society" is the historical outcome of a technology centered on life.

**Governmentality** (*gouvernementalité*), developed in the 1978–79 lectures ("Security, Territory, Population"; "The Birth of Biopolitics"), is the broader rationality of governing — institutions, procedures, analyses, and calculations targeting population. Foucault traces the modern state through reason of state, police science, and liberalism. It is *not* a 1976 term. Do not read it back into *La Volonté de savoir*.

*Discipline and Punish* (1975) does **not** yet make the two-pole distinction. "Population" there is the multiplicity discipline orders. Individualizing discipline vs species-body regulation is a 1976 development.

## Key Claims

- Bio-power individualizes through discipline while simultaneously totalizing through regulation of populations. Sexuality is the hinge.
- Racism is how a power that administers life can still kill: a segment of the population is biologized as a threat to the race's health.
- Capitalism required not only the great instruments of the state but anatomo- and bio-politics as techniques of power present at every level of the social body.
- Resistance itself, under bio-power, demands *life* (needs, body, health, potential) rather than opposing sovereignty's rights with other rights.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Hobbes]]**: named in V. The right of life and death *may* be the transferred natural right of self-defense — left open against Pufendorf. Either way Foucault's point is the *successor* right (foster / disallow), which Hobbes does not have.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: Marx is not named in Part Five; capitalism is. Bio-power as condition of inserting bodies into production and joining "the accumulation of men to that of capital" (p. 141). This complements, rather than repeats, *Discipline and Punish*'s claim that discipline links aptitude to domination *inside* the body.
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: the *zōon politikon* formula is inverted at the threshold of modernity — politics now places biological existence in question.
- **[[Thinkers/Zephyr Teachout]]**: platform surveillance intensifies the *disciplinary* (anatomo-political) diagram more tightly than this population-pole. See [[Concepts/Surveillance Capitalism and Platform Power]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- How the two poles articulate is asserted (cluster of relations; sexuality as one *agencement*) more than mechanized. Subsequent theorists (Agamben, Mbembe) have tried to fill the gap; they are not yet in the vault.
- Governmentality (1978–79) re-describes the population pole as a rationality of governing through freedom. Whether that is a deepening or a shift remains a lecture-course question.

> [!warning] Do not read bio-power back into *Discipline and Punish*. That book is the anatomo-political pole only. See [[Sources/Discipline and Punish - Michel Foucault (1975)]].

> [!warning] Bio-power as condition of capitalism is not the repressive-hypothesis story (sex silenced to save labor-capacity). Volume 1 *refuses* that energetics (p. 114) and *then* gives this different link. See [[Thinkers/Karl Marx]], [[Concepts/Repressive Hypothesis (Foucault)]].
