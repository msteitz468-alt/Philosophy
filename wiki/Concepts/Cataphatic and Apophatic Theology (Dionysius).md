# Cataphatic and Apophatic Theology (Dionysius)

> Two complementary methods for speaking of the Super-Essential Godhead: affirmation from its effects (the [[Concepts/Divine Names (Dionysius)|Divine Names]]), and negation that strips those names until even negation is left behind ([[Concepts/Divine Darkness and Unknowing (Dionysius)|Mystical Theology]]).

## Summary

Dionysius does not treat affirmation and negation as rival doctrines. "While it possesses all the positive attributes of the universe (being the universal Cause), yet in a stricter sense It does not possess them, since It transcends them all, wherefore there is no contradiction between affirming and denying that It has them inasmuch as It precedes and surpasses all deprivation" (MT I).

**Cataphatic (affirmative).** *The Divine Names* celebrates "the Emanation of the Absolute Divine Essence into the universe of things," not the Super-Essential Nature (DN V.1). Names are a "Symbolical Revelation" of "beneficent Emanations" (DN I.4). As Cause, the Godhead "must both be nameless and also possess the names of all things" (DN I.7). Affirmations start from what is most akin (life and goodness truer than air or stone) and descend from universal to particular (MT II–III).

**Apophatic (negative).** "Godlike Minds… can find no more fitting method to celebrate its praises than to deny It every manner of Attribute" (DN I.5). *The Mystical Theology* ranks the corpus and then mounts past it: *Outlines of Divinity* (proper affirmations: single/trinal, Trinity, Jesus' stooping) → *Divine Names* (Good, Existent, Life, Wisdom, Power…) → *Symbolic Divinity* (sense-metaphors: anger, slumber, robes) → the present treatise, where "plunging into the Darkness which is above the intellect we shall find ourselves reduced not merely to brevity of speech but even to absolute dumbness" (MT III). Negations ascend from particular to universal, "like as men who, carving a statue out of marble, remove all the impediments" (MT II). Deny first what differs most (drunkenness, fury) before categories of thought (MT III). The last word of DN already prefers "the Negative method… exceeding all Name, Reason, and Knowledge" (DN XIII.3).

The two methods are one itinerary: procession into names, return by stripping. Unknowing at the top is not mere ignorance: "in ceasing thus to see or to know we may learn to know that which is beyond all perception and understanding (for this emptying of our faculties is true sight and knowledge)" (MT II).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Plotinus]] / [[Thinkers/Proclus]]**: negative theology of the One (*Parmenides* hypotheses; Proclus even negates the negations). Dionysius keeps the method and attaches it to a ranked Christian corpus (Outlines / Names / Symbolic / Mystical).
- **Erigena** (Sparrow-Simpson): negative theology "denies that certain things can be predicated of Deity"; affirmative "asserts propositions which can be predicated." Finite attributes are "figuratively transferred." Strictly God is *plus quam bonitas*, *super bonus*. The religious Trinity is a pastoral symbol for contemplation. Sparrow-Simpson: the proviso that personal language is not what God *is* "paralyses" prayer.
- **John of Damascus**: takes the pair "with discrimination"; theologian controls philosopher.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Aquinas]]**: comments *Divine Names*; keeps both methods under Catholic dogma (Trinity and Incarnation "another realm" from Neoplatonism, Sparrow-Simpson). Analogical predication is the scholastic stabilization of the cataphatic side.
- **Wallis**: flags the standing Neoplatonic tension — ultimate reality imperfectly expressible, yet the Greeks refused to abandon rigorous conceptualization. Dionysius is the Christian form of that tension.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Erigena's Absolute vs Dionysius's own Scripture-only, whole-Godhead naming. The primary text does not say Trinity is a theologians' device; DN II treats Father/Son/Spirit as non-interchangeable differentiated titles.
- > [!warning] Modern critics (Vacherot: "To know God we must cease to think of Him") read the apophatic as anti-intellectual. MT II calls emptying "true sight and knowledge."
