# Forms of Intuition — Space and Time (Kant)

> Space and time are not properties of things in themselves, not Newtonian containers, and not Leibnizian relations abstracted from objects. They are the pure forms of human sensibility: space of outer sense, time of inner sense.

## Summary

Cognition relates immediately to objects only by **intuition**. Objects are *given* by **sensibility** (receptivity to affection) and *thought* by the **understanding** (from which concepts arise). "The capacity (receptivity) to acquire representations through the way in which we are affected by objects is called sensibility" (A 19 / B 33). The undetermined object of an empirical intuition is **appearance** (A 20 / B 34). Its *matter* corresponds to sensation (a posteriori); its *form* — that which orders the manifold — "must all lie ready… in the mind a priori." Isolate sensibility, strip sensation: two pure forms remain, **space** and **time**.

**Space** is the form of outer sense; **time** of inner sense. Time cannot be intuited outwardly, space not as something in us. Space conditions all *outer* appearances; time, because every representation belongs to the mind's inner state, is the immediate condition of inner intuition and the *mediate* condition of outer appearances — hence of **all** appearances (A 33–34 / B 49–51).

Neither is an empirical concept, nor a discursive/general concept of relations. Each is a unique, infinite given intuition (parts only by limitation of the one space / the one time). One cannot represent the absence of space, or remove time, though one can think no objects in them (A 23–25 / B 38–40; A 30–32 / B 46–48).

**Not things in themselves.** "Space represents no property at all of any things in themselves nor any relation of them to each other. It is merely the form of all appearances of outer sense" (A 26 / B 42). The same for time. Against Newton: they are not self-subsisting infinite "non-entities." Against Leibniz: they are not relations abstracted from antecedently existing objects — that would make a priori mathematics a creature of imagination (A 39–41 / B 56–58).

**Official result.** Empirical *reality* (objective validity for all objects of the senses) **and** transcendental *ideality* (nothing if one abstracts from our sensibility). "We therefore assert the empirical reality of space (with respect to all possible outer experience), though to be sure at the same time its transcendental ideality" (A 28 / B 44). We speak of space and time only from the human standpoint.

**Why this answers "how is synthetic a priori possible?"** Geometry is possible only if space is pure a priori intuition, form of outer sense (B 40–41). Alteration and motion (contradictory predicates in one object) are possible only if time is a priori inner intuition (B 48–49). "Our explanation alone makes the possibility of geometry as a synthetic a priori cognition comprehensible" (B 41). Such judgments never extend beyond objects of the senses (B 73).

Things in themselves remain thinkable but "entirely unknown"; even perfect distinctness of appearance never reaches them (A 42–44 / B 59–62). Appearance is not *illusion*: "I do not say that bodies merely seem to exist outside me" (B 69).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Newton.** Absolute space/time as the *sensorium dei* — metaphysically incoherent, Kant thinks, and unnecessary once space and time are forms of sensibility.
- **Leibniz.** Space and time as well-founded phenomena / systems of relations. Kant keeps the anti-absolutist impulse but denies that the relations are abstracted from things in themselves; they are the conditions under which objects can be given at all.
- **Berkeley.** Kant blocks the collapse of transcendental ideality into subjective idealism: appearance ≠ *Schein*; ascribing *absolute* reality to space and time is what makes Berkeley's reduction of bodies to illusion hard to blame (B 69–71). See [[Contradictions/Subjective Idealism vs. Physical Realism]].
- **Husserl.** Shares the turn to conditions of givenness; rejects the leftover *Ding an sich*. See [[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]].
- **Heidegger.** Time as a priori form of inner sense is the hook for the 1929 Kant-book: imagination as root of sensibility and understanding. See [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Transcendental ideality of space and time is the load-bearing premise of [[Concepts/Transcendental Idealism (Kant)]]. If space and time *do* belong to things in themselves, the rest of the *Critique* (antinomies, restriction of the categories, room for freedom) fails.
- > [!warning] Hegel will treat the "human standpoint" as a limit to be sublated, not a last word. See [[Contradictions/Hegelian Monism vs. Kantian Dualism]].
