# Healthy-Mindedness and the Sick Soul (James)

> Two religious families, not two philosophies. Francis W. Newman: God has "the once-born and the twice-born." **Healthy-mindedness** "looks on all things and sees that they are good." The **sick soul** hits a misery-threshold that natural goods cannot cross and cries "Help! help!" The completest religions are **religions of deliverance**.

## Overview

Lectures IV–VII of *Varieties* divide personal religion by **how a temperament handles evil**, not by the 1907 tender/tough table. **Once-born** characters (Newman's label) have no crisis; God is kindness and beauty (Edward Everett Hale: "I always knew God loved me… I had no idea whatever what the problem of life was"). **Twice-born** characters must lose a natural life before sharing a spiritual one. Congenital cheer can harden into "quasi-pathological" anaesthesia to evil; Walt Whitman is the type, his optimism "too voluntary and defiant."

**Healthy-mindedness** may be **involuntary** (immediate gladness) or **systematic** (an abstract policy that "deliberately excludes evil from its field of vision"). Liberal Christianity's drop of hell-fire, and popular evolutionism as a "doctrine of general meliorism and progress," are church-and-science forms of the type. American **Mind-cure / New Thought** (and Mrs. Eddy's Christian Science: evil is a *lie*) is the radical form: fear, not frowardness, is the vice; oneness with Infinite Life is already there; methods are suggestion, "entering the silence," recollection, surrender. "You *are* well… if you did but know it." Mind-cure verifies itself experimentally ("Live as if I were true"). James: the world can be handled by many conceptual systems; science and religion can both be genuine, non-exhaustive keys.

The **sick soul** has two morbid levels: evil as removable mal-adjustment vs. radical vice needing a **supernatural** remedy (Latin "sins" vs. Germanic "Sin"). **Pain-, fear-, and misery-thresholds** vary. Pure naturalism ends in sadness (Goethe, Luther, Ecclesiastes, Greek pensive pessimism). Stoic and Epicurean resignation close the once-born period; they leave the world unreconciled. Pathological melancholy includes Ribot's **anhedonia**, Tolstoy's meaning-collapse ("One can live only so long as one is intoxicated, drunk with life"), Bunyan's and Alline's sin-loathing, and a French panic-fear case ("*That shape am I*, I felt, potentially"). "Here is the real core of the religious problem: Help! help!" "Our civilization is founded on the shambles."

James's verdict: morbid-mindedness **overlaps the wider survey**; systematic healthy-mindedness is "formally less complete." Completest religions (Buddhism, Christianity) are religions of deliverance. Recovery and second birth are reserved for Lectures VIII–X.

## Key Arguments

- Happiness is the ordinary pole of religious interest; a creed that makes a man happy is taken, by "religious logic," as thereby true. That is not yet a theory of truth.
- Healthy-mindedness **votes pluralist** on evil: evil is "irrational," "dirt," not to be "pinned in" as Hegelian/monistic theism must pin it. Spinoza is cited as philosophic healthy-mindedness that condemns repentance as a "deleterious and evil" passion.
- The two types are naturally antagonistic. James does not tell the healthy-minded to become sick; Lecture XX will say a Moody forced to be a Whitman (or vice versa) would impoverish "the total human consciousness of the divine."
- Greeks kept "good… good, and bad just bad"; they are not Whitman's once-born cousins. Luther "by no means belonged to the healthy-minded type in the radical sense."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Not the 1907 temperament table.** [[Concepts/Tender-Minded and Tough-Minded (James)]] sorts *philosophies* (rationalist/empiricist, monist/pluralist). Healthy-minded / sick soul sorts *religious responses to evil*. A tough-minded empiricist can be once-born; a tender-minded rationalist can be a sick soul. Do not merge the columns.
- **Not 1907 [[Concepts/Meliorism (James)]].** In IV–V "meliorism" names the healthy-minded *evolution/progress* religion, not the later midpoint between optimism and pessimism. The 1907 doctrine is a philosophical successor, not a synonym.
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]**: healthy-minded condemnation of repentance — not identified with James's later types, and not Spinoza's *amor Dei intellectualis*.
- **[[Thinkers/Augustine]]**: only *Dilige et quod vis fac* appears here as an antinomian passport. The *Confessions* conversion is Lecture VIII's document, not this typology's source.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]**: not the author of this distinction. The sick soul's "Help! help!" is not ressentiment, and healthy-mindedness is not master morality.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] James ranks morbid-mindedness as formally more complete, then (Lecture XX) refuses to force one type on all men. Completeness and vocation pull apart.
- The French panic-fear letter is not identified as James himself in this range. Do not import the biographical identification from later commentary.
