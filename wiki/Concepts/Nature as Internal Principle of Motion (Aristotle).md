# Nature as Internal Principle of Motion (Aristotle)
> Nature (*physis*) is a source of being moved and of being at rest in that to which it belongs primarily, in virtue of itself — not an artefact's external maker, and not a concomitant.

## Summary

*Physics* II.1 is the vault's first primary definition of **nature**. Of things that exist, some exist by nature (animals and their parts, plants, the simple bodies) and some from other causes. Naturals "present a feature in which they differ from things which are not constituted by nature. Each of them has within itself a principle of motion and of stationariness (in respect of place, or of growth and decrease, or by way of alteration)." A bed or a coat, *qua* product of art, has no innate impulse to change; *qua* wood or stone it does.

So: "nature is a source or cause of being moved and of being at rest in that to which it belongs primarily, in virtue of itself and not in virtue of a concomitant attribute." The doctor's self-cure is concomitant (he happens to be both doctor and patient). Artefacts have the source of their production **external**.

That nature exists is, for Aristotle, too obvious to prove — like a blind man reasoning about colours. Book I had already said physicists must take motion as given by induction; Eleatic monism is not a contribution to physics, because a motionless One leaves no principle *of* anything.

Two accounts of nature then compete: (i) the immediate **material** substratum (Antiphon's buried bed sprouts wood, not bed); (ii) the **shape or form** specified in the definition. Form is nature **rather than** matter, "for a thing is more properly said to be what it is when it has attained to fulfilment than when it exists potentially." Man comes from man; beds do not come from beds. Growth is toward the form. Privation is "in a way form." The physicist studies both, as one studies snubness (neither matter-free nor matter-only). The mathematician separates surfaces in thought; Platonists wrongly separate physical forms. "That for the sake of which" *is* nature as end. The unmoved, separable essence is reserved for first philosophy — the handoff to the [[Sources/Aristotle - Metaphysics|*Metaphysics*]].

Book II.8 then argues that nature **operates for a purpose**. The Empedoclean/necessitarian alternative (rain falls of necessity, not to grow corn; well-fitted teeth survived, "man-faced ox-progeny" perished) fails because natural outcomes come about **always or for the most part**. "It is absurd to suppose that purpose is not present because we do not observe the agent deliberating. Art does not deliberate." If ship-building were in the wood, it would work as nature works. Nature is like a doctor doctoring himself. See [[Concepts/The Four Causes (Aristotle)]] and [[Concepts/Chance and Spontaneity (Aristotle)]].

## Key Claims / Positions

- Nature = internal *archē* of motion and rest, *kath' hauto*.
- Form is nature more than matter; nature is both.
- Nature acts for an end without deliberating.
- Physics studies formed matter; first philosophy studies the unmoved.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: this is the opening move of the whole physical corpus.
- **[[Thinkers/Parmenides]]**: denied; a motionless One is not a physical thesis.
- **[[Thinkers/Empedocles]]** / **[[Thinkers/Democritus]]**: nature reduced to necessity or vortex; Aristotle's "always or normally" test is the reply — see [[Contradictions/Atomist Materialism vs Platonic-Aristotelian Teleology]].
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]** and the Scientific Revolution: expel internal principles and final causes; nature becomes extension plus laws. The *Physics* definition is what they are rejecting.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: *physis* as self-showing emergence is a retrieval of this word against the later "nature" of *Vorhandenheit*. Dreyfus's skilled coping is closer to *phronēsis* than to *physis*, but both oppose treating the natural as a heap of present-at-hand facts.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Aquinas]]**: grace perfects nature — this *physis*, not a Cartesian machine.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Is "always or for the most part" enough to license teleology? Darwinian selection is Empedocles upgraded; Aristotle thinks that pattern still needs an end *in the nature*. See [[Contradictions/Atomist Materialism vs Platonic-Aristotelian Teleology]].
- The unmoved mover of Book VIII is *not* an internal principle of the falling stone. Natural things "contain a source of *suffering* motion," actualized by a generator or the removal of a hindrance.
