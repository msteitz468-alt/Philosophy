# Deployment of Sexuality (Foucault)
> Sexuality is not a stubborn drive that power checks: it is a historical construct, a "great surface network," produced by a *dispositif* superimposed on the older deployment of alliance.

## Summary

Hurley renders *dispositif de sexualité* as **deployment of sexuality**. The word appears in Part Two as a network "quite different from the law" that proliferates specific pleasures and disparate sexualities (p. 49). Part Four is its home.

**Not a drive.** "Sexuality must not be described as a stubborn drive, by nature alien and of necessity disobedient to a power which exhausts itself trying to subdue it… It appears rather as an especially dense transfer point for relations of power" (p. 103) — between men and women, young and old, parents and offspring, teachers and students, priests and laity, an administration and a population. It is "useful for the greatest number of maneuvers," not the most intractable element in power relations.

**Four strategic unities** take shape from the eighteenth century (pp. 103–105):

1. **A hysterization of women's bodies** — the feminine body analyzed as thoroughly saturated with sexuality, integrated into medicine by an intrinsic pathology, placed in organic communication with the social body, the family, and the life of children.
2. **A pedagogization of children's sex** — a sex both "natural" and "contrary to nature," precarious, calling for parents, educators, doctors, psychologists; a campaign of which the war on onanism is only the most visible form.
3. **A socialization of procreative behavior** — economic (socialization of the couple's fertility), political (responsibilization of the couple toward the social body), medical (birth control as pathologized).
4. **A psychiatrization of perverse pleasure** — the sexual instinct isolated as a biological and psychical datum, analyzed for anomalies, assigned a normalizing technology.

Their privileged objects: "the hysterical woman, the masturbating child, the Malthusian couple, and the perverse adult" (p. 105). What is at issue is "the very production of sexuality" (p. 105).

**Alliance vs sexuality** (pp. 106–107). Every society has a **deployment of alliance** — marriage, kinship, names, possessions; rules of licit and illicit; homeostasis via reproduction of relations. From the eighteenth century a **deployment of sexuality** is superimposed on it: mobile techniques rather than rules; extension of control rather than reproduction of relations; sensations and pleasures rather than statutes of partners; the producing/consuming body rather than transmission of wealth; proliferating control of bodies and populations rather than social homeostasis. Sexuality is recent, expanding since the seventeenth century, not governed by reproduction. It is built *on* alliance (penance → flesh), not a replacement of it.

**The family is not a prohibitor.** It is "the interchange of sexuality and alliance": its role is to *anchor* sexuality, not to exclude or restrain it (p. 108). Incest is solicited and refused because the family is both alliance-rule and "hotbed of constant sexual incitement" (p. 109). It is "the crystal in the deployment of sexuality" (p. 111) — "a major factor of sexualization," not "a powerful agency of prohibition" (p. 114).

**Periodization against the repressive cycle** (pp. 115–131). A repression-centered history needs two ruptures (seventeenth-century great prohibitions; twentieth-century loosening). Foucault's two calendars do not match that cycle. (1) *Techniques* go back to Lateran confession and sixteenth-century direction of conscience; a late-eighteenth-century secular/state technology of sex (pedagogy, medicine, demography); then medicine of perversions plus eugenics joined by *dégénérescence*. Two productive moments: mid-sixteenth century and the beginning of the nineteenth. (2) *Diffusion*: the most rigorous techniques hit privileged classes first, not the laboring poor. Working classes long escaped the deployment of sexuality (they were under alliance); sexualization entered in three stages — late-eighteenth-century birth control, ~1830s "moralization of the poorer classes," late-nineteenth-century juridical-medical control of perversions. Sexuality is "originally, historically bourgeois"; the bourgeoisie's "blood" was its sex (pp. 123–127).

**Psychoanalysis's double position.** It examines sexuality outside family control yet "rediscovered the law of alliance" and incest as key to intelligibility (pp. 112–113). It is both a theory of "the essential interrelatedness of the law and desire" and a technique for relieving pathogenic taboo (p. 129). It opposes *dégénérescence* and differentiates those who can buy relief of repression from those subjected to an administrative hunt for incest (p. 130). Reich's antirepressive struggle is a "tactical shift and reversal in the great deployment of sexuality" (p. 131).

**Part Five: the hinge of bio-power.** The two poles of power over life — anatomo-politics of the body and bio-politics of the population — join in nineteenth-century *agencements concrets*; "the deployment of sexuality would be one of them, and one of the most important" (p. 140). Sex is access "both to the life of the body and the life of the species" (p. 146). Passage "from a *symbolics of blood* to an *analytics of sexuality*" (p. 148). "Sex" itself is a **fictitious unity** / **imaginary point** produced inside the deployment (hysteria, onanism, fetishism, *coitus interruptus*) — "the most speculative, most ideal, and most internal element" (p. 155). The Faustian pact: "to exchange life in its entirety for sex itself, for the truth and the sovereignty of sex" (p. 156). "We must not think that by saying yes to sex, one says no to power" (p. 157). "The rallying point for the counterattack against the deployment of sexuality ought not to be sex-desire, but bodies and pleasures" (p. 157). Closing irony: the deployment has us believe that our "liberation" is in the balance (p. 159).

## Key Claims

- Sexuality is produced, not repressed-then-liberated. The family sexualizes; it does not silence.
- Alliance (kinship, licit/illicit, reproduction of relations) and sexuality (sensations, expanding control of bodies and populations) are two deployments, the second superimposed on the first.
- Class: sexuality begins as bourgeois self-affirmation of body, health, and descent, then generalizes with different tools. It is not first a device for castrating the proletariat into labor.
- "Sex" is not the real that sexuality expresses; sexuality is the real historical formation that produced the notion of sex.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: IV.4 refuses the labor-capacity / *Spätkapitalismus* "hyper-repressive desublimation" story (p. 114). Part Five then *re-joins* capitalism without that energetics: bio-power is "an indispensable element in the development of capitalism" (pp. 140–41) — insertion of bodies into production and adjustment of population to economic processes.
- **Freud / Reich**: internal relays, not exits. Freud grounds sexuality in the law (alliance, taboo, Sovereign-Father) against *dégénérescence*/racism — a "retro-version" (p. 150). Reich is a tactical shift (p. 131).
- **[[Thinkers/Ian Hacking]]**: the homosexual-as-species (II.2) and the four personage-types are making-up-people cases inside this deployment.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- "Bodies and pleasures" as rallying point is named, not developed. Whether it has any purchase *inside* a power that "comes from everywhere" (p. 93) is the gap the late ethics tries to fill.
- The four strategies are "relatively autonomous" yet all "went by way of" the family. How much unity the *dispositif* has is left as a research program (the unwritten later volumes of the original plan).

> [!warning] Do not read the deployment as a state apparatus or a class plot. Power here is a "complex strategical situation" (p. 93); overall strategies and local tactics **double-condition** each other without homology of scale (IV.2).
