# Paralogisms of Pure Reason (Kant)

> "I think" is the sole text of rational psychology. It yields a transcendental subject of thoughts = *x*, not a science of a simple, substantial, personal soul.

## Summary

A **logical** paralogism is formally invalid. A **transcendental** paralogism "has a transcendental ground for inferring falsely due to its form" — unavoidable though not insoluble (A 341 / B 399). These are "sophistries not of human beings but of pure reason itself" (A 339 / B 397).

**"I think"** is "the sole text of rational psychology" (A 343 / B 401). The I is "wholly empty… a mere consciousness," a "transcendental subject of thoughts = *x*" (A 346 / B 404). The least empirical mark (pleasure, etc.) would turn the science empirical.

**A four paralogisms.**
1. **Substantiality** — the logical subject of thought passed off as a real subject of inherence. "It signifies a substance only in the **idea** but not in reality" (A 351).
2. **Simplicity** (the "Achilles") — simplicity of the representation I ≠ cognition of a simple soul. "I do not cognize the **real simplicity** of my subject" (A 356).
3. **Personality** — formal identity of apperception in time ≠ numerical identity of the subject (A 363).
4. **Ideality** of outer relation — skeptical doubt of outer objects, answered here by transcendental idealism + empirical realism (A 366–380). In B this material is replaced by the Refutation of Idealism in the Analytic of Principles.

**B recast.** Four analytic features of thinking that do *not* yield synthetic cognition of a substance / simple being / person / independently existent thinker (B 407–409). The governing syllogism is a *sophisma figurae dictionis*: the major uses the category of an object in general; the minor uses only the I as form of thinking (B 410–411). B adds the **Refutation of Mendelssohn**: even a simple soul could vanish by *elanguescence* (remission of intensive magnitude), not disintegration (B 413–415).

**Result.** "No rational psychology as **doctrine**… but only as **discipline**" (B 421). "I think" contains "I exist" but is **not** Descartes's inference (B 422 n.). Practical use of personality is left standing (A 365–366; B 424–426) without theoretical extension.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Descartes.** The *cogito* is the target. Kant grants the empty *I think* and even "I exist"; he denies that this is cognition of a thinking *substance* distinct from body. B even allows that mind and body may be two appearances of one unknown substance. See [[Concepts/Cogito (Descartes)]].
- **Mendelssohn.** *Phaedo*-style proof of the soul's persistence from simplicity fails: intensive magnitude can go to zero without parts.
- **Locke.** Personal identity as continuity of consciousness is closer to Kant's *formal* identity of apperception — which Kant says does *not* prove numerical identity of the subject. See [[Concepts/Identity and Diversity - Personal Identity (Locke)]].
- **Heidegger.** The "I think" as atemporal subject is the Cartesian residue Heidegger says Kant never overcame.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Do not identify the A fourth paralogism (ideality of outer objects) with the B [[Concepts/Schematism and Principles of Pure Understanding (Kant)|Refutation of Idealism]]. B moved the realism argument into the Analytic and shortened the Paralogisms.
- Practical personality without theoretical soul-substance is the same structure as practical freedom without theoretical proof — see [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]].
