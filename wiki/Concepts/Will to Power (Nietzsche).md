# Will to Power (Nietzsche)

## First published in *Zarathustra* (1883), not in *BGE* (1886)

The phrase is already in the book the vault treated as later poetry. Common capitalizes **Will to Power**.

Part I, "The Thousand and One Goals": a people's table of excellencies is "the voice of their Will to Power." "Valuing is creating."

Part II, **"Self-Surpassing,"** is the first full speech. "That is your entire will, ye wisest ones, as a Will to Power." "Wherever I found a living thing, there found I Will to Power." "Only where there is life, is there also will: not, however, Will to Life, but—so teach I thee—Will to Power!" Life's secret: "I am that WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF." Creators of values must first destroy values. Against Schopenhauer: not Will to Life / existence. See [[Concepts/The Superman (Nietzsche)]], [[Concepts/Redemption of the It Was (Nietzsche)]].

"Redemption" restates it once: Will to Power must do "something higher than all reconciliation" — then Zarathustra breaks off. Part III does not use the phrase.

Do **not** treat *Beyond Good and Evil* as the introduction of the name. BGE is the later *analytic* of the same impulse (philosophers, psychology as morphology). *Dawn* (1881) has the **feeling / desire / love / demon of power** and never the slogan — now a page: [[Concepts/Feeling of Power (Nietzsche)]]. GS 1882 still has only the **feeling of power** (§13); the 1887 Book V has expansion of power vs self-preservation (§349).

## Overview (BGE, 1886)

In the Preface and especially Chapter 1 ("Prejudices of Philosophers") of *Beyond Good and Evil*, Nietzsche restates the "will to power" as the most fundamental, most spiritual impulse underlying all great philosophy, the "will to 'creation of the world'," the "will to the causa prima." It is the tyrannical impulse by which philosophy (and life) imposes its image on the world. Behind the "will to truth," logic, valuations, and even the dogmatists' claims to purity lie physiological demands and the will to power.

**Key quote (Ch. 1, aphorism 9, on the Stoics but generalizing):** "In reality, however, it is quite otherwise with you: while you pretend to read with rapture the canon of your law in Nature, you want something quite the contrary... This is an old and everlasting story: what happened in old times with the Stoics still happens today, as soon as ever a philosophy begins to believe in itself. It always creates the world in its own image; it cannot do otherwise; philosophy is this tyrannical impulse itself, the most spiritual Will to Power, the will to 'creation of the world,' the will to the causa prima."

(*Zarathustra* Part II already named it as Life's secret. BGE applies it to philosophers' "confessions" and prejudices.)

## Key Arguments

- Every great philosophy is the confession/auto-biography of its originator; the moral (or immoral) purpose is the "true vital germ." To understand abstruse claims, ask "What morality do they aim at?"
- Behind all logic and its "sovereignty" are valuations—physiological demands for the maintenance of a definite mode of life. The "will to truth" itself is driven by deeper impulses.
- Philosophers (and all impulses) are "imperious"; each attempts to philosophize and make itself "the ultimate end of existence and the legitimate lord over all the other impulses."
- The Stoic (and by extension all dogmatic) project of "living according to Nature" is a fraud: they want to dictate *their* morals and ideals to Nature, to tyrannize it in their own image. "Philosophy is this tyrannical impulse itself."
- Explicitly: the "most spiritual Will to Power" is the will to the "creation of the world" (causa prima). This explains why philosophers create systems in their own image and why "conviction" appears at a certain point in every philosophy.
- The new philosophers ("of the dangerous 'Perhaps'") will have other tastes and will assign perhaps higher value to what was previously called "evil" (pretense, will to delusion, selfishness, cupidity) if it is life-furthering.

The will to power is not (yet) the full "ontology" of later Nietzsche but the diagnostic key for why philosophers (and humans) do what they do: impose, create, overcome, tyrannize (in the spiritual sense).

## Completed BGE (Ch. I remainder through Ch. IX)

June ingest had only §9. The rest of the book states the doctrine, not just the diagnosis.

- **§13.** Self-preservation is not the cardinal instinct. “A living thing seeks above all to **discharge its strength**—life itself is **will to power**”; self-preservation is “only one of the indirect and most frequent results.” Drop “superfluous teleological principles” (self-preservation owed to “Spinoza’s inconsistency”). Method = economy of principles. This is the first *definition*, not the Stoic-tyranny metaphor of §9.
- **§19.** Willing is command and obey inside a “social structure composed of many souls.” See [[Concepts/Soul as Social Structure (Nietzsche)]].
- **§22.** Physicists’ “Nature’s conformity to law” is “interpretation, not text” — a democratic “Ni dieu, ni maître.” Another reader could find “the tyrannically inconsiderate and relentless enforcement of the claims of power,” the “unexceptionalness… of all ‘Will to Power.’” Same calculable course, “not because laws obtain in it, but because they are absolutely lacking, and every power effects its ultimate consequences every moment.” “Granted that this also is only interpretation… well, so much the better.”
- **§23.** Psychology as “the Morphology and development-doctrine of the will to power.” Hatred, envy, covetousness, imperiousness may be “life-conditioning” emotions. “We sail away right over morality.” Psychology again “the queen of the sciences.”
- **§36.** If instinctive life is Will to Power, then “the world seen from within… would simply be ‘Will to Power,’ and nothing else.”
- **§211.** Genuine philosophers: “Their ‘knowing’ is *creating*… their will to truth is—*will to power*.”
- **§259 (the book’s strongest identification).** Life is “essentially appropriation, injury, conquest… exploitation.” A living organization “will have to be the incarnated Will to Power… because life is precisely Will to Power.” Exploitation is a “primary organic function,” not a primitive vice. Universalized non-injury is “Will to the denial of life.”

Do **not** collapse this into GS §13 “feeling of power.” The unpublished notebooks are now ingested — **as notebooks**, not as this book. See the section below and [[Sources/The Will to Power - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]].

## In Johnston's *Genealogy* (1887)

Do **not** back-read the slogan into Essay I. Closest there: "a will to overpower, a will to throw down, a will to rule" (I.XIII).

- **II.12:** "all purposes, all uses, are only signs that a will to power has become master." Origin ≠ purpose. Method sentence for [[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]].
- **II.18:** repressed instinct for freedom *is* beginning bad conscience, equated with will to power.
- **III.11:** the priest's "insatiable instinct and will to power" wants mastery "not over something in life but over life itself."
- **III.14:** "this very will to power of the weakest people!"
- **III.27:** announces a forthcoming *The Will to Power* / *The History of European Nihilism* — a book-title, not a doctrine completed here. The vault now has the Kaufmann compilation **excerpt** (Book I + Book III.I only). Still not a published book. See [[Sources/The Will to Power - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]].

## Prehistory in *The Gay Science*

Do **not** back-read the BGE name into 1882.

- **§13** is the "doctrine of the **feeling of power**." Benefiting and hurting are ways of "exercising one's power upon others." Pain is more efficient than pleasure. Hurting often signals *lack* of power. Pity is "the most agreeable feeling" of those with little pride. Kaufmann's note that Nietzsche was "developing" will to power is editorial.
- **Preface §2 (1887):** what was at stake in philosophizing hitherto was "not at all 'truth' but… health, future, growth, **power**, life."
- **§349 (1887):** self-preservation is a distress-state; life aims at *expansion of power* (explicitly against Spinoza and against Darwin's "struggle for existence").

The term *Wille zur Macht* as the most spiritual tyrannical impulse remains a BGE claim. GS supplies the feeling-of-power psychology and the 1887 expansion-vs-preservation contrast.

## Notebooks (Kaufmann compilation, incomplete)

*The Will to Power* is **not** a book Nietzsche published. *Genealogy* III.27 announced the title; he abandoned the plan; Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche and Peter Gast compiled notebooks; Kaufmann numbered them. This vault file is only Book I (notes 1–134) and Book III.I (466–617). Books II and IV, and note **1067** ("This world is the will to power — and nothing besides!"), are **not here**. Do not quote 1067 from memory.

What the notes actually add, without replacing *Zarathustra* or *BGE*:

- **Preface:** the title names a **countermovement** after the advent of nihilism — "Attempt at a Revaluation of All Values." See [[Concepts/European Nihilism (Nietzsche)]].
- **n.55:** "There is nothing to life that has value, except the degree of power—assuming that life itself is the will to power." Same note frames eternal recurrence as *extreme nihilism* (the meaningless, eternally), not as *Zarathustra*'s Yes.
- **n.480:** "Knowledge works as a tool of power."
- **n.481:** needs/drives interpret; every drive is a "lust to rule."
- **n.552:** "'Truth' is therefore not something there, that might be found or discovered—but something that must be created… It is a word for the 'will to power.'"
- **n.617:** "To impose upon becoming the character of being—that is the supreme will to power." Recurrence as closest approximation of becoming to being. **Not** n.1067.

The same Book III.I **denies will as a mental cause** (n.478, 488, 529). Do not flatten notebook "will to power" into [[Concepts/The Four Great Errors (Nietzsche)]] Error 2. Full epistemology: [[Concepts/Will to Power as Knowledge (Nietzsche)]].

## In *Twilight of the Idols* (1888)

Named, not first defined. Do not back-read this as the origin.

- **Skirmishes 11.** Architecture is "eloquence of power in forms"; "grand style" is the highest feeling of power and sureness — "**the will to power**."
- **Skirmishes 19–20.** Nothing beautiful except man; nothing ugly except "the degenerating man." "His feeling of power, his will to power, his courage, his pride — all fall with the ugly and rise with the beautiful."
- **Skirmishes 14 (anti-Darwin).** When struggle occurs it is "for power." Weak majority + "spirit" (cunning) prevail; species do not grow in perfection.
- **Skirmishes 38.** Liberal institutions, once attained, "undermine the will to power" = "herd-animalization." "The free man is a warrior."
- **Ancients 3.** The Greeks' strongest instinct: "I saw their strongest instinct, the will to power"; institutions as protections against it.

See [[Sources/Twilight of the Idols - Nietzsche (Kaufmann)]].

**Epilogue of *The Case of Wagner* (1888):** master-morality is the speech of well-constitutedness, of *ascending* life, “and of the Will to Power as a vital principle.” Not a new ontology. The pamphlet’s work is decadence and the Wagner case; this line restates the *Genealogy* pair and names WzM as the yes-saying of plenitude against Christian denial. See [[Sources/The Case of Wagner - Nietzsche (1888)]].

## In *The Antichrist* (1888; Mencken)

The **value-table**, not a new ontology. §2: “What is good?—Whatever augments the feeling of power, the will to power, power itself, in man.” Evil = weakness; happiness = power increases. §6: life as instinct for growth / accumulation of forces / power; “whenever the will to power fails there is disaster.” Gods (§16–18) are either will to power (national) or incapacity for power (must be “good”). This is the published first strike of the Revaluation; the notebooks remain notebooks. See [[Concepts/Formula of Values (Nietzsche)]], [[Sources/The Antichrist - Nietzsche (Mencken, Collection 2013)]].

## Vault Relevance / AI / Personal

- **Creative agency and "creation of the world"**: Will to power as the drive to impose form, values, and one's image on the "chaos" of raw sources, data, or circumstances. In the vault: maintenance and synthesis as active *creation* (not passive reflection or "truth"-finding), growing the wiki as one's "realm." In career: book of business or promotion not as accumulation but as creative will to power—overcoming, form-giving, value-creating in the agribusiness/AI domain.
- **Beyond "impulse to knowledge"**: Every synthesis, prompt, or "objective" analysis carries the ingester's deepest impulses and "moral purpose." Useful for self-examination, bias detection, and honest provenance (see also "philosophy as autobiography").
- **Tyrannical but spiritual**: The impulse is "tyrannical" (imposes) yet the highest/spiritual form of power. Supports affirmative, power-enhancing uses of tools/agents rather than purely reactive or "neutral" ones. Warns against philosophies (or AI systems) that deny their own will to power while exercising it.
- **"New philosophers" / free spirits**: The vault's agentic, exploratory mode as preparation for "philosophers of the dangerous 'Perhaps'"—those with the tension of the bow, willing to risk, assign new values, and create rather than merely preserve or react.
- **Personal/career**: In sales/leadership/insurance: the will to power as the drive to create value and "world" for clients and one's practice (not just "service" or compliance). Transcending slave morality (resentment at "the market," mere duty without power) toward noble creation and overcoming. Ties to MasterClass "entrepreneurial leap," "values-driven leadership," deliberate practice as disciplined will to power.

**Contrasts/syntheses with vault cluster**:
- Vs. **Kant** (good will, duty, categorical as "Tartuffery"): will to power as the real (often unconscious) driver behind the "pure" a priori and moral legislation; Nietzsche sees Kant's system as one more imposition in the image of its author.
- Vs. **Spinoza** (conatus as actual essence/striving to persevere): both fundamental "will," but Nietzsche emphasizes the creative, tyrannical, world-creating aspect over mere perseverance; attacks Spinoza's geometrical "mask."
- Vs. **Hume** (custom/habit as great guide, belief as feeling): will to power as deeper than custom; the "will to truth" (or any valuation) is power-driven, not just habitual.
- Vs. **classical (Plato/Aristotle/Stoics)**: Plato's Good in Itself as dogmatist error; Aristotle's function/eudaimonia as another imposed valuation; Stoic "according to Nature" as self-tyranny and fraud. Will to power as the honest recognition of the tyrannical impulse in all life/philosophy.
- With **vault method**: Maintenance as will to power (imposing useful form/perspectives on sources); "dangerous Perhaps" as permission for exploratory synthesis; recognition that our "lint" and "schema" are also expressions of will to power (productive if conscious and affirmative).
