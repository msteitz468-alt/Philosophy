# Radical Freedom and Situation (Sartre)
> Because consciousness *is not* anything, the human is not free to choose among options but *is* freedom itself — a freedom that realises itself only in and through the particular situation it must take up as its own.

## Summary

### From *Being and Nothingness*, Part Four ch. 1 (primary)

**Freedom is the being of the for-itself, not a faculty.** An act is on principle intentional; the careless smoker who explodes a magazine has not acted (BN 433). No factual state — economy, "psychological state," past — can by itself motivate an act (BN 435). Cause (*motif*: situation as means in the light of an end), motive (*mobile*: non-thetic self-consciousness of the project), and end arise in one upsurge; the motive does not *cause* the act. "Freedom is not a being; it is the being of man — i.e., his nothingness of being." "He is wholly and forever free or he is not free at all" (BN 441). "I am condemned to exist forever beyond my essence, beyond the causes and motives of my act. I am condemned to be free" (BN 439). Against Leibniz: Adam is not a God-given essence from which the apple-act follows; "essence comes after existence" (BN 468). Against Bergson: no "profound self"; "Choice and consciousness are one and the same thing" (BN 462). The will is only one mode of original freedom. "When I deliberate, the chips are down" (BN 450). "Not to choose is, in fact, to choose not to choose" (BN 481). "For human reality, to be is to act, and to cease to act is to cease to be" (BN 476).

**Situation.** "There is freedom only in a situation, and there is a situation only through freedom" (BN 489). Situation is the "common product" of brute given and freedom; one cannot split what comes from each. Freedom is autonomy of *choice*, not success: the prisoner is free to *try* to escape, not to walk out (BN 483). The *coefficient of adversity* appears only after an end is posited. Five structures: **place** (birth is receiving a place; I am responsible for the place I take); **past** (irremediable, force-less until recovered; meaning decided by the present project); **environment** (instrumental things whose coefficients can change without me; even torture does not exempt us from constituting the impossibility); **fellowman** (techniques, nationality, race, appearance as *unrealizables* I am for the Other and must assume; "Freedom is total and infinite … it never encounters" its limits — BN 531; the slave in chains is as free as the master *to break them*); **death** — not Heidegger's *Sein zum Tode*. Death is absurd chance, "in no way distinguished from birth" (BN 545); "Death is never that which gives life its meanings; it is, on the contrary, that which on principle removes all meaning from life" (BN 539–40). Finitude is ontological (choice excludes possibles), even if I were immortal. Attitudes toward death cannot be ranked authentic/unauthentic.

**Responsibility.** "Man being condemned to be free carries the weight of the whole world on his shoulders; he is responsible for the world and for himself as a way of being" (BN 553). "If I am mobilized in a war, this war is my war … I deserve it" (BN 554). I am responsible for everything except my responsibility (I am not the foundation of my being). "I am abandoned in the world … condemned to be wholly responsible for myself" (BN 555–56). This is anguish, usually fled in [[Concepts/Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi) (Sartre)|bad faith]].

Welten's slogan (situation does not determine as gravity determines the stone; ethics = do not disown freedom) is right as far as it goes. BN specifies the five-part anatomy and the authorship of the *whole world*, including war.

## From the 1945/46 lecture (2026-08-12)

The public formula is **"man is condemned to be free."** "Condemned, because he did not create himself, yet is nevertheless at liberty, and from the moment that he is thrown into this world he is responsible for everything he does." "There is no determinism — man is free, man *is* freedom." The ground is the lecture's [[Concepts/Existence Precedes Essence (Sartre)|existence-precedes-essence]] argument, not the pour-soi ontology: no God, no human nature, no *a priori* values, therefore no excuse. The lived names of this freedom are [[Concepts/Anguish, Abandonment and Despair (Sartre)|anguish, abandonment, and despair]].

**Situation vs caprice.** "What is not possible is not to choose. I can always choose, but I must know that if I do not choose, that is still a choice." This is "a limit to fantasy and caprice." Gide's *acte gratuit* is rejected: "Gide does not know what a situation is, his 'act' is one of pure caprice." Man "finds himself in an organised situation in which he is himself involved: his choice involves mankind in its entirety, and he cannot avoid choosing" (single; marry without children; marry and have children). The moral choice is *comparable* to constructing a work of art — no *a priori* picture, values appear in the coherence of the finished work — without being an aesthetic morality.

**Invent the law.** The pupil who must choose mother or Free French is the lecture's situation: Christian, Kantian, and sentimental rules all fail to decide. "No rule of general morality can show you what you ought to do: no signs are vouchsafed in this world." "You are free, therefore choose, that is to say, invent." "Man makes himself; he is not found ready-made; he makes himself by the choice of his morality, and he cannot but choose a morality."

**The sum of actions.** "There is no reality except in action." "Man is nothing else but what he purposes, he exists only in so far as he realises himself, he is therefore nothing else but the sum of his actions, nothing else but what his life is." No unused potentialities behind a failed life; no love apart from deeds of love; Proust is his works; a coward is not a temperament but the act of giving up. "The coward makes himself cowardly, the hero makes himself heroic; and that there is always a possibility for the coward to give up cowardice and for the hero to stop being a hero."

**Willing freedom.** Once a man has seen that values depend upon himself, "he can will only one thing, and that is freedom as the foundation of all values" — not in the abstract, but "in and through particular circumstances." "I cannot make liberty my aim unless I make that of others equally my aim." Kant "declared that freedom is a will both to itself and to the freedom of others. Agreed: but he thinks that the formal and the universal suffice for the constitution of a morality. We think, on the contrary, that principles that are too abstract break down when we come to defining action." The lecture is thus more ethically assertive than Welten's "ethics cannot be founded": it founds a *form* (will freedom, including others') while leaving the *content* to invention. See [[Concepts/Existential Humanism (Sartre)]].

## Key Claims / Positions
- The human does not *have* freedom; the pour-soi *is* freedom.
- Freedom is not choice among objects but the ungrounded way I take up my situation.

- > [!warning] [[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]] refuses the total-or-none formulation and denies that not-refusing is a choice. See [[Contradictions/Merleau-Ponty vs Sartre - Situated Freedom vs Radical Freedom]], [[Concepts/Situated Freedom (Merleau-Ponty)]].
- A situation determines nothing; it is taken up freely (the relation, not the situation, is free).
- *Anguish* (reflexive) is the experience of freedom; *fear* (irreflexive) is directed at an object.
- Freedom is realised as a future-oriented *project*; recognising the situation as mine = engagement.
- Ethics cannot be founded on an essence; the lecture adds a universal *form*: will freedom, including others'.
- Man is the sum of his actions; unused potentialities do not count.
- Not to choose is still a choice; Gide's gratuitous act is not a situation.

### How Sartre Differs From Prior Thinkers in the Wiki
- **vs [[Thinkers/Kant]]**: agrees that freedom wills itself and others' freedom; denies that the formal/universal decides the pupil's case. Values are invented, not found in an intelligible heaven.
- **vs compatibilist/determinist debates** (e.g. [[Thinkers/Hume]], [[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]): Sartre sidesteps the free-will/determinism question as malformed; causality does not apply to existence.
- **vs [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: thrownness is kept as facticity; *Sein zum Tode* is refused. Death is chance and the Other's victory, not my ownmost possibility. See [[Concepts/Being-toward-Death and Authenticity (Heidegger)]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]]** (reception, 2013): Berlin’s positive-liberty target is a *higher* rational or collective self imposed on a lower one. Sartre’s pour-soi *is* freedom — no Green-style elite of the real self. Adjacent “positive” vocabularies, different slides. See [[Concepts/Negative and Positive Liberty (Berlin)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- > [!warning] The doctrine of "absolute" freedom-in-situation is widely charged with underweighting material/social constraint — a charge Sartre himself answers in the *Critique* by re-locating freedom within the *practical-inert* and scarcity.
