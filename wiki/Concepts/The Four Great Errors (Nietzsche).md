# The Four Great Errors (Nietzsche)
> Religion and morality rest on four corruptions of reason: effect taken for cause; will as cause; imaginary comforting "whys"; free will invented so priests can punish.

## Summary

A chapter of *Twilight of the Idols* (1888). Nietzsche's own headings:

1. **The error of confusing cause and effect**
2. **The error of a false causality**
3. **The error of imaginary causes**
4. **The error of free will**

They are four, not one. Do not fold (4) into (2), and do not file the chapter as Humean compatibilism or as [[Concepts/Causal Diagrams and DAGs (Pearl)]].

**1. Confusing cause and effect.** "There is no more insidious error"; "the real corruption of reason"; worshipped "under the name of ‘religion’ or ‘morality.’" Cornaro's diet book: he thought a meager diet *caused* his long life; the slow metabolism that *forced* the diet was the cause. "He was not free to eat little or much; his frugality was not a matter of ‘free will.’" General formula of religion and morality: "Do this and that… and then you will be happy!" Nietzsche inverts it — "the first example of my ‘revaluation of all values’": "his virtue is the **effect** of his happiness." License and luxury do not destroy a people; a people approaching physiological destruction *results* in license. "Every mistake… is the result of a degeneration of instinct." "All that is good is instinctive — and hence easy, necessary, uninhibited. Effort is a failing."

**2. False causality.** Faith that we *know* what a cause is comes from "inner facts," "not a single one" of which has turned out true. Primary: "the will as cause." Consciousness/"spirit" and the ego/subject are "afterbirths." "The will no longer moves anything, hence it does not explain anything — it merely accompanies events; it can also be completely absent." Motives are a "surface phenomenon of consciousness… more likely to hide the causes of our actions than to reveal them." The ego "has become a fable." "**There are no mental causes at all.**" Man projected will, spirit, ego onto the world; "even your atom, my dear materialists and physicists"; the thing-in-itself is the "*horrendum pudendum* of metaphysicians." "The ‘spirit as cause’… called God!"

**3. Imaginary causes.** Dream-logic first: a cause is slipped *after* a sensation (the cannon shot); time appears reversed. Waking life does the same with "general feelings." "First principle: any explanation is better than none." The feeling of pleasure/"of strength" is used as a criterion of truth. One seeks a *familiar* cause (the banker's "business," the Christian's "sin," the girl's love). The whole of morality and religion belongs here. Schopenhauer is quoted as generalizing pain into desert (*WWR* II). "One is in a state of hope because the basic physiological feeling is once again strong and rich." "Morality and religion belong entirely to the psychology of error."

**4. Free will.** "The foulest of all theological fictions, intended to make mankind ‘responsible’ in a religious sense — that is, dependent upon priests." "Men were considered ‘free’ only so that they might be considered guilty." "Christianity is religion for the executioner." Immoralists try to take guilt and punishment out of the world and restore "the innocence of becoming."

**§8 (doctrine).** "No one gives a man his qualities — neither God, nor society, nor his parents and ancestors, nor he himself. (The nonsense of the last idea was taught as ‘intelligible freedom’ by Kant — and perhaps by Plato.)" "A man is necessary, a man is a piece of fatefulness, a man belongs to the whole… there is nothing besides the whole." "The concept of ‘God’ was until now the greatest objection to existence. We deny God, we deny the responsibility that originates from God: and thereby we redeem the world."

## Key Claims

- The four errors are *named* and *ordered*. Free will is its own heading, analyzed as a priestly right to punish, not as a puzzle about determinism and "could have done otherwise."
- Happiness → virtue, not virtue → happiness. That inversion is explicitly a "revaluation."
- Will as cause, motives, and ego are rejected as inner-world evidence. This is stronger than "the will is constrained."
- Imaginary causes are a *comfort* psychology: the unknown is danger; the first familiar story feels true.
- "Intelligible freedom" is named and dismissed. See [[Concepts/Freedom as the Key to Autonomy (Kant)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — late, schematic psychology of error under the Revaluation. Continues BGE's grammar/ego critique and *Genealogy*'s priestly right to punish. The Kaufmann notebooks (n.478–479, 531, 551) are the workshop: inner experience inverts cause and effect; "lightning flashes" as double positing; "There are neither causes nor effects." Same Book III.I still talks will to power as knowledge. Do not flatten that into Error 2. See [[Concepts/Will to Power as Knowledge (Nietzsche)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — "intelligible freedom" = "nonsense." Not an engagement with the Third Antinomy's argument; a diagnosis of the *use* of the idea.
- **[[Thinkers/David Hume]]** / [[Concepts/Compatibilism - Liberty and Necessity (Hume)]] — **adjacent, not the same.** Hume keeps necessity (constant conjunction) *and* liberty (unconstrained action per the will). Nietzsche denies the will as cause *and* free will as theological fiction. He does not salvage a cleaned-up "liberty."
- **[[Thinkers/Judea Pearl]]** — **do not merge.** Nietzsche diagnoses folk causal *storytelling* (effect-for-cause; projected will; post-hoc "why"). He does not offer interventions, identifiability, or structural equations.
- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — named in §6: pain as desert; morality as "poisoner and slanderer of life."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Do not write this chapter as a compatibilist solution to Kant. Nietzsche's "piece of fatefulness" plus "no mental causes" is not Hume's two definitions of cause plus liberty.
- > [!warning] "We deny God… and thereby we redeem the world" is not GS §125. The madman addresses unbelievers who have come too early. This is a *doctrine* of irresponsibility and the innocence of becoming.
