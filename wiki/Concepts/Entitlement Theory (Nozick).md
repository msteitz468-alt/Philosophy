# Entitlement Theory (Nozick)
> Justice in holdings is historical: acquisition, transfer, rectification. “From each as they choose, to each as they are chosen.” There is no *central* distribution.

## Summary

Chapter 7 of *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* opens by refusing the phrase. “Distributive justice” is non-neutral: there is “no *central* distribution.” Holdings arise from exchange and gift, like mates, not like pie-cutting.

Three topics, stated as an inductive definition:

1. A person who acquires a holding in accordance with the principle of justice in **acquisition** is entitled to that holding.
2. A person who acquires a holding in accordance with the principle of justice in **transfer**, from someone else entitled to the holding, is entitled to the holding.
3. No one is entitled to a holding except by (repeated) applications of 1 and 2.

“Whatever arises from a just situation by just steps is itself just.” Only *actual* history counts — not what *could* have arisen (a thief vs gifts). A third principle, **rectification** of injustice, uses historical and subjunctive information about the no-injustice path. “I do not know of a thorough or theoretically sophisticated treatment.” Details of all three “I shall not attempt… here.” The book specifies **form**, not content.

**Historical vs patterned.** Entitlement is *historical*. *Current time-slice* principles (utilitarian matrices, welfare economics) judge only who has what now. Broader *end-result / end-state* principles include time-sequences of profiles. A principle is *patterned* if shares vary with a natural dimension (merit, need, IQ, “to each according to…”). Entitlement is historical but **not** patterned. Production and distribution are not separate: things “come into the world already attached to people.” Slogan: “From each as they choose, to each as they are chosen.”

**Liberty upsets patterns.** The [[Arguments/Wilt Chamberlain Argument (Nozick)|Wilt Chamberlain]] case is the demonstration. “No end-state principle or distributional patterned principle of justice can be continuously realized without continuous interference with people’s lives.” The socialist analog: “forbid capitalist acts between consenting adults.”

**Forced labor / part-ownership.** Patterned justice is “recipient” justice; it ignores givers. Redistribution (except rectification) violates rights. “Taxation of earnings from labor is on a par with forced labor.” Seizing labor-results makes others “a *part-owner* of you” — a shift from self-ownership to “(partial) property rights in *other* people.”

**Lockean proviso.** Mixing-labor is obscure (tomato juice in the sea). Locke’s “enough and as good left in common” is restated more weakly: appropriation fails if it worsens others’ *liberty to use* the thing (not mere loss of chance to *appropriate*). Compensation can save it. The proviso’s “historical shadow” binds later transfers (no buying the last water-hole). Not an end-state test. Markets “will not actually run afoul”; the baseline is so low that violation is catastrophe. The baseline “needs more detailed investigation.”

**What this does *not* justify.** “If the set of holdings is properly generated, there is no argument for a more extensive state based upon distributive justice.” Rectification is the one opening: lacking history, a maximin transfer scheme could be a “rough rule of thumb”; past injustice “might be so great as to make necessary in the short run a more extensive state.” “Socialism as the punishment for our sins” is too far.

Chapter 8 applies the theory: no presumption in favor of equality; particular rights “fill the space of rights,” leaving no room for a general right to a material condition; envy, meaningful work, workers’ control, Marxian exploitation, philanthropy, and “having a say” all fail as grounds for a larger state.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]** / [[Concepts/State of Nature, Property, and Revolution (Locke)]]: labor-mixing rejected as stated; “enough and as good” kept in weakened form and shadowed onto transfer. Property is still pre-political.
- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]**: utilitarianism is the type-case of a time-slice / end-state principle. Rawls is praised as unmatched “since Mill” and as an “undeniably great advance over utilitarianism.”
- **Rawls**: the named opponent. The original position *guarantees* end-state principles: parties treat the pie as “manna from heaven.” No historical principle could be chosen first. The difference principle does not take seriously the distinction between persons. People “are entitled to their natural assets, and to what flows from them.” See [[Contradictions/Nozick vs Rawls - Historical Entitlement vs Patterned Justice]].
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: “to each according to…” is the patterned slogan; “full fruits of labor” is historical but (Nozick says) mistaken. The serious attack is Ch. 8, on the labor theory of value.
- **[[Thinkers/James M. Buchanan]]**: Buchanan’s veil of *uncertainty* is not in this chapter. Adjacent: both refuse a patterned public good; they split on whether constitutional rules, rather than side constraints on holdings, are the remedy.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** / [[Concepts/Side Constraints (Nozick)]]: part-ownership of persons is the political violation of treating humanity as an end.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Historical entitlement vs patterned / end-state justice — see [[Contradictions/Nozick vs Rawls - Historical Entitlement vs Patterned Justice]].
- Acquisition is form only. Tomato-juice, entropy, value-added, Henry George are left unresolved.
- Rectification is underdeveloped by the author’s own admission — the only opening he leaves for patterned transfers.
