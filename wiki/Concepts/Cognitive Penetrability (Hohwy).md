# Cognitive Penetrability (Hohwy)
> Predicting is not automatically seeing. Higher-level beliefs can shape experience, but only when uncertainty opens the door and the belief predicts at the right grain.

## Summary

Chapter 6 of *The Predictive Mind* takes the Fodor–Pylyshyn–Churchland debate — does "what we believe determine what we perceive" (p. 118)? — and gives it a [[Concepts/Prediction Error Minimization (Hohwy)|PEM]] verdict: **balanced, not always or never**. "Not any old prediction will be able to determine perception, and no prediction can determine it fully" (p. 117).

Interesting penetrability, for Hohwy, is **synchronic, semantic, high-to-low**: a differing higher-level colour belief changing current experience of the same strawberry (p. 122). He sets aside tumours, mere post-hoc judgement, wholesale input change, and only intra-low-level priors (light-from-above). He relaxes strict input-identity because [[Concepts/Active Inference and Action (Clark)|active inference]] almost always changes input (pp. 119–20).

The rule is uncertainty. "When uncertainty is high, we should expect an increase in the ability of ever higher-level prior beliefs to determine conscious experience" (p. 124). Expected imprecision decreases synaptic gain on PE units, so higher levels get more weight. Impenetrability is the complementary case: early PE suppression plus grain. Müller-Lyer leaves "very little residual prediction error… little work for any higher-level prior" (p. 126). A belief must "predict at the right fineness of spatiotemporal grain" or it is "probabilistically idle" (pp. 127–8). Labs often *block* the active inference that would confirm the true belief. "Belief revision is hostage to prediction error minimization throughout the cortical hierarchy" (p. 128). "Cognitive impenetrability occurs when prediction error is sufficiently suppressed at relatively early processing stages" (p. 126).

There is no anatomical modularity border. Penetrability is expected in a hierarchy and "often restricted" (p. 123).

## Hohwy's case list

**Accepted as possible/interesting (with caveats):** higher-level colour beliefs under noise; scale errors in toddlers (hard to interpret); bistable figures, especially when *causal* information breaks tandem flipping; ventriloquism / Bayes-optimal integration (multi-sensory, not a unimodal module); patterns-in-noise after out-of-control or ESP priming (via precision-downweighting); Carrasco contrast-from-covert-attention.

**Rejected or sidelined as the interesting case:** tumour-causation, mere judgement, input-changing action under a strict definition, light-from-above as high-to-low penetration.

**Explained rather than denied:** Müller-Lyer; the rubber hand vs the known setup; TV ventriloquism vs the true "the doll isn't speaking" belief.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **Fodor / Pylyshyn**: the debate's source. Fodor allows low-level top-down but not high-to-low (pp. 120–1). Hohwy keeps Pylyshyn's content-coherence constraint and answers Fodor 1988 on duck–rabbit/fixation via active inference (pp. 130–1).
- **Bruner's New Look**: Hohwy's historical undercurrent (intro, p. 5); ch. 6 is the disciplined version.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: *Surfing Uncertainty* Ch 6.12 now supplies Clark's own verdict — **safer penetration**. Top-down influence only when it earned its keep globally (Lupyan). Müller-Lyer persists because a sentence does not overturn globally productive intermediate layers, not because perception is Fodorian-impenetrable. Hohwy 2013 is earlier and more detailed (uncertainty opens the door; grain; early PE suppression). They agree that penetration is real and restricted; Clark stresses *global* earning, Hohwy *local* uncertainty and grain. *The Experience Machine* is light on this literature.
- **Theory-laden observation** (philosophy of science): Hohwy's grain and early-suppression story is a way to keep a "reasonably robust theory–observation distinction" (p. 257, Fodor 1983/1984) without a Fodorian module.
