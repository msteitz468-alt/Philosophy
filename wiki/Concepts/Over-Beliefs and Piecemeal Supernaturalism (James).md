# Over-Beliefs and Piecemeal Supernaturalism (James)

> "The most interesting and valuable things about a man are usually his over-beliefs." The common nucleus of religions is an uneasiness plus a deliverance: the higher part of the self is "conterminous and continuous with a MORE of the same quality" outside it. On the hither side that MORE is the subconscious continuation of conscious life. On the farther side James's own over-belief is **piecemeal supernaturalism** — a larger power that produces real effects in nature, which need not be infinite or unique.

## Overview

Lecture XVIII names **over-beliefs**: the dogmatic and idealist constructions built out beyond feeling. They are indispensable and should be treated "with tenderness and tolerance so long as they are not intolerant themselves." They are not the nucleus.

Lecture XX reduces religion to "lowest admissible terms":

**Characteristics of the religious life** (as found): the visible world is part of a more spiritual universe; union with that higher universe is our true end; prayer or inner communion is a process "wherein work is really done"; plus a new zest, and an assurance of safety with loving affections.

**Men's religions need not be identical.** "If an Emerson were forced to be a Wesley, or a Moody forced to be a Whitman, the total human consciousness of the divine would suffer." Parts are not interchangeable. A science of religions cannot *replace* living religion: to understand drunkenness is not to be drunk ([[Thinkers/Al-Ghazali]], reused from the Mysticism lecture). Knowledge about life is not "effective occupation of a place in life."

**Against the "Survival theory."** Science has repudiated the personal point of view; private selves look like Clifford's epiphenomena, "bubbles on the foam." James: that view is shallow. "As soon as we deal with private and personal phenomena as such, we deal with realities in the completest sense of the term." "The axis of reality runs solely through the egotistic places." A science that leaves out the pinch of individual destiny offers "a printed bill of fare as the equivalent for a solid meal."

**Nucleus.** Creeds cancel; a uniform deliverance remains: (1) uneasiness — "something wrong about us as we naturally stand"; (2) solution — "we are saved from the wrongness by making proper connection with the higher powers." The man identifies his real being with the germinal higher part of himself, and "becomes conscious that this higher part is conterminous and continuous with a MORE of the same quality, which is operative in the universe outside of him."

**Hypothesis (hither side).** The MORE, "whatever it may be on its *farther* side," is on its *hither* side "the subconscious continuation of our conscious life" (Myers, 1892: each of us is "an abiding psychical entity far more extensive than he knows"). Invasions from this region take on objective appearances; the theologian's "external power" is thus "not merely apparently, but literally true" as a sense of union. This is only a doorway.

**Over-belief (farther side).** "That which produces effects within another reality must be termed a reality itself." James will call the higher part of the universe **God**. "God is real since he produces real effects." A good hypothesis must predict more than the phenomenon it was invoked to explain; God as *only* the content of the union-experience is not yet prolific enough. James's personal venture: "the world of our present consciousness is only one out of many worlds of consciousness"; they keep discrete "in the main" but "become continuous at certain points, and higher energies filter in." Faithfulness to poor over-beliefs here below may "actually help God in turn to be more effectively faithful to his own greater tasks."

## Piecemeal vs. refined supernaturalism (Postscript)

Thinkers divide into naturalists and supernaturalists; James is a supernaturalist. **Refined / universalistic** supernaturalism (the Kantian academic default) bars ideal entities from interfering causally in phenomenal events: the ideal world is a point of view for judging facts, not a world of facts. **Crasser / piecemeal** supernaturalism admits miracles, providential leadings, and interpolations of the ideal among the forces that determine real particulars.

James cannot accept popular Christianity or scholastic theism, but his belief that "in communion with the Ideal new force comes into the world" classes him with the piecemeal type. Refined supernaturalism "surrenders… too easily to naturalism." "Different principles must occasion differences in fact." "All facts are particular facts." A God who altered no concrete particular is "an incredible proposition."

Karma, as James apprehends it, agrees in principle: judgment carries *execution* with it, is *in rebus* as well as *post rem*. Immortality is **secondary**; "spirit-return" facts are still lacking (Myers, Hodgson, Hyslop respected, not adopted). Religious experience "cannot be cited as unequivocally supporting the infinitist belief." "The only thing that it unequivocally testifies to is that we can experience union with *something* larger than ourselves and in that union find our greatest peace." The power "need not be infinite, it need not be solitary." A "sort of polytheism" is left open, not defended. Common sense "can suffer the notion of this world being partly saved and partly lost." "For practical life at any rate, the *chance* of salvation is enough."

## How This Relates to 1907

- [[Concepts/Meliorism (James)]] (1907) is the later, named doctrine of salvation as a grounded *possibility* with real losers. The Postscript already has partial and conditional salvation and "the chance of salvation." Do not back-name 1902 as "meliorism"; the word in IV–V meant evolutionist progress-religion.
- [[Concepts/Pragmatic Method (James)]]: Lecture XVIII already applies Peirce's principle to God's *metaphysical* attributes ("destitute of all intelligible significance"; "the metaphysical monster… is an absolutely worthless invention of the scholarly mind") and keeps the *moral* attributes as practically live. Lecture XX's "thoroughly 'pragmatic' view of religion" is this: religion postulates new *facts*, not a mere re-expression of the materialistic world.
- [[Concepts/The One and the Many (James)]]: the Postscript's refusal of required infinitude and uniqueness is the religious face of 1907 pluralism. God as later "*primus inter pares*" is continuous with, not identical to, 1902's "anything larger will do, if only it be large enough to trust for the next step."

## Contrasts / Lineage

- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Kant]] / refined supernaturalism**: Kantian barring of ideal causes from the phenomenal series is exactly what James will not grant. [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]] postulates God for morality; James wants differences in *particular fact*.
- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Thomas Aquinas]] / [[Thinkers/Augustine]]**: an omnipotent, simple, unique God is an over-belief religious experience does not force. Piecemeal supernaturalism is not scholastic theism.
- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Spinoza]] / [[Concepts/Deus sive Natura - Substance Monism (Spinoza)]]**: a finished Nature with no interpolations. James's unseen region "produces effects in this world."
- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Plotinus]] / mystic monism**: "pass to the limit" and identify the something with "a unique God who is the all-inclusive soul of the world." James will not follow.
- **With [[Concepts/Philosophy as the No Man's Land between Theology and Science (Russell)]]**: James is occupying that land as a *science of religions* that refuses both survival-theory naturalism and refined supernaturalism.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Is "God is real since he produces real effects" a pragmatic theory of truth or a causal criterion of reality? 1902 states the latter; 1907 will generalize the former. Flag rather than identify.
- The hither-side hypothesis (subliminal) is offered as science-compatible; the farther-side over-belief is not coercive. Later readers who take the subliminal as a *reduction* of God are reading against the Postscript.
- Polytheism / finite gods: left open "to keep the testimony of religious experience clearly within its proper bounds." Not a doctrine James here defends.
