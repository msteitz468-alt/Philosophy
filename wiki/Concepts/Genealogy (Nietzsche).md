# Genealogy (Nietzsche)
> A critique of the *value* of moral values, written in grey — documented, hieroglyphic past — not a blue English hypothesis about origins.

## Summary

The 2026-06-04 ingest of *On the Genealogy of Morals* (Samuel) created five concept pages and then **retracted** a promised method page. This page exists now because [[Concepts/Genealogy (Foucault)]] already treats Nietzsche as the source of a method Nietzsche is not allowed to state in his own words.

Johnston's Prologue is the method statement. The tract is about "the origin of our moral prejudices," but origin-hypotheses are only a path. "For me the issue was the value of morality." The new demand: "we need a critique of moral values, and we must first question the very value of these values." That requires "a knowledge of the conditions and circumstance out of which these values grew" — morality as "consequence, as symptom, as mask, as hypocrisy, as illness, as misunderstanding — but also morality as cause, as means of healing, as stimulant, as scruples, as poison."

The colour is **gray** (Johnston; Samuel: "grey"), not English "blue": "what has been documented, what can be established as the truth, what really took place, in short, the long, difficult-to-decipher hieroglyphic writing of the past in human morality." Paul Rée's *The Origin of the Moral Feelings* (1877) is the named antipode: a "genuinely English" inverted genealogy mixing Darwin's beast with a tender modern sensibility that "no longer bites." Nietzsche does not refute Rée point by point; he puts "in the place of something unlikely something more likely."

Reading is **rumination**. An aphorism "has not been 'deciphered' simply by being read." Essay III is "a model of what I call an 'interpretation'": an aphorism is presented and the essay is the commentary. "One almost needs to be a cow, at any rate not a modern man." Cheerfulness — "the gay science" — is the *reward* of that underground seriousness, not its starting mood.

The first ingest already used "genealogy" as vault lint. This page is the primary, not that metaphor.

***Dawn* (1881) is the earlier tool, not this method.** It undermines [[Concepts/Faith in Morals (Nietzsche)]] and states [[Concepts/Morality of Custom (Nietzsche)]] (§9). It does **not** ask the *value* of values, does not use grey/blue, and does not run origin ≠ purpose (II.12). Paul as “inventor of Christianity” (§68) and historical origin of God-belief making a counterproof superfluous (§95) are seeds. Do not flatten 1881 into 1887. See [[Sources/The Dawn of Day - Nietzsche (Kennedy, Collection 2013)]].

## Key Claims

- Question the *value* of values, not only their origin. What if "the good" is "a symptom of regression… a poison, a narcotic," the "danger of all dangers" if "the highest possible power and magnificence of the human type were never attained"?
- Method is historical-philological and psychological: etymology of "good/bad" (Essay I), creditor-debtor *Schuld* (Essay II), types of the ascetic ideal (Essay III). It is not a utilitarian story of forgotten usefulness.
- **Essay I** uses "genealogy of morals" for the English psychologists' *incompetence* and for Nietzsche's own "fundamental insight" (noble social names as the root of "good").
- **Essay II.12**: origin ≠ purpose. "All purposes, all uses, are only signs that a will to power has become master." Only the ahistorical can be defined. This is the load-bearing anti-teleology later readers (including Foucault) take as "genealogy."
- **Essay III** is an interpretation of one lemma (the opening catalogue + *horror vacui*), not a survey of "ideals" in general.
- Prerequisite: earlier books, especially *Zarathustra* — "deeply wounded by and profoundly delighted with every word."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** (Johnston, 1887): grey documents + critique of value + rumination. *Ressentiment* / resentment, bad conscience, and the ascetic ideal are the *findings*, not the method.
- **[[Thinkers/Michel Foucault]]**: "history of the present"; power-knowledge; bodies and institutions. Nietzsche is *not named* in *Discipline and Punish* I.1; the word "genealogy" carries the method. Foucault keeps the anti-teleology (II.12) and drops the rank-order of values / "magnificence of the human type." See [[Concepts/Genealogy (Foucault)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Paul Rée]]** (named opponent, no primary in the vault): English-style origin of the moral feelings via utility, habit, forgetting. Do not close a debate from Nietzsche's side alone.
- **[[Thinkers/Quentin Skinner]]**: reads *Genealogy* I as an unrecognised exercise in *paradiastole* (rhetorical redescription). That is Skinner's grouping, not Nietzsche's self-description. See [[Concepts/Rhetorical Redescription and Paradiastole]].

## The Author's Own Account of the Technique (Ecce Homo, 1888)

*Ecce Homo*'s self-review of the *Genealogy* is a rarity: a genealogist describing his own rhetoric. It should be read alongside the Prologue, because it says something the Prologue does not — that the essays' cool opening is a **deliberate trap**.

> "In each case the beginning is calculated to mislead, it is cool, scientific, even ironic, intentionally superficial and intentionally reticent."

Then a rise in tempo, and: "At the end, in each case, amid fearful thunderclaps a new truth shines out from between heavy clouds."

His own one-line summaries of the three essays:

1. **Essay I** — "the birth of Christianity out of the spirit of **resentment**, not as is supposed out of the 'Spirit'." (Note this edition renders *ressentiment* as "resentment"; see the translation warning below.) A counter-movement against noble values.
2. **Essay II** — the psychology of conscience: not the voice of God in man but "the instinct of cruelty which turns inwards once it is unable to discharge itself outwardly." Cruelty is named a foundation of culture. See [[Concepts/Bad Conscience (Nietzsche)]].
3. **Essay III** — why an ideal that is "a will to nonentity and to decadence" nevertheless holds power: *faute de mieux*, because it had no competitor until Zarathustra. "This book contains the first psychology of the priest." Closing quotation, as he gives it: "For man prefers to aspire to nonentity than not to will at all."

And the frame: the three essays are "**Three decisive sketches of a psychologist for a Revaluation of all Values**" — preparatory, not the thing itself. See [[Concepts/Revaluation of All Values (Nietzsche)]].

> [!warning] The self-review is 1888 reading 1887, and it flattens. Nothing in it mentions the grey/blue distinction, rumination, or Rée — i.e. the *method* statements the Prologue makes. What it supplies is the rhetorical architecture and the psychological findings. Use it for how the essays are built and for Nietzsche's own compression of their results; do not let it displace the Prologue as the statement of method.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] "Genealogy" in this book is not yet Foucault's "history of the present." Nietzsche wants a true history of "really living morality" in order to *revalue* — the "rank order of values" (I, Note). Foucault uses the method to denaturalize without that aristocratic telos.
- > [!warning] Johnston writes **gray** and **resentment**; Samuel writes **grey** and keeps French *ressentiment*. Cite the translation. The vault page titles keep Samuel's *ressentiment*.
