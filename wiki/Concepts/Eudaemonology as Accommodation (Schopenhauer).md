# Eudaemonology as Accommodation (Schopenhauer)
> An art of living as pleasantly as possible, written after the metaphysics has already judged that we do not exist to be happy. The name is a euphemism.

## Summary

The *Aphorisms on the wisdom of life* close Volume 1. They are the most popular piece in *Parerga*, and they are an **accommodation**. Eudaemonology is an art of living “as pleasantly and happily as possible,” not metaphysics. A happy existence is one “decidedly preferable to non-existence.” “Now, whether human life corresponds to such a concept of existence, or is even capable of doing so, is a question that my philosophy, as is well known, answers in the **negative**, whereas eudaemonology presupposes an affirmative answer.”

To write these aphorisms he “abandon[s] completely the higher, metaphysical–ethical standpoint.” The whole discussion “retains the ordinary, empirical standpoint and adheres to its error.” Value is “only … conditional”; “eudaemonology itself is merely a euphemism.” **Accommodation is not recantation.** The higher standpoint is left standing.

Chapter V then makes the euphemism explicit: the sensible person “aims at painlessness, not enjoyment.” Pleasure/happiness is **negative**; pain is **positive**. “Living happily” = living “less unhappily,” weathering life. “The surest way not to become very unhappy is not to want to become very happy.” Never buy pleasure at the price of pain. Judge a life by what saddens, not what pleases. Do not build happiness on a **broad foundation** of many demands.

The least-bad happiness is the **solitary intellectual life**. “Whoever does not love solitude, also does not love freedom; for only when we are alone, are we free.” Loneliness is the eminent mind’s “lesser of two evils.” Consciousness’s content decides weal/woe: purely intellectual occupation beats agitated real life (if one has the surplus intellect). Health first; then peace of mind in solitude. See [[Concepts/What One Is, Has, and Represents (Schopenhauer)]].

**Stages of life:** we are always only in the present; its hue changes. Childhood = mostly cognition, little will (“lost paradise”). Youth = hunt for happiness. Second half = dread of misfortune; rational characters seek freedom from pain. Time from youth looks infinitely long, from old age a very short past. “An existence whose true value can always only be determined by the absence of pain, not the presence of pleasures.”

He does **not** call his system “pessimism.” The negative verdict is: human life does not, and cannot, match an existence preferable to non-existence.

## Key Claims

- Eudaemonology presupposes the error *WWR* II Ch. XLIX names: “There is only one inborn error, and that is the notion that we exist in order to be happy.” The Aphorisms keep the error named as error. Ch. XVI of Volume 2 already called ancient ethics (except Plato) a species of *eudaemonism*.
- Pleasure is negative; pain is positive. Aim at painlessness, not enjoyment.
- What one *is* outweighs what one has and what one represents.
- Solitude is freedom; society is compulsion. The solitary intellectual life is the least-bad happiness.
- Age recolors the same present: youth-longing / age-dread.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — method: keep the *WWR* verdict, then write a deliberately lower, worldly ethic. Chamfort’s motto already locates happiness as “very difficult … in ourselves and impossible … elsewhere.”
- **[[Concepts/Eudaimonia]]** — uses Aristotle (activity, leisure, self-sufficiency) *inside* the accommodation. Does not adopt flourishing-as-telos. Do not file this as Book I of the *Nicomachean Ethics*.
- **[[Concepts/Ataraxia]]** / **[[Concepts/Contemplation and the Best Life - Theoria (Aristotle)]]** — tranquillity, seclusion, intellectual second life as counsel, not as metaphysical exit or as completion of human nature.
- **[[Contradictions/The Highest Good - Pleasure vs Virtue]]** — cheerfulness is called “highest good” *for a present-bound being* while the metaphysics has already denied that we exist to be happy. A third, conditional use: neither Epicurean pleasure-as-telos nor Stoic virtue-as-sole-good.
- **[[Concepts/Dichotomy of Control (Epictetus)]]**, **[[Concepts/The Present Instant (Hadot)]]**, **[[Concepts/Every Act as if It Were the Last (Marcus Aurelius)]]**, **[[Thinkers/Ryan Holiday]]** — **flag only**. Counsel 5: present alone is real; Seneca “Regard every single day as a single life.” Counsel 16: “abstain and endure.” Counsel 48: fate is the wind, effort the oars. Ground is negativity of pleasure + fatal necessity of character, not Stoic *eph’ hēmin* or Holiday’s “fuel.” Counsel 52 even retracts toward self-blame (“fate is mostly our stupid pranks”).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Accommodation ≠ recantation of the system. He abandons the higher standpoint *in order to develop* the doctrine; he does not say the higher standpoint was wrong.
- > [!warning] Do not weld Seneca’s day-as-life to Marcus’s last-act, or the present-alone counsel to Hadot’s present instant as spiritual exercise. Same borrowed maxims; different spine.
