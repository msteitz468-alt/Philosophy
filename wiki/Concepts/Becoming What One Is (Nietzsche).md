# Becoming What One Is (Nietzsche)

> *Ecce Homo*'s subtitle — "How One Becomes What One Is" — and its solution, which is the opposite of what every reader expects: "**The fact that one becomes what one is presupposes that one has not the remotest idea of what one is.**"

## Summary

The formula is Pindar's (*Pythian* II: become such as you are, having learned what that is), and Nietzsche had already used it in *The Gay Science* §270 — "You shall become the person you are." *Ecce Homo* "Why I am So Clever" §9 supplies the mechanism, and the mechanism is destructive of the humanistic reading the formula usually receives.

The expected account: there is a true self; introspection discovers it; one then lives in accordance with it. Nietzsche denies all three steps.

**Self-knowledge would be ruin.** Knowing what one is, early, would let the organizing idea be measured against a picture — and the picture would either shrink the task to what the young man can already imagine, or crush him with it. So the constitution protects itself by concealment: "Forgetting one's self, misunderstanding one's self, belittling one's self, narrowing one's self and making one's self mediocre is reason itself." The Delphic *nosce te ipsum*, which the whole Western tradition treats as the beginning of wisdom, is here reclassified as a hazard.

**The "blunders" are the method.** A life's wrong turns — the wrong discipline, the wrong friendships, the wrong reverences (in his own case philology, Basel, Wagner) — are not detours from the task but the organizing instinct's way of keeping its bearer occupied and undamaged while the real thing gestates underneath. This is why the chapter can spend nine sections on diet and reading and then claim it has been describing a philosophy the whole time: see [[Concepts/The Small Things - Nutrition Place Climate Recreation (Nietzsche)]].

**Consciousness is a surface.** The claim is continuous with GS §11 (consciousness as the last and least-developed organ) and with the whole vault's line on the unconscious. The organizing idea works below it; consciousness is where its effects are noticed, late.

**"Becoming" is not self-creation either.** It is worth being exact, because the two nearby readings both misfire. The formula is not "make yourself whatever you choose" (the existentialist reading) and not "discover the essence you already have" (the Aristotelian reading). It is: a task is at work in you that you do not know, and the work consists in not obstructing it. The verb is *become*; the object is *what one is*; and neither is under the control of the person doing it.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — GS §270 states it; *EH* "Clever" §9 explains it; the whole of *Ecce Homo* is the demonstration, since the book's structure (physiology, then books, then destiny) is an argument that the destiny was in the diet.
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]** — the nearest structural ancestor and the clearest contrast. On the Aristotelian account a thing has a *telos* it actualizes, and practical wisdom *knows* the end it aims at ([[Concepts/Eudaimonia]], [[Concepts/Potentiality and Actuality (Aristotle)]]). Nietzsche keeps the shape of actualization and removes both the fixed species-nature and the knowledge condition. The end is singular, not human; and it must be unknown to be reached.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]** — existence precedes essence, and [[Concepts/Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi) (Sartre)]] names precisely the flight into a fixed self. Sartre and Nietzsche agree there is no essence to discover, but Sartre makes the project transparent to a consciousness that chooses it, which is exactly what §9 says would abort it. Nietzsche's self-forgetting would look, from *Being and Nothingness*, uncomfortably like bad faith; from *Ecce Homo*, Sartrean lucidity looks like idealism.
- **[[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]** — the ancient exercises of self-examination aim at a transformation of the self through attention. Nietzsche's §9 is the counter-thesis: the transformation happens under inattention. See [[Concepts/Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Michel Foucault]]** — the late "care of the self" and aesthetics of existence take the Nietzschean side while restoring the practice, which is why Foucault reads Hadot against Hadot. See [[Thinkers/Michel Foucault]].
- **[[Concepts/Three Metamorphoses (Nietzsche)]]** — the *Zarathustra* version (camel → lion → child) narrates the same passage as three shapes of spirit. The child who is "a new beginning, a sport, a self-rolling wheel" is what §9 describes from the inside.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

> [!warning] Against [[Concepts/Sovereign Individual (Nietzsche)]]: the *Genealogy* II figure is defined by a "long unbreakable will" and a proud consciousness of his own power — a self that knows itself well enough to promise across time. §9's self is one that must *not* know itself. The two can be reconciled by making the sovereign individual the late outcome rather than the method, but Nietzsche does not perform the reconciliation, and the vault should not perform it for him.

> [!warning] The formula is self-undermining as advice. If becoming what one is requires not knowing what one is, then reading §9 and adopting it is the one route by which it cannot work. Nietzsche's book is addressed to readers who by his own account cannot yet hear it ("some are born posthumously") — see [[Concepts/Style as Communication of a State (Nietzsche)]] — which is one way of taking the problem seriously and no way of solving it.
