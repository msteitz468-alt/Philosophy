# Motor Intentionality and the Intentional Arc (Merleau-Ponty)
> "Consciousness is originarily not an 'I think that,' but rather an 'I can.'" Motricity is not the servant of a representing consciousness.

## Summary

Chapter III of Part One ("The Spatiality of One's Own Body and Motricity") is the book's longest argument. Between third-person movement and represented movement there is a **motor project** (*Bewegungsentwurf*), or **motor intentionality**. Instructions can have an *intellectual* signification and still lack a *motor* signification: they "do not speak to him as a motor subject."

**Schneider** (Gelb and Goldstein; occipital shrapnel, "psychic blindness") is the case, not a medical biography. Concrete, life-necessary movement is preserved "with extraordinary speed and confidence" (wallet-making, handkerchief, grasping a mosquito bite). Abstract movement — commanded pointing, virtual or fictional situations, eyes closed — collapses unless he sees the limb or rebuilds the gesture by preparatory whole-body movements. *Greifen* (grasping) is not *Zeigen* (pointing). In concrete movement "he is his body and his body is the power for a certain world." He can salute only by putting himself "within the affective situation of the whole"; interrupted, "all of his dexterity disappears." Objects have no *physiognomy*; he decodes them by language and traits. Intelligence as categorial operation is largely intact. What is hit is the existential power of placing oneself in a situation.

Neither a causal visual-deficit story nor an intellectualist "symbolic function" / categorial attitude is the fundamental disorder. Empiricism and intellectualism "level out behavior and efface the distinction between abstract movement and concrete movement." That distinction "can only be maintained if there are *several ways for the body to be a body, and several ways for consciousness to be consciousness*." Illness is a *structure* (*Fundierung*): vision founds the power of projection without causing it.

The unifying name is the **intentional arc**. "The life of consciousness… is underpinned by an 'intentional arc' that projects around us our past, our future, our human milieu, our physical situation, our ideological situation, and our moral situation… This intentional arc creates the unity of the senses, the unity of the senses with intelligence, and the unity of sensitivity and motricity. And this is what 'goes limp' in the disorder."

**I can.** "Motricity is thus not… a servant of consciousness." "A movement is learned when the body has understood it." "We never move our objective body, we move our phenomenal body." The body *inhabits* space and time: "I am not in space and in time, nor do I think space and time; rather, I am of space and of time." *Praktognosia* is this motor grasp of a motor signification. Habit is neither knowledge nor reflex: to habituate oneself to a hat, a car, a cane, a typewriter, or an organ is "to take up residence in them." The organist "settles into the organ as one settles into a house."

**What this text does not say.** "Maximum grasp" and "optimal grip" do not appear. Closest phrases: "the motor reactions that will establish between those forces the **optimum equilibrium**"; the normal subject immediately has several **"holds"** on his body; "The scope of this **hold** measures the scope of my existence." Dreyfus's "maximum grasp" is a reception name for this hold, not a quotation.

The same arc is recalled in the sexuality chapter: Schneider cannot put himself into an erotic situation; perception, motricity, representation, and sexual life rest on one arc that weakens.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Motor intentionality is originary, not a use of the *I think*.
- Concrete vs abstract movement is a split in being-in-the-world, not a missing module.
- The intentional arc is what goes limp; that is why several disorders arrive together.
- Habit dilates being-in-the-world by incorporating instruments.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: the positive account that replaces both mechanism and representation.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: this chapter is the source of skilled coping's motor detail — cane, organist, hold. He names the criterion "maximum grasp." See [[Concepts/Skilled Coping and the Five Stages of Expertise (Dreyfus)]], [[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]].
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: ready-to-hand is the ontological frame; MP supplies the motor and pathological grain.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: later tries to mechanize the *I can* as action-oriented prediction. Do not rewrite this page as PP.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Schneider interpretation is contested in later neurology (how much is a single "arc"?). The philosophical load is the *distinction* concrete/abstract, not the localization.
- Dreyfus allows a non-digital situated automaton. That concession is not in this chapter.
