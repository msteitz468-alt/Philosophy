# Truth as Worn-Out Metaphor (Nietzsche)
> Truths are illusions which we have forgotten are illusions — coins that have lost their embossing.

## Summary

The load-bearing formula of *On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense* (1873; Breazeale). Term-layer is this translation: a **movable host**, not Kaufmann’s later-famous “mobile army.”

The intellect’s first power is dissimulation. The *drive for truth* does not come from a wish to know the essence of things. It comes from a peace treaty. To live with the herd, man invents “a uniformly valid and binding designation” for things. “This legislation of language likewise establishes the first laws of truth.” The liar is the one who uses the valid words to make the unreal appear real. Society excludes him because of *harm*, not because of deception as such. “Even at this stage, what they hate is basically not deception itself, but rather the unpleasant, hated consequences of certain sorts of deception.” Man wants “the pleasant, life-preserving consequences of truth” and is hostile to truths that destroy.

A word is “the copy in sound of a nerve stimulus.” Two metaphors, each an overleaping of spheres: stimulus → image, image → sound. Languages side by side show that “with words it is never a question of truth, never a question of adequate expression.” The “thing in itself” (what “pure truth, apart from any of its consequences, would be”) is “not in the least worth striving for.”

Every concept “arises from the equation of unequal things.” No leaf is the same as another; “the leaf” is formed by arbitrarily discarding differences, which “awakens the idea that, in addition to the leaves, there exists in nature the ‘leaf.’” Honesty is a *qualitas occulta* assembled the same way. Nature “is acquainted with no forms and no concepts, and likewise with no species, but only with an X which remains inaccessible and undefinable for us.”

Then the definition:

> What then is truth? A movable host of metaphors, metonymies, and anthropomorphisms: in short, a sum of human relations which have been poetically and rhetorically intensified, transferred, and embellished, and which, after long usage, seem to a people to be fixed, canonical, and binding. Truths are illusions which we have forgotten are illusions— they are metaphors that have become worn out and have been drained of sensuous force, coins which have lost their embossing and are now considered as metal and no longer as coins.

To be truthful is “to employ the usual metaphors… to lie according to a fixed convention, to lie with the herd and in a manner binding upon everyone.” Forgetfulness and habit produce the *sense* of truth. The rational being volatilizes perceptual metaphors into schemata. The conceptual edifice has “the rigid regularity of a Roman columbarium.” The concept is “the *residue of a metaphor*.” Defining mammal and then finding a camel brings a truth of “limited value” — “a thoroughly anthropomorphic truth.” The investigator seeks “the metamorphosis of the world into man.”

Only by forgetting that he himself is an *artistically creating* subject does man live with “repose, security, and consistency.” “The correct perception” — adequate expression of an object in the subject — is “a contradictory impossibility.” Between two absolutely different spheres “there is no causality, no correctness, and no expression; there is, at most, an *aesthetic* relation.” Laws of nature impress us because we bring time, space, and number to them, “with the same necessity with which the spider spins.”

Do not import *perspectivism* (the name), will to power, or *décadence* into this 1873 claim.

## Key Claims

- Truth’s first origin is a social treaty, not a correspondence to things.
- Word ≠ thing: two metaphors (stimulus/image/sound).
- Concept = equation of unequals; nature knows only an X.
- Truth = forgotten metaphor; coin without embossing.
- To be truthful is to lie with the herd, by convention.
- “Correct perception” is impossible; at most an aesthetic relation.
- We know of natural law what we ourselves bring (time, space, number).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]] (1873)** — unpublished. Later published books develop the claim without citing this essay.
- **1878 [[Concepts/Historical Philosophising and the Chemistry of Concepts (Nietzsche)]].** HATH §2: “no eternal facts as there are no absolute truths.” §11: language taken as *aeternae veritates*; logic rests on assumptions to which nothing corresponds. Chemistry of feelings, not metaphor-genesis. Do not collapse 1873 into 1878.
- **1882/87 [[Sources/The Gay Science - Nietzsche (Kaufmann 1974)]].** §54: appearance is not the opposite of essence. §110: useful errors incorporated; truth late and weak. §121: “Life is no argument.” Kin, later, published.
- **1886 [[Concepts/Perspectivism (Nietzsche)]] / [[Concepts/Belief in Antitheses of Values (Nietzsche)]].** BGE names frog-perspectives and “untruth as a condition of life.” This 1873 essay does *not* name perspectivism. File it as the unpublished precursor of that epistemology, not as the doctrine under that name.
- **1888 [[Concepts/How the True World Became a Fable (Nietzsche)]].** Stage 5–6 abolish the true/apparent split. 1873 already refuses “appearance” as a tempting word and denies that essence “appears” in the empirical world. The fable is a late history; this is an early mechanism.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]].** Thing-in-itself “not in the least worth striving for”; time and space produced from ourselves as the spider spins. Reception of Kant, not a reading of the *Critique*.
- **[[Thinkers/William James]].** Later kin (truth as expedient, human twist). James is not discussing this Nachlass essay. See [[Concepts/Pragmatic Theory of Truth (James)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]].** Family resemblance also refuses a hidden “leaf.” Do not file PI as a reading of 1873.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Quote **movable host**, not “mobile army.” Kaufmann’s Portable title and wording are a different English.
- > [!warning] “Aesthetic relation” here is between subject and object as two foreign spheres. It is not yet [[Concepts/Aesthetic Justification of Existence (Nietzsche)]] (the world justified as artwork). Kin, not identity.
- > [!warning] The drive for truth is explained as convention + forgetfulness. The later problem — why *still* will truth after the Christian God dies ([[Concepts/Intellectual Conscience (Nietzsche)]], *Genealogy* III) — is not this essay.
