# Chance and Spontaneity (Aristotle)
> Chance (*tychē*) and spontaneity (*automaton*) are real, but they are **incidental** causes in the class of things that happen for the sake of something — neither always nor for the most part.

## Summary

*Physics* II.4–6 refuses three easy views: that there is no chance; that chance is a divine inscrutable; that the heavens themselves are a vortex of spontaneity (the atomists) while animals are by nature or mind. Aristotle: "we should have expected exactly the opposite" — if anything is orderly, it is the heavens.

**Spontaneity** (*automaton*) is the wider term. **Chance** (*tychē*) is spontaneity in the sphere of agents capable of *praxis* (deliberate action). "Every result of chance is from what is spontaneous, but not everything that is from what is spontaneous is from chance." A horse that happens to come to a place where it is saved acts spontaneously; a man who goes to the agora and happens to collect a debt acts by chance. Good and ill fortune are chance in the sphere of goods.

The definitional conditions: the event is among those that *could* have been for the sake of something; it is neither always nor for the most part; the cause is incidental, not *per se*. Chance is therefore posterior to intelligence and nature (the source-of-change mode of the [[Concepts/The Four Causes (Aristotle)|efficient cause]]). What is contrary to nature is a kind of *automaton* with an **internal** cause.

This is how Aristotle saves teleology without denying accidents. Empedocles' "man-faced ox-progeny" and the rain-for-the-crops objection belong here: some outcomes look endlike but are incidental. What comes about **always or normally** is not chance and is for an end ([[Concepts/Nature as Internal Principle of Motion (Aristotle)]]).

## Key Claims / Positions

- Chance and spontaneity exist as incidental causes.
- *Automaton* ⊃ *tychē*; chance requires agents who act for purposes.
- Both are posterior to *nous* and *physis*.
- "Always or for the most part" marks the natural; the rest can be chance.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Democritus]]**: vortex-cosmology treats the largest things as spontaneous. Aristotle inverts the expectation.
- **[[Thinkers/Empedocles]]**: survival of well-fitted parts is chance-plus-necessity; Aristotle says the regularity of teeth is too high for that.
- **[[Contradictions/Stoic Determinism vs Aristotelian Contingency]]**: chance here is the physical counterpart of logical accident — not an uncaused event, an incidental cause.
- **[[Thinkers/Hume]]**: constant conjunction dissolves *per se* vs incidental. Aristotle needs the distinction to keep teleology.
- **Modern evolutionary theory**: Empedocles' filter is the ancestor. Aristotle would still ask whether the *natures* that get filtered are already for-the-sake-of.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] If chance is only incidental teleology, is there any genuinely brute accident? Aristotle's ontology of the accidental is thin — see also *Metaphysics* VI on being *per accidens*.
