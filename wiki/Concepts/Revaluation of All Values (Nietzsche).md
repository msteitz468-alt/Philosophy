# Revaluation of All Values (Nietzsche)

> The phrase runs through the whole corpus; *Ecce Homo* is where Nietzsche says what it names, how his books divide around it, and — with dates — when he did it.

## What the Phrase Names

Not a new table of values. A **formula for an operation**: the exposure of the existing valuations as valuations, produced by determinate causes, serving determinate types. "Why I am a Destiny" §1:

> "**The Revaluation of all Values, this is my formula for mankind's greatest step towards coming to its senses** — a step which in me has become flesh and genius."

Three things follow from taking it as an operation rather than a doctrine.

**It is destructive by design.** In the *Beyond Good and Evil* self-review he calls it "the revaluation of all existing values, **the great war**," and says of every book after *Zarathustra*: "From that time onward all my writings are bait... If nothing was caught it was not I who was at fault. There were no fish." The later works are recruitment for a demolition, not the construction of a replacement.

**It has a Yes-half and a No-half.** This is the periodization *Ecce Homo* gives its own author, and it is the most useful single thing in the book for reading the corpus: "Now that the affirmative part of my life task was accomplished there came the turn of the negating part both in word and deed." *Zarathustra* is the affirmative half; *BGE*, the *Genealogy*, *Twilight*, *The Case of Wagner* are the negating half — "a criticism of modernity," "a school for gentlemen," "not one single good natured word" in the whole of *BGE*.

**It can itself be performed by the enemy.** §7 uses the phrase a second time and in the other direction: the decadents' own "revaluation of all values into a hostility towards life." Christianity was a revaluation before Nietzsche's was. His is a counter-move in a game already in progress — which is what makes it a *re*-valuation and not a legislation. See [[Concepts/Master Morality and Slave Morality (Nietzsche)]].

## The 1888 Chronology (as Nietzsche gives it)

The *Twilight of the Idols* self-review §3 supplies dates the vault should keep, because they anchor the whole final year:

- **Revaluation** begun immediately after finishing *Twilight*
- Preface of the Revaluation dated **3 September 1888**
- Left Sils Maria **20 September**
- Turin **21 September** — Via Carlo Alberto 6
- Work finished **30 September**, the same day as the *Twilight* Preface

"I cut sign after sign upon tablets of brass with the sureness of Fate."

> [!warning] **What this text does not say.** There is **no mention of a four-book plan, of parts or subtitles, and no identification of the Revaluation with *The Antichrist*** — verified across the whole file. The familiar editorial account (a projected four-part *Revaluation of All Values*, later abandoned, of which *The Antichrist* is Book I) is **reception**, not this source. The *Genealogy* self-review calls its three essays "Three decisive sketches of a psychologist for a Revaluation of all Values," i.e. preparatory; the *Case of Wagner* review speaks of "the destructive thunderbolt of the Revaluation which will send the whole of civilization into convulsions" as still to come. Take the September dates as referring to *a* completed manuscript; the text does not name it. Compare [[Sources/The Antichrist - Nietzsche (Mencken, Collection 2013)]].

Also verified absent from this file: any mention of a *Will to Power* book project. "Will to power" occurs twice in the whole of *Ecce Homo* as extracted, both times in passing and neither as doctrine — once as a jab at German appetite ("gulp down 'Faith' along with science, Christian love together with anti-Semitism and the will to power (to the 'Empire')") and once parenthetically at §4 of the last chapter. Anyone citing *Ecce Homo* for the will to power is citing the wrong book. See [[Concepts/Will to Power (Nietzsche)]].

## How This Sits in the Vault

- **[[Concepts/The Immoralist (Nietzsche)]]** — the revaluation is the operation; the immoralist is the one performing it. §3's self-overcoming of morality through truthfulness is the argument that the operation was inevitable.
- **[[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]]** — the method that makes the revaluation possible. The *Genealogy* self-review gives Nietzsche's own account of his rhetorical technique, which is rare: each essay begins "cool, scientific, even ironic, intentionally superficial and intentionally reticent," rises in tempo, and then "amid fearful thunderclaps a new truth shines out from between heavy clouds."
- **[[Concepts/Belief in Antitheses of Values (Nietzsche)]]** — the *BGE* prejudice the revaluation is aimed at.
- **[[Thinkers/Arthur O. Lovejoy]] and [[Thinkers/Quentin Skinner]]** — worth noting for the History of Ideas leg: "revaluation of all values" is precisely the sort of phrase Lovejoy would track as a unit-idea across centuries and Skinner would insist can only be understood as a move in an argument Nietzsche was making against particular contemporaries in 1888. The dated chronology above is the Skinnerian material; the phrase's later migration into a hundred contexts is the Lovejovian. See [[Concepts/Mythologies in the History of Ideas (Skinner)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]]** — the revaluation is the strongest vault instance of what Berlin fears in the Romantic will: a single creator legislating values for a civilization. Berlin's [[Concepts/Value Pluralism (Berlin)]] agrees with Nietzsche that values are made rather than found, and parts from him on whether one maker's table can be ranked over another's.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

> [!warning] The revaluation is announced everywhere and executed nowhere. Every text in the vault that invokes it is preparatory (*Genealogy*: "sketches... for a Revaluation"), destructive (*BGE*: "the great war"), or prospective (*Case of Wagner*: the thunderbolt still to come). No source in the vault contains a positive table of the new values. This may be principled — a legislation would be the very thing [[Concepts/Becoming What One Is (Nietzsche)]] says cannot be planned in advance — or it may be that the collapse of January 1889 arrived first. The vault should not resolve this by supplying the missing content from *The Will to Power* compilation, which is an editorial artefact of the sister's Nachlass and is not in the vault as a source.
