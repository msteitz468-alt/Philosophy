# Xiao — Filiality (Confucius)

> In Annping Chin's *Analects*, *xiao* is **filiality** — not "filial piety" as a religious slogan. Youzi makes it the **root of humaneness** (1.2). It is respect and the difficult facial expression, not feeding (2.7–2.8). It includes covering for a father (13.18) and three years' mourning because a child does not leave the parents' arms until three (17.21).

## Load-bearing claims in the text

- **Root (1.2).** "It is rare for a person who is filial to his parents and respectful to his elders to be inclined to transgress against his superiors." Roots first; "a moral way will grow." Chin via Huang Kan: the filial son *will* admonish parents, but without liking the transgression — not blind submission. Consistent with 4.18.
- **Three years' ways (1.11).** Observe what the father would like while he lives; reflect on what he has done after he dies. "If for three years you refrained from altering your father's ways, you can be called filial." Chin: observe and reflect, not blindly carry out; even if the father was imperfect, the three years stand (Wang Zhong: otherwise the son could change the father's ways the evening of the death).
- **Rites, not chores (2.5–2.8).** Serve / bury / sacrifice according to the rites (2.5). Give parents no worry except illness (2.6). "Nowadays 'filial' means being able to feed your parents. But even dogs and horses are given food. If you are not respectful, how are you different?" (2.7 — Chin/most Chinese: dogs and horses *provide*; Zhu Xi / most English: we *feed* them). "The difficult part is the facial expression" (2.8).
- **Admonish without transgression (4.18–4.21).** Remonstrate gently; if not followed, remain respectful and do not resent. Age of parents should be known — both as joy and as anxiety.
- **Covering up (13.18).** The Governor of She boasts of Upright Gong, who bore witness against a father who stole a sheep. "Where I came from, those who are considered upright are different from this man. Fathers cover up for their sons, and sons cover up for their fathers. Being upright lies therein." Chin via Cheng Yaotian: neither cheap public-spirited informing nor "family always wins" (then it would not be a moral problem). Use the fullness of private affections to realize what is good for the public.
- **Three-year mourning (17.21).** Zai Wo: a year is already too long; three years ruins the (nonmourning) rites and music. "Yu lacks humaneness! A child does not leave his parents' arms until he is three." The rite is grounded in that first care, not in a cosmic year-cycle.
- **Zengzi (1.9, 19.17).** Tend death rites and do not forget sacrifice to the long-departed; people gain in the cultivation of virtue. Only in mourning for parents is a man able to realize himself to the full.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. *Daodejing* chs. 18–19**: filial piety appears when the great course is abandoned, among the decay-markers to be cut off. Confucius: it is the *root* from which *ren* grows. See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Formula of Humanity as End in Itself (Kant)]]**: 13.18 privileges the father–son relation over public law. Kantian public right cannot make covering for theft a duty. Different architecture: names of father and son must hold (12.11) before the magistrate's "uprightness."
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Friendship - Philia (Aristotle)]]**: Aristotle ranks complete friendship of virtue above family utility. Confucius puts family rites at the root and friendship later (trustworthy in words, 1.4, 12.23–24).
- **Vs. Plato's abolition of the private family** in the *Republic*'s guardian class: Confucius would read that as cutting the root Youzi names.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] 13.18 vs public law: the vault should not dissolve this into "Confucians oppose justice." Chin keeps the tussle as a moral problem.
- 1.11 three years vs a bad father: the text asks for delay and reflection, not perpetual imitation.
- 2.7 translation fork (dogs and horses provide vs we feed them) changes the sting but not the claim: feeding without respect is not *xiao*.
