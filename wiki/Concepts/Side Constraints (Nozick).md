# Side Constraints (Nozick)
> Rights are constraints on action, not goals to be maximized. “Don’t violate *C*” is not “minimize violations of *C*.”

## Summary

Chapter 3 of *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* distinguishes two forms a moral view can take.

A **goal-directed** view builds rights into the end-state to be promoted. The result is a **utilitarianism of rights**: minimize (weighted) rights-violations, even by violating someone — punish the innocent to stop a mob. “The view whose objective is to minimize the violation of these rights allows you to violate the rights… in order to lessen their total violation in the society.”

**Side constraints** forbid you to violate the constraints *in the pursuit of* your goals. Others’ rights are not built into *G*; they bound what you may do to get *G*. The ultraminimalist is then consistent: forcing you to fund another’s protection **violates** your rights, while another’s failing to supply the protection you need **does not**. Consistency is not yet acceptability.

The form is Kantian. “Side constraints upon action reflect the underlying Kantian principle that individuals are ends and not merely means; they may not be sacrificed or used for the achieving of other ends without their consent. Individuals are inviolable.” Political philosophy, Nozick says, cares mainly about **physical aggression**.

The content is argued from **separateness of persons**. “There is no *social entity* with a good that undergoes some sacrifice for its own good. There are only individual people… Using one of these people for the benefit of others, uses him and benefits the others. Nothing more… he is a separate person… *He* does not get some overbalancing good from his sacrifice.” That separateness both explains the *form* of constraints and (he claims) yields **libertarian content**: no sacrificing one for another; nonaggression. A further step is needed for anti-paternalism: each has a life **to lead**.

He does **not** ground rights in this book. Traditional traits (sentience, rationality, free will, moral agency, soul) are insufficient. He conjectures an intervening variable *M* ≈ shaping a life by an overall conception → **meaning of life**, then leaves the is/ought questions “for another occasion.” Moral theory is underdetermined: an elitist hierarchy and absolute side constraints fit all actual cases and split only on superbeings.

Nonaggression does not bar defense against an **innocent threat**; **innocent shields** stay unresolved. Animals “count for something”; “utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people” is floated and then rejected as the whole animal story.

The **individualist anarchist** uses this apparatus against the state: monopoly plus forced funding of others’ protection violate side constraints, so the state is “intrinsically immoral.” Parts I–II are the reply.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** / [[Concepts/Formula of Humanity as End in Itself (Kant)]]: the political reading of FH. “Don’t use people in specified ways,” mainly aggression; not a full ethics of using. Dignity as inviolability, not as a value to maximize. See also [[Concepts/Dignity of Rational Nature (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]** / [[Concepts/Greatest Happiness Principle (Mill)]]: GHP is the rejected end-state form. The [[Concepts/Harm Principle (Mill)|harm principle]] is closer in *content* (nonaggression, anti-paternalism) than in *form* (deontic *C*, not utility).
- **[[Contradictions/Utilitarian Consequentialism vs. Kantian Deontology]]**: “utilitarianism of rights” shows that putting rights in the *goal* is still utilitarian in form. Kantian form is non-aggregative.
- **Rawls**: the difference principle, Nozick will argue in Ch. 7, “does not take seriously the distinction between persons” — the same Kantian charge Rawls had made against utilitarianism. See [[Contradictions/Nozick vs Rawls - Historical Entitlement vs Patterned Justice]].
- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]**: the law of nature already functions as a side constraint (“no one ought to harm another in his life, health, liberty, or possessions”). Nozick postpones the grounding Locke also postponed.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Side constraints vs any aggregative goal — including a utilitarianism *of rights* — is the political form of [[Contradictions/Utilitarian Consequentialism vs. Kantian Deontology]].
- The book does not supply the moral basis of the constraints it spends 300 pages applying. Underdetermination (status-1 vs status-2 persons) is left live.
