# How the ‘True World’ Finally Became a Fable (Nietzsche)
> Six stages from "I, Plato, am the truth" to noon: abolish the true world and the apparent one goes with it. *INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA.*

## Summary

The shortest chapter of *Twilight of the Idols* (1888) is the late schematic history of [[Concepts/Belief in Antitheses of Values (Nietzsche)]]. Subtitle: *The History of an Error.* Kaufmann's heading: "How the ‘True World’ Finally Became a Fable."

It is not an independent argument. It condenses the preceding chapter, "‘Reason’ in Philosophy," whose four propositions are the engine:

1. The reasons this world was called "apparent" are the reasons it is **real**; any other reality is indemonstrable.
2. The criteria of "true being" are criteria of **not-being**. The "true world" is constructed out of contradiction to the actual world — a "moral-optical illusion."
3. Fables of another world have "no meaning at all" unless "an instinct of slander… against life has gained the upper hand." Then we avenge ourselves with a phantasmagoria of a "better" life.
4. Any true/apparent split — "whether in the Christian manner or in the manner of Kant (in the end, an underhanded Christian)" — is "only a suggestion of decadence." The tragic artist who esteems appearance is not a counterexample: "appearance" here is reality again, "by way of selection, reinforcement, and correction." He is **Dionysian**.

The six-stage history then *dates* that error:

1. **Plato.** "The true world — attainable for the sage, the pious, the virtuous man; he lives in it, he is it." Relatively sensible. "A circumlocution for the sentence, ‘I, Plato, am the truth.’"
2. **Christian.** "Unattainable for now, but promised… (‘for the sinner who repents’)." More subtle, "it becomes female, it becomes Christian."
3. **Kant.** "Unattainable, indemonstrable, unpromisable; but the very thought of it — a consolation, an obligation, an imperative." "The old sun, but seen through mist and skepticism… pale, Nordic, Königsbergian."
4. **Positivism.** Unattained, therefore unknown, therefore "not consoling, redeeming, or obligating." "Gray morning… The cockcrow of positivism."
5. **Free spirits.** The idea is "useless and superfluous — consequently, a refuted idea: let us abolish it!" "Bright day; breakfast… Plato’s embarrassed blush; pandemonium of all free spirits."
6. **Noon.** "The true world — we have abolished. What world has remained? The apparent one perhaps? But no! With the true world we have also abolished the apparent one." "Noon; moment of the briefest shadow; end of the longest error; high point of humanity; **INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA.**"

Stage 6 is the load-bearing twist. Abolishing the "true" world does **not** leave a remainder called the "apparent" world. The antithesis itself was the error. See [[Sources/Thus Spoke Zarathustra - Nietzsche (Common 1909)]] — the same incipit as *Gay Science* §342.

**1873 already refuses the tempting word.** *On Truth and Lies* (unpublished): “Appearance” is avoided; “it is not true that the essence of things ‘appears’ in the empirical world”; “correct perception” is a contradictory impossibility. That is a mechanism, not this six-stage history. Do not back-date the fable. See [[Sources/On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense - Nietzsche (Breazeale, Collection 2013)]], [[Concepts/Truth as Worn-Out Metaphor (Nietzsche)]].

## Key Claims

- The "true world" is a *history*, not a discovery: Plato → church → Königsberg → positivism → free-spirit breakfast → noon.
- Kant is not a break with Christianity. He is the old sun "through mist."
- Stage 5 (marketplace abolition) is not the end. Stage 6 abolishes the leftover "apparent" as well.
- Reason chapter: "The ‘apparent’ world is the only one: the ‘true’ world is merely added by a lie." Heraclitus "will remain eternally right" that being is an empty fiction. The senses do not lie; "‘Reason’ is the reason we falsify the testimony of the senses."
- "I am afraid we are not rid of God because we still have faith in grammar."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — 1888 condensation of the BGE prejudice and the GS death-of-God sequence. Stage 5 is the free-spirit cheerfulness of GS §343; stage 6 is the task the madman said had not yet reached men's ears. Notebooks **n.12** give the three psychological forms (aim / unity / true world pulled out); **n.567** reduces the antithesis to "world" and "nothing." Workshop, not this chapter. See [[Concepts/European Nihilism (Nietzsche)]], [[Concepts/Will to Power as Knowledge (Nietzsche)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]** — stage 1, named in the first person. Ancients ch. 2: "Plato is a coward before reality, consequently he flees into the ideal."
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — stage 3; Reason 6: "underhanded Christian." GS already had §193 "Kant's joke" and §335 "long live physics!" This is the *metaphysical* placement: Königsberg as mist, not a Copernican break.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]** — reads the death of God and revaluation as the end of the metaphysics of presence. This chapter is the *table* of that ending. Do not import *Kehre* into the six stages.
- **[[Concepts/Phenomena and Noumena (Kant)]]** — the true/apparent pair Nietzsche is abolishing. He does not argue Kant's Deduction; he files the pair as decadence.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Stage 6 is not GS §125. The madman tells *unbelievers* they have come too early. This chapter's free spirits (stage 5) have breakfast and abolish the idea; noon is a further step. Do not collapse them.
- > [!warning] *INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA* is a title-pointer, not a proof of [[Concepts/Eternal Recurrence (Nietzsche)]]. Recurrence is not argued in this chapter.
