# Faith in Morals (Nietzsche)
> The old faith philosophers built on as the safest foundation. *Dawn* tunnels under it and treats morality as a *problem*.

## Summary

The 1886 Preface to *The Dawn of Day* names the book's task after the fact. For thousands of years philosophers treated *faith in morals* as the safest foundation and built again after every collapse. Nietzsche "began to undermine our *faith in morals*." To look upon morality as a problem looks *immoral* — because morality, like all authority, has forbidden the question. Its security is less intimidation (conscience, reputation, hell, the police) than enchantment: she "enraptures," paralyses or seduces the critical will, even turns it on itself. She is the *Circe of philosophers*.

Kant's answer — the buildings fell because no one had first critiqued reason — is the wrong diagnosis. From Plato on, the architects (Kant included) aimed at certainty and "truth" only in appearance; they were building "*majestic moral edifices*" (*Critique of Pure Reason*, ii. 257). Kant added an indemonstrable "beyond" to make his moral kingdom invisible to reason. He believed in morality *despite* the immorality of nature and history. Confidence in reason is itself a *moral* phenomenon.

The book's remaining "thou shalt" is not a new table. Confidence in morals is retracted *out of morality*: we will not return to what is unworthy of belief (God, virtue, truth, justice, neighbour-love). Formula: ***the autosuppression of morals***. "We immoralists and atheists" are late heirs of German uprightness. See [[Concepts/The Immoralist (Nietzsche)]].

The 1881 books do the mining the Preface names. They do **not** repeat the slogan "faith in morals." Load-bearing 1881 moments:

- **§103.** Two denials of morality: (1) La Rochefoucauld — moral motives are words; (2) Nietzsche — judgments *do* motive, but rest on *errors*. "I deny morality in the same way as I deny alchemy, *i.e.* I deny its hypotheses." Also deny *immorality* as a true reason to feel immoral. "We must learn anew… feel anew."
- **§148.** "When men no longer believe themselves to be evil, they cease to be so."
- **§164.** "There is no exclusively moral-making morality."
- **§208.** "We no longer wish causes to be sinners and effects to be executioners."
- **§453.** Moral interregnum: feelings rest on error and cannot be repaired; physiology and solitude can supply foundation-stones, not the ideals. "We are experiments: if we want to be so!"

Do not import death of God, will to power, *amor fati*, or eternal recurrence. They are not in this book.

## Key Claims

- Faith in morals is the philosophers' unexamined foundation, not a conclusion.
- Treating morality as a problem is forbidden by morality itself.
- Kant's critique of reason is a detour that *protects* the moral kingdom.
- Autosuppression: morals turn against themselves out of a remaining conscience.
- 1881 method: deny the *hypotheses* (alchemy), not merely the hypocrisy of motives.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — 1881 does the work; 1886 names it; 1888 *Ecce Homo* claims "immoralist" as a title with two denials. This page is the 1881/86 object those later titles presuppose.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — Circe's most distinguished victim in the Preface; §197 "make way again for belief by fixing the limits of knowledge"; §207 Categorical Imperative as a German route to *obedience to a person*; §339 duty-must-stay-burdensome as residual ascetic cruelty. See [[Thinkers/Kant]], [[Concepts/Categorical Imperative - Universal Law Formulation (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]** — first of the vain philosophic architects (Preface §3).
- **[[Concepts/Revaluation of All Values (Nietzsche)]]** — 1888 operation. Dawn is the undermining, not the revaluation. Do not weld.
- **[[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]]** — 1887 asks the *value* of values. Dawn asks whether the *hypotheses* of morals are errors. Same campaign, earlier tool.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] **Autosuppression still needs a remaining "thou shalt."** The Preface retracts confidence in morals *out of* morality. Truthfulness must survive the collapse it causes — the same live tension later flagged on [[Concepts/The Immoralist (Nietzsche)]].
- > [!warning] **Do not cite Dawn for the death of God.** §95–96 historically dissolve God-belief; the 1882 parable is [[Concepts/Death of God (Nietzsche)]].
