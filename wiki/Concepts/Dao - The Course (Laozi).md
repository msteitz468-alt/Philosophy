# Dao — The Course (Laozi)

> In Ziporyn's *Daodejing*, *dao* is translated **course**: originally a road, then a purposive "way to get X done," here ironically reversed into nonpurposive process — and *thereby* the real source of values and existences. "Any course can be taken as the right course to take, but no course like that can be the course taken always" (ch. 1).

## The ironic reversal

Ziporyn's Note on the Translation: the earliest concrete meaning of *dao* is a physical road. By extension it is a course of action designed to reach a predetermined end — "almost a word for *purposive activity as such*." The *Daodejing*'s "world-historical gambit" is to reverse this so that *dao* means precisely nondoing. English "course" is chosen because it covers a prescriptive course of study, a value-neutral series of events, the stuff that runs through a series, and the running itself.

The new sense is "a course that is precisely not a 'course' in the older sense": purposeless process that takes no single definite form — and yet, by not being that older course, fulfills what the older course claimed to be.

## Load-bearing claims in the text

- **Nameless / named (ch. 1).** No course taken as *the* right course, and no name that determines what is or should be, can be constant. Nameless: "any beginning of the ten thousand things." Named: "the mother of the ten thousand things." Desirelessness views the mystery; desire views what each "strives and cries for." The two "emerge together as one."
- **Abyss and source (ch. 4).** The course is a gushing/jumbling/emptying, "the very source of all the ten thousand things," blunting edges, merging with dusts, "an image of what precedes even the highest deity."
- **Muddle before heaven and earth (ch. 25).** "Something formed through undivided muddle, there since before the birth of heaven and earth… such may be considered the mother of the world. Not knowing its name, we nickname it 'the course.' But if forced… 'the vastness.'" Vastness means passing on → distancing → returning. Four vastnesses: course, sky, earth, a ruler's sovereignty. Humans conform to earth, earth to sky, sky to the course; **the course conforms to how everything is when left to itself**.
- **Nameless unhewn (chs. 32, 37).** "The constancy of the course lies in its namelessness — its being indeterminable, unhewn." Carving produces names. "The constancy of the course lies in doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone."
- **Overflow without lordship (ch. 34).** "The vast course, how it overflows all banks… All things depend on it for birth… It clothes and feeds the ten thousand things, yet never plays their lord and master."
- **Generation (ch. 42).** "The course generates continuities. Continuities generate polarities. Polarities generate triplicities. Triplicities generate the ten thousand things."
- **Reversal (ch. 40).** "Reversal, opposition: the activity of the course. Weakness, fragility: the functioning of the course. All things in the world are born from what is there, but what is there is born from what is not there."
- **Hides in namelessness (ch. 41).** "The course's clarity looks like darkness. Its advance looks like retreat." "The course hides in nameless indeterminacy" and completes by completing things. What causes no laughter is not the course.
- **Heaven vs. man (ch. 77).** Heaven's course is like stretching a bow: high brought low, low raised, take from excess, add to lack. "The course of man is just the contrary."
- **Benefit without harm (ch. 81).** "To benefit some one thing yet without harming any other — that is the course of heaven. And to take action for some one purpose yet without striving against any other — that is the course of the sage."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. [[Thinkers/Sun Tzu]]'s *tao***: Sun Tzu's first of the five factors is the "Moral Law" (*tao*) — harmony of people with ruler, a *strategic* asset. Giles already noted the equivocation. Laozi's course is not morale and not a calculable factor; it is what cannot be taken as the right course.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Heraclitean Flux and Logos (Heraclitus)]]**: Heraclitus's Logos is the everlasting rational formula of change ("though this Logos is true from everlasting…"). Laozi's course is not a formula one can state; stating it as *the* course already loses it (ch. 1). Both are process-first; only Heraclitus identifies the rule with fire and strife.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/The One (To Hen)]] / [[Schools/Neoplatonism]]**: the One is beyond Being and known by negation, but it stands at the top of a procession. The course is not a supreme term; it is the ignored background and the mother, "never playing lord."
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Negative Theology and Divine Attributes (Maimonides)]]**: both unsay the named highest. Maimonides' terminus is reverent silence before a simple God. Laozi's nameless is *generative* — a bellows that is empty but never exhausted (ch. 5), a mother one feeds at (ch. 20).
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Deus sive Natura - Substance Monism (Spinoza)]]**: both immanentize the highest. Spinoza's substance has infinite attributes and is known geometrically. Laozi's course is not a substance and not known; it is nicknamed, then left to itself.
- **Vs. Confucian *dao*** (now primary, Chin "Way / moral way"): in the *Analects* the *dao* is the moral way of office and kinship — what a gentleman does not abandon, what a state has or lacks (8.13, 18.6–18.7). Chs. 18–19 and 38 treat humankindness and duty as what appears when *this* (nonpurposive) course is lost. Same graph; Chin's "Way" is the older purposive/moral sense Ziporyn says the *Daodejing* ironically reverses. See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The course "conforms to how everything is when left to itself" (ch. 25) can be read as nature-as-norm *or* as the refusal of any norm. Ziporyn's A/B frame chooses the second: B is not a new A.
- Whether *ziran* ("of its own accord," "left to itself") is a separate metaphysical principle or just the course's way of showing up is not decided by this translation. It is folded here and on [[Concepts/Wu Wei - Nondoing (Laozi)]].
