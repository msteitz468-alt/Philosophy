# Man's Need for Metaphysics (Schopenhauer)
> Man is the *animal metaphysicum*. Wonder, death, and misery generate a need that priests farm and philosophy is obliged to meet *sensu stricto*.

## Summary

Chapter XVII of *WWR* Volume 2 is the vault’s primary. If life were “without end and free from pain,” no one would ask why the world exists. The interest of philosophical *and* religious systems has its “strongest and essential point absolutely in the dogma of some future existence after death.”

Two metaphysics answer the need:

1. **Philosophy** — credentials *in itself*; obliged to be true *sensu stricto et proprio*.
2. **Religion** — credentials *outside* (revelation, tradition, childhood imprint); obliged only to be true *sensu allegorico*. “Before the people truth cannot appear naked.”

“In all nations there are monopolists and farmers-general of it, namely the priests,” who must get their dogmas in “before the power of judgement has been roused from its morning slumber.” Religions are “necessary for the people, and are an inestimable benefit to them.” If they oppose the progress of knowledge, “they must be pushed on one side.”

Physics cannot stand without metaphysics. Metaphysics interprets inner and outer experience and remains **immanent**; it does not jump to an extramundane cause. Chair-philosophy that lives *by* the need rather than *for* it is already diagnosed in [[Concepts/University Philosophy (Schopenhauer)]].

This chapter is the systematic source for a cut *Parerga* later popularizes (religion as allegory). Do not ingest *Parerga* II “On Religion” as if it invented the cut.

## Key Claims

- The metaphysical need is peculiar to man; it is not idle curiosity.
- Death and misery, not leisure, are its engine.
- Philosophy and religion share the need and split on the kind of truth they owe.
- Priests monopolize the need by early instruction.
- Metaphysics is immanent interpretation of experience, not a first-cause science.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — Ch. XVII licenses both his hostility to pulpit-philosophy and his respect for the great allegorical religions (Christianity, Brahmanism, Buddhism) later in Book IV.
- **[[Concepts/University Philosophy (Schopenhauer)]]** — the institutional capture of the same need: live *by* philosophy vs live *for* it.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — practical theology as “sop” is the *Parerga* I verdict; here the contrast is *sensu stricto* vs *sensu allegorico*, not moral faith as such.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — *Gay Science* §151: the “metaphysical need” is not the origin of religion. Reception; do not import into 1844.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] He does not call the need “pessimism.” The word is not the load-bearing term of this chapter.
- > [!warning] Do not weld this to [[Concepts/Amor Fati (Love of Fate)]]. The phrase is absent.
