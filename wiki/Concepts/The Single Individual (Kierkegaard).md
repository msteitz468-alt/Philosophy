# The Single Individual (Kierkegaard)
> The particular who, having been in the universal, stands in an absolute relation to the absolute — and therefore cannot speak.

## Summary

Hannay translates *den Enkelte* as "the single individual" (sometimes "particular"), not merely "the individual," because Johannes needs the contrast with *Individet* in contexts where constitution in the universal is not the issue. *Det Almene* is "the universal": not logical generality but the concrete whole (society, nation) Hegel calls *Sittlichkeit*.

The formula, repeated through the three *problemata*: faith is the paradox that **the single individual as the particular is higher than the universal**, justified before it not as subordinate but as superior — though only after having been subordinate to it, and now by means of it standing in an absolute relation to the absolute. The position cannot be mediated. Partnership is unthinkable. The one knight cannot help the other. "Either the single individual becomes a knight of faith himself by putting on the paradox, or he never becomes one."

**Absolute duty to God (Problema II).** If duty is duty to God only by being referred to the universal, one has no duty *to* God. God becomes "an invisible, vanishing point." Faith reverses the order: the individual determines his relation to the universal through his relation to the absolute, not the reverse. The ethical is not done away with; it gets a paradoxical expression. Love of God can cause the knight to give love of neighbour the opposite expression to ethical duty. Luke 14.26 ("hate father and mother… and his own life") is to be taken in "as terrifying a sense as possible," not haggled into *minus diligo*. Abraham must love Isaac *more*, not less; only then is the act a sacrifice. Cain and Abraham are not the same. If the Church demanded the sacrifice, one would have a tragic hero (the Church is qualitatively the same as the State: one enters by common mediation).

Hegel makes the outer higher than the inner. Faith is the paradox that **interiority is higher than exteriority** — a *new* interiority, not the child's first immediacy. Philosophy has substituted the immediate for faith; then of course faith has existed in all ages and one should not stop at it. Socrates' ignorance is already past that cheap faith: it is infinite resignation.

**Concealment (Problema III).** The ethical is the disclosed. The individual's task is to unwrap himself from concealment. Hegel allows no justified incommensurability. Faith is not the first immediacy (the aesthetic) but a later one. Abraham's silence is not aesthetic (he is not saving Isaac by it) and not tragic (he does nothing for the universal). He *cannot* speak: if when I speak I cannot make myself understood, I do not speak. "He speaks a divine tongue — he 'speaks with tongues.'" The preserved word — "God will provide himself a lamb" — is irony, saying something and yet not saying it, and it carries both movements. No poet can reach Abraham.

**Criteria even someone not in the paradox can understand.** The true knight is absolute isolation; the false knight is sectarian (a private theatre of companions who stand in for the universal). The knight is a **witness, never a teacher**. Distress and anguish are the only justification conceivable, and they cannot be conceived in general or the paradox is cancelled. Whether the individual is in temptation or is a knight, only the individual can decide. He can turn back in repentance to the universal at any moment; that possibility can be temptation or truth; enlightenment as to which he can get from no one.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Existing as the individual is the most terrifying thing, and therefore also the greatest. People who think it is easy are not knights.
- The knight knows it is glorious to belong to the universal and can wish that were the task. Those wishes are temptations.
- One who wants only to be a witness confesses that no one needs another person's sympathy in order to walk the path.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Hadot / [[Thinkers/Socrates]]**: the existing thinker, the Individual, not-Christian ≈ not-sage. This page is that figure from inside one aesthetic work.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: existence precedes essence; choosing for oneself chooses for all. Johannes's single individual does *not* legislate for mankind. Sartre's universalizability-without-God is closer to the ethical Johannes suspends.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: *das Man* and leveling already name Kierkegaard's "public" as ancestor. This book is the isolation-side, not the public-side (*A Literary Review* / *The Present Age* is the hole).
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]]**: the individual realizes himself by being taken up in the universal. Abraham acts as though someone could be properly human *prior* to expression in the universal. See [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Hegel - Faith vs Sittlichkeit]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Hannay: what Johannes charts from outside is the view that someone can qualify as essentially human independently of any general specification of the human. That would be unthinkable to Hegelians. Johannes himself still writes as if the ethical-as-universal were the only going principle.
- *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* is named as the place the Christian concept of faith (not Abraham's) is defined. Outstanding.
