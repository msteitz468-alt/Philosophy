# Ecstasy of Divine Love (Dionysius)

> The Super-Essential Goodness does not remain in itself. By "excess of Goodness" the Creator "is transported outside of Himself" in providential activity, "whereby He yet stays within Himself" (DN IV.13). Creation is that ecstasy; the soul's return is the answering one.

## Summary

**Good is the first name.** The Sacred Writers call the Super-Divine Godhead "Goodness" to mark that the Good, under the form of Good-Being, "extends Its goodness by the very fact of Its existence unto all things" (DN IV.1). The sun is the faded image: it lights all that can receive it "through no choice or deliberation, but by the very fact of its existence" (DN IV.1). Angels, human souls, irrational souls, plants, and "whatsoever has no life or soul" exist *because the Good exists* (DN IV.2). Even Not-Being "desires" the Good (DN IV.3). Failure to share the light is unreceptiveness, not a defect of the source (DN IV.4).

**Beautiful = Good.** "The Beautiful is the same as the Good, inasmuch as all things, in all causation, desire the Beautiful and Good" (DN IV.7). The Super-Essential Beautiful is Cause, Attractive, Beginning, Goal, Beloved, Exemplar.

**Yearning and plenitude.** Inferiors yearn upward, peers commune, superiors provide. "Even the Creator of all things Himself yearneth after all things… through nothing but excess of Goodness." The Yearning "which createth all the goodness of the world… allowed Him not to remain unfruitful in Himself, but moved Him to exert the abundance of His powers in the production of the universe" (DN IV.10). This is Lovejoy's Dionysian line (the Latin the vault already cites as *non permisit manere Deum in seipso sine germine*).

***Erōs* and *agapē*.** Dionysius treats "Love" and "Yearning" as one meaning; *erōs* is Scriptural (Proverbs, Wisdom, Ignatius) and is sometimes called "real" to block carnal misreading (DN IV.11–12).

**Ecstasy and jealousy.** Divine Yearning "brings ecstasy," not allowing those it touches to belong to themselves. The Creator "is through the excessive yearning of His Goodness, transported outside of Himself in His providential activities… whereby He yet stays within Himself" (DN IV.13). He is called "jealous" because He is "vehement in His Good Yearning towards the world" and stirs zeal in creatures (DN IV.13). Yearning circles: from, in, and to the Good (DN IV.14–17). The same chapter names the "Bounteous Emanation… overflowing from the Good into creation, and once again returning" (DN IV.14).

**Evil is the opposite of this overflow.** Nothing is inherently bad; existence is good. Evil "hath no place either amongst things that have being or things that have not, yea it is farther removed than the Non-Existent from the Good" (DN IV.19). "Good cometh from the One universal Cause; and evil from many partial deficiencies" (DN IV.30). Matter *qua* matter is not evil: "matter, too, hath a share in order, beauty, and form" (DN IV.28). Demons are not naturally evil; they "are evil in so far as they do not exist" (DN IV.23).

Rolt's notes (n. 311, 335, 360) tighten or moralize beyond the text — *erōs* as shock-word; the Cross as the missing theodicy; all sin as ignorance. Those are notes, not DN IV.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Concepts/Principle of Plenitude (Lovejoy)]] / [[Concepts/Great Chain of Being (Lovejoy)]]**: Lovejoy already cited this chapter as the Christian fusion of plenitude with generative Good. The primary now supplies the English: "allowed Him not to remain unfruitful in Himself" (IV.10) and the ladder of IV.2.
- **[[Concepts/Emanation (Proodos)]]**: sun/light, undiminished rays, procession-and-return. Dionysius adds *erōs* as the motive of the going-forth and calls that going-forth ecstasy.
- **[[Thinkers/Proclus]]**: *erōs pronoētikos*, providential descending love — Wallis noted this as a stress on grace unknown to Plotinus. DN IV.13 is the Christian statement of the same.
- **[[Thinkers/Plotinus]]**: same sun, same *erōs*, same overflow. Clash: Plotinus can identify matter with evil (*Enn.* I.8); Dionysius refuses (IV.28).
- **[[Thinkers/Augustine]]**: evil as privation; the restless heart as yearning. Dionysius gives the metaphysical engine of that yearning from the Creator's side.
