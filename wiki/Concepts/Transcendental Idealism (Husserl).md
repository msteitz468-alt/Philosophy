# Transcendental Idealism (Husserl)
> Phenomenology, executed concretely, *is* transcendental idealism: every imaginable being falls within constituting subjectivity; an outside of possible consciousness is "nonsense."

## Summary

In *Cartesian Meditations* §§40–41 Husserl does not treat idealism as an optional metaphysical gloss on a descriptive method. The traditional problem — how immanent evidence can acquire Objective significance, the problem Descartes "supposed to be solved by divine *veracitas*" — is **inconsistent** once epoché and intentional analysis are carried through. What remains is the claim that **transcendency in every form is an immanent existential characteristic, constituted within the ego**. Phenomenology "is *eo ipso* 'transcendental idealism,' though in a fundamentally and essentially new sense." The proof "is therefore phenomenology itself."

This is the vault's first *primary* statement of the position. Smith's 2013 introduction had read the idealist passages of *Ideas I* as absorbed neo-Kantian idiom and had glossed constitution as "meaning, not existence." §41 will not bear that gloss without remainder.

## What §41 Actually Says

The load-bearing sentences (Cairns):

> "Transcendency in every form is an immanent existential characteristic, constituted within the ego. Every imaginable sense, every imaginable being, whether the latter is called immanent or transcendent, falls within the domain of transcendental subjectivity, as the subjectivity that constitutes sense and being. The attempt to conceive the universe of true being as something lying outside the universe of possible consciousness, possible knowledge, possible evidence… is nonsensical. They belong together essentially; and… they are also concretely one, one in the only absolute concretion: transcendental subjectivity. If transcendental subjectivity is the universe of possible sense, then an outside is precisely — nonsense."

Further: an Objective world common to us all receives "**sense and existence-status** in my subjectivity"; every sort of existent, real or ideal, becomes understandable as a "**product**" of transcendental subjectivity. This is not only sense-bestowal.

The Third Meditation had already prepared the ground. "Pregnant" constitution (§§23, 29) is constitution of *existing* objectivity — being/non-being as correlative titles of reason/unreason — not constitution of mere meant objects. World-objects are given only in essentially one-sided external experience; the world itself is "an infinite idea… the correlate of the idea of a perfect experiential evidence" (§28). Conscious life alone is that "wherein everything transcendent becomes constituted, as something inseparable from consciousness."

## What It Is Not

Husserl names and refuses three neighbours:

1. **Psychological idealism.** The ego of the epoché is not a piece of the world, not *mens sive animus*, not a human psyche that "has" representations (*CM* §11, §41).
2. **Kantian idealism.** Kant "believes it can keep open… the possibility of a world of things in themselves." Husserl closes that opening. The Conclusion will repeat: phenomenology "excludes every naive metaphysics that operates with absurd things in themselves, but does not exclude metaphysics as such" (§64).
3. **Berkeleyan / empirical idealism.** The world is not a mental thing or a private dream. It is *Objective* only as constituted by **transcendental intersubjectivity** — "accruing to me thanks to the constitution in me of others." The Fifth Meditation is required for that clause; without it, §41's "product" language would collapse into solipsism.

A manuscript note marks long stretches of §41 "unsatisfactory." That is an authorial unease about the *write-up*, not a retraction of the identification of phenomenology with this idealism.

## Monadological Close

After the Fifth Meditation, "phenomenological transcendental idealism has presented itself as a **monadology**" — "despite all our deliberate suggestions of Leibniz's metaphysics," its content is drawn "purely from phenomenological explication" (§62). The intrinsically first being, "the being that precedes and bears every worldly Objectivity, is transcendental intersubjectivity: the universe of monads" (§64). See [[Concepts/Pairing and Analogical Appresentation (Husserl)]], [[Concepts/Intersubjectivity and the Life-World (Husserl)]], [[Concepts/Monad (Leibniz)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/David Woodruff Smith]]** (vault's previous Husserl): constitution is "meaning, not existence"; *Ideas I* §49 is "intentional perspectivism" / formal correlation, not ontological dependence. A 1934 letter Smith cites — "No ordinary 'realist' has ever been as realistic… the phenomenological 'idealist' (a word which by the way I no longer use)" — sits in tension with *CM* §41's *eo ipso*. The letter is later than the 1929 lectures; it is not this text.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: Husserl keeps the regress to the *ego cogito* and refuses the rest — *veracitas Dei*, the *res cogitans* as "a little tag-end of the world," the deduction of Nature. The Cartesian problem of Objective significance is declared inconsistent, not solved.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: the Copernican motif is claimed; the *Ding an sich* is refused. Phenomenology is "in a fundamentally and essentially new sense" not Kantian idealism.
- **[[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]]**: the *name* monad and the incompossibility of two actual worlds are taken; pre-established harmony as a created device is not. Communion is intentional, not influx. See [[Concepts/Monad (Leibniz)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: the 1930 "monad" Sartre attacks as a relapse into psychologism is this page's concrete ego — pole + habitualities + meant objects (*CM* §33). Sartre keeps intentionality and rejects the constituting monad. See [[Concepts/Consciousness as Intentionality and Néantisation (Sartre)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Smith's "meaning, not existence" cannot be read off *CM* §41 without remainder. The text says "sense **and** being," "existence-status," "product." Whether later Husserl (the 1934 letter; the *Crisis*) walks this back is a question *Ideas* I and the *Crisis* still have to answer. See [[Contradictions/Husserl vs Naturalism — Phenomenal Consciousness and the Hard Problem]].
- > [!warning] The Fifth Meditation is supposed to save Objectivity from private production. Husserl himself calls the Other "a very puzzling possibility" at the close of §41, never given originaliter or apodictically (§62). The idealism stands or falls with pairing. See [[Concepts/Pairing and Analogical Appresentation (Husserl)]].
