# Master Morality and Slave Morality (Nietzsche)

> [!warning] *Human, All Too Human* §45 (1878) already says good and bad are “for a considerable period, tantamount to noble and servile, master and slave.” That is requital among ruling races vs. hostility among the subjugated — **not** this page’s two moralities. See [[Concepts/Two-fold Historical Origin of Good and Evil (Nietzsche)]].

## First statement: BGE §260 (1886)

The distinction is **sketched** in *Beyond Good and Evil* before *Genealogy* “amplifies and completes” it. June ingest had only BGE Ch. I; the names live in **§260**.

Seeds before the pair: **§195** — with the Jews “the *slave-insurrection in morals* commences” (inversion of valuations; not yet the two-type table). **§201** — once the community is secure, fear of the neighbor revalues strong instincts as “evil”; “fear is the mother of morals.”

**§260 (typology, not yet history).** “There is master-morality and slave-morality”; they appear even “in the same man, within one soul.”

- **Master:** “the antithesis ‘good’ and ‘bad’ means practically the same as ‘noble’ and ‘despicable,’—the antithesis ‘good’ and ‘evil’ is of a different origin.” “The noble type of man regards himself as a determiner of values… he is a creator of values.” Toward inferiors one may act “just as seems good to one… and in any case ‘beyond good and evil.’”
- **Slave:** “Slave-morality is essentially the morality of utility. Here is the seat of the origin of the famous antithesis ‘good’ and ‘evil.’” “According to slave-morality… the ‘evil’ man arouses fear; according to master-morality, it is precisely the ‘good’ man who arouses fear.”

Condition of the type: aristocratic society, slavery, and the **pathos of distance** that makes inner self-surmounting possible (§257). Exploitation as “primary organic function” of life = Will to Power (§259). See [[Concepts/Pathos of Distance and Order of Rank (Nietzsche)]].

The *Genealogy* sentence “Beyond Good and Evil is not the same as Beyond Good and Bad” is **not** in BGE §260. Do not import it into the 1886 statement. BGE uses “beyond good and evil” as the noble’s license toward inferiors, not as a third table.

## Overview (*Genealogy* First Essay, 1887)

In the *First Essay* ("Good and Evil," "Good and Bad") of *On the Genealogy of Morals*, Nietzsche provides the historical-psychological **amplification** of that pair, rooted in different "pathos" and different creators.

**Master morality** (aristocratic, noble): "Good" originates in the self-affirmation of the powerful, high-minded, aristocratic — those who feel *themselves* good and their actions "of the first order." Born of the "pathos of distance" (the chronic, despotic esprit de corps and fundamental instinct of a higher dominant race). "Bad" is the pale, late-born foil for the low, vulgar, plebeian. Spontaneous "yes" to self; negative concept secondary. Etymological evidence across languages confirms "good" = noble/powerful/aristocratic (e.g., Greek, Latin *bonus* as warrior, "blond" vs. dark pre-Aryan, etc.). Masters' right of naming extends to language as expression of power.

**Slave morality** (priestly, herd, ressentiment-driven): The "revolt of the slaves in morals." When the weak/priestly/underdog, deprived of direct outlet for action, make *ressentiment* creative. Says "no" from the outset to everything "outside itself." Action is fundamentally *reaction*. Requires an external "evil enemy" (the aristocrat, distorted by the "venomous eye of resentfulness" into the "evil one") to create its "good" (the self as the poor, weak, suffering, meek, pious). The Jewish transvaluation ("the wretched are alone the good... you aristocrats... are to all eternity the evil") is the most profound and fateful; Christianity its popular form and world-historical victory. "Good and evil" replaces "good and bad."

The two have fought "Rome against Judaea, Judaea against Rome." Judaea (slave) has been provisionally victorious (Christianity, Reformation, French Revolution), but the fight continues at higher, more psychological levels. "Beyond Good and Evil" is explicitly *not* the same as "Beyond Good and Bad."

**Restated as optics in *The Case of Wagner* Epilogue (1888).** No greater antithesis within moral values than master-morality vs Christian valuations (the latter grown on morbid soil; the gospels present the same physiological types as Dostoevsky’s novels). Master-morality (“Roman,” “pagan,” “classical,” “Renaissance”) is the symbolic speech of well-constitutedness, ascending life, and the Will to Power as a vital principle. It *affirms*; Christian morality *denies*. One cannot refute Christianity: it is impossible to refute a diseased eyesight. “True” and “untrue” have no sense in optics. What must be guarded against is the falsity that would regard the antithesis as no antithesis — Wagner’s specialty. See [[Sources/The Case of Wagner - Nietzsche (1888)]], [[Concepts/Decadence (Nietzsche)]].

## Key Arguments

- The origin of "good" is sought in the wrong place by "English psychologists" (utility + forgetting + habit from the recipient's side). The aristocratic self-affirmation is the true homestead.
- Priestly vs. knightly-aristocratic values: the former (introspection, neurasthenia, clean/unclean sharpened) branches off and produces the dangerous intensification that enables the slave revolt.
- The "evil enemy" is a creation of resentful imagination. The aristocrat inter pares is considerate, loyal, proud, friendly; outside the circle a "beast of prey" (Roman, Viking, etc.). Popular morality invents a neutral "subject" behind the doing so that strength can be blamed for being strength ("the bird of prey is evil").
- The Jewish/Christian inversion: from the "trunk of that tree of revenge and hate" grows the "crown" — a new love (the Redeemer) as the most dangerous bait of revenge. "Me too made eternal hate" would be the truer inscription over the gate of the Christian Paradise than Dante's.
- "Good and evil" has triumphed; the "morality of the vulgar man" has triumphed. The "redemption" (from the masters) is progressing; everything is becoming "Judaised, or Christianised, or vulgarised." Napoleon as the "most unique and violent anachronism" and "incarnate problem of the aristocratic ideal in itself."

## Johnston layer (second translation)

- **Pathos of distance (I.II):** "From this pathos of distance they first arrogated to themselves the right to create values, to stamp out the names for values." "Homestead of the concept 'good'" is Samuel, not Johnston.
- **Slave revolt (I.VII, X):** "with the Jews the slave condition in morality begins." Then: "The slave revolt in morality begins when the resentment itself becomes creative…"
- **Blond beast (I.XI):** "the blond beast splendidly roaming around in its lust for loot and victory." Not an endorsement; the noble type's *outside* the *inter pares* circle.
- **Rome / Judea (I.XVI):** Johnston spells **Judea**. Napoleon is "this synthesis of the inhuman and the superhuman" — not Samuel's "Monster and Superman," not Kaufmann's *Übermensch*.
- **Closer (I.XVII):** "that dangerous slogan which is written on the body of my last book: 'Beyond Good and Evil' . . . at least this does not mean 'Beyond Good and Bad.'"
- **Will to power** as slogan is **absent** from Essay I. Closest: "thirst for power" (VI); "a will to overpower, a will to throw down, a will to rule" (XIII).
- **Note** to Essay I: physiologists and doctors must critique tables of value; the philosopher's task is "the rank order of values." "Value for what?" Well-being of the majority ≠ well-being of the fewest.

## *Twilight* restatement (Improvers §4)

*Twilight* "The ‘Improvers’ of Mankind" §4 restates the slave revolt as Christianity = "the counter-movement to any morality of breeding… the anti-Aryan religion par excellence" — "the victory of chandala values… disguised as a religion of love." Same genealogy as Rome vs Judaea, now in zoological/breeding vocabulary. Report it as that 1888 diagnosis; do not expand it into a race program. See [[Concepts/Morality as Anti-Nature (Nietzsche)]], [[Sources/Twilight of the Idols - Nietzsche (Kaufmann)]].

*The Antichrist* adds the political slogan, not a new pair: “the gospel of the ‘lowly’ lowers”; “equal rights for all” forged from mass *ressentiment* against the pathos of distance (A §43); Chandala-morality in the words put in the Master’s mouth (§45). Same warning: diagnosis, not a program. See [[Concepts/Paul as Falsifier of the Evangel (Nietzsche)]].
