# Phenomenal Field and the Return to Phenomena (Merleau-Ponty)
> Empiricism and intellectualism share one "unquestioned belief in the world." The fundamental philosophical act is to return to the lived world beneath the objective world — the phenomenal field, which resists being completely made explicit.

## Summary

The Preface of *Phenomenology of Perception* defines phenomenology as description, not explanation or analysis. "The real is to be described, and neither constructed nor constituted." Perception is "the background against which all acts stand out and is thus presupposed by them." Science is a "second-order expression" of the lived world: geography to the landscape where we first learned what a forest is.

The Introduction then shows why classical theories of perception never reach that background. **"Sensation" is "the most confused notion there is."** Figure-on-background is "the very definition of the perceptual phenomenon," not a later organization of punctual impressions. The "unquestioned belief in the world" (*le préjugé du monde*) and the "experience error" generate the **constancy hypothesis**: a point-for-point correspondence between stimulus and elementary perception. Gestalt theory already broke that hypothesis (figure, lines of force, the indeterminate as a positive phenomenon) and then, Merleau-Ponty charges, remained naturalistic — treating those structures as causes rather than reasons — until the critique is carried through as a reduction.

**Empiricism** tries to rebuild the missing sense with association and the "projection of memories." Both operations presuppose the sense they claim to produce. "To perceive is not to remember." **Intellectualism** (attention as a spotlight; judgment as the act that gives sense to the given) accepts the same ready-made world and then explains deviation from it. "Empiricism does not see that we need to know what we are looking for…; intellectualism does not see that we need to be ignorant of what we are looking for." Attention is "the active constitution of a new object"; the object is its **motive** (*motif*), not its cause. "To perceive… is not to judge, but rather to grasp, prior to all judgment, a sense immanent in the sensible." Motivation is a "fluid" concept between cause and reason.

The **phenomenal field** is "the place of our living communication with the world." "Classical science is a perception that has forgotten its origins and believes itself to be complete." The return is to "the system 'Self–Others–things' in its nascent state." The field "resists in principle being directly and completely made explicit"; second-order reflection can convert it into a *transcendental* field without ever finishing the job. That is why "the most important lesson of the reduction is the impossibility of a complete reduction."

Part Two then fills the field: sensing is solicitation and synchronization ("I must find the attitude that *will* provide it with the means to become determinate and to become blue"); "*one* perceives in me, and not that I perceive"; synesthesia is the rule because the body is a synergetic system, not a sum of organs.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Description, not construction or constitution.
- Constancy hypothesis = empiricism and intellectualism's shared ground.
- Motivation sits between cause and reason.
- The phenomenal field cannot be fully explicit; that is a result, not a failure.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: the method of the whole book. Each later analysis (body, space, others, freedom) is an immanent critique of objective thought "taken literally."
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: "to the things themselves" kept; complete reduction and *Sinn-gebung* as making the world a mere signification refused. Critique of the constancy hypothesis, "if carried to its conclusion, takes on the value of a true 'phenomenological reduction.'"
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: reflective analysis works back toward a condition of possibility distinct from experience and "substitutes a reconstruction for a description." The *Critique of Judgment* is the exception MP takes up (unity prior to the object).
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: figure/ground and the indeterminate as a positive phenomenon become the gestalt functions programs lack.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]** / **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]** / **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: prediction-error accounts rebuild perception as inference. MP's claim is prior: the sense is immanent in the sensible, not computed over sensations. Do not flatten this page into PP (*Surfing Uncertainty* is a concurrent ingest).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Whether a later naturalized phenomenology (Varela, neurophenomenology) can keep the field's resistance to complete explication without becoming one more objective science of the field.
- Gestalt theory is an ally and a foil: it shows figure/ground, then treats it as nature.
