# Mindfulness-Awareness as Method (Varela)
> The missing complement to cognitive science is not more theory of experience but a disciplined, trainable presence with embodied everyday experience — what the 1991 book calls mindfulness/awareness.

## Summary

Chapter 2 of [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)|*The Embodied Mind*]] argues that Western phenomenology faces experience theoretically, "after the fact." Husserl's epoché studies intentional contents "purely internally"; even Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty remain "purely theoretical." The proposed method is **mindful, open-ended reflection**:

> "Mindfulness means that the mind is present in embodied everyday experience." "Reflection is not just on experience, but reflection is a form of experience itself." "Mindfulness/awareness meditation can provide a natural bridge between cognitive science and human experience."

They gloss the pair as *shamatha / vipashyana*. It is **not** concentration, trance, or a mystical altered state. "What mindfulness disrupts is mindlessness," not "active involvement." It does *not* bracket the world (contra Husserlian epoché). It returns from "theories and… the abstract attitude" to embodied everyday life. *Prajñā* is "not knowledge about anything"; there is "no abstract knower… separate from the experience itself."

They contrast the method with Wundtian introspectionism, psychoanalysis, and folk "meditation." They claim that Buddhist doctrines of no-self and nondualism "grew out of this method" and "lay claim to being simply the observations that mind makes when it is allowed to be naturally observant" — to be tested, not believed.

Appendix A is a trap for later "mindfulness" literature: terminology is not interchangeable. *Śamatha* ≠ *vipassanā* ≠ *vipaśyanā* (two senses) ≠ *shikan taza*; schools reuse names; "one cannot tell from terminology alone what meditation is being practiced." Obtain a qualified teacher. Appendix B lists *skandhas*, twelve *nidānas*, *citta / caitta*. Appendix C is a living-traditions bibliography (Buddhist-modernist works, Thompson 2016 notes).

## 2016 Corrections (do not overwrite 1991)

- **Thompson**: the 1991 depiction of mindfulness as inner observation of an observer-independent stream (especially ch. 4's "really" discontinuous consciousness) is wrong. [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]] "rightly objected": inward observation alters experience. The better view already in the book: mindfulness as skillful know-how for enacting situated mind–body states. Classical Buddhist philosophy is not based on this "Buddhist modernist" style.
- **Rosch**: the Dreyfus objection confuses path with result. Academic "Buddhist modernism" as delegitimation is refused. Laboratory and MBSR "mindfulness" often cannot specify the practice; this is body imperialism, not dialogue.

Kabat-Zinn's foreword reads the book as a stepping-stone toward a non-dual understanding and ties it to MBSR as "skillful means." That is reception, not the 1991 definition.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] et al. 1991**: the pragmatic partner of [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]].
- **[[Thinkers/Evan Thompson]] 2016**: enacted know-how, not introspection; later **neurophenomenology** (reported) uses Western phenomenology *and* mindfulness on brain dynamics.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: 1993 review — phenomenology is not inward observation. Accepted by Thompson, not by Rosch.
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: epoché is the contrast class in 1991 (internal, theoretical). Thompson 2016 withdraws the "failed project" verdict.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: [[Concepts/Heterophenomenology (Dennett)|heterophenomenology]] extracts third-person texts. This method trains first-person presence. They share the refusal of an unexamined folk self; they do not share a method.
- Jon Kabat-Zinn / MBSR: named in the 2016 foreword; Appendix A is the warning against collapsing names.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Whether a trained change in experience can be evidence about experience *apart from* the training is the Dreyfus/Thompson vs Rosch split.
- Ch. 4's alpha-rhythm / perceptual-framing experiments are offered as *consonance* with momentariness, not as neural validation. Note 17: they do **not** decide whether moments are contiguous or gapped.
