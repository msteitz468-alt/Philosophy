# Schematism and Principles of Pure Understanding (Kant)

> The categories become cognitions of nature only through temporal schemata. The resulting principles — especially substance, causality, and community — are the synthetic a priori judgments that replace traditional ontology.

## Summary

**Why schemata.** Categories and sensible intuitions are "entirely unhomogeneous." Subsumption needs a third, mediating representation, pure yet both intellectual and sensible: the **transcendental schema**. Time, as form of inner sense, is homogeneous with the category (universal, a priori) and with appearance (in every empirical manifold). Application of the category to appearances is possible only through a **transcendental time-determination** (A 138–139 / B 177–178). A schema is "a general procedure of the imagination for providing a concept with its image." Category-schemata cannot be imaged; they are pure syntheses in time (number; filling of time; persistence; succession according to a rule; simultaneity; existence at a time / at all times). "Without schemata, therefore, the categories are only functions of the understanding for concepts, but do not represent any object" (A 147 / B 186–187).

**Table of principles.** Appearances are subsumed under *schemata*, not under categories *per se*.

- **Mathematical** (constitutive, intuitive certainty): **Axioms of Intuition** — "All intuitions are extensive magnitudes" (B). **Anticipations of Perception** — "In all appearances the real, which is an object of the sensation, has intensive magnitude, i.e., a degree."
- **Dynamical** (regulative of existence, discursive certainty): **Analogies of Experience** and **Postulates of empirical thinking**.

**Analogies (general).** "Experience is possible only through the representation of a necessary connection of perceptions" (B).

1. **First Analogy.** "In all change of appearances substance persists, and its quantum is neither increased nor diminished in nature" (B 224). Substance is the persistent substratum of empirical time-determination; arising/perishing of substance would split the unity of time.
2. **Second Analogy (answer to Hume).** "All alterations occur in accordance with the law of the connection of cause and effect" (B 232). Mere sequence in apprehension is reversible (house vs ship); objective succession requires a rule that makes the order necessary. If cause were drawn from induction, "everything that happens has a cause" would be "merely feigned" (A 196 / B 241). The rule is a condition of experiencing an occurrence at all. Principle of sufficient reason = ground of possible experience of succession. Schema of cause: "the real upon which, whenever it is posited, something else always follows" (A 144 / B 183).
3. **Third Analogy.** "All substances, insofar as they can be perceived in space as simultaneous, are in thoroughgoing interaction" (B 256). Isolated substances would not constitute one experience.

**Postulates.** What agrees with the formal conditions of experience is *possible*; what is connected with the material conditions (sensation) is *actual*; what is determined in connection with the actual according to general conditions of experience *exists necessarily*.

**Refutation of Idealism (B only).** Theorem: "The mere, but empirically determined, consciousness of my own existence proves the existence of objects in space outside me" (B 275). Inner time-determination requires something persistent that cannot be an intuition *in me*; hence outer experience is immediate and inner experience only mediate. Aimed at Descartes' *problematic* idealism and Berkeley's *dogmatic* idealism. This is empirical realism *inside* [[Concepts/Transcendental Idealism (Kant)|transcendental idealism]].

These principles are "precisely the synthetic a priori judgments that are to be demonstrated by Kant's critical replacement for traditional metaphysics" (Guyer/Wood).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Hume.** Custom and the copy principle cannot underwrite the *necessity* of "every event has a cause." Kant agrees the connection is not analytic and not given in any single experience; he relocates it as a condition of *possible* experience. See [[Concepts/Custom and Belief (Hume)]], [[Concepts/Problem of Induction (Hume)]].
- **Descartes / Berkeley.** The Refutation claims to end the "scandal" that outer things must be assumed on faith (B xxxix n.). See [[Contradictions/Subjective Idealism vs. Physical Realism]].
- **Pearl / modern causation.** Kant's Second Analogy is a *transcendental* argument for causal law as a condition of objective time-order, not a method for identifying particular causes. See [[Concepts/Ladder of Causation (Pearl)]].
- **Schopenhauer, *Fourfold Root* §23.** House-scan and ship-downstream are *both* events. Appearances can follow one another without following *from* one another. The correct proof of the apriority of causality is §21 (understanding constitutes intuition), not the Analogy. See [[Concepts/Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Whether the Analogies *independently* prove the categories (as some readers hold) or only specify the deduction is an exegetical controversy (Guyer/Wood). The vault records them as the determinate use of the already-deduced categories.
- The Second Analogy secures causality *in appearances*. The Third Antinomy will then claim that the same action can also have an intelligible cause. See [[Arguments/Antinomies of Pure Reason (Kant)]].
