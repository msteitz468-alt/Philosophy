# One's Own Body and the Body Schema (Merleau-Ponty)
> "Insofar as it sees or touches the world, my body can neither be seen nor touched. … my body is that by which there are objects."

## Summary

Part One of *Phenomenology of Perception* withdraws **one's own body** (*le corps propre*) from the objective world. Perception "ends in objects," and the object as "seen from nowhere" would be invisible: "To see is always to see from somewhere." The house itself is the house seen from everywhere, via horizons, not a non-perspectival *géométral*. Objective thought forgets that perspectivism and treats the body as one more object, *partes extra partes*. Method: take that thought at its word. "In science itself, one's own body evades the treatment that they wish to impose upon it." Withdrawing it "will carry with it the intentional threads that unite it to its surroundings and … reveal to us the perceiving subject as well as the perceived world."

**Phantom limb and anosognosia** are the first test. Peripheral physiology fails (cocaine does not remove the phantom; cerebral lesions without amputation can produce one). Pure psychology fails (the phantom disappears when sensory conductors are cut). A mixed theory "may thus be valid as a statement of known facts, but it is fundamentally obscure": there is no common ground between spatial physiological facts and placeless *cogitationes*. The objective world has "no middle ground between presence and absence." The phenomenal middle is an **ambivalent presence**: "To have a phantom limb is to remain open to all of the actions of which the arm alone is capable and to stay within the practical field that one had prior to the mutilation." The body comprises two layers, **habitual** and **actual**. Manipulables still appeal to "a hand that I no longer have." "The body is the vehicle of being in the world." Soul–body union "is accomplished at each moment in the movement of existence," not by Cartesian decree.

Classical psychology already marked *le corps propre* as non-object (permanence, double sensations, affectivity, kinesthesis) and then reified those marks as contents of a representation. Permanence of one's own body is "on my side," not in the world. "It is *with* me." "My own body is the primordial habit."

**Touching-touched.** The two hands are never simultaneously both touched and touching. The body "attempts to touch itself touching, it begins 'a sort of reflection.'" This is not Sartre's later "double sensation" as two incommunicable orders, and it is not a completed reflection.

**Body schema** (*schéma corporel*) is named in Chapter III. It is not a summary of images and not a body image (Schilder's English). "I hold my body as an indivisible possession and I know the position of each of my limbs through a *body schema*." First scientific uses (associationist summary, image-center, even a static 'form') are rejected. Spatiality of the body is **situational**, not positional: "my body appears to me as a posture toward a certain task." "The 'body schema' is, in the end, a manner of expressing that my body is in and toward the world." It is a system of equivalences of the body *in the world*. Habit "expresses the power we have of dilating our being in the world": hat, automobile, cane are taken up as residences. "The blind man's cane has ceased to be an object for him… the cane's furthest point is transformed into a sensitive zone." Chapter IV: the body's unity is that of a **work of art**, "a knot of living significations," segments that together "*perform* a single gesture." "The body is our general means of having a world."

## Key Claims / Positions

- *Le corps propre* is not a *Körper* among *Körper*; it is that by which there are objects.
- Habitual / actual body explains phantom limb without mixing two series.
- Body schema ≠ body image; it is situational spatiality, a system of equivalences.
- Habit incorporates instruments into the schema.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: the hinge of the book. "The theory of the body schema is implicitly a theory of perception."
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: *Leib* vs *Körper* is the ancestor. MP keeps the distinction and refuses to treat the body as a constituted object in the sphere of ownness.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: agrees the body is lived and not known. Sartre's body is nihilated facticity, nausea, and (under the Look) alienated object; skillful fluency is not his theme. MP is not named in BN's body chapter. See [[Concepts/The Body (Sartre)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: rejected "Cartesian physiology" and union by decree. See [[Concepts/The Two Tests and the Animal-Machine (Descartes)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: cane, synergy, and the hold become the three bodily functions programs lack. See [[Concepts/Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Antonio Damasio]]**: the body must be mapped and felt, not merely possessed. Lesion register, not phenomenological withdrawal.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Whether a sufficiently human-like robot body is a *corps propre* or a sensor suite is the question Dreyfus leaves open and Clark walks through.
- The 1945 "sort of reflection" of the touching hands is not yet the late ontology of flesh (*The Visible and the Invisible*).
