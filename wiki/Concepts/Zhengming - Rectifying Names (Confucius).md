# Zhengming — Rectifying Names (Confucius)

> In Annping Chin's *Analects*, *zhengming* is **rectifying names**: restoring the integrity of titles so that what is said is reasonable, what is reasonable can be accomplished, rites and music can flourish, and punishments — if used — can be just. It is the first thing Confucius would attend to if he took charge of a government (13.3). "To govern [*zheng*] is to correct [*zheng*]" (12.17).

## Load-bearing claims in the text

- **12.11.** Duke Jing of Qi asks about government. "Let the ruler be a ruler, the subject be a subject, a father be a father, a son be a son." The Duke: if not, "even though there is plenty of grain, will I be able to eat it?" Chin: both a local diagnosis (Jing failing as ruler and father) and a claim about the weight inherent in the names.
- **12.17.** Ji Kangzi asks about government. "To govern [*zheng*] is to correct [*zheng*]. When you set an example by correcting your mistakes, who will dare not to correct his mistakes?" Chin: *zhengming* is reforming *oneself* so that names ("father," "ruler") recover integrity.
- **13.3 (the chain).** In Wei, Zilu asks what Confucius would do first. "It would have to be rectifying names." Zilu calls this evasive. The chain:
  1. If names are not rectified, what is said will not seem reasonable.
  2. When what is said does not seem reasonable, nothing will get accomplished.
  3. When nothing gets accomplished, rites and music will not flourish.
  4. When rites and music do not flourish, punishments and penalties take their place and fail to be just.
  5. Then "people will not know where to put their hands and feet."
  - "Thus when a gentleman names something, the name can surely hold up in speech. When he says something, his words can surely be carried out in action."
- **12.7 / 13.6 / 13.13.** Trust of the people is the last thing a state can drop. If the superior is upright, orders are needless; if not, they fail. Correct yourself to correct others.
- **Not yet in Books 1–5.** Liu Baonan's gloss on 4.9 (the *shi* and names) is not the doctrine. Do not back-date 13.3.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. [[Concepts/Dao - The Course (Laozi)]] ch. 1**: "no name that determines what is or should be can be the name that holds always." Confucius wants names that *do* hold — in speech *and* in action. Laozi: carving produces names, know when to stop (ch. 32). Confucius: if you do not restore names first, the rest of government is noise. Same worry about empty words; opposite remedy (subtract names vs repair them).
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Triangle of Reference (Ogden-Richards)]] / [[Concepts/Word Magic (Ogden-Richards)]]**: Ogden and Richards treat the name–thing link as empty at the base of the triangle and hunt word-magic. *Zhengming* is political-ethical, not a semantic theory: the name "ruler" must be one a person can *be*.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Picture Theory of Language (Wittgenstein)]]**: 13.3 is not a theory of propositions picturing facts. It is a demand that office-words be livable.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Categorical Imperative - Universal Law Formulation (Kant)]]**: both care whether a saying can be carried through. Kant tests a *maxim* for contradiction in conception/will. Confucius tests a *name* (ruler, father) for whether conduct can bear it. Different unit (maxim vs role-name), related intolerance of speech that cannot be acted.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Historicist vs fundamental: is 12.11 only about Duke Jing and Wei's Zhe, or a standing claim about names? Chin keeps both.
- 13.3 Chin: if names hold, rites and music flourish and "there probably will be no need for punishments"; if used, they can be just. That is not a Legalist program and not anarchism.
- Do not import later "rectification of names" as a philosophy-of-language school (School of Names, Xunzi's *Zhengming* chapter). Those texts are not in the vault.
