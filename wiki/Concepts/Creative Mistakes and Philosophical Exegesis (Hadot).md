# Creative Mistakes and Philosophical Exegesis (Hadot)
> From the mid-4th century BC to the late 16th century, philosophy is above all the exegesis of a small number of authoritative texts. Forced readings that a modern historian calls mistakes are often how new philosophy is produced.

## Summary

Chapter 2 of *Philosophy as a Way of Life* states the method that [[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]’s whole corpus assumes. For almost two thousand years “philosophy was conceived of, above all, as the exegesis of a small number of texts deriving from ‘authorities,’ chief among whom were [[Thinkers/Plato]] and [[Thinkers/Aristotle]].” The search for truth was “confounded with the search for the meaning of ‘authentic’ texts.” An authentic author “could neither be mistaken, nor contradict himself,” nor disagree with another authentic author. “The most forced exegesis was used in order to accommodate the letter of the text to what was considered the truth.”

Modern historians name the result systematization, amalgamation, mistranslation. Hadot’s name is the **creative mistake** (*contresens*): “very often, mistakes and misunderstandings have brought about important evolutions in the history of philosophy. In particular, they have caused new ideas to appear.” Late antiquity is a “gigantic *metaphora*” of contamination and productive misreading. The ancients themselves would have called the same moves *true* exegesis. The two standpoints must not be collapsed.

Thought proceeds by *bricolage* of traditional formulae (*topoi*) given a new sense. Meaning is determined by group, dogma-tradition, genre, and goal: what the author *had* to say, *could* say, and *meant* to say. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]].

## Load-Bearing Examples (from this source)

- **[[Thinkers/Porphyry]] on *Parmenides* 142b** — the split of infinitive *être* from participle *étant*, “certainly a misinterpretation, but a creative misinterpretation, sprung from the very difficulties of the exegetical method.” Also the *ousia* series; Boethius’ *esse* / *quod est*.
- **[[Thinkers/Plotinus]]** — the inquiry into evil is posed as “in what sense Plato says…” (*Enn.* I.8.6); claims to novelty are claims to interpretation of Plato (*Enn.* V.1.8).
- **Ambrose / Augustine** — Plotinus reused in sermons; Augustine on Psalm 113 via the Greek; Davidson notes Hadot later retracted a 1960 “modern self” reading of Augustine in favor of a theological/exegetical “I.”
- **Celsus / Justin** — the “theft” of texts between pagans and Christians.
- **Husserl’s Augustine** — cited as a modern case of the same *bricolage*.

## Why It Matters for This Wiki

The vault’s own ingest rule — read genre and school before extracting “doctrine” — is Hadot’s rule. Apparent contradiction, association, and “bad writing” in ancient sources are often *zetetic* or formative, not failures of system. Later commentary is **productive misreading**, not mere error. Do not impose Kantian/Hegelian systematicity on pre-Cartesian texts.

This is also how [[Schools/Neoplatonism]] is generated: a history of Plato-exegesis, not a deviation from a pure Plato. See [[Thinkers/Plotinus]], [[Thinkers/Porphyry]], [[Thinkers/Iamblichus]], [[Thinkers/Proclus]], and [[Contradictions/Plato vs Aristotle on the Forms]] (the critique of the Forms already begins inside Platonism, in the *Parmenides*).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Hadot** — historian’s label for a two-millennium engine of novelty; paired with the “hidden form” / genre method of ch. 1.
- **The ancient exegete** — would deny that anything “creative” or “mistaken” is happening; the master already said it.
- **Post-Cartesian refusal of authority** — later becomes, again, “violent exegesis of discourse by discourse.”
- **Davidson** — extends the point with Guarducci’s mystical cryptography as an analogy (Davidson’s illustration, not Hadot’s).
