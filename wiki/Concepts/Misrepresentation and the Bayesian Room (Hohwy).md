# Misrepresentation and the Bayesian Room (Hohwy)
> A percept misrepresents when it is a local prediction-error win that raises long-run error. A room that only minimizes prediction error still perceives — if average mutual information can fall.

## Summary

Chapter 8 of *The Predictive Mind* is Hohwy's most squarely philosophical chapter: misperception, [[Concepts/Rule-Following (Wittgenstein)|rule-following]], representation, and understanding.

**Misperception** is not any local rise in prediction error. It is a pairing of a percept with a cause it is not *on average* best paired with — a local PEM win that worsens long-run average PEM (pp. 174–76). All inference is misperception "to some degree." "On average minimization of prediction error is maximized by perceiving all and only dogs as dogs" (p. 175). Content is what a parameter carries on average (Eliasmith / Usher). Dusk-dog-as-sheep is error because that pairing would raise long-run PE. The system "is not interested in veridicality as such" (p. 143) but mostly gets truth by chasing average PE.

Then the "nastier" **Kripke/Wittgenstein** bump: why does *this* organism follow "reduce prediction error" rather than a bent rule? Hohwy offers a speculative *solution*, not a mere recast. Existence *is* occupying unsurprising states (resisting entropy). "I don't try to follow a rule in any normal sense, I just am… I am my own existence proof." "Dying becomes the ultimate misperception" (p. 181). He admits controversy and a "radical reconceptualization." He does claim a naturalistic answer to the paradox.

**Hierarchical modes of presentation:** objects are picked out by structured, causally statistical definite descriptions across levels — "hypotheses of hierarchical" PEM (pp. 182–83).

**The Bayesian room.** A Searle-style room: Hermann follows Bayes/PEM on the symbols he is handed. If PE minimization raises mutual information with the hidden causes, and if average MI can fall (so success is not trivial), Hermann "does perceive" (pp. 185–87). Syntax plus the right statistical coupling is enough for perception. This is the opposite moral to [[Concepts/Chinese Room Argument (Searle)]].

## Key Claims / Positions

- Error is long-run, not local. Illusions can be locally excellent PEM.
- Rule-following is answered by existence as low-surprisal occupancy, not by a further interpretation.
- The Bayesian room perceives. Searle's operator, if he is minimizing PE against a world, is not merely shuffling.
- Representation is implicit in hierarchical PEM ([[Concepts/Prediction Error Minimization (Hohwy)]], the dam-plugger).

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]] / Kripke**: Hohwy claims to solve the paradox naturalistically. The vault's Wittgenstein page says the paradox shows the *interpretation* model is wrong and that obeying a rule is a *practice* in a form of life. Hohwy replaces practice/form of life with occupying unsurprising states. That is a substantial re-reading, not a commentary.
- **[[Thinkers/John Searle]]**: the Bayesian room is a PEM counter to the Chinese Room. Searle would say mutual information is still syntax. Hohwy says if average MI can fail, the coupling is world-involving enough to count as perception.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: Clark 2023 does not run this argument. Hohwy is doing philosophy of content that Clark left aside.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- > [!warning] Hohwy's "I just am" answer to rule-following drops Wittgenstein's appeal to shared practice and [[Concepts/Forms of Life (Wittgenstein)|form of life]]. It is not obvious the entropy story supplies *normativity* rather than a further regularity Kripke can bend.
- > [!warning] The Bayesian room assumes what Searle denies: that the right statistical coupling *is* semantics. See [[Contradictions/Hofstadter vs Searle - Strange Loops vs Chinese Room]].
