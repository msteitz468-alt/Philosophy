# Symbolic and Emotive Functions of Language (Ogden-Richards)

> The symbolic use of words is **statement** — recording, supporting, organizing, communicating references. The emotive use is to **express or excite feelings and attitudes**. The functions usually occur together; they are in principle distinct. Test: "Is this true or false in the ordinary strict scientific sense?"

## Summary

The first-edition preface already lists this cut as the book's second claimed contribution. Ch. VII draws it fully, using Beauty as the test-bed; Ch. X expands it into five functions and then collapses poetry back to the twofold use.

**The twofold cut (Ch. VII).**
- Symbolic: truth-apt; theoretically verifiable. "The height of the Eiffel Tower is 900 feet."
- Emotive: "Hurrah!", "Poetry is a spirit", "Man is a worm" "may not be making statements, not even false statements."
- Mixed uses are allowed and typical. "Some element of reference probably enters… into almost all use of words." Much poetry consists of true/false statements used "not for the sake of their truth or falsity but for the sake of the attitudes which their acceptance will evoke." The symbolic function is then "instrumental only and subsidiary."
- Beware **True¹** (scientific) vs **True²** (convincing/sincere/beautiful) vs a purely evocative True/False.

**Beauty as multiple definition.** There need not be one subject called Aesthetics. Sixteen uses of "beautiful" are separated (simple quality; form; imitation of Nature; medium; genius; Truth/Ideal; illusion; social effects; expression; pleasure; emotions; a specific emotion; empathy; vitality; exceptional personalities; synaesthesis). Definition I (Beauty as a simple quality) is "survival of primitive word-superstitions." `Good` has already been shown, in Ch. VI, to "stand for nothing whatever" in Moore's indefinable use.

**Five functions (Ch. X)**, said to "appear to be exhaustive":
1. Symbolization of reference
2. Expression of attitude to listener
3. Expression of attitude to referent
4. Promotion of effects intended
5. Support of reference (ease/difficulty; "rallying-points")

(2)–(5) are the emotive work. Sentence-form in mixed speech is a compromise among being suitable, appropriate, judicious, and personal. Prefer **symbolic vs emotive** to "prose vs poetry." "A poem — or a religion… has no concern with limited and directed reference. It tells us, or should tell us, nothing." Religions that exploit the confusion of function are "unmistakably pathological growths."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/A.J. Ayer]] / [[Concepts/Emotive Theory of Ethics (Ayer)]]**: the direct heir. Ayer's "Stealing is wrong" = "You stole that money" plus a tone of horror is the Ch. VII test applied to normative ethics. O&R had already treated `good` as an emotive blank and ethics only as a preview; Ayer makes it a theory. The vault had Ayer without this ancestor.
- **[[Thinkers/I. A. Richards]]**: takes the emotive side into *Principles of Literary Criticism* and *Practical Criticism* (named, not ingested). The "poem tells us nothing" slogan is his.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]**: early W puts ethics/aesthetics beyond the sayable (*Tractatus* 6.421). O&R keep them *sayable* as emotive — not as failed pictures. Later W's [[Concepts/Language-Games (Wittgenstein)|language-games]] (commanding, joking, praying) occupy neighboring ground without a single truth-test.
- **[[Thinkers/Bronislaw Malinowski]]**: **phatic communion** is function (2) made ethnographic — ties created by exchange of words whose meanings are almost irrelevant. Narrative's referential function is "subordinate," he says, per this chapter.
- **Bergson / Croce**: Ch. VII interlocutors. "Virtual knowledge" is denied the name of knowledge; Croce forces one meaning onto Art + perception.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The cut is principled, not empirical: they insist the functions "usually occur together." Do not report O&R as holding that poetry contains no statements.
- > [!warning] Ayer's later specialization is sharper (ethical symbols are *pseudo-concepts*, neither true nor false). O&R leave more room for mixed uses and for "fitting" attitudes that are not truth-apt but also not nonsense.
