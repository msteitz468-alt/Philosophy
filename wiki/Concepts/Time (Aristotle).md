# Time (Aristotle)
> Time is "number of motion in respect of 'before' and 'after'" — not motion, and not independent of motion. No change, no time.

## Summary

*Physics* IV.10–14. Time "either does not exist at all or barely": past is not, future is not yet; the now is not a part. Time is not the revolution of the whole, not the sphere, and not motion itself (motion is in the moving thing and is fast or slow; time is everywhere and is not fast or slow).

Yet "time is not independent of movement and change." We notice time only by marking before and after in motion. The definition: "time is just this — number of motion in respect of 'before' and 'after.'" Number here is the **counted**, not that with which we count. The **now** both continues and divides time: "the 'now' is the link of time… and it is a limit of time (for it is the beginning of the one and the end of the other)." Same in substratum, other in succession.

Time measures motion and rest; eternals are **not in time**. Time is of itself more a cause of decay than of generation. Uniform circular locomotion is the first measure (the heavens as clock).

If only soul (reason) can count, "there would not be time unless there were soul" — though the before/after of motion might remain as the *numerable*. This is not Augustine's *distentio animi*; the numerable is in the motion, the number requires a counter.

Book VIII then uses time's eternity to argue motion's eternity: "If, then, time is the number of motion or itself a kind of motion, it follows that, if there is always time, motion must also be eternal." The now is always beginning and end; there is no first now.

Book VI: time is a continuum, not composed of indivisible moments. That is the reply to Zeno's arrow. Nothing is in motion or at rest *in* a now.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Time = number of motion wrt before/after.
- No time without change; time ≠ change.
- The now is a limit/link, not a part.
- Possibly no time without soul as counter; the numerable remains.
- Time eternal ⇒ motion eternal.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Augustine]]**: time as *distentio* of the soul is a subjectivizing of Aristotle's "no counter, no number" clause, dropping the claim that the numerable is in motion.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: time as a priori form of inner sense is a further subjectivizing; Aristotle's time is of the cosmos first.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: [[Concepts/World-Time and the Vulgar Concept of Time (Heidegger)|world-time vs the vulgar concept]] — Aristotle is named as the source of the "vulgar" sequence of nows. *Physics* IV is more than a sequence: the now as link, time as of motion, soul as counter.
- **[[Thinkers/Newton]]**: absolute time "flows equably" independent of motion — the inverse of Aristotle.
- **[[Thinkers/Ibn Rushd]]**: time uncreated is a premise of [[Concepts/Eternity of the World (Ibn Rushd)]], taken from VIII.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] If time requires a soul to count, what of the eternal motion before there are counters? Aristotle leaves the numerable/number split unresolved — the opening for Augustine.
- Heidegger's charge that Aristotle vulgarizes time as a sequence of nows under-reads the dependence on motion and the now-as-limit.
