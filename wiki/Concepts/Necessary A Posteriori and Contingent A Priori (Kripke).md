# Necessary A Posteriori and Contingent A Priori (Kripke)

> "'A priori' and 'necessary'… I shall not use the terms interchangeably here." A priori is epistemological; necessary is metaphysical. "Necessary a posteriori truths, and probably contingent a priori truths, both exist."

## Summary

The distinction that lets [[Sources/Naming and Necessity - Saul Kripke (1980)]] do its work. Kant had used necessity as a mark of the a priori (B 3–4). Kripke pries the axes apart.

- **A priori**: "those which can be known independently of any experience" (Lecture I, p. 34). An *epistemological* notion.
- **Necessary**: "might the world have been otherwise?" (Lecture I). A *metaphysical* notion. "This in and of itself has nothing to do with anyone's knowledge of anything" (p. 36).
- **Analytic** (stipulative here): true by virtue of meaning in all possible worlds — hence both necessary and a priori. Certainty is a third thing, not necessity.

**Necessary a posteriori.** Goldbach's conjecture: whichever truth-value it has, it has it by necessity; we now have no a priori knowledge of it (Lecture I). Identity statements between rigid designators, if true, are necessary though not known a priori: "we do not know a priori that Hesperus is Phosphorus… If, in fact, they are the same body… in any other possible world it will be true that Hesperus is Phosphorus" (Lecture II, pp. 104–105). "An identity statement between names, when true at all, is necessarily true, even though one may not know it a priori" (Lecture III, pp. 108–109). A world with two planets in those sky-positions is *not* a world in which Hesperus ≠ Phosphorus; we describe it in *our* language (p. 109). **Theoretical identifications** — "gold is the element with atomic number 79," "water is H₂O," "heat is molecular motion" — "are generally identities involving two rigid designators and therefore are examples of the necessary a posteriori" (Lecture III, p. 140). They "are not contingent truths but necessary truths in the strictest possible sense" (p. 125).

The appearance of contingency is **epistemic**: a qualitatively identical evidential situation, or a contingent identity of the *nonrigid* fixing descriptions. "Might have turned out otherwise" does not yield a possible world in which Hesperus is not Phosphorus.

Addendum (b): "Philosophical analysis tells us that they cannot be contingently true, so any empirical knowledge of their truth is automatically empirical knowledge that they are necessary" (p. 159). One can learn a mathematical truth a posteriori (computer, mathematician); if true, it is necessary. Kripke retracts ascribing to Kant the characterization of a priori *truth* as truth that *can* be known independently of experience, and rejects, for "standard human" a priori knowledge, that every necessary truth is knowable a priori.

**Contingent a priori.** The standard metre (Kripke: "meter"): the definition **fixes the reference** of a rigid designator; it is not a synonymous definition. "Even if we define what a meter is by reference to the standard meter stick, it will be a contingent truth and not a necessary one that that particular stick is one meter long. If it had been stretched, it would have been longer than one meter. And that is because we use the term 'one meter' rigidly to designate a certain length" (Lecture II, p. 75; full a priori claim, Lecture I, p. 56). Same pattern: 100°C; Neptune as "the planet causing these perturbations" (n. 33) — a priori from the fixing, not necessary. Heat = *that which is sensed by sensation S* is a priori and not necessary (Lecture III, pp. 135–137).

Frege's "sense" is criticized for conflating meaning with how reference is determined (Lecture I, p. 59).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: necessity and strict universality were marks of the a priori. Kripke keeps Kant's *wording* of a priori and denies the coextension. "Gold is a yellow metal" is a bad analytic example; it "can turn out to be false." See [[Concepts/Synthetic A Priori Judgments (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Hume]]**: the Fork aligned a priori / necessary / denial-is-contradiction against contingent / conceivable. Kripke's two extra cells (necessary a posteriori; contingent a priori) break that alignment. See [[Concepts/Hume's Fork (Relations of Ideas vs Matters of Fact)]].
- **[[Thinkers/W.V.O. Quine]]**: "Two Dogmas" treated the analytic/synthetic line as the home of necessity and then dissolved it. Kripke relocates necessity to metaphysics and lets empirical discovery yield necessary truth. See [[Contradictions/Kripke vs Quine - De Re Necessity vs Holism]].
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: two-dimensional semantics *domesticates* these cases as a necessary secondary intension with a contingent primary one, so they "never rule out any conceivable *world*." That is Chalmers, not Kripke. Kripke's Hesperus case is precisely a claim about what worlds there are, described in our language.
