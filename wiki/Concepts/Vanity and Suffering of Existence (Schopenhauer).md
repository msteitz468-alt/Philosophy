# Vanity and Suffering of Existence (Schopenhauer)
> Time is the form in which the will-to-live is shown the vanity of its striving. Pain is positive. Well-being is negative. Existence is something it were better never to have been.

## Summary

The systematic primary for the *worst of all possible worlds* is *WWR* Volume 2, Chapter XLVI. *Parerga* II Chs. XI–XII are “additional remarks” on vanity and suffering — the cluster reception flattened into “pessimism.” He does **not** title his own system that word.

**XLVI.** Happiness is negative, pain positive. Non-existence of the world would be preferable. Existence is a *debt* contracted at begetting, not a gift. Against Leibniz: this is *the worst of all possible worlds* — possible = able to exist and last; a little worse and it could not continue. “Consequently, since a worse world could not continue to exist, it is absolutely impossible; and so this world itself is the worst of all possible worlds.” Optimism is self-praise of the will-to-live and *pernicious* because it makes happiness the aim; work, misery, and death are the aim (Brahmanism, Buddhism, genuine Christianity). See [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]].

**Vanity** is the *form* of existence: infinite time and space versus the finite individual; “constant becoming without being”; “constant desire without satisfaction.” Time is the form in which the will-to-live, “imperishable” as thing-in-itself, is shown the vanity of that striving. Restlessness is “the original form of existence.” Happiness is not conceivable where Plato’s becoming-without-being rules. **Boredom** is “positive proof that, in itself, existence has no value.” The opposite — eternal rest, “neither coming into being nor passing away” — “must be that to which the denial of the will-to-live opens the way.”

**Suffering.** If suffering is not “the first and immediate object of our life,” existence is inexpedient. Pain is positive and felt; well-being and happiness are negative (elimination of a desire). Leibniz’s *Théodicée* §153 is a “palpable and pitiable sophism.” The world is “just a hell”; existence “something that it were better never to have been”; the “great sin of the world” produces its sufferings *metaphysically*. Original sin is “the only metaphysical truth” in the Old Testament. Regard the world as a penal colony. He attacks “orthodox optimism,” not under the self-name “pessimism.”

Knowledge raises susceptibility to pain. Highest susceptibility exists only with “the possibility of denying the will”; without that, it would be “purposeless cruelty.” See [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]], [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]].

Volume 1’s Aphorisms already used the negativity of pleasure *inside* an accommodation ([[Concepts/Eudaemonology as Accommodation (Schopenhauer)]]). Here the same coin is spent at the higher standpoint: not counsel for a less-unhappy life, but a verdict on existence.

## Key Claims

- Vanity is formal: time, becoming, desire without satisfaction.
- Boredom proves existence has no intrinsic value.
- Pain is positive; well-being is negative. Leibniz on evil-as-privation is a sophism.
- The world is a penal colony. Better never to have been.
- He attacks optimism. He does not baptize his own view “pessimism” in these chapters.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — additional remarks on *WWR* IV, not a new system.
- **[[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]]** — the vault contradiction is now two-sided from Schopenhauer’s *Parerga* as well as *WWR*: *Théodicée* §153 named; “best of possible worlds” still no theodicy.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — later will treat this verdict as decadence making itself imperative. Do not import that into 1851.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] He does apply “pessimistic” once to the *spirit of Christianity* (Claudius). That is not a self-title for the system.
