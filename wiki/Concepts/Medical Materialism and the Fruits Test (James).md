# Medical Materialism and the Fruits Test (James)

> Origins do not settle value. "By their fruits ye shall know them, not by their roots." Calling Paul an occipital lesion, Teresa a hysteric, or George Fox a disordered colon is **medical materialism** — and it is "illogical and arbitrary" unless one already has a psycho-physical theory of which neural conditions confer authority.

## Overview

Lecture I of *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (Gifford Lectures, Edinburgh 1901–02) splits two questions that "medical materialists" run together. An **existential** judgment asks what a state *is* — its nature, origin, history. A **spiritual** judgment (*Werthurtheil*) asks what it is *worth*. "Neither judgment can be deduced immediately from the other." Every mental state, including scientific and atheistic ones, is organically conditioned; so origin-refutation of religion is special pleading. The only available criteria of value are **immediate luminousness**, **philosophical reasonableness**, and **moral helpfulness**. Jonathan Edwards is recruited: "Our practice is the only sure evidence." Teresa's "jewels" are changed dispositions, not the rapture that produced them.

Lectures XIV–XV apply the same test to saintliness "on the whole," by common-sense human standards. Empiricism here is not "wanton" skepticism: unfit cults die when their fruits clash with indispensable human ideals. Judging fruits still tacitly theologizes — disbeliefs are a theology — but that is the honest price of an empirical science of religions.

## Key Arguments

- **Not anthropology, not second-hand religion.** The documents are personal, "acute fever" cases, not institutional habit. Pattern-setters are often "geniuses" with "nervous instability"; Fox can be *détraqué* without his mind being "unsound."
- **Medical materialism named and refused.** Reducing a religious state to a lesion, a viscus, or sex does not undermine spiritual authority. If there were "inspiration from a higher realm," a neurotic temperament might even be "the chief condition of the requisite receptivity." Pathology is studied because morbid cases isolate factors, not because they discredit the type.
- **Three criteria, not one.** Immediate delight is not enough; the state must also survive philosophical criticism and issue in moral helpfulness. The scriptural formula is fruits, not roots.
- **"On the whole" (XIV–XV).** Saintly virtues have excesses: fanaticism (loyalty in convulsive extreme), theopathy (sweet God-absorption with feeble intellect), purity as world-flight, charity that preserves the unfit. The perfect man fits only a perfect environment (Spencer). Saints remain *auctores*, "effective ferment," "leavens" of a better order — but "anyone who makes an out-and-out saint of himself does so at his peril."
- **Utility vs. truth postponed.** XIV–XV raise whether good fruits prove theological truth and send the question to Mysticism and Philosophy. The fruits test is a *value* test first.

## How James Uses This vs. Later Pragmatism

The 1902 test is an **empiricist theory of religious value**, not yet the 1907 genetic theory of truth. Lecture XVIII will apply Peirce's "principle of *pragmatism*" to God's attributes ("every difference must *make* a difference"; "cash-value in terms of particular experience"), but Lecture I does not use that vocabulary. Do not collapse "fruits not roots" into "the true is the expedient."

## Contrasts / Lineage

- **Vs. [[Concepts/Faith and Reason - Enthusiasm (Locke)]]**: Locke treats "strong persuasion" without evidence as enthusiasm to be judged by reason. James agrees that origin-stories (warmed brain, sex, colon) do not *establish* authority — but he also refuses them as *refutations*. Feeling can out-convince "mere logic"; he does not yet say it *ought* to (Lecture III).
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]]**: Lecture III uses Kant's *as if* Ideas only to show that empty concepts can still polarize a life. The fruits test is not Kantian moral faith; it is an empirical rating of documents.
- **With [[Concepts/Pragmatic Method (James)]]**: the 1907 method is the general solvent of which 1902 fruits-testing is the religious application — historically the other way around: Varieties first, *Pragmatism* five years later.
- **With [[Concepts/Conversion and the Subliminal Self (James)]]**: Coe: "The ultimate test of religious values is nothing psychological… but something ethical, definable only in terms of *what is attained*." Same rule: process (sudden or gradual, subliminal or not) does not settle worth.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Fruits can be good and the theology false — James raises this at the close of XIV–XV and does not let utility settle truth. See [[Concepts/Over-Beliefs and Piecemeal Supernaturalism (James)]].
- Does "on the whole" smuggle in a tacit theology of human flourishing? James admits it does.
