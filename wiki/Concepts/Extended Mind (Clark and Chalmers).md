# Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)
> Cognition is not bounded by skin and skull: when an external resource is reliably coupled to us and does the work a brain process would, it is literally part of the mind.

## Summary

The thesis is stated in Andy Clark and David Chalmers, "The Extended Mind," *Analysis* 58.1 (January 1998): 7–19 — now the vault's primary source ([[Sources/The Extended Mind - Andy Clark and David Chalmers (1998)]]). Authors are listed "in order of degree of belief in the central thesis." Two standard replies to "Where does the mind stop and the rest of the world begin?" are refused: the skin/skull boundary, and Putnam/Burge externalism about meaning carried over into mind. The third position is [[Concepts/Active Externalism (Clark and Chalmers)|active externalism]]: the environment drives cognitive processes in the here-and-now, not merely at the far end of a historical chain.

The argument has two stages. **Extended cognition** (§2–3): Tetris rotation by mind, by button, or by neural implant share a computational structure; if the implant is cognitive, the button is too. Kirsh and Maglio's [[Concepts/Epistemic Action (Kirsh and Maglio)|epistemic actions]] (physical rotation used to *find out* whether a piece fits) "demand spread of epistemic credit." That is the [[Concepts/Parity Principle (Clark and Chalmers)|parity principle]]. A coupled organism–world system is a cognitive system in its own right; remove the external component and competence drops as if part of the brain were removed. **Extended mind** (§4): experiences "may be determined internally," but **beliefs** can be constituted by environmental features. That is [[Arguments/Otto and Inga (Clark and Chalmers)|Otto and Inga]] — and Twin Otto, who is a skin-in duplicate with a different notebook and a different belief.

Reliable coupling, not location, marks the core package. Portability ("the Naked Mind") is answered: a slide rule on the belt or a Filofax always to hand is "coupling enough"; the biological brain already suffers sleep, intoxication, and damage. Evolution has "favoured on-board capacities which are especially geared to parasitizing the local environment." Language is "not a mirror of our inner states but a complement to them," and socially extended minds (partner, waiter, collaborator) are possible in principle. The self extends with the standing beliefs; interfering with someone's environment can have "the same moral significance as interfering with their person."

## Key Claims / Positions

- **No sacred membrane.** "When it comes to belief, there is nothing sacred about skull and skin. What makes some information count as a belief is the role it plays."
- **Active, not passive, externalism.** Putnam/Burge twins behave alike; Otto and Twin Otto do not. The relevant question is "is mind in the present?"
- **Consciousness does not extend.** The 1998 paper distinguishes cognitive processes (which need not be conscious) from experiences (which "may be determined internally"). Later Clark and later Chalmers both keep this restriction.
- **Four features of clear cases** (constancy, easy availability, automatic endorsement, past endorsement — the last arguable). The 1998 Internet typically fails; certain files may pass. Intermediate cases can be indeterminate.
- **Methodology, not terminology.** Treating cognition as extended changes how cognitive science should be done: methods once reserved for inner processes apply to the outer (Suchman, Hutchins, Thelen and Smith, Beer, Haugeland, Varela/Thompson/Rosch).

## From *Surfing Uncertainty* (Clark, 2016, ch. 8)

The 2016 book already supplies a neural story, more cautiously than 2023. "Nothing in the PP framework materially alters, as far as I can tell, the arguments previously presented, both pro and con, regarding the possibility of genuinely extended cognitive systems." What PP offers is "a specific, and highly 'extension-friendly', proposal concerning the shape of the specifically neural contribution" (8.9). Inner coalitions and bio-external tools (laptop, iPhone, abacus, "use the world as its own best model") are recruited by the same prediction-error + precision machinery. That is the recruitment puzzle answered *before* the 2023 slogans. Ch 6 refuses Hohwy's use of an "evidentiary boundary" to block this: PP inference need not throw away the world. Ch 9 adds designer environments and a rolling [[Concepts/Productive Laziness and Cognitive Niches (Clark)|cognitive niche]] that *train* generative models, not merely tidy a sensory channel.

## From *The Experience Machine* (Clark, 2023, ch. 6)

Clark calls the 1998 paper "a kind of modern classic" and gives the thesis what it originally lacked — an account of *what brains do*. [[Concepts/Predictive Processing (Clark)]]: "the brain does not care whether key information is stored in [its] own internal states… or outside, in notebooks, apps, and GPS systems." Predictive brains are "the perfect internal organs for the creation of extended minds"; we are "natural-born cyborgs" — "cyborg or hybrid minds created without the need for invasive implants." Susan Hurley's "magical membrane" is the rejected boundary; Jerry Fodor's "if the mind happens in space at all, it happens somewhere north of the neck" is the target. **Woven resources** are "a trusted nonbiological structure that is delicately and constantly coupled to the rest of the system that we recognize as 'you.'" The **recruitment puzzle** — why coupling is effortless rather than a deliberative act — is solved by "knowledge budgeting." Where we draw the self/mind boundary is "at least in part an ethical choice."

## From *Reality+* (Chalmers, 2022, ch. 16)

Ned Block's quip: the thesis "was false when we wrote the article in the 1990s but… has since become true" (smartphones, internet). Note that the 1998 paper itself already judged the Internet likely to *fail* its own criteria unless the user is "unusually computer-reliant, facile… and trusting." The updated argument (Ishi/Omar on the Sydney Opera House): denying parity is "biological chauvinism, or maybe skin and skull chauvinism." Extension requires the resource be "effectively glued to us"; another person can qualify (Ernie/Bert). **AR is a superior mind-extender** because of seamlessness — Chalmers explicitly invokes Heidegger's readiness-to-hand — on a trajectory notebook → smartphone → AR glasses → brain-computer interfaces → uploading. Consciousness itself is *not* claimed to extend. Against tech-pessimists from Plato's Thamus to Nicholas Carr: "Google makes us smarter, not stupider." Moral consequence: stealing a phone becomes "more akin to assault."

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]** and **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: the deepest resonance in the vault. The tool "ready-to-hand" that withdraws into transparent use ([[Concepts/Readiness-to-Hand and Presence-at-Hand (Heidegger)]]) is Otto's notebook. [[Concepts/Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)]] anticipates the anti-internalist claim. Dreyfus already quotes Chapman's "the world is its own best representation" ([[Concepts/Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)]]). Clark and Chalmers *count the notebook as mental*; Dreyfus describes the coupling and doubts that a learner unlike us can share it. The 1998 paper never cites Heidegger.
- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] / Thompson / Rosch**: named kin in the 1998 paper. *The Embodied Mind* is now primary. [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|Enaction]] is enactment of a perceiver-dependent *world*, not parity for a notebook-*belief*. Neighbouring family. See [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)]], [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]].
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: the Joycean machine and center-of-narrative-gravity self, culturally installed via writing and talking-to-oneself, is a close ally — both make the mind a brain-world hybrid. The 1998 extended *self* is the same move at the level of standing belief.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the direct antithesis of the sealed *res cogitans*. Without language, they say, "we might be much more akin to discrete Cartesian 'inner' minds."
- **[[Concepts/Meaning as Use (Wittgenstein)]]**: "Language… is not a mirror of our inner states but a complement to them" — a later-Wittgenstein tone against any picture theory of language as inner-state report.
- **[[Concepts/Knowledge as Perception of Agreement or Disagreement of Ideas (Locke)]]**: contrasts sharply — where Locke locates knowledge in inner ideas, Clark and Chalmers locate some of it in the world.
- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]**: the systematic refusal. Same PEM family; Otto's notebook is the brain "attempting to optimize its sensory input so it, as positioned over against the world, can better minimize error" (2013, p. 240). PEM stays "relative to just the nervous system" (p. 241). See [[Concepts/Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)]] and [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The two authors later split on consciousness while remaining allies on this thesis. See [[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]] vs [[Concepts/Hard Problem of Consciousness (Chalmers)]].
- > [!warning] Near-ally, different grain, with Dreyfus: [[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]].
- > [!warning] Hohwy 2013 keeps the mechanism and drops the extension: [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].
- Feature (4) (past endorsement), tampering, and unread pages are left open in 1998.
- The 1998 paper has no mechanism for recruitment; 2016 supplies an extension-friendly neural story without claiming to settle the debate; 2023 sharpens this into "perfect internal organs" / "natural-born cyborgs."
