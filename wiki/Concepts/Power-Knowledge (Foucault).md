# Power-Knowledge (Foucault)
> Power and knowledge are not opposed — power produces knowledge, and knowledge exercises power; the hyphen in "power-knowledge" (*pouvoir-savoir*) marks their irreducible entanglement.

## Summary

The power-knowledge concept emerges from Foucault's genealogical phase, especially from the GIP (Groupe d'Information sur les Prisons) prison activism (1971–) and *Surveiller et punir* (1975). The key move is rejecting both the Enlightenment view (knowledge is achieved by escaping power, by "speaking truth to power") and the crude Marxist view (knowledge is determined by class interests). The canonical formulation is now quoted from *Discipline and Punish* I.1:

> We should admit rather that power produces knowledge (and not simply by encouraging it because it serves power or by applying it because it is useful); that power and knowledge directly imply one another; that there is no power relation without the correlative constitution of a field of knowledge, nor any knowledge that does not presuppose and constitute at the same time power relations. … it is not the activity of the subject of knowledge that produces a corpus of knowledge, useful or resistant to power, but power-knowledge, the processes and struggles that traverse it and of which it is made up, that determines the forms and possible domains of knowledge.

Power is not a possession or a force exercised top-down by a sovereign — it is relational, exercised in networks of relations, and **productive**: it produces subjects, bodies, and knowledge rather than merely suppressing them. "This power is exercised rather than possessed." Every major institution (prison, clinic, school, factory, army) is simultaneously a site of power and a site of the production of knowledge about the individuals it processes. The prison generates criminology; the clinic generates medicine; the school generates pedagogy. This knowledge feeds back into the exercise of power over the individuals it categorizes.

*The History of Sexuality* I (1976) adds the third term **pleasure** and writes the method chapter of the analytics. The object is "the regime of power-knowledge-pleasure that sustains the discourse on human sexuality" (p. 11). "Indeed, it is in discourse that power and knowledge are joined together" (p. 100). "Between techniques of knowledge and strategies of power, there is no exteriority" (p. 98). The **juridico-discursive** representation of power — negative relation, insistence of the rule, cycle of prohibition, logic of censorship, uniformity of the apparatus (pp. 83–85) — is what *both* the thematics of repression *and* the theory that law constitutes desire still share. "In political thought and analysis, we still have not cut off the head of the king" (pp. 88–89). The circular project: "conceive of sex without the law, and power without the king" (p. 91).

Power is not first a state, a rule-form, or a class domination; those are **terminal forms**. "By power, I do not mean 'Power' as a group of institutions and mechanisms that ensure the subservience of the citizens of a given state… [nor] a mode of subjugation which, in contrast to violence, has the form of the rule… [nor] a general system of domination exerted by one group over another" (p. 92). It is "the name that one attributes to a complex strategical situation in a particular society" (p. 93). "Power is everywhere; not because it embraces everything, but because it comes from everywhere." Five propositions (pp. 94–96): not held; immanent and productive; from below; intentional and nonsubjective; resistance never exterior. "Where there is power, there is resistance, and yet, or rather consequently, this resistance is never in a position of exteriority in relation to power." No "great Refusal" — a plurality of resistances *inside* the strategic field, whose "strategic codification" can make a revolution possible. Four cautionary rules (pp. 98–102): immanence; continual variations; double conditioning of local tactics and overall strategy; **tactical polyvalence of discourses** (the same elements serve contrary strategies — including the "reverse" discourse by which "homosexuality began to speak in its own behalf… often in the same vocabulary, using the same categories by which it was medically disqualified," p. 101).

The **micro-physics of power** describes the capillary distribution of power through the tiniest practices, habits, and techniques of bodies, rather than concentrating it in the state or economy. The **examination** (III.2) is the visible superimposition of the two: a ritual that objectifies subjects, archives them as "cases," and fabricates individuality. "Power produces reality… domains of objects and rituals of truth." Once a "technological threshold" is crossed, power and knowledge reinforce each other circularly; the examination is the technical matrix of the human sciences as investigation was of the natural sciences. Walnut Street's files and four-class distribution by *disposition* (not crime) already show objectification born *inside* tactics of power (II.2). Part Four then names the delinquent as the object-effect of penitentiary knowledge: "Knowable man (soul, individuality, consciousness, conduct, whatever it is called) is the object-effect of this analytical investment, of this domination-observation."

## Key Claims

- Power is not repressive but productive: it creates identities ("the criminal," "the homosexual," "the delinquent"), knowledge (criminology, psychiatry, sexology), and normativity.
- Every form of knowledge is embedded in power relations; there is no "view from nowhere" — but this is not relativism, since genealogy can distinguish better and worse uses of power-knowledge.
- The "specific intellectual" (who speaks from concrete knowledge of a specific institution) is politically more effective than the "universal intellectual" (who speaks in the name of universal humanity).
- Resistance is always already present wherever power is exercised — there is no position outside power, only forms of counter-conduct within it. Volume 1 states this as interiority, not as a later interview slogan: "this resistance is never in a position of exteriority in relation to power" (p. 95).
- Same-sex "reverse discourse" is the textbook case of tactical polyvalence: the psychiatric category becomes a vocabulary of demand.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- If there is no position outside power-knowledge, is Foucault's own genealogical analysis also a power-knowledge operation? The book cannot escape what it analyzes.
- Late Foucault's turn to "care of the self" suggests a space of ethical practice not entirely determined by power — in tension with the totalizing picture of power-knowledge.

> [!warning] Compare with [[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]: Marx grounds ideological distortion in class relations and the mode of production. Foucault's power-knowledge is more diffuse (no single source, no base/superstructure) and analyses the body rather than labor as the primary site of subjection.
