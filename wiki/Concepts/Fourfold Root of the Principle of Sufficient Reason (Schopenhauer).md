# Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)
> Nothing is without a reason why it is rather than it is not — but the reason is four, not one, and it never applies to the world as a totality.

## Summary

The vault's primary is the 1847 second edition of *On the Fourfold Root*, not the 1813 Jena dissertation and not the flattened sentence in [[Sources/The World as Will and Idea - Arthur Schopenhauer (1819)]] that treats "space, time, and causality" as one template. See [[Sources/On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2012)]].

Wolff's formula is kept as the most general expression (§5): *Nihil est sine ratione cur potius sit quam non sit.* That formula is an abstraction (law of homogeneity, §1) from **four** ground-consequent relations, each a synthetic a priori law for one class of objects for the subject.

**§16 (the root).** Cognizing consciousness "divides into subject and object and comprises nothing else. To be object for the subject and to be our representation are the same." Representations stand in a connection determinable *a priori*; nothing exists for us of itself or isolated. That connection *is* the principle of sufficient reason. It splits into four species because there are four classes of objects.

To demand a **proof** of the principle already assumes it: a proof *is* exhibition of a ground — "a proof for the right to require a proof" (§14).

## The four roots

### 1. Becoming — law of causality (§§17–25)

First class: intuitive, complete, empirical representations. PSR here = *principium rationis sufficientis fiendi*. Cause and effect are **states/alterations**, not objects. "There is absolutely no sense in saying that an object is the cause of another." The chain is beginningless; a "first cause" is a contradiction. Causality has three forms: cause in the narrow sense, **stimulus**, **motive**. See [[Concepts/Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)]] for §21 (the understanding *creates* the outer world from sensation). The same triad is already aphoristic in *On Vision and Colours* §1 (inorganic / plant / animal).

### 2. Knowing — truth of judgments (§§26–34)

Second class: concepts, "representations of representations." Reason is the human faculty that abstracts them; animals have understanding, not reason. No judgment is intrinsically true. Four kinds of truth by four kinds of ground:

- **Logical** — grounded on another judgment (syllogism)
- **Empirical** — grounded immediately on first-class intuition
- **Transcendental** — grounded in a priori forms (space, time, causality)
- **Metalogical** — the four laws of thought, including PSR of knowing itself

§33: "one ground presenting itself as fourfold, which I figuratively call the fourfold root." Reason has "absolutely no material, but simply a formal content" (§34). Kant's practical reason / categorical imperative first "provided the occasion" for the post-Kantian fairy tale of a supersensible faculty. See [[Thinkers/Kant]], [[Thinkers/Hegel]].

### 3. Being — space and time (§§35–39)

Third class: pure a priori intuitions of space and time. *Principium rationis sufficientis essendi*: parts of space and time determine one another (position; succession). Distinct from cause and from ground of knowledge. Arithmetic tracks time; geometry tracks space. Euclid often gives *ratio cognoscendi* (conviction) instead of *ratio essendi* (insight) — the "mousetrap proof."

### 4. Acting — law of motivation (§§40–45)

Fourth class: a class with one member, the **subject of willing**, given only to inner sense (time, not space). Identity of the cognizing and willing subject is "the knot of the world" — "the miracle *par excellence*" (§42). **Motivation is causality seen from within** (§43) — "the cornerstone of my whole metaphysics." Will's influence on cognition is *not* causality but that identity (§44). Body = will is **not** argued here; it is pointed to *WWR*. See [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]].

## Two results (§52)

1. PSR is the general expression of four a priori laws from one constitution of the cognitive faculty. Because it is rooted in the intellect, **it cannot be applied to the totality of existing things — the world — including this intellect in which the world resides.** The cosmological proof is just that misapplication.
2. There is **no absolute ground and no ground *per se*** — "no more a ground in general than there is a triangle in general." Only one of the four types, and only *inside* the world of objects.

**§49:** "any necessity is conditioned; thus, absolute, i.e., unconditioned necessity, is a contradiction in terms." Four necessities: logical, physical, mathematical, moral (action from innate character given a motive).

## 1813 vs 1847

Do not flatten. 1813 still used Kant's categories, would not "rigidify in a thing in itself," and treated will as causal. 1847 is rewritten after *WWR*: categories jettisoned; appearance/thing-in-itself restored as Kant's greatest merit; will does not cause the body. The Cambridge text *is* 1847.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — the propaedeutic *WWR*'s first preface demanded. Vault *WWR* I had flattened PSR into one Kantian template. This book specifies four laws and forbids the transcendent use.
- **[[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]]** — first *formally* advances PSR (§12) but "knew nothing further" than why it is so and not otherwise. Wolff's slogan is kept as abstraction; God-as-reason of the *totality* is exactly what §52 forbids. See [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — logical vs transcendental PSR distinguished (§13); Second Analogy proof of causality rejected (§23); twelve categories dropped; calling the thing-in-itself the *ground* of appearance violates Kant's own insight that contingency is itself phenomenal.
- **[[Thinkers/Hume]]** — first asked *whence* the causal law has authority (§12). Hume collapses consequence into sequence; Kant the reverse (§23). Schopenhauer: causality is a priori because it *constitutes* intuition.
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]** — Descartes's mix of cause and cognitive ground becomes *causa sui* / pantheism (§7–8). Ontological proof = sophism switching *ratio fiendi* for *ratio cognoscendi* (§50).
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]]** — 1847 Preface "clumsy charlatan"; §34 "infamous charlatan." Load-bearing claim is the denial of material-cognitive reason, not a Hegel exegesis.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The existing contradiction page said Schopenhauer holds "No Sufficient Reason." That is false for this book. He *affirms* PSR as the form of all representation and *forbids* it of the thing-in-itself and of the world as totality. See [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]].
- > [!warning] 1813 ≠ 1847. Quote the Cambridge text as 1847 unless a sentence is labeled 1813.
