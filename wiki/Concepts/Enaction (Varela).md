# Enaction (Varela)
> Cognition is not the representation of a pregiven world by a pregiven mind. It is embodied action that enacts a world of significance through a history of structural coupling.

## Summary

Stated in the 1991 introduction and defined in ch. 8 of [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)|*The Embodied Mind*]]:

> "cognition is not the representation of a pregiven world by a pregiven mind but is rather the enactment of a world and a mind on the basis of a history of the variety of actions that a being in the world performs."

**Embodied** highlights two points: cognition depends on having a body with sensorimotor capacities, and those capacities are embedded in a biological, psychological, and cultural context. **Action** means perception and action are "fundamentally inseparable in lived cognition."

The **enactive approach** in a nutshell: "(1) perception consists in perceptually guided action and (2) cognitive structures emerge from the recurrent sensorimotor patterns that enable action to be perceptually guided." The reference point is the perceiver's sensorimotor structure in a *perceiver-dependent* world, not recovery of a perceiver-independent one.

They distinguish **weak** representation (aboutness, construal) from **strong** representation: (1) the world is pregiven; (2) cognition is *of* that world; (3) we represent its features and act on those representations. Strong representation is the target. Recovery-of-a-pregiven-world views — Marr-style "shape from shading," color-as-surface-reflectance, cognition as problem-solving in a crystalline task domain — are what they reject.

They also reject the subjectivist opposite ("the egg"): the system simply *projects* its world. Color forces a middle: "world and perceiver specify each other." "In the enactive view, although mind and world arise together in enaction, their manner of arising in any particular situation is not arbitrary."

## Mechanisms Named in the 1991 Text

- **Operational closure / autonomy.** "A system that has operational closure is one in which the results of its processes are those processes themselves." Autonomous systems "do not operate by representation. Instead of representing an independent world, they enact a world as a domain of distinctions that is inseparable from the structure embodied by the cognitive system." Referred to Varela, *Principles of Biological Autonomy*. The word **autopoiesis does not appear** in ch. 7–8; Thompson 2016 reads it back as a source.
- **Structural coupling.** Bittorio, a cellular-automata ring in a soup of 0s and 1s, has no program for "odd sequences." Closure plus coupling *selects* a domain of distinctions from randomness. "We describe Bittorio's world as enacted through a history of structural coupling."
- **Color as paradigm.** Opponent-process appearance has no match in surface reflectance or the structure of light. "The properties that specify what colors are simply have no nonexperiential, physical counterparts." "We will not be able to explain color if we seek to locate it in a world independent of our perceptual capacities." Color is "neither pregiven nor represented but rather experiential and enacted." Trichromatic, tetra-, and pentachromatic spaces are *incommensurable* — not one optimal recovery of a shared world. Berlin & Kay / Rosch–Dani categories are "experiential, consensual, and embodied."
- Illustrations: Held & Hein kittens; Bach-y-Rita camera; Freeman olfaction; Piaget (child as enactive agent, theorist still objectivist); Johnson image schemas; Lakoff & Johnson "experientialist" approach (called "consonant").
- Completed by [[Concepts/Natural Drift (Varela)|natural drift]] (ch. 9): "Evolution as natural drift is the biological counterpart of cognition as embodied action." Formal answers: cognition = that history of coupling; via multilevel sensorimotor subnetworks; adequate when it enters or shapes a world. Intelligence = "enter into a shared world of significance." Brooks: "use the world as its own model."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] / [[Thinkers/Evan Thompson]] / [[Thinkers/Eleanor Rosch]]**: the 1991 definition above. Thompson 2016 restates: the living body is self-producing and enacts relevance; cognition belongs to the relational domain of body–environment coupling. Rosch 2016 splits phase 1 (samsaric world-making) from phase 2 (nondual knowing).
- **Merleau-Ponty**: the named ancestor — double embodiment, *Umwelt*, the *entre-deux*. No thinker page yet; *Phenomenology of Perception* is outstanding.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: same anti-recovery, anti-world-model target. Dreyfus's body is a condition of *relevance* for intelligence; enaction adds operational closure, color science, and evolution. Dreyfus does not take the enacted-world metaphysics as far, and he rejects the book's introspectionist phenomenology.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: 1998 [[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)|extended mind]] lists this book as kin. Clark counts a notebook as *mental*; enaction says a *world* is brought forth. Later PP still talks of generative *models*. Do not collapse.
- **J. J. Gibson**: near-monism — affordances "always there," "Invariance comes from reality." Enaction: the environment is enacted; perception is sensorimotor enactment, not "direct detection."
- **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: later life-first neighbor (Maturana/autopoiesis via Thompson 2016). Seth's "controlled hallucination" still has a generative model of a body. Enaction's 1991 wording refuses recovery *and* projection.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Strong representation vs enaction: [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]].
- They question "that the world is independent of the knower" and still allow a milieu of perturbations (Bittorio's 0s and 1s; organisms must encounter physical and chemical agents). Best gloss: the *cognitive / perceived* world is enacted; they do not, in ch. 8, deny a physical medium.
- Ch. 10 warns: grasping "enaction" as a real third term vitiates the Madhyamaka dialectic. The concept is a raft, not a ground.
