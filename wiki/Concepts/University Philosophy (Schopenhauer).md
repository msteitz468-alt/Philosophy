# University Philosophy (Schopenhauer)
> Philosophy as a profession produces philosophy by government order. Truth takes only a back seat. Seek it anywhere but at the universities.

## Summary

“On university philosophy” (*Parerga* I) is Schopenhauer’s metaphilosophy of institutions: a tirade by design, not a calm critique. Academic philosophy can flag the subject and catch a few minds; that gain is outweighed by “philosophy as a profession” and “philosophy by government order” versus “the free search for truth.” A state will not pay people to contradict pulpits. University philosophy must therefore run “parallel” to established religion and is “essentially… a paraphrase and apology for the religion of the land.” A philosophy that denies Christian fundamentals is said to be “either wrong or, *even if true, still useless*.”

The rare exception is a genuine philosopher who is also a lecturer ([[Thinkers/Kant]]). He kept philosopher and professor separate by not lecturing his own doctrine; he would have been “more magnificent… more pure” without the chair. Two masters — “the world and the truth” — cannot be served together. Earning by philosophy is the ancient mark of the **Sophist** versus Socrates. These are “the fruits of ‘philosophy for hire’.” Philosophy “thrives only in free mountain air.” Professors *playing* philosopher produce “comic philosophers.” See [[Thinkers/Socrates]].

[[Thinkers/Hegel]] is the case. Twenty years of “buffoonery” and “gibberish” paid because they *appeared* to agree with established religion. The Hegelian state as “the absolutely perfected ethical organism” is “a true apotheosis of philistinism.” University philosophy **displaced Kant** with “the three trumpeted sophists” (Fichte, Schelling, Hegel — do not create Fichte or Schelling thinker pages from this). Professors borrow Kant’s terms as toys. They apply **immanent/transcendent** to whether “the good Lord” sits *in* the world or *outside* it — location-fudge, not Kant’s usage of cognition. An “impersonal God is a mere philosophy professor’s fib.” Every professor is *defensor fidei*; philosophy is *supposed* to be theology *in majorem Dei gloriam*.

The **true** purpose of university philosophy, conceded as statecraft not as philosophy: to give future barristers, doctors, teachers an inner orientation matching government intent. Distinguish *applied* (state) philosophy from *pure* philosophy, whose only purpose is truth. Lectern-men live by *primum vivere, deinde philosophari*; they test new systems first against religion, government, and fashion, then smother merit by silence.

Curriculum cap: **teach only logic** (completed, demonstrable) **and a one-semester history from Thales to Kant**, as primer; read originals. Hegelian a-priori history of systems is presumption. Chairs belong only to *already created* sciences (`tradere`). Philosophy “first has to be found.”

## Key Claims

- Official chairs can advertise the subject; they cannot be the home of the free search for truth.
- State pay entails religious parallelism. Truth is second.
- Living *for* a cause vs living *off* it; those who *are* it vs those who *represent* it. “The atmosphere of freedom is indispensable to truth.”
- Hegelry is not a mistake in a system; it is what happens when the criterion is pulpit-agreement plus fees. State-apotheosis = philistinism.
- Immanent/transcendent, used of God’s *location*, is a professor’s theft of Kant’s terms.
- Teach logic + Thales-to-Kant. Stop the shadow-boxing.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — self-position as independent pilgrim; prize essay on the will treated as *non avenu*; nature is aristocratic. The rant is metaphilosophy, not just the biography of a failed Berlin lecturer.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — the one chair-holder who still counted as a philosopher; *Critique* as theology’s letter of notice; then displaced by the three sophists.
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]]** — reception, not a doctrine digest: thought-automaton, *Jetztzeit*, apotheosis of the state, corruption of a generation.
- **[[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]]** — **do not flatten**. Hadot’s diagnosis that modern university philosophy is “the form of life of a professor of philosophy” is a cousin. Schopenhauer’s version is earlier and angrier: independence vs salary; Saturnalia of unconstrained *insight*; not spiritual exercises, not askesis, not a usable ancient model of life.
