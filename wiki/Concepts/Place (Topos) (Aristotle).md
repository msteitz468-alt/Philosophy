# Place (Topos) (Aristotle)
> Place is "the innermost motionless boundary of what contains" — not a body, not form, not matter, and not an empty interval.

## Summary

*Physics* IV.1–5. Physics must treat place: existents are "somewhere" (the non-existent is nowhere — "where is the goat-stag or the sphinx?"); primary motion is locomotion. Mutual replacement and the six natural directions (up, down, and the rest) show that place exists and has **potencies**, not merely relations to us.

What place is not: body (two bodies would coincide); form; matter; a self-subsistent interval between surfaces. Form and matter are not separate from the thing; place can be left behind.

What it is: "the innermost motionless boundary of what contains is place." A vessel is portable place; place is a non-portable vessel. A body is in place iff another body contains it. The heaven, or the All as a whole, is **not** in place; its parts are. Soul and heaven are in place only indirectly. Zeno's regress (if everything is in a place, place is in a place…) stops because the first place is "in" as a **limit**, not as in a place.

**Natural places**: light goes to the outer region, heavy to the middle; each kind remains in its proper place as part to whole. The nested series earth–water–air–aether–heaven is a structure of places, not a container-space.

This is why there is no void ([[Concepts/Atomism - Atoms and Void (Leucippus and Democritus)]]). Void would be "place bereft of body." Motion does not require it (the full can alter; bodies make room for one another). A separated void would destroy natural difference: no up/down/middle, no reason to stop, no ratio of void to full (infinite speed), equal velocities for unequal weights. Rarity and density need no interstitial void: the same matter is potentially large and small.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Place = innermost motionless boundary of the container.
- The cosmos as a whole is not in a place.
- Places have natural potencies (up/down).
- Void is neither needed nor possible.

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Democritus]]** / Leucippus: void as the interval that breaks continuity and makes motion possible. Aristotle's IV.6–9 is the canonical denial.
- **[[Thinkers/Newton]]**: absolute space is exactly the self-subsistent interval Aristotle rejected.
- **[[Thinkers/Leibniz]]**: relational space is closer to Aristotle than Newton is, but without natural places.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: Dasein's spatiality is de-distancing and directionality, not containment. Aristotle's eight senses of "in" include "in the world" as in-place because in the air, and the air is in the world — containment, not dwelling. See [[Concepts/Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Natural place is the first casualty of inertial physics. The definition of place as a boundary can survive; the potencies of up and down cannot.
- Projectile motion (Book VIII: successive media) is the weak point the medieval impetus tradition and then inertia will attack.
