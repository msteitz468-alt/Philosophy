# Necessity of Origin and Natural Kind Essences (Kripke)

> "How could a person originating from different parents, from a totally different sperm and egg, be this very woman? … anything coming from a different origin would not be this object." The same move, applied to stuffs and species, makes "gold is the element with atomic number 79" and "water is H₂O" necessary a posteriori.

## Summary

Lecture III of [[Sources/Naming and Necessity - Saul Kripke (1980)]] extends rigidity from names to origins and to **natural-kind** terms ("common names").

**Origin.** Queen Elizabeth could not have come from a totally different sperm and egg. "If a material object has its origin from a certain hunk of matter, it could not have had its origin in any other matter" (Lecture III, p. 114 n. 56 — the footnote the 1980 preface later says has "problems" he did not then see). This table could not have come from another block, or from Thames ice. These are *de re* essences that can be discovered empirically. They are not dictionary clusters.

**Natural kinds.** Terms like 'gold', 'tiger', 'water', 'heat', 'light' are closer to proper names than to Frege–Russell / cluster descriptions. Identifying marks (yellow, metallic; striped; watery; producing sensation *S*) need be neither necessary nor sufficient. There can be **fool's gold**, **fool's water**, **fool's cats** (Lecture III, p. 128). Kant's "gold is a yellow metal" can turn out false. Given what science finds the **nature** to be, the identifications are necessary:

- gold = the element with atomic number 79
- water = H₂O
- heat = molecular motion
- cats are animals

"Such statements representing scientific discoveries about what this stuff is are not contingent truths but necessary truths in the strictest possible sense" (p. 125). Putnam's talk of "less necessary" is treated as epistemological; Kripke notes Putnam "does not base his considerations on the apparatus of necessary versus a priori truths" (pp. 122–123 n. 62).

**Unicorns** (Lecture I teaser; Addendum (a)): no counterfactual situation is properly describable as one in which there would have been unicorns, because the myth does not fix a unique internal structure. "There is no actual or possible species of which we can say that it would have been the species of unicorns" (p. 157). Analogously for Holmes.

Addendum (g): "a good deal of what contemporary philosophy regards as mere physical necessity is actually necessary tout court" (p. 164). How far this can be pushed is left open.

The heat case is the setup for [[Arguments/Identity Thesis and Pain (Kripke)]]: heat is picked out by an *accidental* property (producing sensation *S*); the identification with molecular motion is then necessary. Pain is not picked out that way.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Putnam** (no thinker page): independent development (*"It Ain't Necessarily So"*; cats/automata). Ally on kinds; not the source of the necessary/a priori split.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: "gold is a yellow metal" as analytic — rejected.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: later uses water/H₂O as the parade case of a posteriori necessity that 2-D semantics then reinterprets. Kripke's claim is stronger than a claim about *descriptions of* worlds: given what gold *is*, it could not have been something else.
- **[[Thinkers/Ian Hacking]]**: *Representing and Intervening* already notes Putnam's causal-historical account of theoretical terms ("electron") with mixed success. This page is Kripke on *kind terms in natural language*, not a theory-change semantics.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: origin (sperm and egg) "implicitly suggests a rejection of the Cartesian picture" (Lecture III, p. 155 n. 77) — contact with Nagel's later monism is loose.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Footnote 56 (necessity of origin for a hunk of matter) is the note the preface flags as having problems and as *not* a proof of "essentialism from the theory of reference" alone.
- How far "physical necessity" is "necessary tout court" is explicitly unfinished (Addendum (g)).
- > [!warning] Do not import "primary intension = watery stuff" as Kripke's analysis. He says the identifying marks are neither necessary nor sufficient; he does not build a two-dimensional semantics.
