# Substrate-Independence (Bostrom)

> Mental states can supervene on any of a broad class of physical substrates; a computer that structurally replicates a brain at synaptic grain would have subjective experience. The simulation argument's philosophy-of-mind premise.

## Summary

§II of [[Sources/Are You Living in a Computer Simulation - Nick Bostrom (2003)]] imports one assumption from philosophy of mind and then immediately weakens it. The common thesis: "mental states can supervene on any of a broad class of physical substrates. Provided a system implements the right sort of computational structures and processes, it can be associated with conscious experiences." Carbon inside a cranium is not essential; "silicon-based processors inside a computer could in principle do the trick as well."

Bostrom does **not** need:

- necessary (analytic or metaphysical) functionalism;
- the claim that behaving like a human, including passing the [[Concepts/Imitation Game (Turing)|Turing test]], would suffice.

He needs only the **attenuated** claim: "it would suffice for the generation of subjective experiences that the computational processes of a human brain are structurally replicated in suitably fine-grained detail, such as on the level of individual synapses." Neurotransmitters and other sub-synaptic chemicals are not ignored: they "affect subjective experience only *via* their direct or indirect influence on computational activities." If there can be no experiential difference without a difference in synaptic discharges, synaptic grain is enough.

This is the condition under which simulated ancestor-people count as observers, so that the ratio *f_sim* in the [[Arguments/Simulation Argument (Bostrom)|simulation argument]] is a ratio of *minds*, not of empty puppets.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nick Bostrom]]**: a working premise, "quite widely accepted," not argued in the paper. The 2014 control-problem work ([[Sources/Superintelligence - Nick Bostrom (2014)]]) can largely *bracket* consciousness; the 2003 argument cannot.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: closest developed defense. [[Concepts/Organizational Invariance and the Qualia Arguments (Chalmers)|Organizational invariance]] and fading qualia ([[Concepts/Machine Consciousness and Mind Uploading (Chalmers)]]) argue the same synaptic-replacement claim Bostrom assumes. Chalmers's [[Concepts/Strong AI and Computational Sufficiency (Chalmers)|strong AI]] is stronger (a class of computations suffices, with natural necessity) than Bostrom's "in fact" version.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]** / **[[Thinkers/Douglas Hofstadter]]**: substrate-independence is the house doctrine — the self as program ([[Concepts/Center of Narrative Gravity (Dennett)]]), consciousness as strange-loop pattern. Bostrom is in this camp on the premise, silent on qualia-deflation.
- **[[Thinkers/Norbert Wiener]]**: "organism as message" is an earlier identity-as-pattern claim, used by Wiener as an *alignment warning*, not as a license for simulated observers.
- **[[Thinkers/John Searle]]**: the direct opponent. [[Concepts/Biological Naturalism (Searle)]] locates consciousness in the brain's specific biological causal powers; syntax never yields semantics ([[Concepts/Chinese Room Argument (Searle)]]). If Searle is right, ancestor-simulations do not add observers and horn (3) of the trilemma is empty.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: not primarily a consciousness claim. Dreyfus denies that formal structure is the right model of *intelligence* ([[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]]). One can accept Bostrom's attenuated consciousness thesis and still hold that GOFAI never implements the relevant organization.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: "what it is like" can in principle be had by a silicon system; Nagel's objection is that we have no conception of how the physical (of any substrate) *is* the experiential, not that the substrate must be carbon.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Load-bearing for [[Arguments/Simulation Argument (Bostrom)]]. If biological-substrate views win, *f_sim* among *conscious* observers collapses and the anthropic step fails — the same warning already on [[Concepts/Machine Consciousness and Mind Uploading (Chalmers)]].
- Bostrom's "in fact" weakening avoids modal claims Chalmers later makes. It also makes the premise hostage to future neuroscience: if sub-synaptic chemistry turns out to make an experiential difference that no computational surrogate captures, the grain he named is too coarse.
