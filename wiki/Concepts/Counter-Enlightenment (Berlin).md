# Counter-Enlightenment (Berlin)

> Berlin’s name for the cluster of eighteenth- and early-nineteenth-century writers who broke the Enlightenment’s monism — one rational method, one human nature, one right life — without (on his reading) becoming relativists. Vico and Herder are the healthy fruit; Maistre is the dark pole.

## Summary

[[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]] does not treat “the Enlightenment” as a mistake to be reversed. The *philosophes*’ services against oppression are “not in question.” What he rejects is the **uniformitarian** claim: man “of a piece with nature,” invariant like Newton’s universe; ends timeless; Voltaire’s peaks-only history (Athens, Rome, Florence, Louis XIV); Spinoza’s claim that any truth could have been found at any time.

The **Counter-Enlightenment**, as this book uses it, is the discovery that **cultures** — interlinked law, religion, art, science, custom, language, myth — are distinct ways of life, “not very old” as an idea. Renaissance classicism, paradoxically, produced the opposite insight: irreconcilable differences of outlook. Bishop Lowth treats the Old Testament as a national epic, not to be judged by Sophocles or Boileau.

Two fruits:

- **Cultural history** ([[Thinkers/Giambattista Vico]], [[Thinkers/Johann Gottfried Herder]], then Scott in fiction): many cultures, different and sometimes incompatible scales of value, yet **understandable** by sympathetic insight (*fantasia*, *einfühlen*). This is [[Concepts/Pluralism versus Relativism (Berlin)|pluralism, not relativism]].
- **The dark pole** ([[Thinkers/Joseph de Maistre]], and, in other essays, Hamann, Möser, a strain of Burke): tradition, prejudice, throne-and-altar, violence as the grain of the world. Berlin reads Maistre as proto-fascist in temperament, not as Mussolini’s author. See the Maistre page for hedges.

Romanticism is adjacent but not identical: Fichte melts objective truth in the normative sphere into authenticity and will, with nationalist sequels. That story continues on [[Concepts/Romantic Will (Berlin)]].

## Who Is In, as This Book Names Them

- **Vico** — “true father” of the modern concept of culture and of cultural pluralism. *Scienza nuova*; *fantasia*; *verum/factum*. Not encapsulated epochs (contra Spengler).
- **Herder** — uniqueness and incommensurability of national cultures; “centre of gravity”; garden of many flowers. Essay 3 still hopes dissimilar contributions need not conflict; essay 4 treats him as a pluralist of uncombinable peaks.
- **Hamann, Justus Möser, Burke, Maistre** — anti-Enlightenment, **not** relativists (Alleged Relativism, pp. 81, 91).
- **Sixteenth-century jurists** (Hotman, Coke, Hale, Pasquier, Dumoulin) — cultural diversity / equally objective different codes; a pre-history.
- **Targets:** Voltaire, Condorcet, Helvétius, Descartes (history unworthy), the *lumen naturale* as everywhere the same. Montesquieu and Hume are **not** relativists of ends: variety of *means*, same central *goals*.

## Method (versus Lovejoy)

This is the History of Ideas method Outstanding Sources named after Lovejoy.

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur O. Lovejoy]]** tracks **unit-ideas** (plenitude, continuity, gradation) as they recombine. He even names “uniformitarianism,” which Berlin cites as the rejected alternative (pp. 88, 93).
- **Berlin** tracks **value-collision** and the political cost of monism. The unit is not a conceptual atom but a **vision of life** that can become an army.

They can be used together. They are not the same method. Lovejoy’s Chain *breaks* when temporalized; Berlin’s monism *kills* when political. Do not say Berlin “applies Lovejoy.”

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Enlightenment (as Berlin paints it):** one method, one nature, one right life; history as progress toward that life. Not “relativist.”
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]] / [[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]:** take historicist conflict and restore a happy ending. Not Counter-Enlightenment.
- **[[Concepts/Utopianism and the Pursuit of the Ideal (Berlin)]]:** the monism the CE attacks; Herder’s many Valhallas make one world-Utopia “internally self-contradictory.”
- Hamann is named, not given a thinker page. *Three Critics of the Enlightenment* (Vico, Hamann, Herder) is a different book, not ingested.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] “Counter-Enlightenment” is Berlin’s grouping. Vico died in 1744; he is not a German Romantic. Do not turn the label into a school the authors joined.
- > [!warning] Berlin admires Vico/Herder’s insight and rejects Maistre’s politics. The concept holds both. Do not sanitize the CE into liberal pluralism, and do not smear Vico with Maistre.
- The 1958 lecture is a different cut (liberty). This page is history of ideas.
