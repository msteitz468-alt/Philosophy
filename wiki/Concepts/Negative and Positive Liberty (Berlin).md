# Negative and Positive Liberty (Berlin)

> Berlin’s 1958 pairing: **negative** liberty is the area of non-interference (“how many doors are open”); **positive** liberty is self-mastery (“who governs me?”). The pairing organized postwar debate. This page is from a 2013 reception volume, not from the lecture as a primary.

## Summary

In “Two Concepts of Liberty” Isaiah Berlin proposes to consider two of “more than two hundred senses” of freedom: the **negative** sense (what is the area within which a person or group should be left to do or be what they are able, without interference?) and the **positive** sense (who or what is the source of control?) (TC, 168–169, 177). He treats “liberty” and “freedom” as synonyms. He is not the first to mark the contrast, but the lecture made it the default Western frame.

The editors (Baum and Nichols) isolate the load-bearing claims: freedom is best construed as “the absence of obstacles to possible choices and activities” (IN, 32); “liberty is one thing, and the conditions for it are another” (IN, 45); liberty is distinct from, and often in conflict with, equality, justice, and democracy. Despite Berlin’s social-democratic sympathies, this package helped later writers on the right claim freedom as their property. The 2013 essays refuse that cession without agreeing on a replacement.

**This is not an ingest of the 1958 lecture.** Quotations are as the collection cites them from Hardy’s *Liberty* (2002). *The Crooked Timber of Humanity* (now ingested) does **not** re-argue the dual; do not update this page from that book.

## Berlin’s Two Concepts (as quoted)

### Negative liberty

> “I am normally said to be free to the degree to which no man or body of men interferes with my activity. Political liberty in this sense is simply the area within which a man can act unobstructed by others.” (TC, 169)

> “By being free in this sense I mean not being interfered with by others. The wider the area of noninterference the wider my freedom.” (TC, 170)

The 1969 refinement blocks a cheap version: freedom is not absence of *frustration* (kill the desire, lose the frustration) but absence of obstacles to *possible* choices — “how many doors are open, how open they are, [and] upon their relative importance in my life” (IN, 32). Lineage the volume names: Hobbes, Bentham.

Corollaries Berlin draws:
- Mere incapacity is not unfreedom: “it is not lack of freedom not to fly like an eagle or swim like a whale” (Helvétius, TC, 169 n. 2).
- Poverty is unfreedom only if other humans have so arranged things that I cannot pay (TC, 170) — and even then he prefers to keep the name “liberty” for the legal door.
- Negative liberty “is not incompatible with some kinds of autocracy.” “There is no necessary connection between individual liberty and democratic rule” (TC, 177).

### Positive liberty

The positive sense “derives from the wish on the part of the individual to be his own master” (TC, 178). Berlin then fuses, as C. B. Macpherson already complained, at least three ideas — including a “debased form” that “imposes ‘rational’ freedom on those said to be not fully rational.”

The slide the lecture fears:
1. Self split into higher (rational, real) and lower (empirical) selves.
2. The real self may be “a tribe, a race, a church, [or] a state” (TC, 179).
3. “To force empirical selves into the right pattern is not tyranny but liberation” (TC, 194).
4. Education becomes compulsion: Fichte, “Compulsion is also a kind of education” (TC, 195).
5. Rousseau: whoever refuses the general will “shall be forced to be free.”

Two forms of self-mastery: **self-abnegation** (do not desire the unattainable — not an enlargement of liberty) and **self-realization** (knowledge liberates; a rational state enacts the laws rational men would have enacted). T. H. Green’s “true” freedom — maximizing the capacity of all “to make the best of themselves” — is the liberal example of the danger.

National self-determination is filed here: anticolonial movements, Berlin says, were generally about group status and recognition, not individual liberty, though they might achieve a “hybrid form of freedom” if the answer to “Who shall govern me?” is “somebody or something which I can represent as ‘my own’” (TC, 206).

### 1969 softening

The two questions “*cannot be kept wholly distinct*” (IN, 35–36). Both liberties can be perverted. “Freedom for the wolves has often meant death to the sheep” (IN, 38). A welfare-state case can be built from negative liberty (IN, 38–39). Tully reads this as abandoning the lecture; Crowder reads it as clarifying what Berlin already believed.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

### Isaiah Berlin (this volume’s Berlin)

The dual is both analysis and Cold War intervention. Orlie: the abiding target is **political rationalism** (one way of life, one solution), not every positive ideal. Nichols: the originality is the triad negative liberty ← value pluralism ← critique of rationalism.

### Defenders and refiners in the volume

- **Dimova-Cookson:** the dual is a “philosophical achievement of lasting merit.” Personal level: negative = uncensored choice; positive = self-direction carved *against* that choice (Kant, Green). Social level: negative liberty must stay apart from justice and conditions; social positive liberty *is* their association (Green, Rousseau) — not totalitarianism. The two “could only occur one at a time.”
- **Crowder:** Berlin does not reduce valuable freedom to the negative concept. Negative liberty is politically distinctive (antipaternalist “liberty to commit blunders”) but *not* overriding. See [[Concepts/Value Pluralism (Berlin)]].

### Retrievals that keep the challenge

- **Christman:** after Berlin, positive freedom must capture effective agency without imposing contestable substantive ideals. Answer: **procedural**, historically diachronic autonomy (non-alienation on reflection) plus social supports as *background*, not as the meaning of autonomy. Recognition-as-identity and Oshana’s substantive independence fail the challenge.
- **Myers:** Berlin blurs democratic self-rule with despotic positive liberty; antipaternalism and anti-scientism can still underwrite **liberal-democratic pluralism**.
- **Hirschmann:** historical allies of *both* concepts denied women liberty. Negative liberty names unjust obstacles but treats them as discrete and agent-caused, not as “gender.” Positive liberty’s unused gift is the **social construction of the choosing subject** — desire produced, not merely blocked. Berlin himself: “If a master can condition his slaves to love their chains he does not thereby prima facie increase their liberty” — then evades it by counting open doors.

### Rejections and replacements

- **Tully:** the dual is a *narrowing*. Progressive liberalism had four interrelated liberties (Constant’s pair + social-economic + “freedom of each and all”). Berlin severs the last three; enabling capacities are constituent, not preconditions.
- **Gould:** Berlin gives “a misleading account of positive freedom.” Retrieve **effective freedom**: choice + enabling conditions + self-transformation of “individuals-in-relations.” Justice = **equal positive freedom**.
- **MacGilvray:** set the typology aside. The live modern contrast is **republican** freedom (independence from *ad arbitrium* power) vs **market** freedom (unconstrained choice + impersonal order). Berlin’s dual fails to capture either.
- **Coole:** the method is asymmetric — austere analysis for negative, rich pathology for positive. Power as *coercion* cannot see structure or Foucaultian **micro-power**.
- **Nichols:** negative liberty already smuggles a choosing subject and a world of meaningful options — a “minimal positive notion.” Value pluralism sits on **ontological monism** (one negative-liberty regime as the precondition of all choice). Heidegger: freedom as disclosure is *prior to* both.
- **Baum (via Taylor and Marcuse):** freedom is control over one’s life, not the number of options. “What *is* chosen” matters. Ecology of freedom: quality of choice inside environmental limits.

### Vault thinkers already present

- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Hobbes]]:** named ancestor of purely negative freedom and of liberty-with-autocracy.
- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]:** private sovereignty and harm principle are the closest vault primary to Berlin’s negative area; Mill also supplies the “each by all the rest” worry and, for Tully, the colonial-despot analogy. See [[Concepts/Harm Principle (Mill)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]:** negative freedom as independence of alien causes → positive freedom as self-legislation. This is the philosophical high-water of the self-mastery lineage. Kant’s “positive” is moral, not Green’s developmental elite or a nationalist higher self. See [[Concepts/Freedom as the Key to Autonomy (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Jacques Rousseau]]:** “forced to be free.” See [[Contradictions/Berlin vs Rousseau - Negative Liberty vs Forced to Be Free]].
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]]:** self-realization and history as consciousness of freedom — the other great positive-liberty target.
- **[[Thinkers/Robert Nozick]]:** later, deontic non-interference. Market freedom in MacGilvray’s sense is the relevant neighbor; do not identify Berlin with Nozick.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]:** freedom as the being of the for-itself, not a higher rational self imposed on a lower one. Adjacent, not the same slide.
- **[[Thinkers/Michel Foucault]]:** Coole’s micro-power foil.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] How many concepts? MacCallum: one triadic concept (agent / from / to). Nelson: all claims are about absence of constraint. Lukes: one core (control over one’s life). Pettit/Skinner (reported): a republican third. MacGilvray: wrong axis. The volume does not resolve this.
- > [!warning] Liberty vs freedom: Pitkin (via the editors) — “freedom” is more holistic; “liberty” more formal and piecemeal. Berlin’s negative sense “has to be a sense of ‘liberty.’” The book examines the politics of *freedom*.
- > [!warning] Tully vs Crowder on whether the 1958 dual narrows or expands liberalism. Same pages, opposite verdicts.
- Reception, not primary: the lecture itself is still outstanding.
