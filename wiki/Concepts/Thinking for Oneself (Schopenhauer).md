# Thinking for Oneself (Schopenhauer)
> We know only what we have thought out. Reading is thinking with someone else’s head. The surest way not to have thoughts is to take up a book at every spare moment.

## Summary

Chapters XX–XXVI of *Parerga* II apply the will/intellect apparatus to fame, learning, writing, and reading. They continue Volume 1’s eudaemonology of *what one is* versus *what one represents*, and the university polemic. No new metaphysics.

**Thinking for oneself** (XXII) is the load-bearing chapter. “We know only what we have thought out.” Knowledge not elaborated by our own thought is a badly arranged library. Reading stamps foreign ideas on the mind as a seal on wax; “the surest way not to have thoughts of our own is for us at once to take up a book when we have a moment to spare.” Original truth is a natural limb; learnt truth is a false tooth. Self-thinkers think first and meet authorities later as confirmation; book-philosophers compile an automaton of opinions. “Scholars are those who have read in books, but thinkers… are those who have read directly in the book of the world.” Thinking cannot be commanded; it waits on occasion and mood. Quoting authorities is *argumentum ad verecundiam*.

**Reading** (XXIV) is the negative half. “When we read, someone else thinks for us; we repeat merely his mental process.” Constant reading reads men stupid; assimilation needs rumination. The art of *not* reading is to refuse the newest. Two literatures: those who live *for* learning (a dozen European works a century) and those who live *on* it (thousands a year, then gone).

The surrounding chapters fill in the market:

- **Judgement and fame** (XX): judgement is almost as rare as the phoenix. “No one is taken for what he is, but for what others make of him.” Merited fame is late; false fame (Hegel as type) is ox-bladders. See [[Concepts/What One Is, Has, and Represents (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Learning** (XXI): scholars study to teach and write; the dilettante treats the subject as end. Professors (dogs) vs independent scholars (wolves). See [[Concepts/University Philosophy (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Authorship** (XXIII): write because you have thought, not to think, and not for copy-money. Form, not subject-matter, is the mind.
- **Language** (XXV): original language is instinctive; translation is chicory coffee. Not a linguistics page.
- **Psychology** (XXVI): hope is “the confusion of the wish for an event with its probability.” Degrees of clearness of consciousness are degrees of the *reality* of existence.

## Key Claims

- Thought first; books later, as confirmation.
- Reading is a substitute for thinking unless rumination follows.
- The art of not reading is part of thinking for oneself.
- Fame is representation. Judgement is rare. Do not live for contemporaries.
