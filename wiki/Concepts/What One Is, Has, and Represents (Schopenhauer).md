# What One Is, Has, and Represents (Schopenhauer)
> Three goods. What one *is* — personality in the widest sense — outweighs property and the opinion of others. The other two are merely relative.

## Summary

Chapters I–IV of the *Aphorisms on the wisdom of life* keep Aristotle’s triad only as a triad, then rank it.

1. **What one *is*** — personality in the widest sense: health, strength, beauty, temperament, moral character, intelligence and its cultivation.
2. **What one *has*** — property and possessions.
3. **What one *represents*** — opinion of others, divided into honour, rank, and fame.

“Accordingly, what we *are*, our personality, is primary and essential for our life’s happiness … Therefore, its value can be called **absolute**, in contrast with the merely relative value of the other two.” Nature’s differences “will be much more essential and drastic” than human ones. “We all are stuck in our consciousness, as in our skin.” The objective half is fate and changeable; the subjective half “is we ourselves, and so is essentially unchangeable.”

**Cheerfulness**, founded mainly on health, is the “genuine coin” of happiness (not paper money): it “makes us immediately happy in the present.” “Nine-tenths of our happiness depends on our health alone.” Wealth contributes least to cheerfulness. Cheerfulness is “the highest good for a being whose reality has the form of an indivisible present between two eternities” — *inside* the accommodation, not as the system’s last word. See [[Concepts/Eudaemonology as Accommodation (Schopenhauer)]].

**Pain and boredom** are the two enemies, in objective and subjective antagonism. Want → pain; security/abundance → boredom. Intellectual obtuseness lessens pain but creates inner emptiness; genius lessens boredom but heightens sensibility, will, and pain. The ingenious seek “freedom from pain … tranquillity and leisure,” even solitude. The **philistine** is a human being “without intellectual needs.” Centre of gravity: outside (ordinary), partly within (dilettante), entirely within (genius).

**What one has** is relative (Epicurus’s three needs; wealth “resembles sea water”). Money is the “absolute good” only as abstract need-satisfier. Assets are “a bulwark against … evils,” not a licence to pleasures. Inherited independence is “invaluable” as leisure for genius; unused, it delivers one to boredom.

**What one represents is overrated.** Honour/rank/fame cannot compete with health and livelihood. Vanity inverts the order: the image in others’ minds is treated as more real than “what we are in and *for ourselves*.” Honour is “objectively, other people’s opinion of our value and, subjectively, our fear of this opinion”; it is **negative** (must not be lost). Fame is **positive** (must be acquired) and “essentially relative.” “Not fame, but that by means of which we earn it, is what has value.” Civic/official/sexual honour have a utility-root; **chivalric honour is “pseudo-honour,”** medieval “law of the fist,” unknown to Greeks, Romans, and Asians. Posthumous fame’s happiness is in the qualities and the work, not the applause.

### Sexual honour as *esprit de corps* (Ch. IV, the load-bearing utility argument)

This is not a flourish inside “honour is overrated.” It is the derivation he says all honour needs: “all honour ultimately depends on considerations of utility.”

*Sexual honour* splits female/male and is, “from both sides… a well-understood *esprit de corps*.” Female honour is the general opinion that a girl has never given herself, a wife only to her husband. Why that opinion *matters*:

> The female sex demands and expects from the male sex everything… the male sex demands of the female initially and immediately only one thing. Therefore, it had to be established that the male sex can obtain from the female this one thing only in exchange for taking over the care of everything and especially for the children that spring from the union; on this arrangement rests the welfare of the entire female sex.

To enforce it, the female sex “must with necessity stick together” and face the male sex “as the common foe,” who holds “all earthly goods” by physical and intellectual superiority — “a foe that must be subdued and conquered.” Maxim: no sexual intercourse outside marriage, “so that every single individual be forced into marriage, as a kind of surrender, and, as a result, the entire female sex is provided for.” A girl who breaks ranks is expelled; the adulteress has broken the “capitulation” and discourages other men from entering it.

Male *esprit de corps* is the counterpart: the man who has made “the surrender that is so very favourable for the opposite party — marriage” must punish adultery, “at least by separating,” or the community of men disgraces him. Exclusive possession was the one thing bargained for.

He then *caps* the value. The basis is “a wholesome and, indeed, necessary, but **calculated and interest-based** *esprit de corps*.” Relative, not absolute. Lucretia and Verginius “degenerate into tragic farce.” Concubinage was honourable “in almost all countries… until the Lutheran Reformation.” A girl who surrenders illicitly is “infinitely” more foolish than wicked. Do not treat this as a sacred law of nature.

Do **not** weld this to [[Concepts/On Women (Schopenhauer)]]. That Vol. 2 chapter is the essentialist rant plus the claim that *European monogamy* over-privileges the “lady” and leaves surplus women as “human sacrifices on the altar of monogamy.” The *esprit de corps* derivation lives here, in the Aphorisms.

Chapters V–VI deepen “what one is”: intellect, health, character, solitude. “What one represents” is mostly a trap (society, envy, paper-money friendship).

## Key Claims

- Personality is the unchangeable measure of possible happiness. The other two goods are relative.
- Health → cheerfulness → the coin of happiness.
- Pain and boredom are the two enemies; they trade off with intellect.
- Honour is fear of opinion (negative). Fame is vanity’s rarest morsel (positive, still relative).
- Sexual honour is a two-sided *esprit de corps*: sex only in exchange for provision and children; marriage as male “surrender”; ostracism of defectors. Utility, not sacrament.
- Chivalric honour is a medieval fake, not an ancient virtue.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — the popular apparatus of the Aphorisms. It is how the accommodation *sorts* goods, not a new metaphysics of the person.
- **[[Concepts/Eudaimonia]]** — Aristotle is cited for activity, leisure, self-sufficiency *inside* this ranking. External goods here are even more demoted than in *NE* I: they are relative, and “what one represents” is mostly a snare.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — later will treat fame, honour, and the “represent” half as herd optics. Do not back-date *pathos of distance* into this triad.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] “Absolute” value of personality is eudaemonological, not metaphysical. The higher standpoint has already judged the individual an appearance of one Will.
- > [!warning] 2026 reception (Willett and similar) reads this paragraph as a *prediction* that if the female front broke, commitment, paternity, female security, trust, and restraint would fall. The text is a **derivation of honour from utility**, plus a warning not to absolutize it. He does not forecast dating apps, pornography, or a “dating recession.” He also argues, in [[Concepts/On Women (Schopenhauer)]], that the European form of the bargain already injures most women. Do not file a YouTube five-pillar collapse as his prophecy.
