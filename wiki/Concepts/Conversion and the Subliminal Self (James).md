# Conversion and the Subliminal Self (James)

> A self "hitherto divided, and consciously wrong inferior and unhappy, becomes unified and consciously right superior and happy" — whether or not "a direct divine operation is needed." Suddenness is a psychological idiosyncrasy, not a miracle-mark. If higher powers exist, they "may get access to us only through the subliminal door."

## Overview

Lectures VIII–XIII of *Varieties* move from the sick soul's discord to its unification. Once-born religion is "rectilinear or one-storied"; twice-born religion is a "double-storied mystery." The twice-born temperament is "discordancy or heterogeneity," worst in the psychopathic type; character-growth is "straightening out and unifying of the inner self." Unification may be gradual (*lysis*) or abrupt (*crisis*), via feeling, action, intellect, or later mystical experience. Religion is only one species of a genus that includes **counter-conversion** (Starbuck's label, via Jouffroy).

**Conversion**, for these lectures: religious ideas shift from the periphery to "the habitual centre of his personal energy." Starbuck: ordinary evangelical conversion is "a normal adolescent phenomenon," "a process of struggling away from sin rather than of striving towards righteousness." Two types (Starbuck): **volitional** (usually gradual) vs. **self-surrender**; even volitional cases need a last "hands off" step. "The personal will must be given up."

Psychology and theology agree that forces "seemingly outside" the conscious self redeem; they diverge when psychology names those forces **subconscious** / **incubation**. James leaves the divergence "in abeyance" as a psychologist, then refuses to let the subliminal *exclude* "all notion of a higher penetration." Myers's name for extra-field consciousness is **subliminal**; **automatism** = "uprushes" from "subliminal parts of the mind." Instantaneous conversion marks a large subliminal region and a "leaky or pervious margin" — a *conditio sine qua non* of *instantaneous* conversion, not of conversion as such. "There may be a gradual growth in holiness without a cataclysm." Methodism treats crisis as required; James does not.

Value is **fruits for life**, not origin or suddenness (Coe: "nothing psychological… but something ethical… *what is attained*"). The hour of change brings a sense of higher control and a "**state of assurance**" (James's preferred name; Leuba's is **faith-state**). The world looks new. Automatisms and photisms are not spiritually essential. Permanence of *attitude* can survive fluctuating feeling (Starbuck). Tolstoy and Bunyan illustrate *lysis*: they find "that by which men live" but stay two-storied; they never become healthy-minded.

Lectures XI–XIII describe conversion's ripe fruit as **saintliness** — "the collective name for the ripe fruits of religion in a character," a "composite photograph… the same in all religions." Four inner marks: a wider life plus sensible conviction of an Ideal Power; friendly continuity and self-surrender; elation as confining selfhood melts; an emotional centre toward "yes, yes." Practical issues: asceticism, strength of soul, purity, charity (later obedience and poverty as monastic branches). If grace operates, "it probably operates through the subliminal door." Charity appears in Stoicism, Hinduism, and Buddhism; the faith-state carries it "by organic consequence." No standalone saintliness page: XIV–XV immediately critique the type by its excesses and fold the value-test back into [[Concepts/Medical Materialism and the Fruits Test (James)]].

## Key Documents

- **Stephen Bradley**: heart-beating, a stream "resembling air" into mouth and heart; thereafter he defies Deists and Atheists.
- **[[Thinkers/Augustine]]**: classic divided self; garden voice "*Sume, lege*"; long *Confessions* excerpt on old/new, carnal/spiritual wills — later reused as *velleitates* vs. the real centre of energy. Not James's main *sudden*-conversion case (those are Bradley, Alline, the Oxford graduate, Ratisbonne, Paul).
- **Tolstoy**: "Faith is the sense of life… the force whereby we live." Change "insensibly and gradually."
- **Bunyan**: "My peace would be in and out twenty times a day." "Now did my chains fall off my legs indeed."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Sources/Confessions - Augustine]]**: James reads the garden as the type of the divided will and of unification by a voice from outside the conscious field. He does not adopt Augustine's theology of prevenient grace; the subliminal is the *door*, not a substitute for God, and "would of itself establish no presumption that [the incoming forces] were more divine than diabolical."
- **Myers / subliminal psychology**: the mediating scientific term Lecture XX will use for the hither side of "the MORE."
- **Leuba**: over-narrows religion to sense of sin; James adds Tolstoy-type *objective* melancholy. Leuba's faith-state ("The objects of faith may even be preposterous; the affective stream will float them along") is kept as psychology, renamed assurance when James speaks in his own voice.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Psychology's "subconscious" and theology's "Spirit" agree on extra-conscious help and diverge on its name. James will not close this in VIII–X; [[Concepts/Over-Beliefs and Piecemeal Supernaturalism (James)]] is the over-belief he later adds.
- "The will to believe" appears once in this range, only to say it "cannot be stretched" to manufacture belief against present perception. Do not treat these lectures as *The Will to Believe*.
