# The Look (Le Regard) and the Other (Sartre)
> The Other is disclosed not by inferring "they too have a consciousness" but by my *being-seen*: under the Look I am turned from a free nothing into a fixed object — "Hell is other people."

## Summary

### From *Being and Nothingness*, Part Three ch. 1 (primary)

**Shame before somebody.** Shame is originally shame *before somebody*. "The Other is the indispensable mediator between myself and me. I am ashamed of myself as I appear to the Other." "Shame is by nature recognition. I recognize that I am as the Other sees me" (BN 222). Being-for-itself refers to being-for-others.

**The reef of solipsism.** Realism and idealism share an *external* negation (a spatial nothingness). Each flips into the other and leaves the Other conjectural; God as "third man" neither founds nor suffices (BN 223–232). A valid theory needs no new proof but a "cogito of the Other": start from the Cartesian cogito; the Other first as subject, not object; *internal* negation; a detotalized totality (BN 250–252).

**Husserl, Hegel, Heidegger.** Husserl makes the world inter-monadic but still measures being by knowledge; the Other is a constitutive *meaning*, not extra-mundane being. "Husserl can not escape solipsism any more than Kant could" (BN 235). Hegel's gain: internal, reciprocal negation; recognition and the master–slave struggle make being-for-others a condition of being-for-itself. His failure: knowledge still measures being; "epistemological and ontological optimism"; "So long as consciousnesses exist, the separation and conflict of consciousnesses will remain" (BN 244). Heidegger's *Mitsein* is a crew, a *we*, not a frontal *you and me*. As an *a priori* it cannot found the ontic encounter (Pierre, Annie) and "isolates me as surely as … solipsism." "We encounter the Other; we do not constitute him" (BN 250).

**The Look.** Other-as-object is a "drain hole" in the park: the world hemorrhages toward a decentralization I do not control (BN 255–256). "'Being-seen-by-the-Other' is the truth of 'seeing-the-Other'" (BN 257). Eyes are destroyed in the look; rustling, farmhouse, shutter already *are* eyes.

**The keyhole.** Alone, I *am* my acts (jealousy as situation). "I have just glued my ear to the door and looked through a keyhole." "But all of a sudden I hear footsteps in the hall. Someone is looking at me!" (BN 259–260). Self haunts unreflective consciousness as object-for-the-Other. Shame *lives* (does not know) being-looked-at. "My original fall is the existence of the Other." "The Other as a look is only that — my transcendence transcended" (BN 263). "I am a slave to the degree that my being is dependent at the center of a freedom which is not mine" (BN 267). A false alarm does not cancel the Other-as-subject — only his *facticity*. Being-for-others is factual, not an ontological structure of the for-itself. Subject-look and object-Other cannot be synthesized; only the dead stay objects.

Welten's park-bench scene is this chapter's "drain hole," compressed. *Huis clos*'s "Hell is other people" is not in the treatise; the structure (shame, keyhole, oscillation subject/object, no synthesis) is. Love, the body, and sexuality are the next two chapters — see [[Concepts/The Body (Sartre)]], [[Concepts/Concrete Relations with Others (Sartre)]].

## From the 1945/46 lecture: inter-subjectivity via the *cogito*

The lecture answers the Communist charge that a Cartesian starting point cannot reach solidarity. The point of departure *is* the *cogito* — "the absolute truth of consciousness as it attains to itself" — because outside that moment all objects are only probable. But "contrary to the philosophy of Descartes, contrary to that of Kant, when we say 'I think' we are attaining to ourselves in the presence of the other, and we are just as certain of the other as we are of ourselves." "The man who discovers himself directly in the *cogito* also discovers all the others, and discovers them as the condition of his own existence." He cannot be anything (spiritual, wicked, jealous) unless others recognise him as such. "I cannot obtain any truth whatsoever about myself, except through the mediation of another. The other is indispensable to my existence, and equally so to any knowledge I can have of myself." The intimate discovery of myself is "the revelation of the other as a freedom which confronts mine, and which cannot think or will without doing so either for or against me." The world of decision is therefore **"inter-subjectivity."**

This is the same Other as BN's Look — a freedom that confronts mine, for or against me — stated without shame, the park bench, or "Hell is other people." The lecture needs the Other as the condition of self-knowledge and of a human community; BN needs the Other as the theft of freedom. Both are Sartre; the popular address stresses the first.

## Key Claims / Positions
- Lecture: the *cogito* already includes the Other as condition of my existence and self-knowledge (inter-subjectivity).
- The Other is disclosed through *being-seen*, not by inference; this overcomes solipsism.
- The Look turns the pour-soi into an object, stealing not my consciousness but my freedom.
- Shame/pride prove I am something for another; "being-seen is the truth of seeing."
- The relation to the Other is structurally *conflict*; "Hell is other people."
- Love wants to possess the Other *as freedom* — impossible, so love requires conflict.
- The body is en-soi for the Other; aesthetic/erotic meaning exists only in the Look.
- Sexuality is incarnation: desire is a freedom desiring the Other's freedom, not a libido.

### How Sartre Differs From Prior Thinkers in the Wiki
- **vs [[Thinkers/Descartes]] and [[Thinkers/Kant]]** (lecture): the *cogito* is kept as the absolute starting point and is denied to be solitary; others are as certain as the self.
- **vs the traditional "problem of other minds"**: refuses to *infer* other minds; recasts the question as the phenomenology of being-an-object-for-another.
- **vs Freud**: rejects the libido/unconscious account of sexuality for an intentional, incarnational one — see [[Contradictions/Sartre vs Freud - Lucidity vs the Unconscious]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Hegel]]**: keeps "I depend on the Other in my being" and discards reconciliation; plurality is an unsurpassable scandal, not a moment of Spirit.
- **vs [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: *Mitsein* (crew, we) cannot found Pierre or Annie; we encounter the Other, we do not constitute him.
- **vs [[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]**: "the other's gaze does not transform me into an object" unless both gazes withdraw into thinking nature. The park's drain-hole vs MP's vortex that draws my world in without freezing me as in-itself. See [[Concepts/Coexistence and the Other (Merleau-Ponty)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- The Other both *threatens* my freedom (objectifying me) and is *required* for me to "be" anything at all — a permanent ambivalence Sartre never dissolves; it becomes, in the *Critique*, the problem of the [[Concepts/Totalization, Series and Group (Sartre)|group]].
