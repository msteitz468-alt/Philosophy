# Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)
> Inner practices in which thought “takes itself as its own subject-matter and seeks to modify itself.” Their object is a transformation of seeing and being — not a body of theory, and not “self-help.”

## Definition

[[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]’s load-bearing essay (ch. 3 of *Philosophy as a Way of Life*) opens with why the adjective must be **spiritual**. “Psychic,” “moral,” “ethical,” “intellectual” are too narrow: imagination and sensibility matter as much as definition; “ethical” covers only the therapeutics of the passions. “Spiritual” names the entire psychism and the raising of the individual to “the life of the objective Spirit” — the perspective of the Whole. The exercises “correspond to a transformation of our vision of the world, and to a metamorphosis of our personality.”

Greek *askēsis* here means the practice of such exercises, **not** “asceticism.” Ignatius’s *Exercitia spiritualia* are “nothing but a Christian version of a Greco-Roman tradition.” The phenomenon belongs to philosophy as such, from Socrates and Plato through late antiquity (against Rabbow, who confined it to Hellenistic “inwardness” and to mere “moral exercises”).

They are not “moral aestheticism” or fabricating a personality. Plotinus’s statue is made by *taking away*. They are not mechanical imitation of schemas, and not accumulating discourse: Porphyry requires that teachings become “nature and life.” Wisdom usually remains an unattained ideal; the exercises must be renewed.

This is the missing primary behind the vault’s Holiday-era pages. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]].

## The Four Learnings

### Learning to live
Philosophy is an “exercise,” an art of living. For the Stoics, woes come from seeking or avoiding what does not depend on us. The work is to reverse a “human” (passion-valued) vision into a “natural” vision that places each event in universal nature.

**Attention (*prosoche*)** is “in a sense, the key to spiritual exercises”: unsleeping vigilance, presence of mind, tension of spirit; concentration on the present (the only thing that depends on us); having the rule of life *procheiron* (“at hand”). *Melete* and *mneme* engrave the rule so it works as a reflex. [[Concepts/Premeditatio Malorum (Premeditation of Evils)|*Praemeditatio malorum*]] pictures poverty, suffering, and death as non-evils. Morning preview, evening examination, dream-examination. Progress is made by dialogue with self or others **and by writing**.

Epicurean exercises share the therapeutic frame and invert the tone: not *tonos* (tension) but *anesis* (relaxation). Physics frees from fear of gods and death; the *tetrapharmakos* is meditated night and day. Do *not* premeditate evils; relive past pleasures and intensify present ones. Friendship is “the spiritual exercise par excellence.” See [[Concepts/The Present Instant (Hadot)]].

### Learning to dialogue
Socrates makes the interlocutor, not the topic, the stake. Dialogue is communal exercise (*gnothi seauton*). Every spiritual exercise is a dialogue as authentic presence to self and other. Platonic dialogues are *model* exercises: they form more than they inform; method (*diairesis*) over the particular answer; psychagogy plus dialectic; conversion of the whole soul toward the Good (*Republic* 518c). See [[Thinkers/Socrates]], [[Thinkers/Plato]].

### Learning to die
Socrates’ death founds Platonism. Philosophy is apprenticeship for death: separating the soul from the body’s passions in order to look from the perspective of the *Logos* — “not trance.” This yields greatness of soul, contempt for death, and the [[Concepts/View from Above and Cosmic Perspective (Marcus Aurelius)|view from above]]. Physics itself becomes a spiritual exercise: contemplative joy; imaginative overflight that shrinks human affairs; thinking “along with the Thought which embraces all things.” See [[Concepts/Memento Mori (Stoic Practice)]], [[Concepts/Being-toward-Death and Authenticity (Heidegger)]] (Hadot notes Heidegger’s distinction from *meditatio mortis*).

### Learning to read
Ancient writings are school products (dialogue, *methodoi*, psychagogy), not systems — hence their apparent contradictions. Reading — pausing, ruminating, letting texts speak — is “one of the most difficult” spiritual exercises. Medieval absorption of *philosophia* into Christianity emptied philosophy of its exercises (relegated to mysticism and ethics); modern theory inherited that split.

## Inventory (worth keeping on this page)

*Prosoche*; *melete* / *mneme*; *praemeditatio malorum*; morning preview / evening examen / dreams; *epilogismoi* and maxims *procheiron*; reading, listening, *zetesis*, *skepsis*; physical definition and dissection of events; indifference to indifferent things; *enkrateia* and social duties (start easy); *tetrapharmakos*; physics / contemplation of the infinite; memory of pleasures (Epicurean *anti*-premeditation); confession, fraternal correction, friendship; Socratic/Platonic dialogue and inner dialogue; training for death; view from above; Neoplatonic purification and return; writing (Marcus’s *hypomnemata*); learning to read.

Philo of Alexandria supplies two lists that Hadot treats as the Stoico-Platonic panorama.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Stoics** — tension, attention, premeditation, three disciplines as lived physics/ethics/logic. [[Thinkers/Epictetus]], [[Thinkers/Marcus Aurelius]], [[Thinkers/Seneca]].
- **Epicureans** — relaxation, *carpe diem*, friendship, refusal of premeditation. [[Thinkers/Epicurus]], [[Thinkers/Lucretius]].
- **Plato / Plotinus** — dialogue as exercise; death-training; sculpting the statue; becoming the All. [[Thinkers/Plotinus]], [[Concepts/Return of the Soul (Epistrophe)]].
- **Christian monasticism** — takeover of the suite (*prosoche*, *nepsis*, examen, *melete thanatou*, *apatheia*), scripturally justified, transfigured by grace and humility. [[Thinkers/Augustine]].
- **Foucault** — renames them “techniques of the self” and, Hadot argues, aims them at the self rather than at the Whole. See [[Contradictions/Hadot vs Foucault - Care of the Self vs Cosmic Universalization]].
- **Holiday** — transmits a subset (citadel, *premeditatio*, *memento mori*) as self-help. The scholarly definition is this page.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Foucault’s “techniques of the self” vs Hadot’s cosmic *askēsis* — see [[Contradictions/Hadot vs Foucault - Care of the Self vs Cosmic Universalization]].
- Wittgensteinian therapy dissolves confusions and “leaves everything as it is”; Hadotian exercise aims at metamorphosis. Allies against philosophy-as-system; opposed on what success looks like. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as Therapy (Wittgenstein)]].
