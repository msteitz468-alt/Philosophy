# Morality of Custom (Nietzsche)
> Morality is obedience to custom. The free man who will depend on himself is, to that morality, *immoral*.

## Summary

*The Dawn of Day* Book I §9 is the vault's first primary for *Sittlichkeit der Sitte*. Kennedy titles the aphorism "Conception of the Morality of Custom." Morality is "nothing else (and, above all, nothing more) than obedience to customs, of whatsoever nature they may be." No tradition, no morality. Tradition is obeyed *because it commands*, not because it is useful — fear of a higher, more-than-personal intelligence. "The free man is immoral, because it is his *will* to depend upon himself and not upon tradition." Primitive "evil" = the individual, free, unaccustomed. The most moral man either obeys most often or makes the greatest sacrifices. "The individual shall sacrifice himself—so demands the morality of custom." Socratic self-control for *personal* happiness, and the Christian seeking *his own* salvation, look evil to the community. Original minds acquire a bad conscience.

This is **1881**, not the 1887 *Genealogy*. *Genealogy* II will call the sovereign individual the ripest fruit of this long work and will add the mnemonics of blood and the creditor–debtor *Schuld*. Do not read that fruit back into §9. Here the free man is simply *immoral* to custom; he is not yet the being competent to promise. See [[Concepts/Sovereign Individual (Nietzsche)]].

Surrounding Book I aphorisms do the work the title names:

- **§1.** Things that endure get so permeated by reason that an origin in unreason looks "paradoxical and sacrilegious."
- **§14.** New valuations break custom only via madness (or feigned madness).
- **§16.** Civilisation's first principle: any custom is better than none.
- **§18.** In threatened small communities, cruelty is festive; **voluntary suffering** becomes virtue; being pitied is an insult. That long prehistory, not "world-history," formed mankind.
- **§19.** Custom *stores* old useful/harmful experience, but the *feeling* of custom attaches to age and sanctity; therefore "morality is opposed to the formation of new and better morals: it stupefies."
- **§149** (Book III). Conforming in *practice* (baptism, army, church wedding) while reserving "intellectual liberty" re-sanctions the custom and lulls [[Concepts/Intellectual Conscience (Nietzsche)]]. Little unconventional *actions* matter more than opinions.

Kennedy's English is **morality of custom**. The German string *Sittlichkeit der Sitte* is not in this file. Do not invent Hollingdale's "morality of mores."

## Key Claims

- Morality = obedience to tradition as command, not as utility.
- The individual who depends on himself is *immoral* by definition of this morality.
- Custom stupefies: the feeling of sanctity blocks better morals.
- Any custom beats none (civilisation's first principle).
- Practice confirms custom more than reserved opinion does.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — 1881: the first free-spirit treatise on morality *as* custom. 1887: the same long work becomes the tree on which the sovereign individual is the late fruit. 1888 *Ecce Homo* reviews *Daybreak* as the start of the campaign; it does not restate §9.
- **[[Concepts/Sovereign Individual (Nietzsche)]]** — 1887 only. This page is the prehistory that page presupposes.
- **[[Concepts/Two-fold Historical Origin of Good and Evil (Nietzsche)]]** — 1878 HATH §45 (requital vs hostility). Not the same cut as custom vs the free individual.
- **[[Concepts/Custom and Belief (Hume)]]** — Hume's custom is habit that licenses belief. Nietzsche's custom is *commanded tradition*. Kinship of the word, opposite grammar. Do not flatten.
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]]** / [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Hegel - Faith vs Sittlichkeit]] — Hegelian *Sittlichkeit* is ethical life in the social whole. Nietzsche's *Sitte* is prehistoric obedience. Same German root, not the same concept. Do not file Dawn under the Kierkegaard contradiction.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — *Dawn* §339: demanding that duty stay burdensome is a residue of ascetic cruelty, and forbids duty from becoming habit/custom. That is Kant *against* custom becoming easy, not a theory of *Sitte*.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] **1881 free man ≠ 1887 sovereign individual.** §9's immoral self-depender is not yet the being who can promise. Flagged, not welded, on both pages.
- > [!warning] **Custom vs *Sittlichkeit*.** Do not use this page to fill Hegel's ethical life or Kierkegaard's attack on it.
