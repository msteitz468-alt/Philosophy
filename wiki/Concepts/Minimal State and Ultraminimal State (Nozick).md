# Minimal State and Ultraminimal State (Nozick)
> A night-watchman state limited to protection against force, theft, fraud, and enforcement of contracts is justified; anything more violates rights. It is reached from Lockean anarchy by an invisible hand, not by a compact.

## Summary

*Anarchy, State, and Utopia* distinguishes two statelike entities and then argues that the first morally requires the second.

The **ultraminimal** state monopolizes the use of force (except immediate self-defense) but protects only paying customers. The **minimal** (night-watchman) state adds tax-funded protection of everyone in the territory — “Friedman vouchers” in Nozick’s picture. The minimal state *appears* redistributive because some pay for others’ protection. “Redistributive,” he insists, names **reasons**, not structures: returning stolen money or compensating a rights-violation is not redistributive. The question of Part I is whether protection of independents has a non-redistributive ground.

The ground is the **principle of compensation** (Chs. 4–5). Independents who stay in Locke’s state of nature (§95: no one may be forced into civil society) may enforce their own rights against clients of the dominant agency. Isolation fails; waiting until after a possibly lethal misenforcement is too late. The agency may prohibit independents’ *risky* private procedures. Those disadvantaged by that prohibition must be compensated by those who gain security. The cheapest compensation is protective services versus clients — sometimes below price for the poor. The reason is compensatory, not redistributive.

A dominant protective agency then satisfies two necessary conditions for being a state: it is the only generally effective enforcer of a prohibition on unreliable procedures, and it protects those non-clients it so prohibits. After a slight weakening of Weber (sole *effective* judge of violence, not sole *authorizer*), “the protective association dominant in a territory… *is* a state.” It has no special rights. The *de facto* monopoly grows “by an invisible-hand process and *by morally permissible means*, without anyone’s rights being violated.” See [[Concepts/Invisible-Hand Explanation of the State (Nozick)]].

Part II’s conclusion: “the minimal state remains as the most extensive state that can be justified.” Part III identifies the minimal state with the [[Concepts/Framework for Utopia (Nozick)|framework for utopia]]. Last line of the book: “How *dare* any state or group of individuals do more. Or less.”

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]**: the starting point, not the destination. Locke thought a compact necessary for civil society; Nozick thinks the dominant association arises without one, as money did. Independents may stay out. Clients are in civil society *inter se*; clients and independents remain in the state of nature with each other.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Hobbes]]**: the comparison class is the *best* realistic anarchy, not the war of all against all. The resulting sovereign is night-watchman, not Leviathan, and is not a party that absorbs all rights.
- **[[Thinkers/James M. Buchanan]]** / [[Thinkers/Gordon Tullock]]: both start from self-interested individuals escaping anarchy. Buchanan/Tullock design a constitution (qualified majorities, bicameralism) at a constitutional stage. Nozick’s state is not designed; it is filtered. Hart/Rawls “principle of fairness” would make unanimous consent unnecessary — Nozick rejects it; consent remains necessary.
- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]**: shared anti-paternalism (preface: the state may not prohibit activities for people’s *own* good). The harm principle licenses more than night-watchman functions once “harm” is read aggregatively.
- **Individualist anarchist** (Rothbard, in the book’s acknowledgments): monopoly + forced funding violate [[Concepts/Side Constraints (Nozick)|side constraints]]; the state is “intrinsically immoral.” Part I’s claim is that both transitions (agencies → ultraminimal → minimal) are morally permissible, and that operators of the ultraminimal *must* produce the minimal state.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Third pole on [[Contradictions/Hobbesian Absolutism vs. Lockean Constitutionalism]]: not absolute sovereignty, not a Lockean compact with a right of revolution, but a *de facto* monopoly with no special rights.
- Open in the text: the transition to the *minimal* state still leans on people doing what morality requires (or further incentives). Ch. 6 argues the argument does not “prove too much” (forbidding rivals). Rectification of past injustice “might be so great as to make necessary in the short run a more extensive state.”
