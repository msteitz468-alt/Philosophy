# Phenomena and Noumena (Kant)

> Appearances thought under the categories are *phaenomena*. A *noumenon* is a boundary concept: we may think things in themselves, not cognize them. There is no positive two-world division.

## Summary

The Analytic of Principles ends by drawing the consequence of schematism: the categories have determinate use only on spatiotemporal data, and space and time themselves are transcendentally ideal, so the categories have determinate cognitive use only on appearances.

**Phenomenon.** An appearance thought through the categories — an object of possible experience.

**Noumenon, negative sense.** "If by a noumenon we understand a thing insofar as it is not an object of our sensible intuition… this is a noumenon in the negative sense" (B 307). This is the official, permitted sense.

**Noumenon, positive sense.** An object of a non-sensible (intellectual) intuition — which we do not have. "The concept of a noumenon is therefore merely a **boundary concept**… only of negative use" (A 255 / B 310–311). We may think things in themselves under the name of "an unknown something" (B 312); we do not cognize them through the categories. Division of objects into two worlds is not permitted *in a positive sense* (B 311).

**Amphiboly.** Transcendental amphiboly = confusing the pure object of the understanding with appearance. Leibniz "intellectualized the appearances" as Locke "sensitivized" concepts (A 271 / B 327). The identity of indiscernibles holds for *intelligibilia*, not for drops of water in different places; *realitas phaenomenon* can oppose (forces); matter in space is a sum of relations, not monads; space and time are forms of sensibility, not an order of *substantiae noumena*.

This chapter is the theoretical source of the *Groundwork*'s [[Concepts/Two Standpoints: Sensible World and Intelligible World (Kant)|two standpoints]]. The Groundwork (4:458) then insists that the world of understanding is only a *standpoint* reason takes in order to think itself as practical — not an intuition of a second realm. The B *Critique* already warns against treating an undetermined being of understanding as a cognizable object.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Hegel.** The thing-in-itself is a self-contradiction: to define it as outside thought is already to have thought it. See [[Contradictions/Hegelian Monism vs. Kantian Dualism]].
- **Husserl.** Fulfilled perceptual intention reaches the thing *itself*; the leftover *Ding an sich* is rejected. See [[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]].
- **Schopenhauer.** The thing-in-itself is identified with Will — a positive use Kant forbids.
- **Two-object vs two-aspect readings.** Scholarly dispute (Guyer/Wood flag it). The vault records Kant's official pairing and the Groundwork's "standpoint" wording without closing the debate.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] If the noumenon is *nothing* for us, freedom/God/immortality are empty; if it is a second knowable world, the restriction of the *Critique* is abandoned. Official line: thinkable, not knowable; fillable by practical reason ([[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]]).
- The A edition still talks of *noumena/intelligibilia* more generously; B names the positive/negative distinction and tightens the ban. Prefer B for the official doctrine.
