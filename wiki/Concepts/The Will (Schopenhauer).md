# The Will (Schopenhauer)

> The blind, insatiable, non-rational metaphysical force that constitutes the noumenal essence of all reality.

## Summary

In *The World as Will and Representation* (1819), [[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer|Arthur Schopenhauer]] identifies the Kantian *thing-in-itself* (the ultimate reality behind sensory appearances) as **Will** (*Wille*). 

Unlike individual human willpower or conscious desire, the metaphysical Will is a single, unified, aimless force that drives all nature. It manifests at various levels of objectification—from physical gravity and chemical attractions, to biological growth and reproduction, and finally to conscious human desire. Because the Will is characterized by endless, unsatisfied striving, it is the root cause of all suffering in the world.

---

## Core Characteristics

### 1. The Noumenal Essence of the World
Schopenhauer accepts [[Thinkers/Kant|Kant]]'s division of the world into:
* **Representation** (*Vorstellung*): The world as it appears, structured by the [[Concepts/Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)|fourfold root]] — not a single lump called "space, time, and causality." PSR is the form of *this* side. It does not apply to the Will.
* **Thing-in-Itself**: The true reality behind appearances, which Kant held to be unknowable.

*On the Fourfold Root* (1847) §43: **motivation is causality seen from within** — "the cornerstone of my whole metaphysics." §42: identity of cognizing and willing subject is the "miracle *par excellence*." §21: no causal connection between act of will and bodily action; they are immediately one. The full identity of body and Will as noumenon is *WWR*, not argued in FR. See [[Sources/On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2012)]].

Schopenhauer argues that we have a unique, direct access to the thing-in-itself through our own bodies. When we act, we do not merely observe our body moving from the outside; we feel the inner drive—the Will—that moves it. Extrapolating from this internal experience, Schopenhauer concludes that the same Will is the inner essence of *all* things.

*WWR* Volume 2, Chapter XVIII **qualifies** that access: the will is “the nearest and clearest *phenomenon* of the thing-in-itself”; it has “cast off its veils, but still does not appear quite naked.” Time remains. After denial, further determinations may be absolutely unknowable. Do not quote “will = thing-in-itself” without this clause. See [[Concepts/Primacy of the Will in Self-Consciousness (Schopenhauer)]].

### 2. Unity and the Principium Individuationis
Because space and time are properties of our cognitive representation (not of the noumenon), the thing-in-itself must be free from space and time. 
* Consequently, the Will is **one** and undivided. 
* The apparent multiplicity of individual things in the world is an illusion created by the cognitive filter of space and time (the *principium individuationis* or principle of individuation). 
* In reality, a stone, a tree, a tiger, and a human are all objectifications of the exact same metaphysical Will.

### 3. The Source of Suffering and Pessimism
Because the Will is unified and infinite, yet objectifies itself in finite, competing individuals, it is constantly in conflict with itself (e.g., the predator eating the prey is the Will consuming itself). 

In conscious beings, the Will manifests as **desire**. Since the Will has no final goal or satisfaction:
* Every satisfied desire is immediately replaced by boredom or new desires.
* Striving is endless, and suffering is the default state of life.
* Life is an endless oscillation between the pain of unsatisfied desire and the boredom of satisfaction.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

> [!WARNING]
> Schopenhauer's Will-driven pessimism directly contradicts the rationalist optimism of [[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz]], who argued that the universe is governed by divine sufficient reason and is the "best of all possible worlds." **He does not deny sufficient reason *simpliciter*.** He affirms it as the form of representation and forbids it of the Will and of the world as totality. See [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism|Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]], [[Concepts/Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)]].

### Crux of Self-Abnegation
If the Will is the sole cause and essence of everything, how is it possible for humans to **deny** or quiet the Will (through asceticism or art)? 
* This is the central tension of Schopenhauer's ethics: the Will must somehow turn against itself. 
* Schopenhauer describes this as a rare, mysterious state where the Will, having achieved full self-knowledge through human intellect, decides to cease its own striving.

---

### From *WWR* Volume 2 (1844; Payne)

**Primacy.** Will is substance; intellect is accident, brain-function, tool, lantern. The strong blind man carries the sighted lame man. What is subjectively intellect appears as brain; what is subjectively will appears as the entire organism. Identity of act and body, not psychophysical causation.

**Grades without knowledge.** Gravity, water, crystal, plant: the same will, a *prius* of intellect. Matter is “the mere visibility of the will.” “What objectively is matter, subjectively is will.” Teleology is immanent (a motive that acts on a being by whom it is not known), not a designer. Kant refutes the physico-theological proof; Schopenhauer explains the fact.

**Characterization.** “The *will-to-live*… is the only true description of the world’s innermost nature.” “Life is a business whose returns are far from covering the cost.” Puppets with an internal clockwork; no final goal; the species, not the individual, is nature’s concern. Sexual impulse is the most complete manifestation; genitals are the focus of the will. See [[Concepts/Metaphysics of Sexual Love (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]].

### From *Parerga* Volume 1 (1851), not a new metaphysics

The will is still the real. The Sketch’s last step: the thing in itself “can certainly never be given… by way of mere *representation*”; “it enters consciousness… as *will*.” “The will alone is left as the real.” Fragments §14 names the system **immanent dogmatism** and the will **will to life**; things *are* its visibleness. The “narrow gateway” is the body as twofold. See [[Concepts/Doctrine of the Ideal and the Real (Schopenhauer)]].

Two sideways applications, not restatements: fate as the will operating beyond individual consciousness, the secret impresario of the life-dream ([[Concepts/Transcendent Fatalism (Schopenhauer)]]); clairvoyance as the will evading the *principium individuationis*, “the path through the thing in itself” ([[Concepts/Spirit-Seeing and Idealistic Explanation (Schopenhauer)]]). The Aphorisms treat excess intellect *beyond service of the will* as a second, painless life — eudaemonology, not Book IV.

### From *Parerga* Volume 2 (1851; Payne), still not a new metaphysics

Chs. I–V restate: thing-in-itself = will (after Democritus/Locke’s matter and Kant’s *x*). “It is because it wills, and wills because it is.” Pantheism is necessarily optimism, hence false. Chs. VI–VII: the correct cut is will / representation, not spirit / matter; matter is the visibility of the will; vital force “is absolutely identical with the will”; sciences stay on the objective side. Chs. X–XIV: intellect dies, will does not; generation is the world-knot; denial is *nolle*, not annihilation of a substance. Ch. XIX: beauty is pleasure without willing — will-free subject of knowing; music “speaks not of things, but simply of weal and woe.” See [[Sources/Parerga and Paralipomena Volume 2 - Schopenhauer (Payne 2000)]], [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]].

**From Nietzsche 1872 (reception, not this page’s primary):** *The Birth of Tragedy* §16 endorses music as “an immediate copy of the will itself” and uses the *principium individuationis* as Apollo. The *use* is already a step off: art saves the Greek from Buddhistic denial of the Will; existence is justified as aesthetic phenomenon. See [[Sources/The Birth of Tragedy - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]], [[Concepts/Aesthetic Justification of Existence (Nietzsche)]].
