# Disciplinary Society and the Panopticon (Foucault)
> Discipline "'makes' individuals." The Panopticon is not a dream building but the diagram of a mechanism of power reduced to its ideal form — a transferable figure whose vocation is to become a generalized function.

## Summary

*Surveiller et punir* (1975; Sheridan: *Discipline and Punish*) opens with Damiens quartered in 1757 against Faucher's 1838 prison timetable. The contrast is not progress but a change of *objective* and of political technology. The Macey biography already had the outline; the primary text supplies the mechanism.

**Docile bodies (III.1).** By the late eighteenth century the soldier is no longer recognized by natural signs; he is *made*. **Disciplines** are methods of meticulous control of bodily operations that impose a relation of docility-utility, distinct from slavery, service, vassalage, and monastic asceticism. "Discipline increases the forces of the body (in economic terms of utility) and diminishes these same forces (in political terms of obedience)." Four techniques: the art of distributions (enclosure, partitioning, functional sites, rank); the control of activity (timetable, temporal elaboration of the act, body-gesture correlation, body-object articulation, exhaustive use); the organization of geneses (exercise); the composition of forces (tactics). They confer four individualities — cellular, organic, genetic, combinatory. Beside the jurists' contract stands a military dream of society: "not to the general will but to automatic docility." See [[Concepts/Political Technology of the Body (Foucault)]].

**The means of correct training (III.2).** Discipline's success comes from three instruments:

1. **Hierarchical observation** — a machinery that coerces by seeing. Architecture is no longer built to be seen, but to transform those inside: "Stones can make people docile and knowable." The camp is "the diagram of a power that acts by means of general visibility." Supervisors are perpetually supervised; this power is not possessed as a thing.
2. **Normalizing judgement** — an "infra-penality" of the slightest departures (time, activity, speech, body, sexuality). Disciplinary punishment is essentially corrective: "To punish is to exercise." The perpetual penality "compares, differentiates, hierarchizes, homogenizes, excludes. In short, it *normalizes*" — opposed term by term to the law's binary permitted/forbidden.
3. **The examination** — their combination: "a normalizing gaze, a surveillance that makes it possible to qualify, to classify and to punish." It reverses visibility (power invisible; subjects compulsorily seen); writes the individual into an archive; makes each a "case"; and inverts individualization from ascending (the memorable sovereign) to descending (the calculable child, patient, madman, delinquent).

Power here is not only negative: it "produces reality… domains of objects and rituals of truth. The individual and the knowledge that may be gained of him belong to this production."

**Panopticism (III.3).** Two political dreams: **leprosy** → exclusion / "pure community" (the Great Confinement); **plague** → segmentations / "disciplined society." The nineteenth century applies plague techniques to the leper's space of exclusion (asylum, penitentiary, hospital). Bentham's Panopticon (ring of cells, unseen central tower) has as its major effect "conscious and permanent visibility that assures the automatic functioning of power." "Visibility is a trap." The inmate "becomes the principle of his own subjection"; inmates are "caught up in a power situation of which they are themselves the bearers." Power is automatized and disindividualized.

The Panopticon "must not be understood as a dream building: it is the diagram of a mechanism of power reduced to its ideal form… a figure of political technology that may and must be detached from any specific use." It is polyvalent (prison, hospital, school, workshop). **Disciplinary society** is the historical movement from enclosed "discipline-blockade" to an indefinitely generalizable "discipline-mechanism." The panoptic schema "was destined to spread throughout the social body; its vocation was to become a generalized function." Disciplines are "techniques for assuring the ordering of human multiplicities," substituting "mildness-production-profit" for "levying-violence." Accumulation of men and of capital cannot be separated. "The 'Enlightenment', which discovered the liberties, also invented the disciplines." Formal egalitarian law is underwritten by non-egalitarian micro-powers (disciplines as "counter-law"). "Our society is one not of spectacle, but of surveillance. … We are neither in the amphitheatre, nor on the stage, but in the panoptic machine." Closing homology: "Is it surprising that prisons resemble factories, schools, barracks, hospitals, which all resemble prisons?"

The prison is one application, not the capture of society by the prison. Discipline is "neither an institution nor an apparatus"; it would be "wrong to believe that the disciplinary functions were confiscated and absorbed once and for all by a state apparatus." For the prison as omni-disciplinary realization and for the *production* of delinquency, see [[Concepts/The Delinquent and the Carceral Continuum (Foucault)]]. For the contrast pole, see [[Concepts/Sovereign Power and the Spectacle of the Scaffold (Foucault)]].

## Key Claims

- Reformers invented a new economy of power — "not to punish less, but to punish better" — whose "respectable name" is humanity. They are not treated as hypocrites, and they did *not* design the universal prison (their semio-technique was incompatible with it).
- The examination is the technical matrix of the human sciences, as investigation was of the natural sciences.
- "Disciplinary society" does not mean the prison has taken over. It means enclosed techniques disseminate and become a transferable panoptic mechanism.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Jeremy Bentham** (no thinker page): Foucault does not treat him as a villain. He is the formulator of a *technical programme* — a closed "inspection-house" *and* a way to "unlock" the disciplines into a social network. Foucault rejects the gibe that Bentham is merely "the Fourier of a police society."
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: disciplines raise both docility and utility; they are the bodily condition of labour-power. See [[Concepts/Political Technology of the Body (Foucault)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Zephyr Teachout]]**: the processor panopticon (chickenization; platform surveillance) is a private-government intensification of the same diagram — the processor sees all, the surveilled cannot see each other. Teachout does not cite Foucault; the structural analysis converges. See [[Concepts/Chickenization of Labor]], [[Concepts/Surveillance Capitalism and Platform Power]].
- **[[Thinkers/Ian Hacking]]**: historical ontology / "making up people" shares the examination's production of kinds, with less suspicion of genuine progress.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Foucault's account can read as a relatively coordinated late-18th-century transition. He himself stresses petty techniques, irregular chronology, and French-system limits (I.1 n.3). Historians have questioned novelty and systematicity.
- *Discipline and Punish* does not yet distinguish individualizing discipline from later population-level [[Concepts/Biopolitics and Governmentality (Foucault)|biopower]]. "Population" here is the multiplicity discipline orders. *The History of Sexuality* I (1976) makes the distinction: disciplines are the first pole of bio-power, an **anatomo-politics of the human body** (body as machine, usefulness/docility), paired with a later **bio-politics of the population** (Hurley pp. 139–40). The two remain separate in the eighteenth century (army and schools vs demography) and join in nineteenth-century *agencements concrets*, of which the deployment of sexuality is one of the most important. Do not collapse the 1975 book into that pairing.

> [!warning] Do not write "surveillance state." Foucault says "society of surveillance" / "disciplinary society." Police is one takeover among several (school, family, workshop).
