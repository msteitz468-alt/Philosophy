# Transcendental Illusion and Ideas of Reason (Kant)

> Reason's subjective maxims look like objective principles. The resulting dialectic is natural and unavoidable: it "irremediably attaches to human reason."

## Summary

Cognition runs senses → understanding → reason. Understanding is the faculty of *rules* / unity of appearances; reason is the faculty of *principles* / "unity of the rules of understanding under principles." Reason applies not to objects but to the understanding (A 299 / B 356; A 302 / B 359).

In syllogisms reason seeks "the unconditioned for conditioned cognitions." The logical maxim becomes a synthetic principle of pure reason only if, when the conditioned is given, "the whole series of conditions… which is itself unconditioned" is also given (A 307–308 / B 364).

**Transcendental illusion** is not a logical slip. Logical illusion vanishes when the rule is attended to; transcendental illusion "does not cease even though it is uncovered" (A 297–298 / B 353–354). The "subjective necessity of a certain connection of our concepts" is taken for "an objective necessity, the determination of things in themselves." Critique *uncovers* the illusion and *protects* us from being deceived; it cannot make the illusion disappear. There is a "natural and unavoidable dialectic of pure reason" (A 298 / B 354–355).

**Ideas.** "A concept made up of notions, which goes beyond the possibility of experience, is an **idea** or a concept of reason" (A 320 / B 377). A **transcendental idea** is one "to which no congruent object can be given in the senses" (A 327 / B 383–384): the unconditioned / totality of conditions to a given conditioned. Three ideas, three inferences, three special metaphysics:

| Inference | Idea | Pseudo-science |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical | Soul | Rational psychology ([[Concepts/Paralogisms of Pure Reason (Kant)|Paralogisms]]) |
| Hypothetical | World-whole | Rational cosmology ([[Arguments/Antinomies of Pure Reason (Kant)|Antinomies]]) |
| Disjunctive | God / being of all beings | Transcendental theology ([[Concepts/Existence is Not a Real Predicate (Kant)|Ideal]]) |

"The thinking subject is the object of psychology, the sum total of all appearances (the world) is the object of cosmology, and… the being of all beings is the object of theology" (A 334–335 / B 391–392). No objective deduction of the ideas is possible; only a subjective derivation from reason's nature (A 336 / B 393).

The ideas keep an "excellent and indispensably necessary **regulative** use" as a *focus imaginarius* for the systematic unity of inquiry. Constitutive misuse — treating them as given objects — is the dialectic. See [[Concepts/Regulative Use of the Ideas of Reason (Kant)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Wolff / Baumgarten.** Special metaphysics (rational psychology, cosmology, theology) is exactly what the Dialectic dismantles. Ontology is replaced by the Analytic (A 247 / B 303).
- **Plato.** Kant takes the word *idea* and the thought of a standard beyond experience, but denies corresponding objects in the senses or in a knowable intelligible world.
- **Hegel.** The "unavoidable dialectic" becomes the *method* of truth, not a pathology of reason. See [[Concepts/Dialectical Method (Hegel)]].
- **Popper.** Critique as determination of *boundaries* (not mere limits) is the ancestor of demarcation. See [[Concepts/Problem of Demarcation (Kant and Popper)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] If the dialectic is *natural and inevitable*, what exactly does "critique" accomplish? Kant's answer: we stop being *deceived* even though the illusion does not vanish — like the optical illusion that remains after we know it is one.
