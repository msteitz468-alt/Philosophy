# Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)
> The prediction-error minimizer is isolated behind a veil of sensory input. Tools and niches tidy that channel; they do not take the mind out of the skull.

## Summary

This is Hohwy's distinctive thesis, argued in ch. 11 of *The Predictive Mind* and announced from the introduction: we are "trapped inside our skulls with only sensory data to go by" (p. 224); causes "remain hidden behind the veil of sensory input" (p. 50); we are "mental islands set over against the world" (p. 258). The book never uses the later slogan "Markov blanket." The load-bearing words are **seclusion**, **veil of sensory input**, **sensory boundary**, **skull-bound**, **internality**.

Perception is fragile, not a robust bottom-up readout. The world answers queries; it does not impress itself on us. Hohwy refuses a forced "direct vs indirect" choice. Indirectness is real — priors plus a generative model of hidden causes — but he also refuses the homuncular sense-data picture [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] rejects. "The relation of perceptual states to the states of the world is as direct as a causal relation is direct and, also, as indirect as a causal relation is indirect" (p. 228). Distinct existences, reliably coupled only inside a narrow comfort zone. The "mirror" is passive and conservative: models are nudged by surprise until they recapitulate sensory regularities; we are "mere pawns in the causal nexus" (p. 229).

The **Bayesian body** is not an immutable anchor. Rubber-hand and full-body illusions show a "swift sell-out" of body priors: efficient, high-precision error suppression beats the known truth. Bodily self-awareness is itself an internal model (pp. 230–36).

Seclusion is **explanatorily prior** to situatedness. "Because the brain is isolated behind the veil of sensory input, it is then advantageous for it to devise ways of optimizing the information channel from the world to the senses" (p. 238) — illuminate the room, build environments, radio, language. Culture and technology raise the precision of the world-to-senses *channel*. The mind stays "solidly set over against the rest of the world" and "secluded from the hidden causes" (pp. 238–39). The sensory boundary "is not arbitrary, but rather a principled, indispensable, and epistemically critical element for identifying what is inferring what" (p. 240). Doubts about the *totality* of sensory evidence (evil demon) cannot be answered from inside.

Hohwy's concession to Clark, and the refusal, are one paragraph: he agrees that situatedness makes environment and brains "genuinely continuous," "but only in the sense that it makes the causal link from the senses to the world more reliable… the mind remains secluded from the hidden causes of the world" (p. 239). A wholly extended mind would require PEM "relative to a distributed system taking input from hidden causes extraneous to it." Conceivable (miner + remote robot), but Hohwy thinks the model shatters when the miner runs himself over. "The best explanation of the kinds of example Clark and others highlight is that they are cases where prediction error is optimized… relative to just the nervous system" (p. 241).

Chapter 10 already refuses to let action buy embodiment: unity needs agency, but PEM remains an "internal mirror of nature." "It is better… to live with the risk of radical scepticism than to belie the core idea that perception and action are both inferences on the statistical properties of the sensory input" (p. 221).

## Key Claims / Positions

- **Veil, not window.** Sensory input is evidence for queries, not a stamp of the world.
- **Causal, not sense-data, indirectness.** No inner screen; still no going around the sensory boundary.
- **Body is inferred too.** The rubber hand is a feature, not a bug: PEM will sell out the body to suppress precise error.
- **Tools optimize the channel.** Notebooks, lights, language, built environments are the secluded brain's way of making its evidence better — they are not parts of the minimizer.
- **The boundary is principled.** It is how one identifies what is inferring what. Collapse it and the PEM story loses its agent.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]**: seclusion is the moral of PEM. The mechanism *implies* a skull-bound mind.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: the same mechanism is, in 2016, an [[Concepts/Action-Oriented Engagement Machine (Clark)|action-oriented engagement machine]], and in 2023 "the perfect internal organ for the creation of extended minds." *Surfing Uncertainty* Ch 6 is Clark's reply: "evidentiary boundary" is Hohwy's term; the demon is a "red herring"; inference need not throw away the world. See [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: Hohwy rejects the passive wax-imprint *and* ends up with something that *looks* Cartesian — a mind set over against the world, hostage to an evil demon about the totality of its evidence. The inference is Helmholtzian, not a *cogito*, but the geography is skull-bound.
- **[[Thinkers/George Berkeley]]** and [[Concepts/Simulation Hypothesis (Virk)]]: the veil invites idealism and simulation. Hohwy's comfort is that hypotheses "only [get] away with predictions that actually fit the incoming input well" (p. 240) — a naturalist wager, not a proof.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: Hohwy's secluded internal model is the opposite of "the world is its own best representation." Clark tried to keep Dreyfus and add a mechanism; Hohwy keeps the mechanism and drops the world-involving mind.
