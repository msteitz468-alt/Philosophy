# The Present Instant (Hadot)
> Happiness is only in the present. One instant is an eternity, and it must be had *now* — then resituated in the cosmos. Goethe’s formula names an ancient conversion, not a mood.

## Summary

Chapter 8 of *Philosophy as a Way of Life* takes Goethe’s line — “Only the present is our happiness” (*Faust* II, Helen; also *Wilhelm Meister*) — as the name of a **philosophical conversion**, not of idealized Greek serenity. Epicurean and Stoic therapies free us from past and future so we can live the instant. Shared axioms: happiness only in the present; one instant equals an eternity; it must be had *now*. The instant is then placed in the Whole: “the infinite value of the slightest existence.”

The present here is not a mathematical now. It is a lived “thickness” of attention — the Stoic [[Concepts/Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)|*prosoche*]]. “It’s now or never. … we must think in accordance with truth, act in accordance with justice, and lovingly accept what comes to pass.” That last clause is Marcus’s triad in one sentence. See [[Concepts/The Three Disciplines - Perception, Action, and Will (Hadot)]].

## Stoic vs Epicurean

“The Epicurean enjoys the present moment, whereas the Stoic wills it intensely; for the one, it is a pleasure; for the other, a duty.” Goethe is “half Stoic and half Epicurean.” The two attitudes are Hadot’s “opposite but inseparable poles.”

- **Epicurean**: *carpe diem*; do *not* premeditate evils; intensify present and remembered pleasures; friendship. See [[Thinkers/Epicurus]], [[Concepts/Ataraxia]].
- **Stoic**: delimit past and future; will the instant as one’s fate; *praemeditatio* prepares the will so nothing arrives against expectation. See [[Concepts/Premeditatio Malorum (Premeditation of Evils)]], [[Thinkers/Marcus Aurelius]].

[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]’s yes to one unique moment is the same consent to the whole: “If we say ‘Yes!’ to one single, unique moment: we have thus said yes, not only to ourselves, but to the whole of existence.” Hadot does not use the phrase *amor fati* here; the substance is this page’s. The *published* sites of that yes are now primary: GS **§276** (*amor fati* as New Year’s vow — not yet recurrence), **§341** (the demon: “even this moment and I myself”), and *Zarathustra* Part III’s gateway inscribed **"This Moment"** — two eternities abut there; the drunken song’s joy that says Yea to one instant wants *all* things again. The gateway is an ontological hinge, not a Hadot-style spiritual exercise. Do not write Hadot’s apparatus back into those texts. See [[Concepts/Amor Fati (Love of Fate)]], [[Concepts/Eternal Recurrence (Nietzsche)]], [[Sources/The Gay Science - Nietzsche (Kaufmann 1974)]], [[Sources/Thus Spoke Zarathustra - Nietzsche (Common 1909)]].

Goethe: “the Instant is eternity”; “Every circumstance, every instant is of infinite value, for it is the representative of an entire eternity.” Systole/diastole; the “healthiness of the moment” (letter to Zelter).

## Tied Exercises

- **[[Concepts/View from Above and Cosmic Perspective (Marcus Aurelius)]]** — the instant is worthless unless dilated into cosmic consciousness: “the consciousness that we are a part of the cosmos, and the consequent dilation of our self throughout the infinity of universal nature.”
- **Sage and world** — the sage “never ceases to have the whole constantly present to his mind. … The sage is a part of the world; he is cosmic.” Seneca looks at the world *tamquam spectator novus*, “as though I were seeing for the first time” — which is the same as seeing it for the last time. See [[Thinkers/Seneca]].
- **Practical physics** — imagination traversing the universe (all schools except Skepticism). Klee: *natura naturans* over *natura naturata* (translator points to [[Thinkers/Spinoza]], *Ethics* I.29).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Marcus** — present-moment triple rule (judgment, action, inner disposition); “I love along with you.”
- **Seneca** — *spectator novus*; rehearsal of death as last-time seeing.
- **Epicurus / Horace** — *carpe diem* as exercise, not slogan.
- **Goethe** — the modern name of the conversion; poetry itself as view-from-above.
- **Nietzsche** — yes to one moment = yes to existence; institutions “prevent mankind from feeling their life.”
- **Holiday** — “live in the present moment” as a Perception chapter, without Hadot’s Stoic/Epicurean split or the cosmic resituation.
