# Selfless Minds (Varela)
> Cognitive science uncovers a nonunified cognizing subject and then ignores the discovery in life. Buddhist psychology describes the same fragmentation as experience, and treats the "I" as unfindable in the storm.

## Summary

Parts II–III of [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)|*The Embodied Mind*]] juxtapose two descriptions of a mind without a self.

**Cognitivism** (ch. 3–4). Cognition pairs with *intentionality*, not consciousness. Processes can be unconscious *and cannot be brought to consciousness at all* (Fodor modularity). Dennett: "You enter the brain through the eye… scratching your head and wondering where the self is." Jackendoff's "mind–mind problem": computational vs phenomenological mind; awareness is a *projection* from intermediate-level representations; he denies consciousness causal efficacy — "not good for anything." The self is not needed for cognition.

**Mindfulness / Abhidharma** (ch. 4). Hume cannot catch himself without a perception; Kant posits a transcendental ego; Descartes's *cogito* stops short of examining the "I." Meditators report impermanence, selflessness, *dukkha*. The five *skandhas* (forms, feelings, perceptions/impulses, dispositional formations, consciousnesses) are descriptive heaps, not ontology. No lasting, single, independent self in any aggregate or their sum. "There is no experiencer… no landing platform for experience. This actual experiential sense of no one home is called selflessness or egolessness." "We entered the very eye of the storm of experience, we just simply could discern there no self, no 'I.'" "The very fact that the aggregates are full of experience is the same as the fact that they are empty of self." Consciousness (*vijñāna*) is proto-intentional but not Husserl/Brentano representation; six sense-consciousnesses (Jackendoff-like disunity). Continuity is candle-to-candle causal succession.

Perceptual framing / apparent motion (~0.1–0.2 s) is offered as *consonance* with a minimum "now," tied to cortical alpha (~0.15 s). Note 17: this does **not** decide whether moments are contiguous (Theravāda) or gapped. Thompson 2016 recants reading such results as revealing that consciousness is *really* discontinuous apart from the observation.

**Emergence** (ch. 5–6). Connectionism: cognition as "the emergence of global states in a network of simple components"; "symbols, in their conventional sense, play no role." Still not enough: learned states are said to *represent* items; adequacy is still correspondence to a capacity; science and lived experience stay unjoined.

**Society of mind** is taken as architecture and then criticized. [[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]] / Papert: mind as a "disunified, heterogenous collection of networks of processes"; agencies from agents; insulation. They use that logic. Minsky quotes the Buddha but will not drop the Self; science and experience "come apart." Minsky on free will: "We're virtually forced to maintain that belief, even though we know it's false." Their reply: "real freedom comes not from the decisions of an ego-self's 'will' but from action without any Self whatsoever." Object-relations therapy usually *feeds* self-grasping.

**Codependent arising** (*pratītyasamutpāda*) answers coherence-without-self: twelve *nidānas* on the Wheel of Life. Karma = psychological habit-causality, not fate. Break-point is craving → grasping via mindfulness. Vasubandhu: *dharmas* are "ultimate" yet *not* substances; primary mind : mental factors :: agency : agents. Ego-self = "historical pattern among moment-to-moment emergent formations." After the self is unfindable, "the world" as not-self also loosens — the turn toward [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]].

Without a transformative practice, theoretical no-self yields Nietzschean nihilism. See [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)]].

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: subpersonal mechanisms, discharged homunculi, narrative self. Ally on fragmentation; no first-person method; consciousness is not "not good for anything" in *Consciousness Explained* the way Jackendoff has it.
- **[[Thinkers/Douglas Hofstadter]]**: *The Mind's I* (with Dennett) is cited as the popular book they find procedurally inadequate. [[Concepts/Emergence and Consciousness (Hofstadter)|Strange-loop emergence]] is a different self-from-parts story (self-representation at sufficient complexity), not Abhidharma heaps.
- **[[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]]**: architecture accepted; experiential split charged. See [[Concepts/Society of Mind (Minsky)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Hume]]**: cannot find a self. They add a method Hume lacked.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: transcendental unity of apperception as the philosophical *positing* of what is not found.
- **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: later "self as controlled hallucination" is a predictive-processing cousin, not this argument.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Theoretical no-self + unchanged grasping = the nihilist horn of [[Concepts/Cartesian Anxiety (Varela)]].
- Dreyfus/Thompson 2016: you cannot read meditative discontinuity back into ordinary coping. The 1991 juxtaposition treats the two descriptions as mutually illuminating, not as one reducing the other.
