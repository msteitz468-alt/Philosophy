# Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)
> Daily seeing is already the work of the understanding. The outer world is produced from a few sensations; it is not given by the five senses.

## Summary

*On the Fourfold Root* (1847) **§21** remains the vault's *comprehensive* statement — he says so in *On Vision and Colours* itself, sending the reader there for the full theory. The first rehearsal is the 1854 Chapter I of [[Sources/On Vision and Colours - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2012)]] (composed 1815, printed 1816). It is not Schelling's "intellectual intuition" of the Absolute. Schopenhauer means ordinary empirical seeing.

> "the understanding first creates and produces this objective external world from the raw stuff of a few sensations in the sense organs."

Professorial philosophy treats intuition as a matter of the five senses and says nothing of this intellectual basis. For Schopenhauer, sensation is "a completely subjective process internal to the organism because it is beneath the skin." Time and space (pure sensibility) plus the unique form of the understanding — **causality** — construct objects from that raw stuff. Touch and sight supply data that can be spatialized; hearing does not construct space.

This is how he keeps Kant's idealism without Kant's Achilles' heel: he does **not** apply causality transcendently from sensation to a thing-in-itself. The causal law *constitutes* empirical intuition; after that it cannot be used to posit a cause *behind* the representation. See [[Concepts/Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Phenomena and Noumena (Kant)]].

**Animals have understanding, not reason.** "All animals, down to the lowest, must have understanding, i.e., cognition of the causal law." They perceive a world of spatio-temporal objects. Reason (concepts, language, inference) is human. This is the opposite of Kant's use of reason as the dignity that makes humans morally considerable and animals not.

**§23** then rejects Kant's proof of the apriority of causality in the Second Analogy (house scanned vs ship downstream). Both are events, alterations of relative position. Appearances can follow one another without following *from* one another. Hume explained all consequences as mere sequences; Kant wants no sequences but consequence. The correct proof of apriority is §21: causality is a prerequisite of experience itself. See [[Concepts/Schematism and Principles of Pure Understanding (Kant)]].

**§21** also: "there is absolutely no causal connection between the act of will and the action of the body; rather, both are immediately one and the same." That sentence belongs to the 1847 rewrite; 1813 still had will as cause. It is not yet the full *WWR* identity of body and Will as thing-in-itself. See [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]].

Hume's two origin-hypotheses for the idea of cause (will moving a limb; resistance of bodies) are rejected. The concept is not copied from those experiences; it is the form that makes them possible.

### What 1816/1854 adds (the rehearsal, not a second theory)

*On Vision and Colours* §1 is the forerunner. Distinctive load:

- Without understanding, a vista is "the sensation of a highly varied affection of his retina that resembles the many blobs of colour on an artist's palette."
- One object from two inverted retinal impressions because intuition is intellectual (optical axes; homologous retinal points). The **stereoscope** is "a more striking proof of the intellectual nature of intuition."
- **Illusion** (*Schein*) is deception of the understanding and opposes *reality*; **error** is deception of reason and opposes *truth*. Reason cannot abolish illusion (crossed eyes and fingers; stick in water; moon on the horizon; concave/convex mirrors; a painting as relief).
- Three levels of causality, aphoristically: inorganic body (cause = effect); plant (stimulus); animal (motive = causality through cognition). Animals have understanding; plants do not.
- Colour is "an unnecessary addition" clothing bodies. Light and colour are affections of the eye *before* the understanding assigns them to a cause. Crossed eyes cannot alter colour. "'The body is red' means that in the eyes it produces the colour red." *Seyn* = *Wirken*; *wirklich* = *wirkend*.

The colour *theory* (what that retinal affection *is*) is not this page. See [[Concepts/Physiological Theory of Colour (Schopenhauer)]].

### From *WWR* Volume 2, Book I (Payne)

The principal work’s supplements recast §21 as **knowledge of perception**. “The senses give us mere *sensation*, which is still far from being *perception*.” “It is true that space is only in my head; but empirically my head is in space.” Ch. V: animals have understanding without reason; “the life of the animal is a continual present”; they do not laugh. Ch. VII: perceptions are “the ready money, the concepts are the notes”; perception “is itself knowledge… unconditionally true.” See [[Concepts/Theory of the Ludicrous (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Genius (Schopenhauer)]].

## Key Claims

- Empirical intuition is intellectual: understanding + space/time produce objects from sensation.
- Causality is the understanding's one function, not one of twelve categories.
- Animals share this understanding.
- Kant's Second Analogy is the wrong proof of a fact §21 already secures.
- Do not confuse this with German-Idealist intellectual intuition of the Absolute.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — rehearsed in *On Vision and Colours* (1816 / 1854, now primary); completed in FR 1847 §21; restated in *WWR* II Chs. II–VII as knowledge of perception.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — transcendental idealism kept; the clockwork of twelve categories dropped; perception is not mere sensation plus later categories.
- **[[Thinkers/Hume]]** — custom of sequence is not the origin of the causal concept.
- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]** / later embodiment — nearest later kin on seeing as already structured, but do not import *I can* into §21.
