# Histrionics and Theatrocracy (Nietzsche)
> The musician is becoming an actor. The stage must not become master of the arts.

## Summary

The practical conclusion of *The Case of Wagner* (1888) is not “stop listening to Wagner.” It is three requisitions (§12):

- That the **stage** should not become master of the arts.
- That the **actor** should not become the corrupter of the genuine.
- That **music** should not become an art of lying.

§7 states the transformation: “the musician is now becoming an actor, his art is developing ever more and more into a talent for *telling lies*.” He will treat this more thoroughly under “Concerning the Physiology of Art” (promised, not in this pamphlet). The restlessness of Wagnerian optics — one must change attitude every second — is already a form of hysteria. Wagner is not a sport of nature or a contradictory genius; the consistency with which a critical physiological condition converts itself into a method, a reform of principles, a crisis in taste, is the interest.

§8: “Was Wagner a musician at all? In any case he was something else to *a much greater degree* — that is to say, an incomparable *histrio*, the greatest mime, the most astounding theatrical genius that the Germans have ever had, our *scenic artist par excellence*.” He belongs to some other sphere than the history of music. Wagner *and* Beethoven is blasphemy — and does not even do Wagner justice. He was not instinctively a musician. The tyrant in him, his actor’s genius, drove him to become musician and poet. He increased the powers of speech of music to an incalculable degree; he is the Victor Hugo of music as language — *provided* that under certain circumstances music may be something which is not music but speech-instrument, *ancilla dramaturgica*. Not in the theatre’s care, it is simply bad music. “When a musician can no longer count up to three, he becomes ‘dramatic,’ he becomes ‘Wagnerian.’” He never calculates with a musician’s conscience; he strains after effect. Talma’s rule contains the actor’s psychology and morality: “everything which has to strike people as true, must not be true.” “Wagner’s music is never true.” But it is *supposed* to be so, “and thus everything is as it should be.”

§9: even the sketch of the action is the actor’s. First an overwhelming scene, a basso-relievo of attitudes; characters are drawn out of it. Drama demands inexorable logic; Wagner loved the *word* “drama” (and affected superiority to “opera”). He was not enough of a psychologist; he put idiosyncrasy in the place of a psychological plot. Very modern, very Parisian, very decadent.

§11: historically this is **the rise of the actor in music**. “Wagner and Liszt.” Great success, mob success, is no longer the achievement of the genuine; to get it a man must be an actor. Victor Hugo and Richard Wagner prove the same thing: in declining civilisations, wherever the mob is allowed to decide, genuineness becomes superfluous. The actor’s golden age is dawning. Coincidence with the rise of the “Empire”: both prove obedience and long legs. Expressiveness at all costs — the Wagnerian ideal, the ideal of decadence — is hardly compatible with talent. What is required is virtue: training, automatism, “self-denial.” Taste, a good voice, even talent, are out of the question. Wagner is sung only with ruined voices; this has a more “dramatic” effect.

**Theatrocracy** is the Postscript’s name for the worst cultural cost. “The craziness of a belief in the pre-eminence of the theatre, in the right of the theatre to rule supreme over the arts, over Art in general.” This should be shouted at Wagnerites a hundred times: the theatre is something *lower* than art, secondary, coarsened, “suitably distorted and falsified for the mob.” Wagner altered nothing: “Bayreuth is grand Opera — and not even good opera.” The stage is Demolatry in the realm of taste, “an insurrection of the mob, a *plébiscite* against good taste.” He captivated the masses and depraved taste — even the taste for opera.

The other costs in the same Postscript: layman’s arrogance organising societies and posing as judges *in rebus musicis*; indifference to severe schooling, replaced by belief in genius, “cheeky dilettantism” (the *Meistersinger* formula); then the corruption of ideas (the youthlet as moon-calf “idealist”) and of the nerves. Bayreuth is another word for a Hydro.

***Nietzsche Contra Wagner*** states the same actor-first practice without the three requisitions: Wagner’s theory was “drama is the object, music is only a means”; his practice was “the attitude is the end.” In Bayreuth “the individual lies”; “one *becomes* a neighbour.” Anti-theatrical at heart: stage success drops a man from sight. See [[Sources/Nietzsche Contra Wagner - Nietzsche (Ludovici, Collection 2013)]].

## Key Claims

- Actor > musician in Wagner; music as servant of the scene.
- Truth-effect, not truth: Talma’s rule.
- Mob success selects the actor; genuineness becomes a drawback.
- Theatrocracy: theatre claiming the right to rule Art.
- The three requisitions are the pamphlet’s only “programme.”

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — the cultural form of [[Concepts/Decadence (Nietzsche)]]: anarchy of atoms in the work becomes the actor’s domination of the arts. Distinct from the Cagliostro diagnosis (modernity’s seducer); this page is the *institution* (stage, Bayreuth, the mob).
- **[[Concepts/Philosophy as Involuntary and Unconscious Autobiography (Nietzsche)]]** — the actor is the man ahead of mankind in one view: what must strike people as true must not be true. A public, theatrical version of the philosopher’s “advocates who do not wish to be regarded as such.”
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]** — the vault already has the old quarrel of philosophy with the theatre (*Republic*). Do not back-read *Republic* X into *CW*. Nietzsche’s target is Bayreuth and “declining civilisations,” not mimesis as such.
- **[[Concepts/Art and Truth (Heidegger)]]** — Heidegger’s later art-essay is not about Wagner’s histrionics. Do not merge.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Nietzsche grants that Wagner’s influence on conductors, singers, and even musical science (Riemann’s phrasing) has been *beneficent* in exacting more from the profession. The three requisitions are not a denial of that local gain.
