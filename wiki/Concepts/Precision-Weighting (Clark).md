# Precision-Weighting (Clark)
> The brain's estimate of how reliable a prediction or sensory signal is — the "volume control" that decides whether your guesses or your senses win — and, on Clark's account, what attention *really is*.

## Summary

In [[Concepts/Predictive Processing (Clark)]], predictions and sensory prediction-errors must be combined, and everything turns on their relative weighting. **Precision** is the brain's best estimate of the reliability of a signal in a given context — "the estimated value of this information, in this context, for this task" — implemented physiologically as altered post-synaptic gain on neuronal populations. Only predictions (or sensory signals) estimated as *reliable* strongly shape experience.

Clark's boldest identification: **precision-weighting is attention.** *Surfing Uncertainty* Ch 2 is the technical source: "Attention, thus construed, is a means of variably balancing the potent interactions between top-down and bottom-up influences by factoring in their so-called 'precision'" (2.3). Attention is a *dimension* of the generative model (estimates of current and action-contingent precision), not a spotlight-mechanism. Feldman & Friston: "a selective sampling of sensory data that have high-precision (signal to noise) in relation to the model's predictions." Ch 5 adds the large-scale job: precision sculpts **effective connectivity**, assembling transient task-specific coalitions. Ch 9 adds a human trick: **words as tools for manipulating precision** — cheap artificial contexts that temporarily modify error impact at chosen hierarchical levels.

The 2023 popularization calls precision "the single most powerful tool in the predictive processing toolkit." Precision effects can run **nonconsciously** — "the tip of the predictive iceberg" — as in subliminal face/shock pairing experiments.

## Key Claims / Positions — Pain, Placebo, and Computational Psychiatry

- **Pain is a construction, not a readout.** "Brains build human experience only by combining their own predictions with sensory evidence." Nociceptor firing is only *one source of evidence*: the construction worker who felt searing pain from a nail that (it turned out) passed harmlessly between his toes shows pain with no injury; other cases show no pain despite massive nociception. Pain is a *perception*, and chronic pain can become "a disease in its own right" — the third, "nociplastic," pain category.
- **Placebo and nocebo run deep** (down to the spinal cord), driven by expectations of relief or harm; Irene Tracey's fMRI work shows even religious imagery modulating pain.
- **Functional disorders** — genuine impairment (tremor, weakness, tubular visual fields, functional blindness) with no structural cause — are explained by *aberrant precision/attention*, not faking. Jon Stone's light-triggered functional blindness, Hoover's sign for functional leg weakness, and Rob McIntosh's numbness-weakness correlations are Clark's cases.
- **Autism (2016 is not a single slogan).** Pellicano & Burr: **hypo-priors** — more incoming stuff treated as signal, less as noise. Van de Cruys: not merely weak priors but **inflexible precision modulation** (a noisy signal treated as precise). Clark: no deep conflict; weakened hyperpriors. The 2023 "overweighted sensory precision" line is one pole of this, not the whole 2016 story.
- **Psychosis / schizophrenia (2016 is more careful than 2023).** Ch 2: highly weighted **false errors** fuse hallucination and delusion. Ch 7: the same clinical picture can be told as high-precision sensory PE *or* as **reduced higher-level precision** (RHLP) / failed sensory attenuation — Bayesian-equivalent, neurally distinct. SPEM triad is produced by RHLP. Clark flags the paradox: patients look prior-dominated, but the causal arrow may start as weakened prior influence vs sensory evidence, later spawning strange high-level theories. The 2023 slogan "psychosis = high-precision prediction-error" flattens this. PTSD and Chadwick's coincidences are 2023 material.
- **No single correct balance.** Optimal precision-weighting is *relative to environment*; "computational psychiatry" reconceives disorders as differing balances in information processing rather than fixed symptom clusters. This is the mechanism later exploited in [[Concepts/Hacking the Prediction Machine (Clark)]].

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: attention-as-precision fits the Multiple Drafts picture in which no content is "definitively conscious" until it wins the competition for influence; precision is a candidate mechanism for that weighting.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: precision governs *how much* the mind's own contribution shapes a given experience — a dial on the Kantian point that mind co-constitutes appearance.
- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]** (2013, earlier): already identifies attention with expected-precision optimization ("attention is nothing but optimization of precision expectations," p. 71) and already runs a two-pole computational psychiatry. **Autism matches**: both treat it as overweighted sensory precision / "enslavement by the senses" (Hohwy p. 161). **Psychosis differs**: Hohwy's generic delusion story is *low* sensory gain and overweight priors — an exaggerated expectation that PE is *noisy* (p. 158, 165). Clark 2023's "psychosis = high-precision prediction-error" is not Hohwy's 2013 account. Hohwy's nuance: against a subdued PE landscape, large residuals look extra exceptional and demand explaining-away — salience of residual error, not high sensory precision as the primary lesion.
- **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: same identification — attention *is* inferred precision; this explains Simons's inattentional blindness (gorilla). Strong priors overwhelming data *are* hallucination ([[Concepts/Beholder's Share (Seth)]]). No independent psychiatry taxonomy.
