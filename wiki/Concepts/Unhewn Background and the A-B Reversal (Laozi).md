# Unhewn Background and the A-B Reversal (Laozi)

> Ziporyn's name for the through-tone of the *Daodejing*: a consistent inversion of valued A-items (being, strength, name, adulthood, action, high, bright, complete) into disvalued B-items (nonbeing, softness, nameless, infancy, nondoing, low, dark, unhewn). The **unhewn** (*pu*, raw block, undyed blankness) is B as raw material, as the stuff and rule of A, and as what A returns to — the one thing that is constant (*chang*/*heng*).

## The A/B frame (Ziporyn's Note)

Early Chinese readers are assumed to seek A and avoid B. The corpus "promote[s] the B and demote[s] the A." This is not a one-time slogan but the structure of the juxtapositions, reversals, and recycled pericopes. *Chang*/*heng* (constant, ordinary, sustainable, ever-present) points to B in all its roles: prior to the A/B split; the wood the cup is still made of; the rule that guides A's rise and fall; the B into which A reverts.

The unhewn is the concrete emblem. Ch. 15: ancient adepts were "solid and weighty, like raw blocks of unhewn wood." Ch. 19: after cutting off sagacity, humankindness, skill, what remains is "vision of the undyed blankness, embrace of the unhewn rawness." Ch. 28: know the masculine while maintaining the feminine, lucid/opaque, honorable/disgraceful — reversion to newborn, to "utmost absence," to **the unhewn**. "When the unhewn gets shattered, it is made into vessels and tools"; the sage uses "the great cutting and carving that severs nothing." Ch. 32: "The constancy of the course lies in its namelessness — its being indeterminable, unhewn. When it gets carved up the names appear." Ch. 37: desires are settled with "the nameless unhewn." Ch. 57: the people, of their own accord, "revert to their unhewn simplicity."

## Load-bearing reversals in the text

- **Opposites already there (ch. 2).** "When all in the world know the beautiful to be beautiful, there already is the ugly." Presence/absence generate; difficult/easy complete; high/low collapse.
- **This over here / that over there (chs. 12, 38).** The sage discards "that over there" (eye, flower, thin) and takes up "this over here" (stomach, fruit, thick).
- **Return to the root (ch. 16).** "However lushly they may flourish and grow, all things return, each reverts to its root. This reversion to the root is the real stillness… the real constancy. Knowing this constancy is the real illumination."
- **Partial then whole (ch. 22).** Bent then straight, hollow then filled — the sage "embraces unity" by not displaying himself.
- **Exaltation rooted in debasement (ch. 39).** Lords style themselves "the lonely, the orphaned, the destitute." They "desire not to be gleaming and glossy like the jade, but rather clumpy and coarse like the rocks."
- **The activity of the course (ch. 40).** "Reversal, opposition: the activity of the course. Weakness, fragility: the functioning of the course. All things in the world are born from what is there, but what is there is born from what is not there."
- **Looks like its opposite (chs. 41, 45).** Clarity looks like darkness; advance like retreat; highest virtuosity like a valley. "Great completeness seems defective… Great fullness seems empty… Great straightness seems bent. Great skill seems clumsy."
- **Learning vs. the course (ch. 48).** Learning adds; the course subtracts.
- **Disaster/blessing (ch. 58).** Each hides in the other; "the correct and normal becomes the unexpected and strange. And the good becomes the monstrous."
- **Straight words reversed (chs. 78, 81).** "Straight words always seem reversed." "True words are not beautiful. Beautiful words are not true."

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Vs. [[Concepts/Heraclitean Flux and Logos (Heraclitus)]]**: Heraclitus also binds opposites ("the upward and the downward path are one and the same"; "strife is justice"). He does **not** tell you to inhabit the downward path. Laozi does: stay with the female, the opaque, the disgraceful, the unhewn. Unity of opposites vs. *asymmetric occupation of B*.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Beyond Good and Evil (Nietzsche)]] / [[Concepts/Master Morality and Slave Morality]]**: Nietzsche diagnoses belief in "antitheses of values" as the metaphysician's prejudice and then revalues toward the noble A-pole (strength, distance, yes-saying to power). Laozi agrees that the antitheses are unstable (ch. 2) and then occupies the *slave*-coded pole (soft, low, last). Formal cousins; material enemies.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Aufhebung (Sublation)]]**: Hegel keeps both poles by raising them. Laozi does not synthesize A and B into a higher A; he lets A revert. Ch. 40 is reversal, not *Aufhebung*.
- **Vs. [[Concepts/Negative Theology and Divine Attributes (Maimonides)]]**: both prefer unsaying. Maimonides unsays to protect a simple God from composition. Laozi unsays because naming *is* the carving that shatters the unhewn into tools. The unhewn is not God.
- **Function of absence (ch. 11).** "Profit lies in the having of something… but function lies in the having of nothing." The empty hub, the hollow vessel, the windows of the room. B is not a lack to be filled; it is what makes A work.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Occupying B can become a new A ("I am the one who is unhewn"). Ch. 24 already mocks self-displayers as "spoiled leftovers or tumorous growths." The frame has to apply to itself.
- Ziporyn's A/B scheme is an interpreter's grid, not a term in the Chinese. It is the most explicit whole-corpus claim this edition makes; later Zhuangzi ingest may strain it.
