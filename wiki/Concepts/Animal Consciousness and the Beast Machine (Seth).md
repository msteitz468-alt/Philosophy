# Animal Consciousness and the Beast Machine (Seth)
> Consciousness tracks life more than intelligence. All mammals are in; octopuses are an independent experiment; nematodes and bacteria are not. You don't have to be smart to suffer.

## Summary

Chapter 12 of *Being You* draws the ethical and comparative moral of [[Concepts/Beast Machine Theory (Seth)]]. Medieval animal trials presupposed inner lives; Cartesian *bêtes-machines* denied them. Today "it would be strange and almost perverse to argue that only humans are conscious," but the *extent* and *kind* of nonhuman inner universes remain open.

Language, reports, and "high-level" cognition (metacognition) are **not** criteria: "Absence of language is not evidence for absence of consciousness." Treating intelligence as a litmus test is both anthropocentric and anthropomorphic, and it licenses methodological laziness. "Not only can consciousness exist without all that much intelligence – you don't have to be smart to suffer – but intelligence can exist without consciousness too."

The beast-machine thesis "makes the case that consciousness is more closely connected with being *alive* than with being *intelligent*." This widens the circle relative to an intelligence criterion, but "it does not mean that wherever there is life there is also consciousness."

**Verdicts by clade:**

- **All mammals** — "pretty confident," not by resemblance but by shared mechanisms: six-layered cortex, interconnected thalamus, brainstem, conserved sleep/wake and anaesthesia dynamics. Contents and selfhood still diverge (*Umwelt*; Gallup mirror-passers: some great apes, a few dolphins/orcas, one Eurasian elephant; monkeys fail). Monkeys have conscious selfhood but "are not furry little people."
- **Birds** — "pretty strong case for sentience" (pallium–cortex mapping).
- **Octopus** — an independent ~600-Myr experiment (Godfrey-Smith: "most other of all"). Distributed, myelin-poor, possibly without a single centre; camouflage is open-loop and colour-blind; body-ownership may be absent; self/other is taste-based; "there might even be something it is like to be an octopus arm."
- **Fish / insects** — analgesia-seeking (zebrafish) and hypersensitivity (*Drosophila*) are "suggestive, none of it conclusive." Anaesthetics work "across *all* animals."
- **Cutoff** — nematode (302 neurons): "difficult to ascribe any meaningful conscious status"; paramecium "just doesn't make the grade."

Welfare: look to pain and physiological-integrity responses, with Birch-style precaution. Cephalopods already included in EU 2010.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Criterion is shared mechanism where available, then beast-machine physiological regulation / capacity for suffering — not IQ, not human similarity, not language.
- Consciousness *profile* (Birch et al.) rather than a single scalar "how conscious."
- Nagel is **not named** in this chapter; the bat appears only as echolocating *Umwelt*. The 1974 "something it is like" definition was adopted in ch. 1.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: the mark of consciousness Seth accepted at the outset. Ch. 12 does the comparative work Nagel said we cannot do for the *specific* character of echolocation, while granting general types (pain, hunger). Seth is more willing to ascribe consciousness down the tree on mechanistic grounds. See [[Concepts/Subjective Character of Experience (Nagel)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the foil. The *bête-machine* denied animal inner life; Seth's beast machine is why animals *have* it.
- **[[Thinkers/Alan Turing]]**: language as a test of *thought*, not of sentience. Seth severs the two.
- **Dehaene**: infant and monkey workspace signatures (from the in-progress *Consciousness and the Brain*) would support Seth's mammal verdict by a different criterion (access/broadcast). Seth will not take workspace as sufficient for machines.
