# Genealogy (Foucault)
> Genealogy is a "history of the present" — a Nietzschean method that tracks the contingent power struggles through which norms, institutions, and forms of subjectivity came to seem natural and necessary.

## Summary

Foucault's decisive encounter with Nietzsche occurred in the summer of 1953, when he read the *Untimely Meditations* in Italy. Nietzsche's challenge — "where would you find a history of love, of avarice, of envy, of conscience, of tradition, or of cruelty?" — functioned as a program for Foucault's entire career. The genealogical method was fully deployed in the works of the 1970s, especially *Surveiller et punir* (1975) and the Collège de France lecture series. The primary statement is now in *Discipline and Punish* I.1: the book is "a correlative history of the modern soul and of a new power to judge; a genealogy of the present scientifico-legal complex from which the power to punish derives its bases, justifications and rules." Four rules follow: treat punishment as a complex social function (not merely repressive); treat it as a political tactic; treat penal law and the human sciences as one epistemologico-juridical formation; and read the entry of the soul into justice as a transformation in how power invests the body. The closing sentence of I.1 refuses "a history of the past in terms of the present" and accepts "writing the history of the present." Nietzsche is *not* named in that chapter; the word "genealogy" carries the method.

Genealogy differs from archaeology in two respects:
1. **Power is central:** Genealogy traces how power relations invest discursive formations, not just the internal rules of discourse. The question becomes: whose interests does this knowledge serve, and through what mechanisms?
2. **Materiality:** Genealogy works on bodies, practices, institutions, and texts — not exclusively on discourse. It studies what Foucault calls "the effective history" (*historia effectiva*) — the actual consequences and uses of ideas and practices in specific power relations.

Genealogy is explicitly **anti-teleological:** it refuses to read history as progress toward a goal. What appears as liberation or reform is typically a transformation and intensification of control. This is the central irony of *Surveiller et punir*: the shift from public torture to the penitentiary is not humanization but the installation of more diffuse, efficient, and productive power.

## Key Claims

- There are no origins, only beginnings and their distortions: genealogy finds not foundations but accidents, power plays, and contingent victories.
- Normalization is the genealogist's primary target — the process by which historically particular norms become naturalized as universal or necessary.
- The "specific intellectual" (not the universal one) is the political form suited to genealogy: one who speaks from concrete knowledge of a specific institution, not in the name of universal humanity.
- Genealogy is explicitly motivated by present-day problems — it is a "history of the present" that aims to denaturalize current arrangements and show their contingency.
- In Part Four the method *inverts a chronology*: refuse the success→failure→reform story of the prison; ask what the supposed failure *serves*. Prison + failure + reform are one fourfold carceral system, not three stages. See [[Concepts/The Delinquent and the Carceral Continuum (Foucault)]].
- *The History of Sexuality* I does not use the word "genealogy" as a self-description, but the method is the same inversion: refuse the 17th–20th-c. repressive cycle; write two other calendars (techniques; class diffusion); start from positive mechanisms (incitement, implantation, will to knowledge), not from prohibition. The object is a "political history of truth" and a "history of bodies," not a history of mentalities (pp. 69, 152). "Sexuality" is the correlative of a discursive practice, not a natural kind waiting to be liberated.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]**: Foucault drew the genealogical method from *On the Genealogy of Morals* and the *Untimely Meditations*. Nietzsche's own method page is now [[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]] (Johnston + Samuel). Shared: origin ≠ purpose (GM II.12: "all purposes, all uses, are only signs that a will to power has become master"); no progress story; grey documents over blue theory. Dropped by Foucault: the *critique of the value of values* toward a "rank order" and "magnificence of the human type"; rumination as reading art; the priest/resentment moral psychology as the *finding*. Nietzsche is *not named* in *Discipline and Punish* I.1.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: Marxist historiography also traces how ruling ideas serve ruling-class interests. Foucault's genealogy differs: power is not reducible to class, it is dispersed through micro-practices, and the primary site of analysis is the body/subject rather than the mode of production.
- **[[Thinkers/Ian Hacking]]**: Hacking's "historical ontology" shares genealogy's project of showing how human kinds are produced by historical processes — but is more locally focused and less suspicious of the possibility of genuine progress.
