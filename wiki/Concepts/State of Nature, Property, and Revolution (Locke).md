# State of Nature, Property, and Revolution (Locke)

> In the *Two Treatises of Government* (in the Complete Works volume): State of nature is perfect freedom and equality under the law of nature (reason, preservation of mankind). Property arises by mixing labor with the commons (with spoilage and "enough and as good" provisos). Government by consent of the governed to protect natural rights (life, liberty, property); legislative supreme but bound; executive with prerogative; dissolution and right of revolution when trust breached (tyranny, etc.).

## Key from Summaries in Source/Entity

First Treatise refutes patriarchal absolutism (Filmer); no hereditary divine right from Adam.

Second: State of nature (freedom/equality/law of preservation); property by labor; social contract; consent; right of revolution.

## How Nozick Uses This (2026-08-12)

*Anarchy, State, and Utopia* is the first vault primary that reads the Second Treatise as a starting point rather than a summary. Nozick quotes §§4, 6–8, 10, 13, 123–126 on freedom, the law of nature, punishment as reparation and restraint, and the inconveniences of self-judging. He insists those inconveniences be tested against *all voluntary arrangements within rights* before the state is credited as remedy. Protective associations, then a dominant agency, arise by an invisible hand; Locke was wrong that a compact is necessary (§95 independents may stay out; money §§46–47, 50 as the analogy). Mixing-labor is rejected as stated; “enough and as good” is restated as a weaker proviso on others’ *liberty to use* and shadowed onto transfer. Revolution and dissolution of government are not his topic: the dominant agency has no special rights to revolt against.
