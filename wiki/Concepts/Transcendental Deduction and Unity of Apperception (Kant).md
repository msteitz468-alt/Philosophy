# Transcendental Deduction and Unity of Apperception (Kant)

> "The **I think** must be able to accompany all my representations; for otherwise something would be represented in me that could not be thought at all." (B 131–132)

## Summary

A **deduction** answers *quid juris*, not *quid facti*. Locke's empirical derivation explains how we came to possess concepts; a **transcendental deduction** explains how concepts can relate to objects *a priori* (A 84–87 / B 116–119). Principle of every such deduction: a priori concepts must be conditions of the possibility of experience (A 94 / B 126). Kant says this chapter cost him the most labor (A xvi) and rewrote it almost entirely for B.

**A-deduction.** A threefold synthesis — apprehension in intuition, reproduction in imagination, recognition in the concept (A 97–103) — is grounded in sense, imagination, and **transcendental apperception**: "This pure, original, unchanging consciousness I will now name transcendental apperception" (A 107). An object = *X* is the formal unity of consciousness in the synthesis. The categories are conditions of thinking in possible experience: "we ourselves bring into the appearances that order… we call nature" (A 125). Understanding is legislation for nature (A 126–128). Productive imagination and "transcendental affinity" carry more weight here than in B.

**B-deduction (§§15–27).** All combination is an act of understanding (§15). The *I think* is the original-synthetic unity of apperception, supreme principle of the understanding (§16). An object is a manifold united in a concept (§17). A judgment is "nothing other than the way to bring given cognitions to the **objective** unity of apperception" (§19, B 141) — not mere association. Hence all sensible intuitions stand under the categories (§20). Deduction proper is completed when the unity already in empirical intuition is shown to be the category's (§21, §26). Result: "The categories consequently have no other use for the cognition of things except insofar as these are taken as objects of possible experience" (B 147–148). "We cannot think any object except through categories; we cannot cognize any object that is thought except through intuitions that correspond to those concepts" (B 165–166).

**Think vs cognize the self.** "In the transcendental synthesis… I am conscious of myself not as I appear to myself, nor as I am in myself, but only that I am" (B 157). The *I think* yields *that* I am, not cognition of *what* I am. Inner sense presents us only as we appear (§24). This is why the [[Concepts/Paralogisms of Pure Reason (Kant)|Paralogisms]] cannot turn apperception into a science of the soul.

**Against "preformation."** The categories are not implanted subjective necessities (B 167–168); that would surrender objective necessity to the skeptic. They are the epigenesis of pure reason: conditions we must use if there is to be a nature for us at all (*natura formaliter spectata*, §26).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **Descartes.** The *cogito* claims a cognition of a thinking *substance*. Kant's *I think* is the vehicle of all concepts, "a mere consciousness," a "transcendental subject of thoughts = *x*" (A 346 / B 404). It contains "I exist" but is *not* Descartes's inference (B 422 n.). See [[Concepts/Cogito (Descartes)]].
- **Locke / Hume.** Between the two cliffs (B 127–128): not innate ideas, not custom. The deduction is the middle path of [[Contradictions/Rationalism vs Empiricism on Innate Ideas]].
- **Heidegger.** The A-edition's productive imagination as common root of sensibility and understanding is the 1929 Kant-book's center; Heidegger claims Kant "shrank back" in B by subordinating imagination to understanding. See [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]].
- **Husserl.** Constitution of objects in transcendental consciousness is a descendant; Husserl rejects the leftover *Ding an sich* and the fixed table of categories.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Precisely what the unity of apperception entails, and its exact relation to the representation of objects, "continue to generate lively philosophical discussion" (Guyer/Wood). The vault records both editions rather than picking a reconstruction.
- The restriction of the categories to possible experience is the theoretical half of [[Concepts/Two Standpoints: Sensible World and Intelligible World (Kant)|two standpoints]]: the same *I* is appearance in inner sense and thinkable as intelligence.
