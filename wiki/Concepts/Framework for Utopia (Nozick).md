# Framework for Utopia (Nozick)
> Utopia is not one designed society. It is a framework for utopias — a filter of voluntary communities equivalent to the minimal state.

## Summary

Chapter 10 of *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* was written first (APA, 1969) and “starts (and stands) independently” of Parts I–II. It converges on the same result.

**The problem.** The “totality of conditions” for utopias, “taken jointly, are inconsistent.” The best imaginable world for me is not that for you. “Utopia, though, must be, in some restricted sense, the best for all of us.”

**The model.** Every rational inhabitant of a possible world may imagine another and emigrate. A *stable association* is one none would leave for another they believe would itself be stable. An “east-berlin” forbids some emigration. No stable association can be an exploiting monarchy; if *A* is stable, no proper subset *S* is better off as *S* alone. Admission is structurally a competitive market: each receives **marginal contribution** (or a unanimous alternative; gifts allowed). Diversity of complementary excellences, not narcissistic primacy.

**Projected onto earth.** The model becomes the **framework**: many communities one may enter if admitted and leave. Earth diverges (cannot create people; communities impinge; information and travel costs; suppression of exit). People differ. “There will not be *one* kind of community.” “Utopia is a framework for utopias… no one can *impose* his own utopian vision upon others.” Utopia is **meta-utopia**. The “smorgasbord conception.”

**Design vs filter.** Even if one society were best, **design** (an a priori blueprint) is almost hopeless given human “thickness.” **Filter**: live, leave, modify; “no pattern is *imposed* on everyone” unless all choose it. This is the utopian form of the [[Concepts/Invisible-Hand Explanation of the State (Nozick)|invisible hand]].

**Communities need not be libertarian.** “Though the framework is libertarian and laissez-faire, *individual communities within it need not be*.” Paternalism, banned books, sexual rules are allowed inside; internal opt-out is not required (kibbutz). Nation vs face-to-face community plus jointly owned land; majority rule on public streets is left open. Changing a community’s character: compensation-for-deviation can be *contracted*, so a society-imposed preferred structure is “unnecessary.” No one has the right to impose a “doubly total” common highest goal. “It goes without saying that any persons may attempt to unite kindred spirits, but… none have the right to impose their vision of unity upon the rest.”

**Slavery.** “The comparable question about an individual is whether a free system will allow him to sell himself into slavery. I believe that it would.” It would also allow a permanent refusal of that contract. “Any individual may contract into any particular constraints over himself and so may use the voluntary framework to contract himself out of it.”

**Independence and convergence.** “The framework for utopia that we have described is equivalent to the [[Concepts/Minimal State and Ultraminimal State (Nozick)|minimal state]].” The morally favored state “is the one that best realizes the utopian aspirations of untold dreamers and visionaries.” Dual nature: we *live* in particular communities; the framework is what allows non-imperialistic visions to be tried. Last line of the book: the minimal state treats us as inviolate individuals, as ends, with dignity. “How *dare* any state or group of individuals do more. Or less.”

Left open: the role and control of a central authority; leaving (debts, education contracts, family, take-out assets, punishable offenses); children (must be *informed* of alternatives; communities may resist); details of the framework “will not be set down in advance.”

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]** / [[Sources/On Liberty - John Stuart Mill (1859)]]: “experiments of living” and “different styles of life” without Mill being named. The framework is the political form of that idea. Mill would not allow a community to become a slave-contracting east-berlin for those who did not consent.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Jacques Rousseau]]**: rejects one imposed common highest goal / “one” for all. The general will is imperialistic utopianism.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: worker-controlled factories and communes may grow by voluntary adhesion; no forced pattern. “Individualism” vs “socialism” is named as the contrast.
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]** / [[Sources/The Republic - Plato (Cambridge, 2000)]]: the designed Kallipolis is the type of design-utopia the filter refuses.
- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]**: public ways and the Lockean proviso of Ch. 7; rights/boundaries not to be crossed without consent.
- **[[Thinkers/James M. Buchanan]]**: both prefer filter/exchange to imposed pattern. Buchanan designs constitutional rules; Nozick designs almost nothing — even the framework’s details are not fixed.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Does Ch. 10 really stand independently? The opening still assumes “no state more extensive than the minimal state can be justified.”
- Selling oneself into slavery is affirmed and will strike most readers of [[Concepts/Side Constraints (Nozick)|side constraints]] and Kantian inviolability as a strain. He notes that other writers disagree.
- Children and informed exit are flagged, not solved — the point at which a filter of communities most resembles a nation.
