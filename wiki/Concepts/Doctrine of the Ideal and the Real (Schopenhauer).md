# Doctrine of the Ideal and the Real (Schopenhauer)
> Descartes opened the chasm; Locke started the cut; Berkeley found the ideal; Kant completed it; Schopenhauer claims to have solved it — the will alone is left as the real.

## Summary

The opening essay of *Parerga and Paralipomena* I (1851) is a short history of modern philosophy organized around one problem: **the ideal and the real** — “what in our **cognition** is objective and what subjective, thus what is to be ascribed to any things distinct from ourselves and what to ourselves.” Images in the head are immediately known; their relation to independently existing things is the problem. Ancients and scholastics were not distinctly aware of it (a trace in Plotinus, *Enn.* III.7, already “Kant’s ideality of time”). The distinctly pronounced problem is “the characteristic theme of *modern* philosophy.”

[[Thinkers/Descartes]] is father of the problem twice over: reason no longer rests on Bible or Aristotle; and *dubito, cogito, ergo sum* is “the equivalent” of “The world is my **representation**” — one stresses immediacy of the subject, the other mediacy of the object. His theistic exit (a truthful God) shows how hard the problem is. See [[Concepts/Cogito (Descartes)]].

The post-Cartesian line mishandles the chasm with God and substance. Malebranche’s occasional causes (we see all things in God; physical causes are *causes occasionelles*) is already “essentially the pantheism of Spinoza.” [[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz]]’s pre-established harmony is a “monstrous absurdity”: two worlds, no *influxus physicus*, a “superfluous duplicate.” The real Cartesian knot is not mind–body heterogeneity but that the immediately given world is “utterly **ideal**.” Causality cannot bridge to the real: it is of “subjective origin” and “merely the bond that connects the representations.” See [[Contradictions/Occasionalism vs Natural Causation]].

[[Thinkers/Spinoza]] cuts in the wrong place. **Extension** “lies entirely within” **representation**. He takes the extended world as the real and relocates “the only truly real, the **will**,” into the ideal (will as a faculty of affirming/negating; “will and intellect are one”).

[[Thinkers/Locke]] first brings the problem into focus without “hyperphysical hypotheses.” His **real** is **matter** and “impulse.” Primary/secondary qualities are “the origin of the distinction between thing in itself and **appearance**” — Kant’s “true genetic link” to Locke, not to Leibniz–Wolff. Berkeley then creates “proper and true *idealism*”: the **intuitive** world exists as such only in representation; matter in itself is absurd. He found the ideal, not the real. See [[Concepts/Subjective Idealism (Berkeley)]], [[Thinkers/George Berkeley]].

[[Thinkers/Kant]] abstracts the brain as well: Locke’s primaries become secondary; the thing in itself is “a mere x.” Hume, overshooting Locke’s empiricism about causality, occasioned Kant’s a priori of space, time, and causality. Schopenhauer’s “last” step: the thing in itself can never be given from outside by representation; we ourselves are real; it enters consciousness “as *will*.” The entire **intuitive** world — body, space, time, causality, Spinoza’s extended, Locke’s matter — belongs to the **ideal**. “The will alone is left as the real.” Ethics is thereby “immediately connected with metaphysics.” See [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Phenomena and Noumena (Kant)]], [[Concepts/Transcendental Idealism (Kant)]].

The Appendix bars Fichte, Schelling, and [[Thinkers/Hegel]] from “the venerable company of thinkers.” They are “not philosophers” but “sophists.” Fichte abolishes the real (caricature of Kant). Schelling denies the distinction (identity of real and ideal). Hegel equates thinking/concepts with the essence in itself; logic becomes metaphysics. The genuine problem is the relation of **intuition** to being-in-itself, not of concepts (which are only mediated abstractions). Do not create Fichte or Schelling thinker pages from this rant.

*WWR* Volume 2, Chapter I is the 1844 restatement, not a popular history. “The world is my representation” is as certain as Euclid once understood. True philosophy must be *idealistic* because only consciousness is immediately given; an object without a subject is inconceivable. This is *transcendental* idealism, not a denial of *empirical* reality (vs Jacobi’s faith). “It is true that space is only in my head; but empirically my head is in space.” Intellect and matter are *correlatives*, two poles of the world as representation. The inner path to the real is [[Concepts/Primacy of the Will in Self-Consciousness (Schopenhauer)]].

## Key Claims

- The ideal/real problem is *the* modern problem. Descartes names it; half a dozen European minds work it; Schopenhauer claims to have solved it.
- Causality cannot jump the chasm: it is a subjective bond among representations.
- Locke’s secondary qualities originate the appearance / thing-in-itself cut; Berkeley first states true idealism; Kant completes the ideality of space, time, and causality.
- The thing in itself is not reached on the path of purely *objective* cognition (always representation). It is reached by *shifting the standpoint*: the body is given as representation in space *and* in self-consciousness as will. Fragments §13: this is “the narrow gateway to truth.”
- “The represented thing and its representation are the same, but only the *represented* thing, not the thing *in itself*; the latter is always *will*.”
- Fichte/Schelling/Hegel are excluded as sophists. The problem is intuition vs being-in-itself, not self-moving concepts.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Arthur Schopenhauer]]** — the Sketch is his popular history of the cut his system claims to close. Fragments §13–14 deepen it: Schulze showed Kant could not *introduce* the thing-in-itself via causality; the *matter* stands, the *manner* falls. Own philosophy is “immanent dogmatism”: analyses *what this world is*, does not explain it as the effect of an outside cause. “My philosophy is only the thinking-through-to-the-end of his [Kant’s].”
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]** — *cogito* as the reverse of “The world is my representation.” Not a new reading of the *Meditations*; a placement.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — completes the cut; thing-in-itself left as *x*; then “sop” of practical theology. See Fragments §13 on [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — later files this whole two-world apparatus as decadence ([[Concepts/How the True World Became a Fable (Nietzsche)]]). Do not import that diagnosis into 1851.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] He does not call this “pessimism.” The word is absent from the Sketch. The vault’s [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]] is from *WWR*, not from this history.
- > [!warning] Do not weld Spinoza’s mind–body identity to the ideal/real problem; Schopenhauer says they are independent. Do not weld Leibniz’s mind–body problem to Descartes’ chasm; he treats the former as fiction.
- > [!warning] Footnote on “idealism”: do not let lay usage turn it into spiritualism. Idealism vs realism is about the *object*; spiritualism vs materialism is about the *subject*.
