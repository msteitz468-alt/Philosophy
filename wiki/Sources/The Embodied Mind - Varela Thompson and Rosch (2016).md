# The Embodied Mind — Varela, Thompson, and Rosch (1991 / 2016)
> Cognition is not the representation of a pregiven world by a pregiven subject. It is embodied action that enacts a world of relevance; science and lived experience must circulate rather than split.

**Full citation**: Francisco J. Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch, *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*, revised edition (MIT Press, 2016; first edition 1991). New foreword by Jon Kabat-Zinn; new introductions by Evan Thompson and Eleanor Rosch. ISBN 978-0-262-52936-5 / eISBN 978-0-262-33548-5. LCCN 2016016506.

## Argument Spine
Cognitive science and everyday experience must enlarge each other. Merleau-Ponty's double sense of embodiment — the body as lived structure and as milieu of mechanisms — is the missing axis. Classical cognitivism (mind as symbol-crunching representation of a pregiven world) discovers a nonunified self and then ignores the discovery in life. Connectionism explains selfless emergence but still treats cognition as recovering a pregiven world. The alternative is **enaction**: a living body is an autopoietic, sense-making system that brings forth a world through a history of structural coupling (**natural drift**, not optimal adaptation). That view has no ground beyond its own history. The matching first-person method is mindfulness/awareness (Buddhist modernist *satipaṭṭhāna*), and the matching philosophy is Madhyamaka **groundlessness** — a middle way between objectivism and nihilism (the **Cartesian anxiety**). The 2016 introductions keep the spine, recant the 1991 dismissal of Western phenomenology, deny that Abhidharma/Nāgārjuna simply "derive from meditation," and relocate mindfulness as enacted skillful know-how rather than inner observation of a private stream.

## Summary

The 2016 MIT revised edition reprints the 1991 text unchanged and adds a Kabat-Zinn foreword plus two introductions. The 1991 book is a circulation program, not a grand mind–body theory. Merleau-Ponty's double embodiment is the missing axis of cognitive science. Cognitivism (cognition = computation over symbolic representations) discovers a fragmented self (Dennett, Jackendoff) and then ignores the discovery in life. Connectionism explains emergence without a homunculus and still treats success as correspondence to a pregiven capacity. Minsky's society of mind is used as architecture and charged with leaving a theoretical no-self that grasping cannot inhabit.

The alternative is **enaction**: perception is perceptually guided action; cognitive structures emerge from recurrent sensorimotor patterns; autonomous systems have operational closure and enact a domain of distinctions through a history of structural coupling. Color is the paradigm of a domain "neither pregiven nor represented but rather experiential and enacted." Evolution as **natural drift** (proscriptive viability, not Mount Fitness) is the biological homologue; Brooks's "use the world as its own model" is the AI exhibit.

The first-person partner is **mindfulness/awareness** (not Husserlian epoché, not Wundtian introspection, not trance). The philosophical close is Madhyamaka **groundlessness**: objectivism and nihilism share a grasping root; emptiness is not the void; compassion is inseparable from *śūnyatā*. They refuse Rorty's "conversation of the West" for Heidegger's planetary thinking.

The 2016 apparatus keeps the spine and recants three 1991 strategies: Western phenomenology is not a failed project; Abhidharma/Nāgārjuna do not simply derive from meditation; mindfulness is not inner observation of an observer-independent stream (Dreyfus 1993 was right — Thompson; Rosch disagrees). Autopoiesis and neurophenomenology are earlier/later Varela programs reported in 2016, not defined in the 1991 body.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Cognition is enacted, not recovered.
- The self is unfindable in experience and unused by the cognitivist mechanism; theoretical no-self without a path is nihilism.
- Cartesian anxiety is the either/or craving for an absolute inner or outer ground.
- Adaptationism : representationism :: natural drift : enaction.
- Science can investigate enacted worlds in detail and cannot exhibit reality-in-itself.
- The book is normative: undo self-centered ways of being as they shape science. It is not (Thompson 2016) a work of Buddhist modernism.

## Whole-Work Assessment

This is the missing constructive sequel to Dreyfus in the AI & Cognitive Science leg, and the primary for the vault's "4E" hole Outstanding Sources named. Until this ingest, Varela/Thompson/Rosch appeared as a kin-list in the 1998 extended-mind paper and as a one-line "neurophenomenology" on Husserl. The book supplies the argument: strong representation (pregiven world + recovery) is optional; operational closure plus structural coupling plus natural drift is the alternative; mindfulness is the method; Madhyamaka is the close. It opens a contradiction the vault did not have — [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]] — neighboring but not identical to Dreyfus vs computationalism (rules vs know-how) and Hohwy vs Clark (secluded vs extended). Clark extends *belief*; enaction enacts a *world*.

The Argument Spine from Step 1 held. Corrections: (1) **autopoiesis is not a 1991-body term** — ch. 7–8 use operational closure and structural coupling; no standalone Autopoiesis page from this source. (2) **Neurophenomenology is post-1991** — no concept page. (3) Thompson 2016 and Rosch 2016 **disagree** on Dreyfus, Buddhist modernism, and how far Western phenomenology can go; the 1991 text is unchanged. (4) "4E" is not used. Merleau-Ponty's *Phenomenology of Perception* remains the outstanding body-phenomenology primary.
