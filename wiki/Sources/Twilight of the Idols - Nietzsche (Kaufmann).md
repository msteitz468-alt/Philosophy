# Twilight of the Idols — Friedrich Nietzsche (1888; Kaufmann trans.)

> Written in a few days as a recreation from the Revaluation: eternal idols sounded with a hammer used as a tuning fork. The old truth is coming to an end. Not *Götterdämmerung* — twilight of the *idols*.

## Argument Spine

*Götzen-Dämmerung, oder, Wie man mit dem Hammer philosophiert* (written autumn 1888; published 1889) is the late, short war-book Nietzsche himself later called the work to begin with if one wants to see how everything before him was standing on its head. The Preface (Turin, 30 September 1888 — "the day when the first book of the Revaluation of All Values was completed") frames it as cheerfulness and prankishness in the midst of that blacker task: a psychologist's leap sideways that is also "a great declaration of war." Idols here are not just idols of the age but *eternal* idols — "that which to date has been called truth" (*Ecce Homo*'s later gloss) — touched "with a hammer as with a tuning fork." The title puns Wagner's *Götterdämmerung*. The book then sounds Socrates (decadence, dialectics as revenge), "Reason" in philosophy (Egyptianism: being over becoming), the six-stage history of the "true world" error (ending *INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA*), morality as anti-nature, the four great errors of causality (especially free will), the "improvers" of mankind (taming vs breeding), what the Germans lack, a long sheaf of untimely skirmishes, and what he owes the ancients (Thucydides vs Plato; last disciple of Dionysus; teacher of the eternal recurrence). The Hammer speaks a *Zarathustra* tablet: Become hard!

This edition is Walter Kaufmann's translation (the *Portable Nietzsche* text: "Skirmishes of an Untimely Man," not Hollingdale's "Expeditions"; "What does not *destroy* me," not "kill"). Extracted from the 2013 *Friedrich Nietzsche Collection: 22 Classic Works* EPUB. Do not create a Kaufmann thinker page.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The late, short war-book that *Ecce Homo* later recommended as the place to start. Four things reception had flattened are now sharp.

(1) **Maxim 8 is not Marcus 5.20.** "Out of life's school of war: What does not *destroy* me, makes me stronger." Kaufmann's verb is destroy. Holiday's "that which doesn't kill me" as sports-psych fact is reception. *Amor fati* is not named in this book.

(2) **"The Problem of Socrates" is the full case GS §340 hinted.** Consensus of the sages is a shared physiology; dialectics is a last weapon; reason is a counter-tyrant; "to have to fight the instincts" *is* decadence; he forced the hemlock. Same last-words motif, new diagnosis.

(3) **The True World fable is a six-stage history of an error, and stage 6 abolishes the apparent world with the true one.** Kant is Königsbergian mist, not a Copernican break. *INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA* is a title-pointer, not a restatement of §341.

(4) **The four great errors are four headings.** Confusing cause and effect; false causality (will as cause is a fable); imaginary causes; free will as the foulest theological fiction. Do not collapse (4) into Humean compatibilism or Pearl. "We deny God… and thereby we redeem the world" is a doctrine of the innocence of becoming, not the madman's parable.
