# The Meaning of Meaning — C. K. Ogden and I. A. Richards (1923)

> A science of symbolism: the word–thing relation is never direct, only **Symbol → Thought/Reference → Referent**; word-magic and most "philosophical" problems are what happens when that triangle is collapsed.

## Argument Spine

Traditional philosophy, philology, and logic have failed to treat meaning because they have not analyzed **sign-situations**. Ogden and Richards claim that a causal, contextual theory of interpretation makes language treatable as a system of signs, and thereby opens a route to problems hitherto reserved for the metaphysician. The load-bearing diagram is the **triangle of reference**: words relate to things only *indirectly*, through an act of interpretation. Confusing the sides of the triangle produces **word-magic** — treating names as souls, Platonic Ideas as name-souls hypostatized, Russell's world of Being as a verbal construction. Language has two distinct functions, the **symbolic** (statements that can be true or false) and the **emotive** (attitudes, appeals, poetry); mixing them generates pseudo-controversies (vitalism vs mechanism, religion vs science, much aesthetics). "Meaning" itself is a tangle of at least sixteen uses; the practical instruments are a set of **canons of symbolism** and a transferable **technique of definition**. The supplements extend the same program into ethnography (Malinowski's *context of situation* and *phatic communion*) and medicine (Crookshank). Later editions tie the book to Basic English, *Practical Criticism*, and "linguistic therapy."

## The Sixteen Definitions of "Meaning" (Ch. IX)

**Group A (dismissed as hypostatization):** I intrinsic property; II unique unanalysable relation.

**Group B:** III dictionary annexes; IV connotation (Mill; artificial); V essence (connotation hypostatized); VI projected activity (Schiller); VII intention / volition (dangerous pun on "mean"); VIII place in a system (vague); IX practical consequences (James; reported); X theoretical consequences / implication; XI emotion aroused.

**Group C (kept):** XII natural-sign relatum; XIII that to which the interpreting process is adapted (core accepted sense for words; four subclauses + symbols rider); XIV Good Use (what the user *ought* to refer to, group-relative); XV what the user *believes* himself to refer to; XVI interpreter-side counterparts (richest in misunderstanding).

For true interpretations XII and XIII(b) coincide — which "removes the necessity for any Correspondence Theory of Truth."

## Whole-Work Assessment

The Argument Spine of Step 1 held. The book does three jobs the vault could not do from Ayer, the *Tractatus*, or Locke.

First, it *draws the triangle*. [[Concepts/Triangle of Reference (Ogden-Richards)]] is the missing diagram behind every later vault fight about whether words hook the world (Locke's ideas, Wittgenstein's pictures, Peirce's interpretants, Ayer's verification). The load-bearing claim is negative: the base is empty. [[Concepts/Word Magic (Ogden-Richards)]] is the history of filling it in, from name-souls to Russell's World of Being.

Second, it supplies a positive theory and a practical instrument. [[Concepts/Contextual Theory of Reference (Ogden-Richards)]] makes reference causal-mnemic and relocates truth as context-completion. [[Concepts/Canons of Symbolism (Ogden-Richards)]] plus the technique of definition are the prose discipline; the sixteen definitions are the solvent for the word "meaning" itself.

Third, it is the missing ancestor of Ayer's emotivism. [[Concepts/Symbolic and Emotive Functions of Language (Ogden-Richards)]] is the 1923 cut; Ayer (1936) routes it through verification and makes ethics the paradigm. The vault had been telling the Ayer story without this book.

Malinowski's supplement is not a footnote: [[Concepts/Context of Situation and Phatic Communion (Malinowski)]] adds ethnographic method and a non-referential social use the triangle does not cover. Appendix D is the vault's first Peirce dossier ([[Thinkers/C. S. Peirce]]) — reproduced, not adopted.

Corrections of emphasis vs the preface: (1) the "five functions" of Ch. X do not replace the twofold cut; poetry-section collapses back to symbolic vs emotive. (2) They cite Wittgenstein on mathematics *and* reject picture-isomorphism — not a simple alliance. (3) James is definition IX, not the theory of meaning. (4) This book is *not* Putnam's "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (still outstanding).

Contradictions opened as warnings, not new pages: triangle vs [[Concepts/Picture Theory of Language (Wittgenstein)]]; context-completion vs [[Concepts/Correspondence Theory of Truth (Russell)]]. No dedicated contradiction page — the tensions are real but not yet a recurring vault-level fight with two equal primaries on the same question.
