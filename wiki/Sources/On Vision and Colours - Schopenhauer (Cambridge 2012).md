# On Vision and Colours — Arthur Schopenhauer (1816 / 1854; Cambridge 2012)

> Colour is a modification of the retina's own activity, not a property packed into light. Goethe supplied the data; this treatise supplies the theory. A Kantian primer: if colour is entirely in the eye, the a priori forms of cognition are easier to see.

## Argument Spine

Goethe's *Zur Farbenlehre* (1810) destroyed Newton's theory and delivered *data* for a theory of colour, but stopped at the urphenomenon and did not say what colour *is*. Colour as physiological appearance — what happens in the eye — is the first and theoretically governing half of the science; physical and chemical colours are subordinate. The theory: the retina has an activity (full activity = white / light; rest = black / darkness). That activity can be divided intensively (grey), extensively (shape), and **qualitatively** (colour). Each colour is a qualitatively divided fraction of the retina's activity; its complement is the remaining fraction; both together restore the full activity (white). Colour is therefore entirely in the eye — "the body is red" means it produces red in the eyes. Chapter I is a forerunner of *Fourfold Root* §21: all intuition is intellectual; colour is the raw sensation the understanding later assigns to a cause.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault already had FR 1847 §21 as “everyday seeing is intellectual,” with a one-line note that *On Vision and Colours* had rehearsed it. This 1854 book is that rehearsal, and it is also something FR is not: a theory of **what colour is**.

Four things are now sharp. (1) Colour is the **qualitatively divided activity of the retina** — not a part of the ray of light, not a Locke-style power left in the body, not noumenal Will. (2) Goethe supplied the *data* and the *skieron*; this treatise supplies the unifying point *in abstracto*. The two corrections (polarity is retinal; white from two complements) are why Goethe called him an opponent while he remained the champion. Newton is wrong in kind (sought in light what belongs in the eye); right only by accident of wording on white-from-colours. (3) Chapter I is the first draft of FR §21: palette-blobs, stereoscope, illusion vs error, animal/plant/inorganic. FR remains the comprehensive statement; this is the first date. (4) The 1854 preface offers the book as a **Kantian primer**: if colour is entirely in the eye, the a priori forms of cognition are easier to see.

The Argument Spine held. Correction to the stub: Runge’s colour sphere is cited (equator = maximum energy) but Runge is not a co-author; do not ingest the Stahl/Runge volume as if it were this work. The 1851 *Parerga* II colour chapter was the storehouse folded back here — that session was told not to create a colour-science page from the supplements; this treatise is the colour primary.
