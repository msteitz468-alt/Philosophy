# The History of Sexuality, Volume 1 — Michel Foucault (1976; Hurley trans., Pantheon 1978)

> The vault's second primary Foucault text: a genealogy of the "repressive hypothesis" and of the modern *dispositif* of sexuality — how the West was made to speak sex as truth, and how that speech belongs to a power over life rather than a law that says no.

## Argument Spine

*La Volonté de savoir* (Gallimard, 1976; Robert Hurley trans. as *The History of Sexuality, Volume 1: An Introduction*, Pantheon 1978) is not a history of sexual liberation and not a history of Victorian silence. It opens by restating the familiar story — seventeenth-century frankness, then the imperial prude, then Freud as cautious first light — and poses three doubts: is the repression historically true; is power in our societies primarily repressive; and is the critique of repression a break with that power or part of the same network? The object is not to decide yes or no to sex but to describe the regime of **power-knowledge-pleasure** that puts sex into discourse: "the over-all 'discursive fact.'" Negative elements (taboo, censorship, denial) are local tactics inside a mechanism of incitement, a dissemination of polymorphous sexualities, and a **will to knowledge** that constitutes a science of sexuality. The closing irony: the deployment of sexuality has us believe that our "liberation" is in the balance. Sex is not the hidden reality that sexuality expresses; it is a speculative, imaginary point the deployment itself requires. The counterattack is not sex-desire but **bodies and pleasures**. Between those poles the book builds an analytics of power that refuses the "juridico-discursive" representation (power as law, king, prohibition) and names the modern successor of the sovereign's right to take life or let live: a power to **foster life or disallow it to the point of death** — anatomo-politics of the body plus bio-politics of the population, joined in the nineteenth century by the deployment of sexuality.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault's second primary Foucault text converts the Macey-derived sexuality/biopower cluster from reported doctrine into quoted argument. Four things the biography blurred are now sharp. (1) *The repressive hypothesis is not simply false.* Prohibition is not a ruse; making prohibition the constitutive principle of the history is. The speaker's benefit and the "great sexual sermon" explain why the story is so easy to tell. (2) *Scientia sexualis is not "the West has no ars erotica."* The contrast is structural and then immediately qualified: Christian direction of conscience had methods in common with erotic art; *scientia sexualis* can function as an *ars erotica* via pleasure in the truth of pleasure. (3) *The family sexualizes.* It is the crystal of the deployment, not an agency of prohibition; alliance and sexuality are two deployments, the second superimposed on the first. Sexuality is originally bourgeois. (4) *Bio-power is defined here, governmentality is not.* Take life / let live → foster life / disallow it; anatomo-politics + bio-politics; sexuality as the hinge; "sex" as fictitious point; bodies and pleasures vs sex-desire; last-sentence irony of liberation.

The Argument Spine of Step 1 held, with corrections of attribution. Reich is named in Part One (p. 5) and IV.4 (p. 131), *not* in the juridico-discursive chapter (IV.1) — that chapter attributes the shared power-as-law to the thematics of repression *and* to the psychoanalytic theory that law constitutes desire. Deleuze / *Anti-Oedipus* is not named. Hobbes *is* named (p. 135); Marx is *not* named in Part Five, though capitalism is. Lateran 1215 is Part Three, not Part Two. Volume 1 does not make the 1984 ethics move; "bodies and pleasures" is a 1976 rallying point.

Contradictions opened or sharpened: productive incitement vs Marx's ideology-as-hiding; bio-power as condition of capitalism *after* refusing labor-capacity energetics; Hobbes named as ancestor of the old right only; Freud as relay of the deployment (third pole on [[Contradictions/Sartre vs Freud - Lucidity vs the Unconscious]]); "no exteriority" of resistance vs the closing counterattack of bodies and pleasures.
