# Are You Living in a Computer Simulation - Nick Bostrom (2003)

> Nick Bostrom's 2003 paper argues a **trilemma**, not a conclusion that we are simulated: at least one of (1) almost no civilizations reach a posthuman stage, (2) almost no posthumans run ancestor-simulations, or (3) we almost certainly live in a simulation.

**Full citation**: Nick Bostrom, "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?", *Philosophical Quarterly* 53, no. 211 (2003): 243–255. First version 2001.

## Summary

The abstract states the result and its immediate corollary: *at least one* of three propositions is true — (1) "the human species is very likely to go extinct before reaching a 'posthuman' stage"; (2) "any posthuman civilization is extremely unlikely to run a significant number of simulations of their evolutionary history (or variations thereof)"; (3) "we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation." It follows that "the belief that there is a significant chance that we will one day become posthumans who run ancestor-simulations is false, unless we are currently living in a simulation."

**§I** states the basic idea without the formalism. If later generations run many detailed simulations of their forebears, and if those simulated people are conscious, then "the vast majority of minds like ours do not belong to the original race." Anyone who rejects the conclusion that they themselves are simulated is "not entitled to believe that we will have descendants who will run lots of such simulations." The paper also flags two extra payoffs: methodological/metaphysical questions, and "naturalistic analogies to certain traditional religious conceptions."

**§II, The Assumption of Substrate-Independence.** The argument needs only an *attenuated* thesis: it is not an essential property of consciousness that it run on carbon neural networks; "silicon-based processors inside a computer could in principle do the trick as well." Bostrom does not assume necessary functionalism, nor that passing the [[Concepts/Imitation Game (Turing)|Turing test]] would suffice. He needs only that "the computational processes of a human brain are structurally replicated in suitably fine-grained detail, such as on the level of individual synapses." Neurochemicals matter, but "they affect subjective experience only *via* their direct or indirect influence on computational activities." See [[Concepts/Substrate-Independence (Bostrom)]].

**§III, The Technological Limits of Computation.** No timescale is assumed. Conservative lower bounds already suffice: Drexler's sugar-cube nanomechanical design (~10²¹ ops/s), a planetary-mass computer (~10⁴² ops/s), Lloyd's 1 kg theoretical ceiling (5×10⁵⁰ ops/s). Brain emulation estimates: ~10¹⁴ ops/s (retinal contrast-enhancement method) to ~10¹⁶–10¹⁷ (synapse count × firing rate). An ancestor-simulation of all human history is ~10³³–10³⁶ operations. A single planetary-mass computer "could simulate the entire mental history of humankind … by using less than one millionth of its processing power for one second." Full-universe quantum simulation is unnecessary: only what simulated humans would notice. Distant astronomy can be compressed; the interior of the Earth omitted; microscopic structure filled in *ad hoc* when someone looks through an electron microscope. The simulator can track belief-states and "fill in sufficient detail … on an as-needed basis," or edit anomalous brains, or "skip back a few seconds and rerun." (This is the vault's earliest statement of what Virk later calls [[Concepts/Conditional Rendering and Quantum Indeterminacy (Virk)|conditional rendering]].)

**§IV, The Core.** Let *f_P* be the fraction of human-level civilizations that reach a posthuman stage; *N* the average number of ancestor-simulations they run; *H* the average number of individuals who live before that stage. Splitting *N* as *f_I* × *N_I* (fraction of posthumans *interested* in running such simulations × how many they run) yields

*f_sim* = (*f_P* *f_I* *N_I*) / (*f_P* *f_I* *N_I* + 1).

Because *N_I* is immense, at least one of (1) *f_P* ≈ 0, (2) *f_I* ≈ 0, (3) *f_sim* ≈ 1 must hold. This is the [[Arguments/Simulation Argument (Bostrom)|simulation argument]].

**§V, A Bland Indifference Principle.** Conditional on (3), credence that *one* is simulated should be close to 1. More generally: if a fraction *x* of observers with human-type experiences live in simulations, and one has no information marking one's own case as special, then Cr(*SIM* | *f_sim* = *x*) = *x*. The principle is weaker than Laplacean indifference (immune to Bertrand's paradox) and weaker than the Doomsday argument, which requires treating oneself as a random sample from all people *who will ever have lived* even though one knows one lives in the early twenty-first century. The junk-DNA analogy: if *x*% of people have sequence *S* with no observable manifestations, assign credence *x* to having *S*. Betting: if almost everyone is simulated and everyone bets accordingly, almost everyone wins. See [[Concepts/Bland Indifference Principle (Bostrom)]].

**§VI, Interpretation.**

- **Horn (1)** implies high credence in *DOOM* — extinction before posthumanity — unless we have evidence that we are specially lucky or unlucky. Compatible with a long plateau at roughly current technology. The "most natural interpretation" is extinction by a powerful dangerous technology; molecular nanotechnology / grey goo is the named candidate. (This is the same existential-risk cluster as [[Sources/Superintelligence - Nick Bostrom (2014)]].)
- **Horn (2)** requires *strong convergence*: virtually no posthuman civilization contains relatively wealthy independent agents who both desire ancestor-simulations and are free to run them. Possible mechanisms: an ethical ban (plus enforcement), or posthumans losing the desire (scientific value negligible; pleasure cheaper via direct reward-center stimulation).
- **Horn (3)** is "conceptually most intriguing." The observed cosmos is "just a tiny piece of the totality of physical existence." "While the world we see is in some sense 'real', it is not located at the fundamental level of reality" — the paper's own no-illusion clause, later developed as [[Concepts/Simulation Realism (Chalmers)]]. Nested simulations (virtual machines, stacked) are possible; computational cost at the basement level counts against many levels and predicts termination as we approach posthumanity. Posthuman simulators are like gods relative to inhabitants (creator, superior intelligence, "omnipotent" and "omniscient" *in the relevant sense*); all but basement-level agents are subject to deeper-level sanctions. This yields a **naturalistic theogony**, a possible afterlife, and a "universal ethical imperative … 'from nowhere'" — everyone, including the basement civilization, has reason to behave as if watched. Selective "me-simulations" and "shadow-people" (zombies simulated only enough not to be noticed) are considered; one would need ~100 billion times as many me-simulations as ancestor-simulations for most simulated persons to be in a me-simulation. A farfetched theodicy: memories of suffering as illusions, entertainable only when one is not currently suffering. **Practical upshot**: "the implications are not all that radical"; "the truth of (3) should have no tendency to make us 'go crazy'." Empirical study of the universe we see remains the best guide.

**§VII.** In "the dark forest of our current ignorance, it seems sensible to apportion one's credence roughly evenly between (1), (2), and (3)." Closing line: "Unless we are now living in a simulation, our descendants will almost certainly never run an ancestor-simulation."

## Key Claims / Positions

- The result is a **disjunction**, not a high posterior on being simulated. Even split among the three horns is Bostrom's own recommended credence in current ignorance.
- Substrate-independence is assumed in a weak, empirical form (synaptic-level replication suffices); it is not proved here.
- Compute estimates are conservative lower bounds; orders-of-magnitude error would not change the argument.
- The indifference step is *bland* — not Doomsday, not Laplace.
- Conditional rendering, nested levels, naturalistic theogony, and a no-illusion clause ("real" but not fundamental) are already in this paper.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Horn (1) is the same civilizational-failure mass that *Superintelligence* prices as default without a solved control problem. The two arguments trade probability: raising credence in existential catastrophe lowers credence in (3), and conversely.
- > [!warning] Substrate-independence is exactly what [[Thinkers/John Searle]] denies ([[Concepts/Biological Naturalism (Searle)]], [[Contradictions/Hofstadter vs Searle - Strange Loops vs Chinese Room]]). If Searle is right, *f_sim* is not well-defined for conscious observers and the anthropic step fails.
- > [!warning] Virk reads Bostrom as committed to an NPC/horn-(3) conclusion. The paper is officially neutral and recommends an even split. See [[Concepts/Simulation Hypothesis (Virk)]].
- Chalmers later broadens ancestor-simulations to "humanlike sims," adds sim blockers, and quotes ≥25% rather than an even three-way split ([[Arguments/Simulation Argument (Bostrom)]]).
