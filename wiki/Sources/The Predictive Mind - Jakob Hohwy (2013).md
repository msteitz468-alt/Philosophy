# The Predictive Mind — Jakob Hohwy (2013)

## Argument Spine
The brain is a hypothesis-testing mechanism whose *only* job is to minimize the error of its predictions of current sensory input. One reiterated statistical mechanism — hierarchical prediction-error minimization (PEM), with expected precisions and active inference — is supposed to explain perception, action, attention, and everything mental in between. Perception is causal inference from effects (sensory data) to hidden causes; sensory input is feedback to the brain’s queries, not a stamp on a passive mind. The mind is both robustly world-guided and *indirect*, secluded behind a veil of sensory input. Perception, action, and attention are three ways of doing the same thing. The book is an inference to the best explanation: PEM unifies, is mechanistically simple, and is theoretically fecund. Hohwy names Karl Friston as the technical source and Andy Clark as the philosopher taking the framework “in different directions than what I argue for.”

## Whole-Work Assessment

*The Predictive Mind* is the vault's systematic statement of **prediction-error minimization**, the missing counterpart to Clark's 2023 popularization. Hohwy's lasting contribution is not a new mechanism — the Helmholtz/Friston family was already in via *The Experience Machine* — but a stricter claim about what the mechanism *is* and where it *lives*. PEM is *all* the brain does; perception, action, and attention are three ways of doing it; the mind that does it is secluded behind a veil of sensory input. Clark treated predictive brains as "the perfect internal organs for the creation of extended minds." Hohwy, writing earlier and naming Clark 2012/2013 as already taking "different directions," treats Otto-style tools as the secluded brain optimizing its *channel*. That split is now a dedicated contradiction.

The book also does philosophy of perception and content that Clark skipped: binding as shallow common-cause inference; a precision/uncertainty verdict on Fodor-style penetrability; a courtroom model of reality testing; opposite expected-precision errors for delusions (low sensory gain) and autism (sensory enslavement); a Bayesian-room counter to Searle; a speculative entropy answer to Wittgensteinian rule-following. On consciousness Hohwy is *not* Clark's ally. He declines the hard problem, keeps conceivability open, and offers only unity-via-one-active-inference-policy plus attention-as-precision. The same Friston machinery does not force Dennettian deflation.

What the book does *not* use: "Markov blanket" (later PP jargon — do not put it in Hohwy's 2013 mouth); "controlled hallucination"; "self-evidencing" as a name. Technical free energy is acknowledged and then mostly set aside for epistemic PEM. Higher cognition, language, and social cognition are explicitly out of scope. Friston remains a reported source, not a primary. Clark's systematic reply is now ingested: [[Sources/Surfing Uncertainty - Andy Clark (2016)]].
