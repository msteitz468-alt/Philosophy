# Cartesian Meditations — Edmund Husserl (Cairns trans., Nijhoff 1960)

> The vault's first Husserl *primary*: the 1929 Sorbonne lectures rewritten as five meditations that keep Descartes's radical beginning and reject nearly all Cartesian doctrine — transcendental phenomenology as neo-Cartesianism that ends, not in a *res cogitans*, but in a universe of monads.

## Bibliographic Note

*Cartesianische Meditationen: Eine Einleitung in die Phänomenologie.* Elaboration of two Sorbonne lectures, "Einleitung in die transzendentale Phänomenologie," 23 and 25 February 1929 (Cairns n.1). First published in French, trans. Gabrielle Peiffer and Emmanuel Levinas (Paris: Armand Colin, 1931). German text: S. Strasser ed., Husserliana I (The Hague: Martinus Nijhoff, 1950). This file is Dorion Cairns's English translation (first published 1960; this copy is the seventh impression, 1982, Martinus Nijhoff / Kluwer). Cairns works primarily from Husserliana I, treating most of Husserl's appendix emendations as text; he also uses Typescript C ("Cartes. Meditationen / Originaltext 1929 / E. Husserl / für Dorion Cairns," annotated 1933) for emphasis, quotation marks, and some preferred readings, and checks the 1931 French. Translator's conventions: *Ich* → Ego (capital), *Ego* → ego (small); *Gegenstand* → object, *Objekt* → Object.

This is the vault's first Husserl primary. The 2026-06-29 Smith introduction already named the book and paraphrased the Fifth Meditation's analogical apperception as unsatisfactory even to Husserl. *Ideas* I remains outstanding.

## Argument Spine

Transcendental phenomenology is "almost" a neo-Cartesianism: it takes from Descartes the eternal motif of a philosophy as all-embracing science grounded on an absolute foundation, begun by a personal overthrow of accepted sciences and a regress to the ego of the cogitationes. Precisely by radicalizing that motif it must reject nearly all Cartesian doctrine — the deduction of God and Nature from innate principles, the *res cogitans* as a little tag-end of the world, the idea of philosophy as a deductive computation. The First Meditation executes the Cartesian overthrow and finds, not a thinking substance, but the transcendental ego; world-evidence is not apodictic. The Second opens the field of transcendental experience (cogito–cogitatum, synthesis, time, horizon). The Third ties constitution to reason, evidence, and actuality. The Fourth develops the ego as pole, substrate of habitualities, and concrete monad, converts analysis into eidetics, and states transcendental idealism as genuine phenomenological explication of the *ego cogito*. The Fifth answers the solipsism objection by reducing to the sphere of ownness and constituting the Other by pairing and analogical appresentation, then a community of monads and an Objective Nature. The Conclusion: phenomenology does not exclude metaphysics; the intrinsically first being is transcendental intersubjectivity; "Noli foras ire… in interiore homine habitat veritas." The path is universal self-knowledge, monadic then intermonadic — not an axiom, *ego cogito*, but an all-embracing self-investigation.

## Whole-Work Assessment

*Cartesian Meditations* closes the hole Outstanding Sources named: Husserl in the vault was a Smith introduction. It supplies the *first-person* argument Smith only mapped — neo-Cartesianism that keeps the radical beginning and rejects the doctrine; the "tag-end of the world" as Husserl's own indictment of Descartes; *ego — cogito — cogitatum* as the working triad (noema almost unused as a noun); pregnant constitution as constitution of *existing* objectivity; the ego as pole, habitualities, and concrete monad; association as the innate A priori of passive genesis; pairing as apperception, not inference; one community of monads, hence one Objective world; the Augustine close.

It also *corrects* Smith on two points the secondary had taught as doctrine. First, constitution is not safely "meaning, not existence": §41 says sense **and** being, existence-status, product, and "an outside is precisely — nonsense." Smith's 1934 letter ("a word which by the way I no longer use") is later than this text and cannot be read back into it. Second, "Husserl was never fully satisfied" with the Fifth Meditation is a reception judgment. The text calls the solipsism objection groundless (§62), the Other never originaliter, and the write-up incomplete (birth, death, generation postponed). Those are not the same claim.

The Argument Spine of Step 1 held. One correction of emphasis: *Lebenswelt* is present in §§58–59 (cultural *Umwelt*s as concrete life-worlds) but is not this book's theme — that remains the *Crisis*. The Second Meditation's vocabulary is *cogitatum* / objective sense / transcendental clue, not the West Coast noema. Leibniz is a named interlocutor, not a source of substance-metaphysics: windowlessness is kept *de re*, harmony is transformed into confirmation, incompossibility of two actual worlds is endorsed.

Contradictions opened or sharpened: §41 idealism vs Smith's realist absorption (same contradiction page, now two Husserls); pairing vs Sartre's Look; pairing vs Nagel's objective phenomenology; first-person-singular epoché vs Dennett's "first-person-plural" charge; Husserl's monad vs Leibniz's. *Ideas* I remains the outstanding Husserl primary.
