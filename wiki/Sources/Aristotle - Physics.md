# Aristotle — Physics
> The foundational treatise on *physis*: nature is an internal principle of motion and rest. Eight books (Hardie & Gaye) on the principles of change, the four causes, motion, the infinite, place, void, time, the continuum, and the eternal unmoved first mover.

**Full citation**: Aristotle, *Physics* (*Physikē akroasis*). Translated by R. P. Hardie and R. K. Gaye. Bekker 184a–267b. On disk: `raw/assets/Aristotle - Complete Works/Physics (Aristotle).txt` (left in the Complete Works folder; `raw/assets/` is not filed away).

## Argument Spine
There is a science of nature whose object is things that have within themselves a principle of motion and rest. To know them we must know their principles and causes. Change is not from non-being (against Parmenides) but the actuality of what is potential *qua* potential, involving form, privation, and a persisting substratum. Nature is both matter and form, and form is the end: nature acts for something. Motion, being continuous, brings in the infinite (which exists only potentially), place (the innermost motionless boundary of the containing body), the denial of void, and time (the number of motion in respect of before and after). Because the continuum is infinitely divisible, Zeno's paradoxes fail. Motion is eternal; an infinite regress of moved movers is impossible; there is a first unmoved mover. *Metaphysics* XII will identify that mover's life as thought thinking itself; *Physics* VIII establishes that there must be one, without magnitude, causing eternal circular locomotion.

## Summary

The *Physics* is not a treatise of mathematical physics. It is the most general account of **natural things** — things that move — standing at the head of the physical, cosmological, and biological works.

**Book I.** Knowledge of nature is acquaintance with principles. Method: from what is clearer *to us* (confused wholes) to what is clearer *by nature*. Eleatic monism is not physics: a motionless One leaves no principle, and motion is given by induction. Becoming always has an underlying something; the principles of change are **form, privation, and substratum/matter** — two in a way, three in a way. Nothing comes from not-being without qualification; a thing may come from the privation, "which in its own nature is not-being." Matter is the primary substratum that persists.

**Book II.** Nature defined: internal source of motion and rest *kath' hauto*. Form is nature rather than matter. Canonical **four causes**. **Chance** (*tychē*) and **spontaneity** (*automaton*) are incidental causes in the for-the-sake-of class. Nature operates for a purpose (Empedocles' ox-progeny and the rain-for-crops objection fail the "always or normally" test). Necessity in generation is **hypothetical**, in the matter; the physicist states especially the end.

**Book III.** Motion = fulfilment of the potential *qua* potential, an incomplete actuality. No actual infinite body; the infinite exists as a day exists. Infinite by division of a finite continuum, not by an ever-larger cosmos.

**Book IV.** Place = innermost motionless boundary of the container; the All is not in a place; natural places. Void denied (atomists). Time = number of motion wrt before/after; no time without change; possibly no time without a soul to count.

**Books V–VI.** Motion ≠ all change (becoming/perishing are not motions). Continuum infinitely divisible; no first part of a process. Zeno: dichotomy and Achilles confuse two infinites; the arrow assumes time is composed of moments; the stadium bungles relative velocity.

**Books VII–VIII.** Everything in motion is moved by something; infinite regress of moved movers is impossible. Motion never began; time is eternal. Animals are imperfect self-movers. The first movent is unmoved, one, eternal, **without magnitude**, causing continuous **rotatory locomotion**. No *noēsis noēseōs* here — that is *Metaphysics* XII.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Nature is an internal principle of motion and rest.
- Four irreducible causes; nature acts for ends; chance is incidental teleology.
- Motion is incomplete actuality of the potential *qua* potential.
- Infinite, place, void, time: potential infinite only; place as boundary; no void; time as number of motion.
- Continuum answers Zeno.
- Motion is eternal; there is a first unmoved mover without magnitude.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The *Physics* is the missing *physis* treatise the vault's Aristotle cluster was standing on. Four causes, act/potency, and the unmoved mover were already in from the *Metaphysics*; this ingest supplies their **physical** home and the concepts Metaphysics never treats: nature as internal principle, the definition of *kinēsis*, chance/spontaneity, the potential infinite, place, the denial of void, and time as number of motion. Book VIII is the kinematic ancestor of Aquinas's *prima via* and of Ibn Rushd's eternity thesis; it is **not** yet *noēsis noēseōs* — Lambda adds the theology. The Argument Spine held. One correction: "nature does nothing in vain" is not in Book II as a slogan; the book says nature "operates for a purpose" and that art does not deliberate. The rain/teeth/Empedocles stretch is the teleology argument, and it is already an answer to a selectionist objection.
