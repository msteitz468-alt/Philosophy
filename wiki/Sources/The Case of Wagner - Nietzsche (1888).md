# The Case of Wagner — Friedrich Nietzsche (1888)
> Wagner is modernity in concentrated form. The philosopher must first be a Wagnerite — then recover.

## Bibliographic Note

Friedrich Nietzsche, *Der Fall Wagner. Ein Musikanten-Problem* (Leipzig, 1888). Short polemic: Preface + twelve numbered sections + Postscript + Second Postscript + Epilogue. Written in Turin after *On the Genealogy of Morals* (1887) and in the same year as *Twilight of the Idols*, *The Antichrist*, and *Ecce Homo*.

## Argument Spine

The philosopher’s first and last demand is to overcome his age in himself and become “timeless.” Nietzsche and Wagner are both children of the age, i.e. *décadents*. The only difference is that Nietzsche recognised the fact and struggled. His “greatest preoccupation hitherto” has been the problem of *decadence*; “‘Good and evil’ form only a playful subdivision of this problem.” Morality, once the eye is trained on decline, shows itself as impoverished life, the will to nonentity, great exhaustion. The greatest event of his life was a *recovery*: “Wagner belongs only to my diseases.” The pamphlet is not malice for its own sake. Wagner is *harmful*, and still indispensable to the philosopher, who must be the evil conscience of his age and therefore possess its best knowledge. “Wagner is modernity in concentrated form.” One must first be a Wagnerite.

Bizet’s *Carmen* is the counter-example, not a rival cult: light feet, south, *limpidezza*, love as fatality rather than Senta-sentimentality. *Il faut méditerraniser la musique.* Wagner’s opera is the opera of *salvation*. The *Ring* ran aground on Schopenhauer and was retranslated into nonentity: the philosopher of decadence allowed the artist of decadence to find himself. Wagner is not a defective genius but a typical *décadent*, complete, without free will. Style-formula: life no longer animates the whole; anarchy among the atoms. He is less a musician than an *histrio* — scenic artist, Victor Hugo of music as language, *ancilla dramaturgica*. Music that must “mean” an infinity of things is Hegel’s heir. The three requisitions: the stage must not master the arts; the actor must not corrupt the genuine; music must not become an art of lying. The Epilogue generalises: modern man is a biological contradiction of values; the Case of Wagner is a *windfall* for the diagnosis of the modern soul.

## Section-by-Section Summary

**Preface.** Written “to relieve my mind.” Praising Bizet at Wagner’s expense is not malice alone. Turning his back on Wagner was fate; liking anything else afterwards was a triumph. Nobody had been more dangerously involved in Wagnerism. If he were a moralist he might call it self-mastery; the philosopher does not like the moralist. He is a decadent who recognised it. The mission required siding against all that was morbid in himself, “including Wagner, including Schopenhauer, including the whole of modern *humanity*.” Highest wish: Zarathustra’s eye from an enormous distance. Recovery is the greatest event; Wagner belongs only to his diseases. Still: the philosopher is not free to pass Wagner by. Through Wagner, modernity speaks her most intimate language.

**§§1–2. Bizet contra Wagner.** *Carmen* heard for the twentieth time. Wagnerian orchestration is the *Sirocco*; Bizet does not sweat. First principle of his aesthetics: “All that is good is easy, everything divine runs with light feet.” Contrast to the “polypus in music,” to “endless melody.” Wagner is the most ill-mannered genius on earth — he repeats until we believe. Bizet makes him productive; that is the only touchstone of the good. *Carmen* also *saves*. Farewell to the damp north and the Wagnerian ideal. African gaiety; happiness short, sudden, without reprieve. Love translated back into Nature: not Senta-sentimentality but “love as fate, as a fatality, cynical, innocent, cruel.” Don José’s last cry. Artists misunderstand love as selflessness that still wants to *possess*; even God is no exception. Benjamin Constant: love is the most egoistic of sentiments.

**§3. The opera of salvation.** *Il faut méditerraniser la musique* (he points to *Beyond Good and Evil*). He was one of the most corrupted Wagnerites and able to take Wagner seriously. The old magician rattled “resignation,” “loyalty,” and “purity,” and retired from the corrupt world with a song of praise to chastity. Wagner pondered nothing so deeply as salvation: someone always wants to be saved — Tannhäuser, Dutchman, Kundry, Lohengrin, *Meistersinger*, Isolde, the *Ring*’s Almighty delivered by a free spirit and an immoralist. *Lohengrin* stands for the Christian “Thou must and shalt *believe*”; it is a crime against the highest to be scientific. Adoring women are the ruin of the artist-genius. Goethe’s danger for romanticists: “To choke over the rumination of moral and religious absurdities.” In short: *Parsifal*. Holiness is the mob’s remaining higher value; to philosophers that horizon slams the door on their real beginning.

**§4. History of the *Ring*.** Half a lifetime Wagner believed in the Revolution as only a Frenchman could. Evil comes from “old contracts” (customs, laws, morals, institutions). Siegfried declares war on them; Wagner, not the saga, invented the incestuous origin as a declaration of war on morality. Free love, twilight of the old gods, evil got rid of. Then the ship dashed on a reef: Schopenhauer. Optimism became a shame. Escape: interpret the wreck as the goal. *Bene navigavi cum naufragium feci.* Brünnhilde must now versify the fourth book of *The World as Will and Idea*. Nonentity, the Indian Circe, beckons. “It was the *philosopher of decadence* who allowed the *artist of decadence* to find himself.”

**§5. The artist of decadence.** He will not look on while this *décadent* spoils our health — and music. “Is Wagner a man at all? Is he not rather a disease? Everything he touches he contaminates. *He has made music sick.*” A typical *décadent* who establishes his depravity as law, progress, fulfilment. That Europe did not feel he was decadent *is* a sign of decadence: instinct is weakened; what ought to be eschewed now attracts. Wagner increases exhaustion, therefore attracts the exhausted. Problems of hysteria; a hospital ward of heroes. *Wagner est une névrose.* He is the modern artist *par excellence*, the **Cagliostro of modernity**. Three stimulants of exhausted people: *brutality*, *artificiality*, and *innocence* (idiocy). Master of hypnotic trickery. Only with morbid music can money be made today.

**§6. Satire of success.** Beauty is for the few; aim at size, the sublime, the gigantic. No thoughts — chaos makes people wonder. “Infinity but without melody.” Melody is immoral; proof: Palestrina; application: *Parsifal*. Never give pleasure; that belongs to the bad eighteenth century. A dose of *cant*. “Man is corrupt: who will save him?” Do not reply. See Wagner’s “Religion and Art.”

**§7. Musician becoming actor.** Beneath the jests, the decline of art and of the artist’s character: the musician is becoming an actor; his art a talent for *telling lies*. (He defers a fuller “Physiology of Art.”) Wagner was not a contradictory genius; he was *complete*, a typical *décadent* in whom every sign of free will was lacking. **Formula of decadent style:** life no longer animates the whole. Words leap out of the sentence, the sentence out of the page, the page at the cost of the whole. “There is always anarchy among the atoms, disaggregation of the will, — in moral terms: ‘freedom of the individual,’ — extended into a political theory ‘*equal* rights for all.’” First we notice an hallucination of *attitudes*, then the semiotics of tone. He is a master of small things, our greatest musical *miniaturist*; he disguises inability to create organic forms as a “dramatic style.” Opposite of old Kant: Wagner sets up a principle wherever capacity fails; Kant endowed man with a “capacity” whenever a principle failed. Popular Wagner is bad taste. The unknown Wagner is the melancholic of five-to-fifteen-bar fragments. “Wagner had the virtue of *décadents*,—pity.”

**§8. The *histrio*.** Even the non-musician cannot help being spoiled. Wagner is quite a great actor. Youthlets benumbed, pale, breathless, knowing nothing about music. He is the greatest mime, the most astounding theatrical genius the Germans have had, *scenic artist par excellence*. Wagner *and* Beethoven is blasphemy. He was not instinctively a musician; the tyrant, the actor’s genius, drove him to become musician and poet. He increased the powers of speech of music; he is the Victor Hugo of music as language — provided music may be *ancilla dramaturgica*. Without the theatre, simply bad music. “When a musician can no longer count up to three, he becomes ‘dramatic.’” He never calculates with a musician’s conscience; he strains after effect. Talma’s rule: everything which has to strike people as true must not be true. “Wagner’s music is never true.” *Leitmotif* as an ideal toothpick.

**§9. Not a dramatist.** He begins with an overwhelming scene, then draws characters. Not Corneille’s public — the nineteenth century. Drama demands inexorable logic; Wagner loved the *word* “drama.” Not enough of a psychologist; he puts idiosyncrasy in the place of a psychological plot. Very modern, very Parisian, very decadent. Erda summoned because a female voice is required, then dismissed. Translate Wagner into the bourgeois: the heroines are almost indistinguishable from Mme Bovary. Always five paces from the hospital. Wagner’s heroines never have children. *Parsifal* is Lohengrin’s father — “chastity works miracles.”

**§10. Wagner’s writings; music as Idea.** Three procedures: (1) everything Wagner cannot do is bad; (2) he could do more, but his strong principles prevent him; (3) everything he can do, no one else must do — Wagner is godly. Music that is “not music alone” is a rhetorician’s necessity: he could not create wholes, so he had to press “this means” into the foreground. “Music can never be anything else than a means.” He was the commentator of the Idea. Hegel and Schelling were misleading Germany while Wagner was young; Germans take seriously only the obscure “idea.” Lucidity is an objection. Schopenhauer called that age dishonest, but “the pessimistic old counterfeiter was in no way more ‘honest.’” Hegel is a matter of *taste* — European taste — and Wagner applied it to music. He is **Hegel’s heir.** Music as “Idea.” The German youthlet is conquered not by music but by “infinity” and “meaning.” What *halcyonians* miss in Wagner is *la gaya scienza*: light feet, wit, fire, grave grand logic, stellar dancing, the vibrating light of the South, the calm sea — perfection.

**§11. Rise of the actor.** Historically: the actor in music. “Wagner and Liszt.” In declining civilisations, wherever the mob decides, genuineness becomes superfluous; the actor alone kindles great enthusiasm. His golden age. Coincidence of Wagner’s rise with the rise of the “Empire”: both prove obedience and long legs. Definition of a German: an obedient man with long legs. Expressiveness at all costs is the ideal of decadence; hardly compatible with talent. What is required is virtue — training, automatism, “self-denial.” Wagner commanded as one who had exercised an inexorable will over himself: perhaps the greatest example of self-violence in the history of art.

**§12. Three requisitions.**

- That the stage should not become master of the arts.
- That the actor should not become the corrupter of the genuine.
- That music should not become an art of lying.

Signed Friedrich Nietzsche.

**Postscript. “What Wagner has cost us.”** One pays dearly for having been a follower. Early German resistance honoured an instinct (an illness is not refuted with arguments). *Theatrocracy*: the craziness of believing the theatre should rule the arts. The theatre is lower than art, coarsened and falsified for the mob. Bayreuth is grand Opera — and not even good opera. A *plébiscite* against good taste. Wagner flattens every nihilistic (Buddhistic) instinct into music; he flatters every Christianity. *Parsifal* is his greatest masterpiece in the art of seduction. “I admire this work. I would fain have composed it myself.” Health and brightness in the earlier work now act like a shadow. *Cave canem.* Bayreuth is a Hydro; a typical telegram would read *bereits bereut*. Fatal for women; the female Wagnerite is the incarnation of his cause. Old Minotaur: every year Europe cries “Away to Crete!”

**Second Postscript.** This is not a glorification of any other musician. Decay is universal. Wagner is not the cause of the ruin of music, as Bernini is not the cause of the ruin of sculpture; he accelerated the fall and had the ingenuousness of decadence — he believed in it and did not halt. Brahms is not the antagonist Germany needed. Melancholy of impotence; he thirsts after abundance; the musician of dissatisfied women. Fifty steps further: the female Wagnerite. All claims to the grand style today are false to us or false to themselves. The first condition of the right thing is no longer in our bodies — Handel’s strong race, Rossini’s overflowing animal spirits. Corruption is a fatality; not even a God can save music.

**Epilogue. What is modern.** A philosopher wants to wash his hands after the Case of Wagner. An age has the virtues of *ascending* life or of degeneration. Aesthetic is bound up with these biological principles: decadent aesthetic and *classical* aesthetic; “beauty in itself” is a chimera. Within moral values the antithesis is **master-morality** vs **Christian** valuations (grown on morbid soil; the gospels present the same physiological types as Dostoevsky’s novels). Master-morality (“Roman,” “pagan,” “classical,” “Renaissance”) is the speech of well-constitutedness, ascending life, and the Will to Power as a vital principle. It *affirms*; Christian morality *denies*. One cannot refute Christianity: it is impossible to refute a diseased eyesight. “True” and “untrue” have no sense in optics. What must be guarded against is the falsity that would regard the antithesis as no antithesis — Wagner’s mastery. Modern man represents a *contradiction of values*; he sits between two stools; he says yea and nay in one breath. With this, modernity is almost defined. No wonder Wagner dwelt amongst us. The Case of Wagner is a *windfall* for philosophers; the essay was inspired by gratitude.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault’s first 1888 Nietzsche primary, and the first text in which *decadence* is the master problem rather than a colour-word. The Preface is load-bearing for the whole late corpus: good and evil are a *subdivision* of decline; Nietzsche includes himself; recovery, not refutation, is the event. That re-reads *Genealogy* (ascetic ideal, *Parsifal*) and *Gay Science* §370 (Wagner as romantic impoverishment) as earlier approaches to the same case.

What it adds:

1. **Decadence as style, physiology, and politics at once.** The anarchy-of-atoms formula joins literary style, “equal rights,” and exhausted nerves. Do not reduce it to “Nietzsche disliked late Romantic music.”
2. **Wagner as method.** The philosopher needs Wagner the way a physician needs a complete case. Cagliostro of modernity; diagnosis of the modern soul by incision into contradictory instincts.
3. **Actor vs musician.** The three requisitions are the pamphlet’s practical conclusion. Theatrocracy is the cultural form of decadence.
4. **Schopenhauer named as philosopher of decadence.** The *Ring*’s wreck is the conversion scene. Do not treat late Wagner as a mere application of Schopenhauer; Nietzsche says Schopenhauer *allowed Wagner to find himself*.
5. **Master vs Christian morality restated** from the *Genealogy*, now as optics that cannot be argued away, and as the frame for “what is modern”: innocence in contradiction.

The early Wagnerism this pamphlet presupposes (“I have loved Wagner”) is now primary: [[Sources/The Birth of Tragedy - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]]. 1872 hopes a German rebirth from Bach–Beethoven–Wagner; 1888 writes Wagner as disease. Both stand. *Nietzsche Contra Wagner* is the dossier (1876 Bayreuth; physiological objections; two sufferers). The *Ecce Homo* chapter remains reception.
