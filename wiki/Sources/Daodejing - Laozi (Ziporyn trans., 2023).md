# Daodejing — Laozi (Ziporyn trans., 2023)

> Eighty-one short chapters traditionally titled *Daodejing* ("The Canon of the Way and Its Virtue"), here in Brook Ziporyn's 2023 Liveright translation. The received text is treated not as a single-authored book but as a curated commonplace book of an ancient genre; Ziporyn reconstructs a "minimally discernible position": the ironic reversal of purposive *dao* into nonpurposive "course," of acquired *de* into unlearned "virtuosity," and of valued A-items into the ignored B-background that generates them.

## Bibliographic Note

Laozi, *Daodejing*, translated by Brook Ziporyn. First edition, New York: Liveright (W. W. Norton), 2023. ISBN 9781324092476. Converted from EPUB. The book contains a substantial Introduction, "A Note on the Translation," the 81 chapters in the received (Wang Bi) sequence, Acknowledgments, and chapter endnotes tracking variants (Guodian, Mawangdui, Beijing University slips, Yan Zun, Heshang Gong, Xiang'er, Fu Yi).

This is the vault's **first Chinese philosophical primary** and the missing Daoist counterpart to [[Sources/The Art of War - Sun Tzu (Giles trans., 1910)]]. Confucius's *Analects* is now in: [[Sources/The Analects - Confucius (Chin trans., 2014)]].

## Nature of the source

A **primary** classical text — terse, aphoristic, composite — plus a philosophically load-bearing modern apparatus. Ziporyn's Introduction discards the traditional story (Li Er / Lao Dan as Zhou archivist, the rebuke of Confucius, the water-buffalo departure, the five-thousand-character text dashed off at the Western Pass for the border guard Guanyin). Modern scholarship finds late attestation for the man, and the Guodian bamboo slips (4th c. BCE) contain less than half the received material, in thoroughly shuffled order. Mawangdui silk manuscripts (early 2nd c. BCE) reverse the two halves (chs. 38–81 first). Ziporyn's working hypothesis: think of *Daodejing* as "Classic Passages on the Course and Virtuosity from the Old Masters" — a best-of of a defunct genre, selected and rearranged over centuries, with a consistent tone despite many hands.

The 81 chapters omit all proper nouns (unique in the independently circulating classical Chinese canon). They skip steps, juxtapose apparent irrelevancies, and recycle the same pericopes in different grammatical contexts (e.g. *sheng er bu you*, "producing but not possessing," in chs. 2, 10, 34, 51). Traditional division: chs. 1–37 on *dao*, chs. 38–81 on *de*, with chs. 1 and 38 as the general statements.

**Cautions.** (1) Translation layer: Ziporyn is one more voice in what he calls a "deranged accelerating roundelay" of ~500 English versions; he translates *dao* as "course," *de* as "virtuosity"/"power," *wuwei* as "nondoing," *chang*/*heng* as constant/sustainable/ever-present. Do not mix his wording with Waley, Lau, or Addiss without marking the switch. (2) Authorship is traditional, not historical; the thinker page is a *name of convenience* for the position of the corpus. (3) Reception has always been wildly divergent (anarchism vs. Legalist control; metaphysics vs. hygiene vs. sexual yoga vs. managerial technique). The wiki follows Ziporyn's "minimally discernible position," not the later religious Daoist overlay.

## Ziporyn's A/B frame (from the Note on the Translation)

The through-tone of the corpus is an **A/B reversal**. Category A: being, having, strength, hardness, name/fame, adulthood, masculinity, fullness, action, high, bright, flavorful, complete, formed — what early Chinese readers were assumed to *value*. Category B: nonbeing, lacking, weakness, softness, nameless, infancy, femininity, empty, nondoing, low, dark, flavorless, incomplete, formless — what they were assumed to *disvalue*. Again and again the text promotes B and demotes A. *Chang*/*heng* names the B-background that remains the same through the rise and fall of straw-dog-like A-items: raw material, then the stuff and rule of A, then the B into which A returns.

Three key terms are **ironically repurposed**:
- **Course (*dao*)**: originally a road, then a purposive "way to get X done"; here, nonpurposive process — and *thereby* the real source of values and existences.
- **Virtuosity (*de*)**: originally the skill attained by practicing a *dao* (archery course → archery skill); here, the unlearned potency things already have (heartbeat, digestion) — "ironic virtuosity attained from the ironic course."
- **Constancy (*chang*/*heng*)**: the ordinary, sustainable, ever-present B that is "the one thing that is constant throughout."

See [[Concepts/Dao - The Course (Laozi)]], [[Concepts/De - Virtuosity and Potency (Laozi)]], [[Concepts/Unhewn Background and the A-B Reversal (Laozi)]].

## Argument Spine (minimally discernible position)

1. No course taken *as the right course*, and no name that determines what is or should be, can be the course or name that holds always (ch. 1). The nameless is any beginning of the ten thousand things; the named is their "mother." Desirelessness views the mystery; desire views what each strives for. The two "emerge together as one."
2. Value-pairs generate each other (ch. 2). The sage abides in **nondoing** and **noninstruction**: things arise, none possessing or possessed; achievements complete, no credit claimed — "never dwelling… never gone."
3. The course is a gushing/emptying abyss, source of the ten thousand things, merging with their dusts, an image preceding even the highest deity (ch. 4). Heaven and earth "are not humane"; things are "straw dogs" (ch. 5). The valley-spirit is "an obscure she-beast," root of heaven and earth (ch. 6).
4. **Water** is the emblem: it benefits by never competing, putting itself where none want to go (ch. 8). Function lies in absence — hub, vessel, room (ch. 11). Soft and yielding overcome the firm (chs. 36, 43, 76, 78).
5. All things revert to the root; that reversion is stillness, constancy, illumination (ch. 16). The highest rulers are "not even known to exist"; the people say they did it "of ourselves, all on our own" (ch. 17). When the great course is abandoned, humankindness and duty arise; when wisdom appears, "vast swindles begin" (ch. 18). Cut off sagacity, humankindness, duty, skill, profit — and embrace "the undyed blankness" and "the unhewn rawness" (ch. 19).
6. Something formed through "undivided muddle," before heaven and earth, is nicknamed "the course"; if forced, "the vastness." Humans conform to earth → sky → course; **the course conforms to how everything is when left to itself** (ch. 25).
7. The constancy of the course is namelessness, the unhewn (ch. 32). Carving produces names; know when to stop. The course does nothing yet leaves nothing undone; desires are settled with "the nameless unhewn" (ch. 37).
8. **Ch. 38 (de half):** highest virtuosity is not grasped as virtuosity and *does* nothing, without motives. Descent: course lost → virtuosity → humankindness → duty → ritual. Ritual is "the thinning out of loyalty and trust, the beginning of disorder."
9. **Reversal is the activity of the course; weakness is its functioning.** All things are born from what is there; what is there is born from what is not there (ch. 40). Course generates continuities → polarities → triplicities → the ten thousand things (ch. 42).
10. Learning is daily addition; the work of the course is **daily subtraction until nondoing**, "a nondoing that leaves nothing undone" (ch. 48). The sage has no intentions of his own (ch. 49). Course generates, virtuosity rears, producing without possessing — "obscure power, the power of their obscurity" (ch. 51).
11. Politics: more taboos, poorer people; more laws, more thieves. "I take no action and the people transform themselves… revert to their unhewn simplicity" (ch. 57). Ruling a large country is like frying a small fish (ch. 60). Using wisdom to govern is to pillage the state (ch. 65).
12. Rivers and oceans reign because they get below (ch. 66). Three treasures: loving care, frugality, never putting ourselves first (ch. 67). Skillful victory is never to engage at all (ch. 68). The sage helps each thing "be as it is of its own accord — never venturing any doing at all" (ch. 64).
13. Alive = soft; dead = hard (ch. 76). Heaven's course is like drawing a bow: take from excess, add to lack; man's course is the contrary (ch. 77). Nothing softer than water, yet none outdo it against the hard; "Straight words always seem reversed" (ch. 78). Small state, few people, unused weapons, neighbors heard but never visited (ch. 80). True words are not beautiful; the sage accumulates nothing; heaven benefits without harming; the sage acts "without striving against any other" (ch. 81).

## Key Quotes (Ziporyn)

- "Any course can be taken / as the right course to take, / but no course like that / can be the course taken always." (ch. 1)
- "This is why the sage abides in the work of nondoing, / practicing the teaching of noninstruction." (ch. 2)
- "Heaven and earth are not humane. / To them all things are straw dogs." (ch. 5)
- "The highest good is like water— / Water, so deft, / so good at benefiting things / by never competing with them, / by putting itself / where none want to go." (ch. 8)
- "Thirty spokes share a single hub— / the function of the cart happens where the absence is." (ch. 11)
- "When the great course is abandoned, / humankindness and duty arise." (ch. 18)
- "the course conforms to how everything is / when left to itself—" (ch. 25)
- "The constancy of the course lies in doing nothing / yet leaving nothing undone." (ch. 37)
- "The highest virtuosity is not grasped as virtuosity." / "Ritual propriety is the thinning out of loyalty and trust, the beginning of disorder." (ch. 38)
- "Reversal, opposition: the activity of the course. / Weakness, fragility: the functioning of the course." (ch. 40)
- "'The work of learning is daily addition.' / But the work of the course is daily subtraction, / subtracting and further subtracting until nondoing is reached— / a nondoing that leaves nothing undone." (ch. 48)
- "I take no action and the people transform themselves." (ch. 57)
- "This is how he helps each thing be as it is of its own accord— / never venturing any doing at all." (ch. 64)
- "Nothing in the world is softer and weaker than water. / Yet in attacking the hard and rigid none can outdo it." (ch. 78)
- "True words are not beautiful. / Beautiful words are not true." (ch. 81)

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Vs. the Confucian virtues the text itself names — humankindness, duty, ritual, sagacity, filial piety (chs. 18–19, 38) — these are *symptoms of the course's loss*, not the remedy. The Confucian reply is now primary: [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- > [!warning] Vs. [[Thinkers/Sun Tzu]]: shared water, formlessness, and "win without fighting" imagery, but Sun Tzu's *tao* is a **strategic asset** (morale/legitimacy) and his water is a tactic for imposing will. Laozi's course is nonpurposive; "to do something with it is to ruin it" (ch. 64). See [[Concepts/Calculation and the Five Factors (Sun Tzu)]], [[Concepts/Shih - Momentum, Formlessness, and Striking Weakness (Sun Tzu)]].
- > [!warning] Vs. [[Thinkers/Heraclitus]]: both teach a unity of opposites and a process that outlasts named things. Heraclitus's [[Concepts/Heraclitean Flux and Logos (Heraclitus)|Logos]] is the *rational rule* of strife; Laozi's course is the nameless B that generates and reclaims A, and it *inverts* the valued pole rather than declaring strife justice.
- > [!warning] Vs. [[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]: both invert received value-pairs. Nietzsche's revaluation promotes strength, mastery, and the pathos of distance ([[Concepts/Beyond Good and Evil (Nietzsche)]], [[Concepts/Master Morality and Slave Morality]]); Laozi promotes softness, lowliness, and not putting oneself first. Same gesture, opposite direction.
- > [!warning] Vs. [[Thinkers/Kant]]: nondoing without motives (ch. 38) is not Kantian duty. Kant's good will acts *from* the moral law; the *Daodejing*'s highest virtuosity "harbors no motives or purposes" and treats ritual/duty as decay. See [[Concepts/Duty (Kant)]], [[Concepts/Formula of Humanity as End in Itself (Kant)]].
- > [!warning] Vs. [[Thinkers/Aristotle]]: eudaimonia is excellent *activity* of the rational soul; the course's work is daily *subtraction* until nondoing. See [[Concepts/Eudaimonia]], [[Concepts/Contemplation and the Best Life - Theoria (Aristotle)]].
- Reception split the text itself already records: Ziporyn notes commentators cannot agree whether it teaches laissez-faire approaching anarchism or tight Legalist control of information. Ch. 3 ("empty hearts and minds") and ch. 65 ("not to enlighten the people but to befuddle them") feed the authoritarian reading; chs. 17, 57, 80 feed the anarchist one. Flag, do not reconcile.

## What this source adds

The vault's first classical Chinese *philosophy* (Sun Tzu was a practical manual). It opens a **Daoism** school page and supplies the missing referent for every prior "Taoist resonance" note. It gives the vault a non-Western via negativa that is *generative* rather than theological (the nameless is a mother, not a simple God), a political quietism that is not Stoic role-acceptance, and a value-reversal that is not Nietzschean mastery.
