# The Gay Science — Friedrich Nietzsche (1882 / 1887; Kaufmann trans., Vintage 1974)

> The vault's missing Nietzsche primary: *amor fati* is in via Holiday; God is dead and the eternal recurrence have been reported from BGE, *Genealogy*, Deleuze, Heidegger, and Hadot. This is the book that first states all three.

## Argument Spine

*Die fröhliche Wissenschaft* (first edition Chemnitz 1882; second edition Leipzig 1887 with Preface, Book V, and the Appendix of songs) is the last of the "free spirit" series (*Human, All Too Human*, *The Wanderer and His Shadow*, *Dawn* — now primary: [[Sources/The Dawn of Day - Nietzsche (Kennedy, Collection 2013)]]) and the book in which Nietzsche first publishes the death of God (§108, §125), *amor fati* (§276), and the thought-experiment of eternal recurrence (§341), then hands the next book to Zarathustra (§342, *Incipit tragoedia*). Kaufmann: it is "a microcosm in which we find almost all of Nietzsche" and "complete in itself. For it is a work of art." The title is not "joyful wisdom" (*Wissenschaft* never means wisdom) and not mere cheerfulness (*heiter*): *fröhlich* plus the Provençal subtitle *la gaya scienza* name a light-hearted, convention-defying *science* — any rigorous quest for knowledge that can also *sing*, the troubadours' *gai saber* (art of poetry) joined to an "artistic Socrates." Nietzsche's 1887 Preface frames the book as the saturnalia of a convalescent: philosophy as "interpretation of the body"; what was at stake in philosophizing hitherto was "not at all 'truth' but something else — let us say, health, future, growth, power, life." Book V, written after *Zarathustra* and *Beyond Good and Evil*, is the aftermath of the death of God among "we fearless ones" — still pious toward truth, needing a new infinite, the great health.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault's missing Nietzsche primary between the uningested free-spirit books and the already-ingested BGE + *Genealogy*. Four things reception had flattened are now sharp.

(1) ***Amor fati* is coined at §276 as a New Year's vow** — see the necessary as beautiful, make things beautiful, refuse accusation, wish to become a Yes-sayer. It is **not** in-text the same doctrine as the demon of §341. Holiday's "fuel" is not in the aphorism. The later *Ecce Homo* weld is real in the corpus and is marked as later.

(2) **"God is dead" is a sequence, not a slogan.** §108 shadows, §109 de-deified nature, §124 no land, §125 the madman telling *unbelievers* they have come too early, §343 (1887) cheerfulness *and* the collapse of European morality, §344 still pious toward *truth*. Marketplace atheism is the problem the madman addresses.

(3) **Eternal recurrence is published as a thought-experiment.** §341: a demon, two responses (curse vs "you are a god"), a possible crushing, a weight on *actions*, a craving for the "ultimate eternal confirmation and seal." Deleuze's selective ontology (only the active returns) is a productive reading that this aphorism does not make. Allusions at 109/233/285 are not the doctrine.

(4) **Book V is 1887**, after *Zarathustra* and *BGE*. Intellectual conscience (§2, 1882) and "we too are still pious" (§344) are the same honesty at two dates. The great health (§382; Preface) is the book's weather: philosophies as symptoms of bodies; health acquired by being given up.

The Argument Spine of Step 1 held. Corrections: *amor fati* is not tied to recurrence *in this book* (the stub left that open); §243 is not the *Genealogy*'s origin of good and bad; Socrates is §340, not Book III; Kaufmann's four-point recurrence schema and "most scientific hypothesis" are his, not §341.
