# Human Compatible - Stuart Russell (2019)

> Stuart Russell's 2019 reconstruction of the AI control problem from inside the field: the *standard model* (machines that optimize a fixed, specified objective) is itself the failure mode; the replacement is machines that are uncertain about human preferences, learn them from behavior, and therefore defer, ask, and allow themselves to be switched off.

## Source Details

- **Author**: Stuart Russell (b. 1962), UC Berkeley; co-author with Peter Norvig of *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*.
- **Publication**: Viking / Penguin, 2019. ISBN 9780525558613 (hardcover) / 9780525558620 (ebook). Subtitle: *Artificial Intelligence and the Problem of Control*.
- **Raw files**: Calibre TXT conversion of the 2019 Penguin MOBI (`Human Compatible - Stuart Russell (2019).txt`) plus the original MOBI. No running heads — cite by chapter.
- **Domain position**: Outstanding Sources named this the "real successor to *Superintelligence*." It is. Bostrom diagnoses; Russell recants the textbook definition he himself taught and offers a research programme (uncertainty + assistance games). Wiener 1960 is the epigraph-quality sentence. Mitchell is not named; Ch. 6's "not-so-great debate" is Pinker / Brooks / LeCun / Ng / AI100 / Kelly.

## Argument Spine

Success at the standard model of AI — machines that optimize a fixed, exogenously supplied objective — would be the biggest event in human history and perhaps the last, because a sufficiently capable optimizer of a misspecified or incomplete objective will not remain under human control (King Midas; the gorilla problem). The solution is not a better specification of the objective. It is to abandon the standard model: machines whose only objective is the realization of *human* preferences, which they are initially uncertain about, and which they learn from human behavior. Uncertainty is the mechanism of control: it produces deference, permission-asking, caution, and a willingness to be switched off. The resulting research programme is "provably beneficial AI" — an aspiration, not a promise. Ch. 10's title is a question.

The preface's own map holds: Chs. 1–3 intelligence; Chs. 4–6 the problem of control; Chs. 7–10 a different approach. Appendices A–D are pedagogical (search, logic, probability, learning) and are not load-bearing for wiki pages.

## Core Thesis (as executed)

Two definitions, then a recantation. Humans are intelligent to the extent that their actions can be expected to achieve *their* objectives. Machines, under AIMA, to the extent that theirs achieve *theirs*. "We don't want machines that are intelligent in this sense." Replacement: machines are beneficial to the extent that their actions can be expected to achieve *our* objectives, which they do not know.

The standard model is shared with control theory, economics, OR, and statistics. It works while the machine is weak or resettable. Combined with competence it produces King Midas (omitted values driven to extremes) and the gorilla problem (species-level loss of supremacy). Instrumental goals are not instincts: "you can't fetch the coffee if you're dead." Orthogonality is "just a given" inside the model. Written prohibitions are loophole'd. Boxing fails. The plug fails. "Putting in values is, of course, exactly the mistake I am saying we should avoid."

Three principles (researcher guides, not robot laws): (1) only objective = realize human preferences; (2) initially uncertain what they are; (3) evidence is human behavior. Formal apparatus: IRL → assistance games (CIRL) → off-switch game. If Robbie is not certain the act is what Harriet would do, waiting dominates. "Provably beneficial" unpacks axioms; real-world axioms fail. Ch. 9 is the human remainder (Harsanyi, many people, preference change, sadism). Ch. 10 refuses to close: Dr. Evil, no present regulatory spec, enfeeblement as a cultural commons problem. "It remains to be seen how the endgame turns out."

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault already had Wiener's 1950 statement and Bostrom's 2014 systematization. What it lacked was a primary from *inside* the field that treats the control problem as a mistake in AI's definition of success. Russell can recant the standard model because he wrote the textbook that taught it. The load-bearing contribution is not a new doom story — gorilla / Midas / coffee-off-switch are restatements — but the claim that *uncertainty about the objective changes the induced instrumental goals*, plus a pair of toy theorems (paperclip game, off-switch game) and an honest Ch. 10. Orthogonality is not reopened. Value-loading is. Lecture-vs-treatise tension from other ingests does not apply; the book is popular-academic and says what it is doing. Flagged, not reconciled: Russell vs Pinker/Brooks on whether intelligence entails "nice" goals (already the orthogonality page's warning); Bostrom value-*load* vs Russell preference-*learn*; enfeeblement as a problem no theorem in this book solves. *The Alignment Problem* (Christian) remains the empirical/history complement Outstanding Sources asked for.
