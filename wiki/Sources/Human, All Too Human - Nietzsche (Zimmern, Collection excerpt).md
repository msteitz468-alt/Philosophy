# Human, All Too Human — Friedrich Nietzsche (1878 / 1886 Preface; Zimmern)

> The first "book for free spirits": metaphysics' antitheses dissolve into a chemistry of human, all-too-human ingredients. This file is only the 1886 Preface plus Volume I, chapters I–III.

## Argument Spine

*Menschliches, Allzumenschliches: Ein Buch für freie Geister* (first edition 1878, dedicated to Voltaire's centenary; second edition 1886 with a new Preface) is the break with Schopenhauer and Wagner: an anti-romantic, "historical-philosophical" book that refuses miraculous origins for the highest things. The 1878 argument, as far as this excerpt goes, is that apparent antitheses (reason/unreason, altruism/egoism, truth/error) are errors of popular or metaphysical comprehension; there are "no eternal facts as there are no absolute truths"; what is needed is a *chemistry* of moral, religious, and aesthetic feelings that traces magnificent results to despised ingredients. The 1886 Preface is a **second voice**, written eight years later in the season of *Beyond Good and Evil*: it redefines the "free spirit" as a spirit that has *become* free through a "great liberation," convalescence, and the problem of *Rangordnung*, and it already talks "beyond good and evil." Do not flatten 1878 into 1886, or 1878 into the vault's later Nietzsche (*Gay Science*, BGE, *Genealogy*).

## Whole-Work Assessment

This excerpt is the vault's first Nietzsche *primary* from the free-spirit series that GS already named as its predecessor. It does three things the later books do not do in these words. (1) It states the method: a chemistry of moral, religious, and aesthetic feelings and historical philosophising against the philosophers' lack of historical sense — "no eternal facts as there are no absolute truths" (§2). (2) It gives the two-fold historical origin of good and bad as requital among ruling races and hostility among the subjugated, then dissolves responsibility into necessity and guiltlessness (§§45, 107) — the account GM later calls clumsy, and not yet ressentiment. (3) It chemically analyses the saint and the need of salvation as compound, multi-conditioned, optically inflated (§§132–144) — not yet the 1887 ascetic ideal's *horror vacui*. The 1886 Preface is a second voice in the same file: it already talks "beyond good and evil" and *Rangordnung* while dedicating an 1878 book to free spirits who "do not really exist." The collection is truncated (no chs. IV–IX, no Volume II). *Dawn* is now ingested: [[Sources/The Dawn of Day - Nietzsche (Kennedy, Collection 2013)]]. *Zarathustra*, *Twilight*, *The Antichrist*, and *Ecce Homo* are in. The Argument Spine held: 1878 chemistry is not 1886 BGE, and "master and slave" in §45 is not master/slave morality.

## The 1888 Self-Review (added 2026-08-12 from *Ecce Homo*)

[[Sources/Ecce Homo - Nietzsche (Collection 2013)]] devotes a chapter to this book and supplies the occasion the text itself withholds. Nietzsche calls it "**the memorial of a crisis**" and "a book for free spirits," published in **1878 on the centenary of Voltaire's death**, and gives the crisis a location: it was begun at the **first Bayreuth festival (1876)**, in the middle of an estrangement he describes as a physical event — "What had happened? **Wagner had been translated into German!**" He fled to Klingenbrunn in the Bohemian forest and made notes under the working title "**The Ploughshare**." The break, he insists, was not only with Wagner but with his own instincts, with philology and the Basel chair, and toward physiology, medicine and natural science; illness is credited as the liberator and the "return to myself." Written principally at **Sorrento**, finished at Basel, dictated to **Peter Gast** — "he was the real writer while I was only the author." He also reports the crossing in the post with Wagner's *Parsifal*: "Did it not sound as if two swords had crossed?… Incredible! Wagner had become pious." And §6 discloses that the passage on **Paul Rée** secretly refers to himself.

Two things this adds to the page's assessment. The definition — a free spirit is "**a spirit that has become free, that has once more taken possession of itself**" — is the tightest in the corpus and is now recorded on [[Concepts/Free Spirit (Nietzsche)]]. And the method-image for what the 1878 chemistry actually does to an ideal: "**One error after the other is quietly laid on ice; the ideal is not refuted — it freezes.**"
