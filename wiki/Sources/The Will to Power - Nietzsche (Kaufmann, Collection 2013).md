# The Will to Power — Friedrich Nietzsche (notebooks 1883–1888; Kaufmann trans.; collection excerpt)

> Not a book Nietzsche published. A posthumous arrangement of notebook entries under a title he planned and then abandoned. This file is only Book I (European Nihilism, notes 1–134) and Book III.I (The Will to Power as Knowledge, notes 466–617).

## Identification

The queue file is the 2013 compilation *The Friedrich Nietzsche Collection: 22 Classic Works*. Volume-title ≠ work (Observation 18). Extracted work-file filed to `raw/Ingested/The Will to Power - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013).md` (collection lines 17611–20170). The English is **Walter Kaufmann** with **R. J. Distinctive Kaufmann markers: "uncanniest of all guests"; "the highest values devaluate themselves"; dated note-heads.

The collection itself prints the warning: **"Note: Books II & IV are not available."** Book III in this file is only Part I. Kaufmann Vintage 1967 in full has four books (I European Nihilism; II Critique of the Highest Values Hitherto; III Principles of a New Evaluation; IV Discipline and Breeding). Do **not** import missing books from memory. In particular, do **not** quote note 1067 ("This world is the will to power — and nothing besides!") unless it is in this file — it is not.

Nietzsche announced a book *The Will to Power: Attempt at a Revaluation of All Values* (see *Genealogy* III.27). He then dropped that plan. *The Antichrist* became Book I of the Revaluation (now ingested: [[Sources/The Antichrist - Nietzsche (Mencken, Collection 2013)]]); *Twilight* is dated "the day when the first book of the Revaluation of All Values was completed." Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche and Peter Gast later compiled notebooks under the abandoned title. Kaufmann's edition rearranges the 1906 Grossoktav notes with dates. This ingest treats the notes as **notebooks**, not as a finished treatise equal to *BGE* or the *Genealogy*.

## Argument Spine

The Preface (November 1887–March 1888) states the whole: what is coming "can no longer come differently: the advent of nihilism." The speaker is "the first perfect nihilist of Europe who, however, has even now lived through the whole of nihilism, to the end, leaving it behind, outside himself." The title names a **countermovement** that can come only *after and out of* perfect nihilism: "The Will to Power: Attempt at a Revaluation of All Values." Nihilism is necessary because "the values we have had hitherto thus draw their final consequence." New values require that experience.

Book I diagnoses European nihilism: not social distress but the Christian-moral interpretation turning against itself; "the highest values devaluate themselves"; three psychological forms (loss of aim, of unity, of a true world). Book III.I then treats knowledge itself as will to power: no innocent subject, no thing-in-itself, logic and causality as useful falsifications. The published books already in the vault (*Gay Science*, *Zarathustra*, *BGE*, *Genealogy*, *Twilight*) name will to power, death of God, and the will to truth. These notes are the **workshop** of that diagnosis — thicker on nihilism as a European event, thicker on the epistemology of knowledge as interpretation — and they are **not** a fifth published doctrine.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault already had will to power from *Zarathustra* (named) and *BGE* (analytic), death of God from *GS*, the will to nothingness from *Genealogy* III, and decadence from *Wagner* / *Twilight*. What this excerpt adds is the **history and typology of European nihilism** and the **workshop epistemology** of knowledge as will to power — under a hard constraint: this is not a book Nietzsche published.

Four things reception and the existing Will to Power page had already warned about are now sourced.

(1) **Definition.** n.2: the highest values devaluate themselves. Root = Christian-moral interpretation (n.1), not distress. Mechanism = the same Christian truthfulness *GS* §357 / *GM* III.27 published. Active/passive (n.22–23) and complete/incomplete (n.28) are in the notes; they are not Deleuze's forces.

(2) **n.55 vs n.617 vs §341.** Recurrence as the meaningless eternally (n.55) is not the demon's *if* and not n.617's stamp of being onto becoming. Four voices, not one doctrine.

(3) **n.481.** "No, facts is precisely what there is not, only interpretations." Perspectivism is *named* here as the anti-positivist heading. It does not replace *GS* §354 or *BGE* §22.

(4) **Incompleteness is load-bearing.** Books II and IV are missing. Note 1067 is not in the file. Do not quote it. *Twilight* already said the first book of the Revaluation is *The Antichrist*, not this compilation.

The Argument Spine held: nihilism as necessary consequence of prior values; the title as countermovement after that experience; knowledge as tool of power. Correction to the stub: Book I.I ends at n.56, not ~68; n.481 is in III.I.3, not in the judgment half; "last man" does not appear.

*Dawn*, remainder of *Human, All Too Human*, *The Antichrist*, and *Ecce Homo* remain outstanding.
