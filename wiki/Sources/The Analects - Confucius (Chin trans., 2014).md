# The Analects — Confucius (Chin trans., 2014)

> The *Lunyu* ("selected sayings"), compiled by disciples and later disciples: conversations and remarks of Kong Qiu (Confucius, 551–479 BC) of Lu. This edition is Annping Chin's 2014 Penguin Classics translation with running commentary. The EPUB filename said "1979 / Penguin Books Ltd"; the file is Chin 2014, not D. C. Lau 1979.

## Bibliographic Note

Confucius, *The Analects* = *Lunyu*, translated with an introduction and commentary by Annping Chin. New York: Penguin Books, 2014. eBook ISBN 978-0-698-15351-6. Converted from EPUB. Apparatus: Preface, Introduction, Note on the Translation, dynastic chronology, twenty Books with Chin's inline commentary after each entry, appendices (disciples, historical figures, Chinese terms, major topics, commentators cited), endnotes, bibliography. Chin's exegetical spine is Qing evidential research, especially Liu Baonan's *Lunyu zhengyi*; she refuses to privilege Zhu Xi as the single state-sanctioned reading.

This is the vault's **first Confucian primary** and the missing counterpart to [[Sources/Daodejing - Laozi (Ziporyn trans., 2023)]]. Mencius, Xunzi, and the *Zhuangzi* remain outstanding.

## Nature of the source

A **primary** classical text — fragmentary, dialogical, compiled, not authored as a treatise — plus a philosophically load-bearing modern apparatus. Chin: Confucius "refrained from putting forth theories" and "did not think that he must be right"; the book is what disciples made of what he said. Dingzhou bamboo (first half of 1st c. BC) shows particle/character/partition variants, "no fundamental differences" from the received text. Warring States Shanghai Museum slips add conversations; excavated *Laozi* material has already pushed scholars off the "single mother source" theory. Formation of the *Analects* is still open.

**Cautions.** (1) Translation layer: Chin renders *ren* **humaneness**, *li* **rites / ritual propriety**, *yi* **rightness**, *de* **virtue / integrity**, *dao* **Way / moral way**, *junzi* **gentleman** (sometimes "man of position"), *shu* **putting oneself in the other's place**, *zhong* **doing one's best**, *xiao* **filiality**, *xue* **learning**, *zhengming* **rectifying names**. Ziporyn's *Daodejing* used humankindness / duty / ritual / virtuosity / course. Do not mix without marking the switch. (2) Chin explicitly refuses to treat Confucius's ideas as "a set of concepts and categories that could find their counterparts in the Western philosophical tradition." Wiki concept pages below are the vault's compilation layer, not her method. (3) Mencius and Xunzi "took his teachings in different directions"; do not import their positions as Confucius's. (4) Books rarely have a thread; Chin withholds synopses so as not to invent one. (5) Books 16 and 20 may be later accretion (Chin). Book 19 is disciples, not the Master.

## Argument Spine

1. The *Analects* is China's central point of reference: kept alive first by competing disciples, then by Mencius and Xunzi, then by Han institutional embedding, then (after a long Buddhist interval) by Song revival, then by Yuan exam restriction to the Four Books. Disagreement *inside* the tradition, not a single commentary, kept Confucius "vigorous and relevant."
2. Confucius is a historical *shi* of Lu: poor, skilled in menial tasks, Jisun service, minister of works then of crime, 498 BC exile, fourteen years seeking a ruler who would use him ("not a bitter gourd"), return in 484 as "elder statesman."
3. Ethical thesis: fulfillment of **humaneness** is beginning and end; "restrain the self and return to the rites" is the way (12.1). Filiality is the root (1.2). Acting principle: *shu* — "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not want" (15.24). He does not speculate about inborn nature or the nature of knowledge (5.13); "People are similar by nature; they become distinct through practice" (17.2).
4. Political thesis: government runs on trust and moral capital; **rectifying names** first (13.3); make the people rich, then instruct them (13.9); *de* + *li* produce shame and self-reform, statutes produce evasion (2.3); refuse killing as the path of reform, yet admit a hundred years of good men before cruelty and capital punishment can be done away with (13.11). Flexibility in practice; aim is "greater benefits for a greater number of people."
5. Pedagogical thesis: transmit, do not innovate (7.1); one corner, student returns three (7.8); *Odes* teach speech, rites teach balance, music completes (8.8, 16.13). Integrity alone is not enough: good men have to be tested in politics.
6. Against recluses he does not sneer: he sees their point (14.39, 18.5–18.8) and stays — "Whom can I be with if not with other human beings?" (18.6). Heaven does not speak; seasons move (17.19).

## Key Quotes (Chin)

- "Is it not a pleasure to learn [*xue*] and, when it is timely, to practice what you have learned? … Is it not gentlemanly not to become resentful if no one takes notice of your learning?" (1.1)
- "being filial to one's parents and being respectful to one's elders are the roots of one's humanity [*ren*]" (1.2)
- "If you guide the people with ordinances and statutes and keep them in line with [threats of] punishment, they will try to stay out of trouble but will have no sense of shame. If you guide them with exemplary virtue [*de*] and keep them in line with the practice of the rites [*li*], they will have a sense of shame and will know to reform themselves." (2.3)
- "At seventy, I followed what my heart desired without overstepping the line." (2.4)
- "Being human and yet lacking humaneness—what can such a man do with the rites?" (3.3)
- "The gentleman does not abandon humaneness, not even for the duration of a meal." (4.5)
- Zengzi: "The Master's way consists of doing one's best to fulfill one's humanity [*zhong*] and treating others with an awareness that they, too, are alive with humanity [*shu*]." (4.15)
- "I transmit but do not innovate. I love antiquity and have faith in it." (7.1)
- "The Master did not speak about prodigies, extraordinary shows of strength, chaos, and spirits." (7.21)
- "The *Odes* are to stimulate [our mind and spirit]. The rites are to steady us. Music is the final lesson." (8.8)
- "Restrain the self and return to the rites [*keji fuli*]. This is the way to be humane. … Humaneness rests with the self." (12.1)
- "Do not impose on others what you do not desire for yourself." (12.2)
- "Let the ruler be a ruler, the subject be a subject, a father be a father, a son be a son." (12.11)
- "To govern [*zheng*] is to correct [*zheng*]." (12.17)
- "The character of those at the top [*junzi*] is like that of the wind. The character of those below [*xiaoren*] is like that of grass." (12.19)
- "If names are not rectified, what is said will not seem reasonable. … people will not know where to put their hands and feet." (13.3)
- "Fathers cover up for their sons, and sons cover up for their fathers. Being upright lies therein." (13.18)
- "The one ruler who was able to order the world without taking any [deliberate] action [*wuwei*] was Shun. … He held himself respectfully and faced south—that was all." (15.5)
- "It is perhaps the word *shu*. Do not impose on others what you yourself do not want." (15.24)
- "Unless you learn the *Odes*, you won't be able to speak. … Unless you learn the rites, you won't be able to find your balance." (16.13)
- "People are similar by nature; they become distinct through practice." (17.2)
- "What does Heaven ever say? Yet the four seasons move in order, and the hundred things come to life. What does Heaven ever say?" (17.19)
- "We cannot flock with birds and beasts, can we? Whom can I be with if not with other human beings?" (18.6)
- "I have no preconceptions about what one can or cannot do." (18.8)

## Whole-Work Assessment

The *Analects* closes the civilizational hole Outstanding Sources named: Sun Tzu and the *Daodejing* did not fill it. It gives the vault a third classical Chinese voice whose *dao* is a moral way of office and kinship, not a strategic asset and not a nonpurposive course. It answers — without being written as an answer — the *Daodejing*'s charge that humankindness and ritual are decay. It also refuses the Western move Chin warns against: there is no treatise on human nature, no deduction of the state, no definition of *ren*. The wiki's concept pages are a compilation layer on top of a book that works by cases, disciples, and the tug both ways (kill / do not kill; withdraw / stay; cover the father / be upright).

What shifts in the vault: [[Schools/Daoism]] is no longer one-sided; [[Concepts/De - Virtuosity and Potency (Laozi)]] and [[Concepts/Wu Wei - Nondoing (Laozi)]] now have the Confucian *de* and 15.5 *wuwei* as primary counterparts; Hadot's way-of-life claim gains a non-Greek instance that wants *office*, not only spiritual exercise; Kant's formulas gain a reciprocity that is analogical and role-bound, not autonomous.

What it does not close: Mencius and Xunzi (nature good / nature bad) remain uningested; 17.2 must not be filled with them. The *Zhongyong* book is not 6.29. Zhuangzi is still the other Daoist hole.
