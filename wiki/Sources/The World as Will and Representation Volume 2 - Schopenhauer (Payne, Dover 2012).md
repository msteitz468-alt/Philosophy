## Argument Spine

Volume 2 does not replace Volume 1. It supplements the same four-book architecture: the world as representation (Books I and III) and the world as will (Books II and IV). The first sentence of the system is still “The world is my representation”; Chapter I restates that as the surest truth of modern philosophy (Descartes → Berkeley → Kant) and as *phenomenon of the brain*. The will remains the thing-in-itself, reached through the double knowledge of the body, not by applying causality beyond representation.

Chapter L states the whole as **immanent** in Kant’s sense: it explains the inner connexion of outward and inward experience and refuses extramundane grounds. The *ἕν καὶ πᾶν* is already ancient; Schopenhauer’s claim is to have said *what* the one is (will) and how it appears as the many. He distinguishes himself from pantheists and from Spinoza: their God/substance is an unknown *x* and an optimism; his will is the one thing immediately known, and ethically it is “the crucified Saviour, or else the crucified thief.” His *tertium* between fatalism and a free extra-mundane creator: the act of will from which the world springs is *our own*.

## Whole-Work Assessment

Volume 2 does what the 1844 preface (in Volume 1 editions) promised: it supplements the four books. It does not recant them.

(1) **Idealism is restated as honesty and as physiology.** Ch. I: only consciousness is immediate; the world is a phenomenon of the brain; intellect and matter are correlatives. Chs. II–VII: perception is understanding’s work; animals understand and do not think; concepts are notes on perceptual cash. The *Fourfold Root* §21 and *Vision and Colours* rehearsal now have their place in the principal work.

(2) **The will is nearest, not naked.** Ch. XVIII is the load-bearing qualification the vault’s 1819 pages lacked: we ourselves are the thing-in-itself, but inner observation still leaves time, and after denial more may remain unknown. Ch. XIX is the primacy battery. Chs. XXIII–XXVIII give inorganic will, matter as visibility, immanent teleology, and the will-to-live as the world’s only true name.

(3) **Book III specifies genius and demotes history.** Genius is surplus perceptive intellect; madness is the same uncoupling used by the will to break memory. History is not a science. Music still copies the will itself — the line *Birth of Tragedy* will quote.

(4) **Book IV is now primary for the doctrines *Parerga* II popularized.** Death: intellect dies, will does not (palingenesis). Sexual love: the species choosing the next generation. Affirmation is procreation; denial is *nolle*. Ch. XLVI is the *worst of all possible worlds* proof. Ch. XLIX names the inborn error that we exist to be happy — the system-side of eudaemonology as accommodation. Ch. L: immanent philosophy; Spinoza as OT to his NT; the *tertium* that the world’s act of will is our own.

He still does not name his *system* pessimism. The word appears as a classification of religions and as a charge.

The Argument Spine of Step 1 held. Correction: Affirmation and Denial, Indestructibility, and Vanity were created first by the *Parerga* II ingest; this ingest rewrote them from the systematic text rather than duplicating pages.
