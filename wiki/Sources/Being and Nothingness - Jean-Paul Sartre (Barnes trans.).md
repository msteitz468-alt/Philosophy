# Being and Nothingness — Jean-Paul Sartre (1943; Barnes trans.)

> Phenomenological ontology: two regions of being (in-itself / for-itself), nothingness as the structure of consciousness, and the human as a failed project of becoming God.

**Full citation**: Jean-Paul Sartre, *Being and Nothingness: An Essay on Phenomenological Ontology*, trans. Hazel E. Barnes (Philosophical Library / Washington Square Press; this file is a 2007 Calibre conversion of that complete Barnes edition, which revises her 1953 *Existential Psychoanalysis* selections). French: *L'être et le néant. Essai d'ontologie phénoménologique* (Gallimard, 1943). Citations use Barnes page numbers from the running heads.

This is the vault's first primary ingest of Sartre's systematic ontology. Outstanding Sources listed it as the missing work behind the Welten biography ([[Sources/Sartre - Ruud Welten (2020)]]) and the 1945/46 lecture ([[Sources/Existentialism is a Humanism - Jean-Paul Sartre (1946)]]). ## Argument Spine

The Introduction ("The Pursuit of Being") rejects hidden-world dualisms (interior/exterior, being/appearance, potency/act, appearance/essence) in favour of the phenomenon, then shows that this conversion produces a new dualism — finite/infinite — and the question of the *being of the appearance*. Against Berkeley/Husserl *esse est percipi*, Sartre establishes three load-bearing results: (1) the **pre-reflective cogito** — every positional consciousness of an object is at the same time non-positional self-consciousness; the Cartesian *cogito* is a secondary, reflective act; (2) an **ontological proof** from intentionality — consciousness is consciousness of a transphenomenal being that is not consciousness, so neither realism nor idealism explains the relation; (3) **two regions of being**: the in-itself (*être-en-soi*: being is; being is in-itself; being is what it is; *massif*, uncreated, *de trop*) and the for-itself (*être-pour-soi*: being that is what it is not and is not what it is). The book's task is to recover the concrete — man-in-the-world — without first isolating the terms and then trying to glue them.

The four Parts then derive nothingness from the question and from human conduct (I); unfold the for-itself's structures — presence to self, facticity, value as lack, possibility, selfness, temporality, transcendence (II); establish the Other via the Look, the body, and concrete relations of conflict (III); and reduce doing and having to the project of being, via freedom, situation, responsibility, and existential psychoanalysis (IV).

The Conclusion refuses an ontological *why* of the for-itself (that is metaphysics). Ontology can only say that everything takes place *as if* the in-itself, in a project to found itself, gave itself the modification of the for-itself. The ideal totality would be the *ens causa sui* (in-itself-for-itself / God); the real is "an abortive effort to attain to the dignity of the self-cause." "Everything happens as if the world, man, and man-in-the-world succeeded in realizing only a missing God." Ontology cannot formulate ethics, but it glimpses one: abandon the **spirit of seriousness**; existential psychoanalysis is moral description; freedom discovering itself in anguish as the unique source of value. Whether freedom can take *itself* as value is deferred to a future work (the promised ethics, never delivered as a treatise).

## Whole-Work Assessment

*Being and Nothingness* converts the vault's Sartre cluster from a biography-plus-lecture into a quoted phenomenological ontology. The Argument Spine of Step 1 held. Three corrections of emphasis. (1) The lecture slogan "existence precedes essence" is not BN's sentence; BN says consciousness's *existence implies its essence* and, later, "freedom precedes essence." The paper-knife remains the public argument; the treatise is regional ontology of the for-itself. (2) Welten's "authenticity is impossible" overreads a footnote that *withholds* the description; the lecture then *uses* authenticity as the plane of judgment. Keep the two registers. (3) The Look is not the park-bench one-liner; it is the keyhole, shame as recognition, and a systematic rejection of Husserl, Hegel, and Heidegger on the Other. Conflict is the original meaning of being-for-others; the We does not realize *Mitsein*. The Conclusion's missing God, useless passion, and spirit of seriousness are the book's ethical glimpse, not an ethics — and they sit against the lecture's cowards/scum. Contradictions opened or sharpened: Sartre vs Freud (now from the censor argument, not only Welten); Sartre vs Heidegger on death and on which ekstasis is primary; lecture vs treatise on inter-subjectivity and on moral verdict. The promised ethics remains an internal hole.
