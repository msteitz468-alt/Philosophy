# On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense — Friedrich Nietzsche (1873; Breazeale)
> Truths are illusions we have forgotten are illusions — coins that have lost their embossing.

## Bibliographic Note

Friedrich Nietzsche, *Über Wahrheit und Lüge im außermoralischen Sinne*. Written summer 1873, unpublished in his lifetime; first printed from the Nachlass in 1896. Two parts: an unnumbered opening (language, concept, the definition of truth) and a section headed **2** (science as columbarium; metaphor-drive in myth and art; rational man vs intuitive man).

## Argument Spine

The human intellect is a minute, species-bound device for staying alive, not a window on the world’s axis. Its first power is *dissimulation*. The drive for truth does not come from a love of the essence of things. It comes from a peace treaty: to live in the herd, man invents uniformly valid designations; the liar is the one who misuses them. “Truth” at this stage is the pleasant, life-preserving consequences of convention. Words are not copies of things: a nerve stimulus becomes an image (first metaphor), the image a sound (second metaphor). Concepts arise by equating what is never equal (“leaf,” “honesty”). Formula: “What then is truth? A movable host of metaphors, metonymies, and anthropomorphisms… Truths are illusions which we have forgotten are illusions.” To be truthful is to lie according to a fixed convention, with the herd. Science then builds a columbarium of those concepts. The metaphor-drive is not thereby killed; it escapes into *myth* and *art*. Two types: the rational man (concepts, foresight, freedom from pain) and the intuitive man (illusion and beauty, “overjoyed hero”). The stoical man, who wants sincerity, executes his masterpiece of deception in misfortune: a mask, no cry, walks slowly out from under the storm.

## Section-by-Section Summary

**Part 1. Intellect, language, concept, truth.** Fable of clever beasts on a cooling star: when the intellect is gone, “nothing will have happened.” The gnat also takes itself for the flying centre. Intellect allotted as a device for detaining delicate beings in existence; pride in knowing is a fog over the value of existence. First power: dissimulation (the weak’s substitute for horns and teeth). Man is the peak of the art: mask, role, borrowed splendour. Eyes glide over “forms”; nature threw away the key to the body; “hanging in dreams on the back of a tiger.”

Social existence requires a treaty against the *bellum omni contra omnes*. Legislation of language establishes the first laws of truth. The liar uses valid designations to make the unreal appear real (“I am rich”). Society excludes him because of harm, not because of deception as such. Man wants the pleasant consequences of truth and is hostile to destructive ones.

A word is “the copy in sound of a nerve stimulus.” Inference to an external cause already misapplies sufficient reason. “The stone is hard,” gendered trees, “snake” that would fit a worm: languages side by side prove designations are never adequate. The “thing in itself” is “not in the least worth striving for.” Two metaphors: stimulus → image → sound. Chladni figures for the deaf man. We possess “nothing but metaphors for things.”

Every concept equates unequal cases. No leaf is the same as another; “the leaf” is formed by discarding differences. Honesty as *qualitas occulta* from unequal actions. Nature knows “no forms and no concepts, and likewise with no species, but only with an X.”

Formula: “What then is truth? A movable host of metaphors, metonymies, and anthropomorphisms: in short, a sum of human relations which have been poetically and rhetorically intensified, transferred, and embellished, and which, after long usage, seem to a people to be fixed, canonical, and binding. Truths are illusions which we have forgotten are illusions— they are metaphors that have become worn out and have been drained of sensuous force, coins which have lost their embossing and are now considered as metal and no longer as coins.”

To be truthful is “to employ the usual metaphors… to lie according to a fixed convention, to lie with the herd.” Unconsciousness and forgetfulness produce the *sense* of truth. Rational man volatilizes perceptual metaphors into schemata. The edifice of concepts has “the rigid regularity of a Roman columbarium.” Concept is “the *residue of a metaphor*.” Defining mammal then finding a camel is “a thoroughly anthropomorphic truth.” Investigator seeks “the metamorphosis of the world into man.”

Only by forgetting that he is an *artistically creating* subject does man live with repose. “The correct perception” (adequate expression of object in subject) is “a contradictory impossibility.” Between subject and object there is “at most, an *aesthetic* relation.” “Appearance” is avoided: the essence of things does not appear in the empirical world. Repeated images feel necessary; an eternally repeated dream would be judged reality.

Laws of nature: we know only effects as relations to other laws. What we know of them is “what we ourselves bring to them—time and space, and therefore relationships of succession and number.” We produce these “with the same necessity with which the spider spins.” “It is we who impress ourselves in this way.”

**Part 2. Science, metaphor-drive, two types.** Language first constructed concepts; science takes over the labour. Science works on “this great columbarium of concepts, the graveyard of perceptions,” like a bee filling cells. The man of action binds his life to reason so as not to be swept away; the scientific investigator builds his hut next to the tower.

“The drive toward the formation of metaphors is the fundamental human drive.” Concepts are its prison, made of its own products. It escapes into *myth* and *art*, confusing categories, wanting a world “as colorful, irregular… charming, and eternally new as the world of dreams.” Art tears the web; the waking man then thinks he is dreaming. Pascal’s king/workman dream. Mythic Greeks live a waking life closer to dream (nymphs, Zeus as bull, Athena with Peisistratus).

So long as it deceives *without injuring*, the intellect is free and celebrates its Saturnalia. The free intellect smashes the framework of concepts and is guided by intuitions; there is no word for those intuitions.

**Rational man vs intuitive man.** One fears intuition; the other scorns abstraction. The latter is as irrational as the former is inartistic. Both want to rule life. In Greece the intuitive man wins: art’s mastery over life; “Olympian cloudlessness,” “a playing with seriousness.” The conceptual man only wards off misfortune. The intuitive man reaps illumination, cheer, redemption — and suffers more, falling into the same ditch. The **stoical man**, who seeks sincerity and freedom from deception, “executes a masterpiece of deception” in misfortune: a dignified mask, no cry, no change of voice; “when a real storm cloud thunders above him, he wraps himself in his cloak, and with slow steps he walks from beneath it.”

## Whole-Work Assessment

The vault’s first *unpublished* Nietzsche primary, and the missing 1873 epistemology between *The Birth of Tragedy* (1872, ingested) and *Human, All Too Human* (1878). Four things reception had flattened are now sharp.

1. **The definition is 1873, not a late slogan.** “Movable host” / worn-out coins / forgotten illusions. Later *Gay Science* and *BGE* (“untruth as a condition of life”) develop this without naming the unpublished essay.

2. **Truth’s origin is social, not metaphysical.** Peace treaty, herd designations, hate of harm rather than of deception. This is earlier than 1878 chemistry and 1887 genealogy, and is not yet either.

3. **Concept = equation of unequals.** “Leaf,” “honesty,” mammal/camel. Nature knows only an X. The thing-in-itself is “not in the least worth striving for” — already emptied, five years before HATH §16’s Homeric laughter.

4. **Part 2 is not a mere appendix.** The metaphor-drive *must* escape into myth and art. Rational vs intuitive man, and the stoic as a masterpiece of deception, belong to this essay. Kin to BT’s artist vs Socratic man; not the same duality (Apollo/Dionysus are not named).

The Argument Spine held. Correction: do not file this as “perspectivism.” The name is absent. Between subject and object there is an *aesthetic* relation, not a named perspective. Do not weld Kaufmann’s “mobile army” onto this page.
