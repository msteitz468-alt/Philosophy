# On the Genealogy of Morals — Friedrich Nietzsche (1887; Johnston, from 22 Classic Works)

> Second English primary of the 1887 polemic. The vault already has the Horace B. Samuel / Internet Archive text as [[Sources/The Genealogy of Morals - Nietzsche (1887)]]. This page is the Ian Johnston translation extracted from the 2013 *Friedrich Nietzsche Collection: 22 Classic Works* EPUB. Collection title page does not name the translator; wording matches Johnston (Vancouver Island University): "We don't know ourselves, we knowledgeable people," "PROLOGUE," "What do ascetic ideals mean?"

## Argument Spine

*Zur Genealogie der Moral* (1887) is a "polemical tract" whose real question is not the *origin* of moral feelings (Paul Rée, Darwinizing "English psychologists") but the *value* of morality — specifically the worth of the "unegoistic," pity, self-denial, self-sacrifice that Schopenhauer had deified into a No to life. Nietzsche demands a critique of moral values themselves: knowledge of the conditions under which they grew (morality as symptom, mask, illness, *and* as stimulant, poison, means of healing). The colour of that knowledge is *gray* (Johnston; Samuel: grey) — documented, hieroglyphic past — not the "blue" of English hypotheses. Cheerfulness — "the gay science" — is the reward of a long underground seriousness. The three essays take up hypotheses first sketched in *Human, All Too Human* and now "riper, brighter, stronger." Essay III is explicitly a model of *interpretation*: an aphorism is presented and the essay is the commentary. The reader must ruminate, "almost… be a cow." Prerequisite: earlier writings, especially *Zarathustra*. Dated in other editions Sils-Maria, July 1887; this extract does not print the date line.

## Whole-Work Assessment

Second English primary of a book the vault already had from Samuel (2026-06-04). The first ingest was right on Essays I–II and thin on III. This pass does three things the Samuel page could not.

(1) **A method page.** The 2026-06-04 ingest claimed [[Concepts/Genealogy (Nietzsche)]] and then retracted it. Foucault's method page had been speaking for Nietzsche. Johnston's Prologue states the method: critique of the *value* of values; **gray** documents vs blue English hypotheses; rumination; Essay III as a model of interpretation. II.12 (origin ≠ purpose; purposes are stamps of a will to power) is the anti-teleology Foucault keeps. The rank-order / "magnificence of the human type" is what Foucault drops.

(2) **Essay III is now on disk.** Science is *not* the opposing ideal but "its newest and most pre-eminent form." Last unbelievers "still believe in the truth." GS V "The Extent to Which We Also Are Still Devout" (Kaufmann §344) is cited by title, not number. Christian truthfulness kills the Christian God, then must ask what the will to truth *means*. Close: "man will sooner will nothingness than not will." The 2026-06-04 catalogue-only digest is superseded for this claim-set.

(3) **Translation layers stay distinct.** Johnston: "knowledgeable people," gray, English *resentment*, "beyond morality," "entitled to make promises," "inhuman and the superhuman," no Sils-Maria date. Samuel quotes on existing concept pages are labeled, not overwritten. **"Will to power" is not in Essay I.**

Argument Spine of Step 1 held, with one correction: colour is Johnston's **gray**, not Samuel's grey. No new contradiction page (Observation 16: Rée is a named opponent without a primary). James's existing reply on Ascetic Ideal is untouched. *Zarathustra* (Common 1909) is the collection-mate this Prologue requires; it was ingested in parallel.
