# The Mystical Theology and The Divine Names — Dionysius the Areopagite

> Two complementary treatises of late-fifth-century Syrian Christian Neoplatonism: *The Divine Names* works through the scriptural titles of God as symbols of the Super-Essential Godhead's overflow into being; *The Mystical Theology* strips those titles away, plunging the initiate into the "Divine Darkness" of Unknowing.

**Full citation**: Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, *The Mystical Theology and The Divine Names*, trans. C. E. Rolt (Society for Promoting Christian Knowledge, London / Macmillan, New York, 1920; Dover unabridged reprint, 2004). Preface and closing essay "The Influence of Dionysius in Religious History" by W. J. Sparrow-Simpson. The translator died at 37; the Introduction is his. Greek titles: *Περὶ θείων ὀνομάτων* (*De Divinis Nominibus*) and *Περὶ μυστικῆς θεολογίας* (*De Mystica Theologia*). Author unknown; writings placed near the end of the fifth century (they reflect [[Thinkers/Proclus]], who began lecturing at Athens in 430). First recorded appeal: Council of Constantinople, 533 (Severus of Antioch, Monophysite).

Two other Dionysian treatises (*Celestial Hierarchy*, *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*) are not in this volume.

## Argument Spine

The Super-Essential Godhead (*ὑπερούσιος θεαρχία*) is beyond being, mind, life, unity, and every name; yet it overflows into a world of Being and so can be named from its effects. *The Divine Names* is **cataphatic** (affirmative) theology: the scriptural titles — Good, Being, Life, Wisdom, Power, Peace, One — do not express the Godhead in itself but the Godhead as manifested, each name applying to the whole Godhead (not a part) except the Trinitarian distinctions. *The Mystical Theology* is **apophatic** (negative) theology: the same names are denied, then even the denials are denied, until Moses enters the Darkness of Unknowing and is united to the Unknowable "by a rejection of all knowledge." Affirmation and negation do not contradict because the Cause precedes both. The two treatises are one itinerary: procession into names, return through their stripping.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The two treatises give the vault the missing primary that Lovejoy, Wallis, and the Aquinas cluster already pointed to. They are one itinerary: *Divine Names* processes into the scriptural titles (Good first; Being, Life, Wisdom as one Providence under more and less universal names; Power; Time/Eternity; Peace; Perfect and One); *Mystical Theology* returns by stripping them, through Moses' Darkness of Unknowing, until even "one," "unity," "Godhead," and "Goodness" are denied. Affirmation and negation do not contradict because the Cause precedes both.

What this source *adds* rather than merely confirms: (1) the unified/distinguished name-rule (whole Godhead vs non-interchangeable Trinity and Incarnation) — Wallis's "Christian dress" is not a smear of Proclus onto a Unitarian Absolute; (2) DN IV.10's English for Lovejoy's plenitude line, plus the explicit refusal to seat evil in matter (IV.28), a correction of Plotinus *Enn.* I.8; (3) the Being > Life > Wisdom *title* order inverted for *creaturely* rank; (4) God as "both Eternity and Time" and "before/above" both (X.3), a third voice on [[Contradictions/Temporal Creation vs Eternal World]] that is neither Ghazali's first instant nor Avicenna's co-eternal world; (5) MT V's denial even of the names DN XIII had called "most important."

The Argument Spine written at Step 1 held. Two corrections of emphasis: Sparrow-Simpson's influence essay does **not** treat Eckhart, Ruysbroeck, or Cusanus (Rolt I names Ruysbroeck; the essay runs Maximus → Lateran 649 → Erigena → Damascene → Hugh → Aquinas → 17th–19th c. critics). Rolt's Bradley-Absolute reading is interpolation and is flagged as such on the Super-Essence page; Sparrow-Simpson already called it unguarded. The *Celestial* and *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* remain uningested — the sacramental/theurgic half of the corpus is still reported (Wallis) rather than primary.
