# Critique of Pure Reason — Immanuel Kant (Guyer/Wood, Cambridge)

> How are synthetic a priori judgments possible? Objects must conform to our cognition: space and time are forms of sensibility, the categories are forms of thought, and theoretical reason knows appearances, not things in themselves.

**Full citation**: Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, trans. and ed. Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood (The Cambridge Edition of the Works of Immanuel Kant, Cambridge University Press, 1998). German original: *Kritik der reinen Vernunft*, Riga, A 1781 / B 1787. Standard citations use A/B pagination printed in the margins.

This is the vault's first primary ingest of the first *Critique*. Kant is already in via two English editions of the 1785 *Groundwork* ([[Sources/Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals - Immanuel Kant (Gregor, Cambridge 1998)]], [[Sources/Metaphysics of Morals - Immanuel Kant]]). Outstanding Sources listed the Copernican turn as the missing hinge for almost every later cluster. This volume prints A and B separately wherever Kant rewrote (prefaces, introduction, Aesthetic, Deduction, Phenomena/Noumena, Paralogisms), plus Kant's handwritten emendations to his own A copy.

## Argument Spine

The *Critique* is a science of the conditions of possible experience, not a survey of objects. Mathematics, physics, and everyday nature-knowledge already contain judgments that are *synthetic* (they go beyond the subject-concept) yet *a priori* (no particular experience could underwrite their necessity). How such judgments are possible is "the general problem of pure reason" (B 19). Kant's answer is the Copernican turn (B xvi–xviii): if objects must conform to our cognition, the forms we contribute — space and time as pure intuitions, the twelve categories as concepts of an object in general — can be known a priori as conditions of any object of experience. That same answer is a limit: the categories have determinate use only on spatiotemporal appearances (*phenomena*). Things as they are in themselves (*noumena*) can be *thought* but not *known*. Reason, unconstrained by sensibility, generates ideas of soul, world-whole, and God, and then argues illicitly for their reality (Paralogisms, Antinomies, Ideal). The ideas keep a *regulative* use for inquiry, and practical reason can still *believe* what theoretical reason cannot know (freedom, God, immortality). The Doctrine of Method then contrasts mathematical and philosophical proof, and sketches the critical path between dogmatism, empiricism, and skepticism.

## Whole-Work Assessment

The first *Critique* is now primary in the vault. The Argument Spine of Step 1 held, with four corrections of emphasis. (1) The phrase "synthetic a priori" is not in the Prefaces — B xvi poses a priori determination of objects "before they are given"; the official "How are synthetic judgments a priori possible?" is B 19. (2) The Copernican experiment is B-Preface only; the Introductions do the table of judgments, not the turn. (3) The Third Antinomy shows only *non-conflict* of freedom and nature, not that we are free — the *Groundwork* still has to do that work. (4) The Canon prepares practical philosophy (three questions; highest good; "I am morally certain") without stating any CI formula.

What this source changes: [[Contradictions/Rationalism vs Empiricism on Innate Ideas]] is no longer "left open pending CPR"; the Copernican synthesis is now quoted. [[Arguments/Ontological Argument]] and [[Contradictions/Temporal Creation vs Eternal World]] stop citing Kant second-hand. [[Contradictions/Hegelian Monism vs. Kantian Dualism]] and [[Contradictions/Free Will - Spinozan Necessity vs Kantian Autonomy]] now rest on phenomena/noumena and the Third Antinomy, not only on the *Groundwork*'s two standpoints. Hume's Fork, Quine's analytic/synthetic attack, and Popper's "Kant's problem" all have the official wordings they were aimed at. The 1797 *Metaphysics of Morals* and the second and third *Critiques* remain the next Kant holes; *Prolegomena* is the shorter companion still on the outstanding list.
