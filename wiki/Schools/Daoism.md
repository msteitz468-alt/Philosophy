# Daoism

> The classical Chinese tradition whose earliest surviving independently circulating text is the *Daodejing*. This page records the **philosophical** position of that corpus (Ziporyn 2023), not later religious Daoism (immortality, liturgy, the Celestial Masters). The *Zhuangzi* — Outstanding Sources' remaining Daoist hole — is not yet in the vault.

## Summary

"Daoism" is a later label. Ziporyn treats the *Daodejing* as a curated commonplace book of an ancient genre ("Classic Passages on the Course and Virtuosity from the Old Masters"), not the manifesto of a founder. What the 81 chapters share is a tone: no proper nouns, sudden reversals, recycled pericopes, and a consistent **A/B inversion** — promote the ignored background (soft, low, empty, nameless, nondoing) over the valued foreground (hard, high, full, named, purposive).

The school's load-bearing claims, as this vault now has them:

- **Course (*dao*)**: not a how-to for a predetermined end, but nonpurposive process that "conforms to how everything is when left to itself" (ch. 25). See [[Concepts/Dao - The Course (Laozi)]].
- **Virtuosity (*de*)**: not skill acquired by practice, but unlearned potency; highest *de* is not grasped as *de* and does nothing without motives (ch. 38). See [[Concepts/De - Virtuosity and Potency (Laozi)]].
- **Nondoing (*wuwei*)**: subtraction until "a nondoing that leaves nothing undone" (ch. 48); assist the self-so, do not carve (ch. 64). See [[Concepts/Wu Wei - Nondoing (Laozi)]].
- **Unhewn (*pu*)**: the nameless rawness that names and ritual shatter into tools; the real constancy is reversion to the root (chs. 16, 19, 28, 32). See [[Concepts/Unhewn Background and the A-B Reversal (Laozi)]].
- **Water / the low**: benefit by never competing; reign by getting below; soft overcomes hard (chs. 8, 66, 76, 78). See [[Concepts/Water, Softness, and the Low Place (Laozi)]].

## Politics inside the school

The same chapters support two later readings Ziporyn flags and does not close:

- **Quietist / near-anarchist**: highest rulers unknown to exist (ch. 17); people transform themselves (ch. 57); small state, unused weapons, neighbors heard but never visited (ch. 80).
- **Authoritarian / proto-Legalist**: empty hearts and fill bellies (ch. 3); "not to enlighten the people but to befuddle them"; using wisdom to govern is to "pillage" the state (ch. 65).

The vault records both. Do not flatten the school into "go with the flow."

## What this page is not

- **Not religious Daoism.** Alchemy, hygiene, sexual yoga, and a personal deity named Dao are receptions Ziporyn lists; they are not argued from this source.
- **Not Confucianism's inner twin.** Chs. 18–19 and 38 treat humankindness, duty, ritual, and sagacity as *symptoms of the course's loss*. The Confucian reply is now primary: [[Thinkers/Confucius]], [[Schools/Confucianism]], [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- **Not Sun Tzu.** Shared water and formlessness do not make the *Art of War* a Daoist treatise. Sun Tzu's *tao* is morale/legitimacy, a calculable factor of victory.

## Relation to other vault schools

- **Vs. [[Schools/Pythagoreanism]] / [[Schools/Neoplatonism]]**: those build a hierarchy toward a One or a number-cosmos. Daoism's highest is not a term in a hierarchy; it is the ignored background of every hierarchy.
- **Vs. Stoicism** (no dedicated school page; see [[Thinkers/Epictetus]], [[Thinkers/Marcus Aurelius]]): Stoic equanimity accepts a providential order and plays an assigned role. Daoist nondoing does not assent to impressions or serve Zeus; it subtracts purposive intervention so things revert to the unhewn.
- **Vs. [[Schools/Existentialism]]**: Sartre's existence-precedes-essence makes the agent a legislator. Laozi's sage has "no intentions of his own" (ch. 49) and helps each thing be of its own accord.
- **Vs. Pyrrhonism** ([[Thinkers/Sextus Empiricus]]): both undercut determination. Skepticism suspends; Daoism *inhabits* the B-pole.

## Open

The *Zhuangzi* is the other classical Daoist primary listed in Outstanding Sources. Until it is ingested, this school page is *Daodejing*-shaped: more political and gnomic than the later inner-chapter irony of Zhuang Zhou.
