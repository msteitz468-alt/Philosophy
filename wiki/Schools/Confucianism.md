# Confucianism

> The tradition that takes the *Analects* as its root text. This page records the **Analects-shaped** position in Annping Chin's 2014 translation — not Mencius, not Xunzi, not Zhu Xi's state commentary, and not the later Four Books as a closed canon. Those are reception Chin reports; they are not vault primaries.

## Summary

"Confucianism" is a later Western label. Confucius was a *ru* (6.13) — a ritual specialist and professional man with textual knowledge — who taught disciples to become gentlemen fit for office. After his death, competitive interpretations kept the name alive; Mencius and Xunzi "took his teachings in different directions"; Han patronage embedded the assumptions in bureaucracy, law, education, and ritual; a long Buddhist interval (Six Dynasties through Tang) was answered by Song revival; Yuan restricted the civil-service examinations to four Confucian books, of which the *Analects* was one.

What this vault now has from the root text:

- **Humaneness (*ren*)**: beginning and end; restrain the self and return to the rites (12.1). See [[Concepts/Ren - Humaneness (Confucius)]].
- **Rites (*li*)**: harmony is prized, rules still bind (1.12); *Odes* stimulate, rites steady, music completes (8.8). See [[Concepts/Li - Ritual Propriety (Confucius)]].
- **Gentleman (*junzi*)**: moral worth, not (only) birth; not a vessel (2.12). See [[Concepts/Junzi - The Gentleman (Confucius)]].
- **Filiality (*xiao*)**: root of *ren* (1.2); three-year mourning (17.21). See [[Concepts/Xiao - Filiality (Confucius)]].
- **Rectifying names**: first task of government (13.3). See [[Concepts/Zhengming - Rectifying Names (Confucius)]].
- **Reciprocity (*shu*)**: the one-word lifelong guide (15.24). See [[Concepts/Shu - Reciprocity (Confucius)]].

## Politics inside the school

- **Virtue-rule**: North Star (2.1); *de* + *li* produce shame and self-reform, statutes + punishment produce evasion (2.3). Shun "order[ed] the world without taking any [deliberate] action" by holding himself respectfully and facing south (15.5).
- **Trust first**: food, army, people's trust — under distress drop army then food; without trust the state dies (12.7). Livelihood then instruction (13.9).
- **No-kill as ideal, long view as practice**: the head of government need not kill (12.19, wind/grass); "only after good men have been in government for a hundred years" is there even the possibility of doing away with capital punishment (13.11).
- **Remonstrance, not echo**: counselors *he* (harmonize), not *tong* (echo) (13.23). Near-demise: no one goes against the ruler's words when those words are not good (13.15). Advisor resigns if he cannot steer the lord (16.1).
- **Stay in office knowing the Way cannot be realized** (18.7). Not quietist; not revolutionary.

## Splits the *Analects* already records

- **Youzi vs Zengzi** (Book 1, Chin): familial rites → social harmony vs introspection → self-realization. Mencius: after the Master's death some would serve Youzi as they had served Confucius; Zengzi refused.
- **Book 19**: Zixia (associate with the acceptable, reject the rest; shop of details) vs Zizhang (respect the worthy, charity to the common lot; "grand presence" Zengzi cannot practice *ren* beside) vs Ziyou (mourning stops when grief is full) vs Zigong (apotheosis of the Master). Hanfeizi, Chin notes, already counted ten Confucian ways.
- **Books 16 and 20**: Chin flags possible Warring States / early Han interpolation (lists, "Master Kong" voice, Yao–Shun "hold to the middle"). Do not rest doctrine on 20.1–20.2.

## What this page is not

- **Not Mencius or Xunzi.** Chin: they cover "much ground… from the self to society and nature" that Confucius did not. 17.2 is about *practice*, not "nature is good."
- **Not Zhu Xi as default.** Chin follows Qing evidential research (Liu Baonan) against five hundred years of state-Zhu reading.
- **Not [[Schools/Daoism]]'s inner twin.** Chs. 18–19 and 38 of the *Daodejing* treat humankindness, duty, ritual, and sagacity as decay-markers. The *Analects* treats them as the work. See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- **Not Sun Tzu.** Shared *shi* world and a preference against wasteful slaughter do not make the *Art of War* a Confucian treatise.

## Relation to other vault schools

- **Vs. [[Schools/Daoism]]**: opposite valuation of *ren* / *li* / *xiao*. Shared *wuwei* word (15.5 vs Ziporyn chs. 2, 37, 48) with opposed content.
- **Vs. Aristotelian ethics** (no dedicated school page; see [[Thinkers/Aristotle]]): both habituate; Aristotle ranks *theōria* first; Confucius ranks office and relationships and will not theorize nature or Heaven's way (5.13).
- **Vs. Stoicism** ([[Thinkers/Epictetus]], [[Thinkers/Marcus Aurelius]]): Stoic role-acceptance inside a providential order. Confucian office is a test of *ren* one may resign; Heaven "does not speak" (17.19).
- **Vs. Hadot's Greco-Roman *philosophia***: both are ways of life, not systems. Hadot's schools are Greek; this one is Zhou *ru* teaching aimed at government. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]].

## Open

Mencius, Xunzi, and the *Zhongyong* / *Daxue* remain outstanding. Until they are ingested, this school page is *Analects*-shaped: more political and relational than Song metaphysics, more fragmentary than a treatise.
