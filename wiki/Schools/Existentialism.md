# Existentialism
> The 20th-century family of philosophies that begin from the subjective and hold that, for the human being, existence precedes essence — that man first exists and only then makes himself.

## Summary

The first dedicated school page for a label the vault has used in frontmatter since the 2026-06-29 Welten ingest. The definition used here is Sartre's own, from the 1945/46 lecture that tried to rescue the word from fashion ("this musician or that painter is 'existentialist'") and from three public reproaches (Communist quietism, Catholic gloom, Christian arbitrariness).

Sartre says the question is complicated only because there are **two kinds of existentialists**. On the Christian side he names Karl Jaspers and Gabriel Marcel, both professed Catholics. On the atheistic side he places [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]] as well as the French existentialists and himself. "What they have in common is simply the fact that they believe that *existence* comes before *essence* — or, if you will, that we must begin from the subjective." That is the whole shared doctrine. Everything else in the lecture — anguish, abandonment, despair, condemned freedom, invented values, inter-subjectivity, the two humanisms — is what *atheistic* existentialism adds when it draws the consequences of God's absence "right to the end."

The lecture is also a *defence*: existentialism is "of all teachings the least scandalous and the most austere," intended "strictly for technicians and philosophers," a doctrine that "does render human life possible" and affirms that every truth and every action imply both an environment and a human subjectivity. What scandalises is not pessimism but the optimism of choice.

## Key Claims / Positions

### How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]** (this source): existence precedes essence; no human nature because no God to conceive it; man is what he makes of himself; choosing for oneself chooses for all; anguish / abandonment / despair; condemned to be free; *cogito* that already includes the Other; existential humanism as self-surpassing, not the cult of Man. See [[Concepts/Existence Precedes Essence (Sartre)]], [[Concepts/Anguish, Abandonment and Despair (Sartre)]], [[Concepts/Existential Humanism (Sartre)]].
- **Christian existentialists (Jaspers, Marcel)** — named, not quoted. They share the existence-before-essence thesis while retaining God. The lecture's abandonment argument (no *a priori* values once God is gone) does not bind them.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: Sartre enrols him among the atheistic existentialists and borrows "abandonment" and "human reality" (*Dasein*). Heidegger's 1947 *Letter on Humanism* (*Über den Humanismus*) is already listed among his works and is the famous refusal of this enrolment — ontology of Being, not a philosophy of the human subject. The vault does not yet have that letter as a source; the tension is flagged, not resolved.
- **[[Thinkers/Søren Kierkegaard]]**: now a vault primary (*Fear and Trembling*, 1843). Not placed in Sartre's two-column taxonomy. Johannes *de silentio*'s Abraham is not Sartre's legislator: he already believes God exists; the trial is getting Isaac back for this life while drawing the knife; he does nothing for the universal. Sartre took the anguish and dropped the theological frame. See [[Concepts/Teleological Suspension of the Ethical (Kierkegaard)]], [[Concepts/The Single Individual (Kierkegaard)]]. [[Concepts/Anxiety (Angst) (Heidegger)]] still treats Kierkegaard as ancestor; that formula is from *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844), still outstanding.
- **[[Schools/Phenomenology]]**: overlapping movement, not identical. Sartre's existentialism is an existential phenomenology (consciousness as *néant*, radical freedom) that he later pushes toward dialectic. Husserl is not an existentialist on Sartre's definition; Heidegger is claimed for both.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Sartre's roster is contested from inside. Heidegger refused the "existentialist" and "humanist" labels (*Letter on Humanism*, 1947). The school page records Sartre's claim; it does not settle Heidegger's.
- > [!warning] Welten reads Sartre as an *anti-humanist* who refuses any base set of human values. The lecture claims the name "humanism" for self-surpassing. See [[Concepts/Existential Humanism (Sartre)]].
- The vault still lacks primary Jaspers, Marcel, de Beauvoir, and Camus. Kierkegaard is no longer missing; this page is no longer only Sartre's map.
