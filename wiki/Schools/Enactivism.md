# Enactivism
> The research program named in *The Embodied Mind* (1991): cognition is embodied action that enacts a perceiver-dependent world, not recovery of a pregiven world by a pregiven mind.

## Summary

[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]], [[Thinkers/Evan Thompson]], and [[Thinkers/Eleanor Rosch]] propose the term **enactive** in the 1991 introduction as the third stage of cognitive science, after **cognitivism** (symbols) and **emergence** (connectionism). The chapter title is "Enaction: Embodied Cognition." The two-point definition (ch. 8): "(1) perception consists in perceptually guided action and (2) cognitive structures emerge from the recurrent sensorimotor patterns that enable action to be perceptually guided."

The school sits at the vault's missing joint between [[Schools/Phenomenology]] (Merleau-Ponty's double embodiment; Heideggerian background) and the AI & Cognitive Science leg. It is not Dreyfus's critique of GOFAI under another name: it offers a positive biological and evolutionary mechanism ([[Concepts/Natural Drift (Varela)|natural drift]], operational closure, structural coupling) and a first-person method ([[Concepts/Mindfulness-Awareness as Method (Varela)|mindfulness/awareness]]). It is not Clark's later 4E umbrella: the 1991 book never says "4E," and it closes with Madhyamaka [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)|groundlessness]], which Clark does not.

Thompson 2016 reports that "the embodied cognition approach is now central to cognitive science" and that enaction is usually read as one version of that paradigm. He also warns that later "grounded cognition" and internalist mindfulness-in-the-scanner work can drop the reflexive claim: science itself is enacted sense-making, not a view of things-in-themselves.

## Key Claims / Positions

- Cognition is not representation of a pregiven world.
- The living/autonomous system has operational closure; coupling *modulates* self-organizing activity and enacts a domain of significance.
- Color is the paradigm of a domain "neither pregiven nor represented but rather experiential and enacted."
- Evolution is natural drift (viability, bricolage), the homologue of enaction, not optimal adaptation (the homologue of representationism).
- Experience is not an epiphenomenal leftover; it needs a disciplined method.
- Objectivism and nihilism are one grasping. The school is a middle way, not a new foundation.

## How This Relates to Other Schools and Thinkers

- **[[Schools/Phenomenology]]**: named ancestor (Merleau-Ponty). 1991 verdict that even Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty remain "purely theoretical" is Thompson's 2016 recantation. Rosch 2016: their engaged agent is still *samsara*.
- **[[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]]**: Dreyfus is the Heideggerian gadfly; enactivism is the constructive sequel that Dreyfus only partly envisaged (connectionism + lived body). He reviewed this book and rejected its introspectionist phenomenology.
- **[[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)]]**: 1998 paper lists Varela/Thompson/Rosch among philosophical kin. Extension of *belief* into notebooks is a different thesis from enactment of a *world*.
- **[[Schools/Daoism]]**: Saraha's epigraph ("Those who believe in substantiality are like cows; those who believe in emptiness are worse") rhymes with Ziporyn's refusal of both occupation of B and clinging to A. The book's middle way is Madhyamaka, not Daoist. Do not collapse them.
- **Buddhist Abhidharma and Madhyamaka**: partners in the dialogue, not the name of the school. Thompson 2016 refuses "Buddhist cognitive science."
