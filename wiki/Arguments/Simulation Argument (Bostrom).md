# Simulation Argument (Bostrom)

> At least one of three propositions is true: almost no civilization reaches a posthuman stage; almost no posthumans run ancestor-simulations; or we almost certainly live in a simulation. A disjunction, not a proof that we are sims.

## Summary

The canonical statement is Nick Bostrom, "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?" (*Philosophical Quarterly* 53, 2003) — now ingested as [[Sources/Are You Living in a Computer Simulation - Nick Bostrom (2003)]]. Earlier vault pages rendered the argument via Virk (popular, garbled on the probability) and Chalmers (rigorous reconstruction). Those remain reception; this page is now keyed to the paper.

**The result.** Let *f_P* be the fraction of human-level civilizations that survive to a posthuman stage; *f_I* the fraction of posthuman civilizations interested in running ancestor-simulations (or containing individuals who are, and who have the resources); *N_I* the average number of such simulations those interested civilizations run. The fraction of observers with human-type experiences who live in simulations is

*f_sim* = (*f_P* *f_I* *N_I*) / (*f_P* *f_I* *N_I* + 1).

Because posthuman compute makes *N_I* extremely large (a planetary-mass computer can run the mental history of humankind in a millionth of a second of a millionth of its power), at least one of the following must be true:

1. *f_P* ≈ 0 — almost no civilization reaches posthumanity.
2. *f_I* ≈ 0 — almost no posthuman civilization runs a significant number of ancestor-simulations.
3. *f_sim* ≈ 1 — almost all such observers are simulated.

**The further step.** Conditional on (3), the [[Concepts/Bland Indifference Principle (Bostrom)|bland indifference principle]] says one's credence that *one* is simulated should be close to 1. Hence the unofficial slogan "we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation" is the *reading of horn (3)*, not the paper's conclusion.

**The official corollary** (abstract and last sentence): "the belief that there is a significant chance that we will one day become posthumans who run ancestor-simulations is false, unless we are currently living in a simulation." Equivalently: "Unless we are now living in a simulation, our descendants will almost certainly never run an ancestor-simulation."

**Recommended credence.** Bostrom does not pick a horn. "In the dark forest of our current ignorance, it seems sensible to apportion one's credence roughly evenly between (1), (2), and (3)."

## What the Argument Needs

- **[[Concepts/Substrate-Independence (Bostrom)]]**, in the attenuated "in fact" form: synaptic-level structural replication of a brain would generate subjective experience. Not strong functionalism; not a Turing-test criterion. If this fails, simulated beings are not observers and *f_sim* is not a ratio of minds.
- **Compute enough for huge *N_I*.** Conservative, already-known nanotech designs suffice; orders-of-magnitude error in the 10³³–10³⁶-operation ancestor-simulation estimate would not change the logic.
- **Bland indifference**, not Doomsday and not unrestricted Laplacean indifference.

An **ancestor-simulation** is a detailed simulation of a civilization's evolutionary history "or of people like their forebears." Full-universe quantum simulation is not required. Only what simulated humans would notice need be rendered; microscopic structure can be filled in when someone looks; the director can edit brains or rewind. (This is the paper's own statement of what Virk later popularizes as [[Concepts/Conditional Rendering and Quantum Indeterminacy (Virk)|conditional rendering]].)

## Reading the Three Horns (Bostrom §VI)

- **(1) Doom.** High credence that humankind goes extinct before posthumanity, unless we have evidence that we are specially lucky or unlucky. Compatible with a long plateau at roughly current technology, or with collapse back to primitive societies. "Most natural" reading: a powerful dangerous technology — molecular nanotechnology / grey goo is the named example. This is the same mass *Superintelligence* later treats as the default without a solved [[Concepts/AI Control Problem (Bostrom)|control problem]].
- **(2) Convergence.** Virtually no posthuman society contains relatively wealthy independent agents who have human-like desires *and* the freedom to act on them. Candidate mechanisms: an ethical prohibition plus reliable enforcement (Bostrom notes we do not now regard creating a human race as immoral), or posthumans losing the desire (scientific value negligible; pleasure cheaper by stimulating reward centers).
- **(3) Simulation.** The observed cosmos is a tiny piece of physical existence; basement physics may differ. "While the world we see is in some sense 'real', it is not located at the fundamental level of reality" — Bostrom's own no-illusion clause, later expanded as [[Concepts/Simulation Realism (Chalmers)]]. Nested simulations (stacked virtual machines) are possible; basement compute cost counts against many levels and predicts termination as we approach posthumanity. Simulators are like gods relative to inhabitants (creator, superior intelligence, locally omnipotent/omniscient); this yields a **naturalistic theogony**, a possible afterlife, and a "universal ethical imperative … 'from nowhere'" — later developed as [[Concepts/Simulation Theology (Chalmers)]]. Practical advice: do not "go crazy"; empirical study of the universe we see remains the guide.

## Chalmers's Refinement (*Reality+*, ch. 5)

Chalmers follows **Hans Moravec** ("Pigs in Cyberspace," 1992) as originator and quotes Bostrom's trilemma exactly. His changes:

- Rejects the focus on **ancestor** simulations ("I don't see good reason to believe that there will be exact ancestor simulations"), broadening to **humanlike sims**.
- Reorganizes objections into **sim blockers** ("it will never happen") vs. **sim/nonsim signs** ("we're special").
- Preferred form: "1. If there are no sim blockers, most humanlike beings are sims. 2. If most humanlike beings are sims, we are probably sims [by an indifference principle]. 3. So: If there are no sim blockers, we are probably sims." Bostrom's trilemma omits at least five further blockers, yielding an eight-way conclusion.
- Numerical upshot: "the chance that we are sims is at least 25 percent or so" — a floor, not Bostrom's even three-way split.
- Anti-skeptical payoff: this probability defeats simplicity- and Moore-style refutations ([[Arguments/Cartesian Skepticism and the Simulation Riposte (Chalmers)]]).

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Nick Bostrom]]**: officially neutral among the three horns; even split in current ignorance. Not committed to horn (3), and not committed to Virk's "NPC version."
- **[[Thinkers/Rizwan Virk]]**: accepts the statistics, reads Bostrom as an NPC theorist, and substitutes the RPG version — conscious players, simulated avatars ([[Concepts/Simulation Hypothesis (Virk)]]). The NPC reading is Virk's, not the paper's. His popular "this will be more than 1!" garbles *A*/(*A*+*B*).
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: keeps the statistical skeleton, drops exact ancestors, adds blockers, quotes ≥25%, and uses the live probability to block skeptical *refutations* while denying that simulation would undermine ordinary reality.
- **Elon Musk**: popularized a horn-(3) slogan ("one in billions" that we're in base reality) from game-technology extrapolation ([[Concepts/Road to the Simulation Point (Virk)]]).
- **Robin Hanson**, "How to Live in a Simulation" (*JET* 2001): cited by Bostrom as sparked by a privately circulated draft.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Horn (1) is the existential-catastrophe scenario of [[Sources/Superintelligence - Nick Bostrom (2014)]]. Raising credence in doom lowers credence in (3), and conversely: the two arguments trade probability mass.
- > [!warning] Whether simulated beings can be conscious is exactly [[Contradictions/Hofstadter vs Searle - Strange Loops vs Chinese Room]] and [[Concepts/Biological Naturalism (Searle)]]. The argument assumes [[Concepts/Substrate-Independence (Bostrom)]].
- The indifference step is contested in anthropic reasoning generally; Bostrom distinguishes it from Doomsday ([[Concepts/Bland Indifference Principle (Bostrom)]]).
- Bostrom hopes (3) is true because it decreases (1) — unless simulators would terminate a run before posthumanity, in which case (2) is the better hope.
