# Putnam Brain in a Vat Argument
> From causal constraints on reference: a lifelong computer-envatted brain cannot think about brains or vats, so the thought "I am a BIV" cannot be truly thought. Semantic, not a proof that one knows one is not a BIV.

This page is written from Pritchard and Ranalli's 2016 reconstruction (the reconstruction Putnam 1994 said was clearer than his own) plus Chalmers's later use. It is **not** an ingest of Putnam 1981.

## The Argument (as reconstructed)

**The skeptical target.** BIV skepticism: one cannot know one is not a [[Concepts/Brain in a Vat|BIV]]; everyday propositions are known to entail that one is not; by closure, one cannot know the everyday propositions. Putnam does not deny closure and does not claim anti-skeptical *knowledge*. He claims one cannot **truly think** the thought that one is a BIV. The two claims "are logically distinct, in both directions."

**The semantic engine.** [[Concepts/Semantic Externalism (Putnam)|Content externalism]]: without the right causal (and communal) relations, one cannot think thoughts about trees — or about brains, vats, and computers. A lifelong BIV stimulated by supercomputers lacks those relations. So:

- If one is not a BIV, "I am a BIV" is false.
- If one *is* a BIV, one cannot think *that* thought; one thinks a different thought (about a BIV-in-the-image), which is also false.

Either way the thought is false, and this is supposed to be a priori. Putnam (1981b: 16) calls the investigation **transcendental**: preconditions of reference, "not (as Kant hoped) wholly independent of empirical assumptions."

**Scope restriction.** Putnam is "quite explicit": the brain has **always** been envatted; stimulation is (and always has been) by **supercomputers**. Recent envattment leaves prior causal connections to trees intact. The result "will not apply to all forms of BIV skepticism."

### Wright's first-person display (the version Putnam endorsed)

1. In my language, "BIV" is meaningful.
2. My language is disquotational, so "BIV" refers to BIVs.
3. In the BIV's language, "BIV" does not refer to BIVs. So my language is not the BIV's.
4. If I am a BIV, my language is the BIV's.
5. Therefore I am not a BIV.

### The transcendental display

- (T1) A necessary condition of thinking that I'm not a BIV is that I'm not a BIV.
- (T2) I can think that I'm not a BIV.
- (TC) I'm not a BIV.

## The Four Lines of Attack (this source)

**Brueckner (1986): question-begging.** His longer reconstruction yields only: my utterances of "I'm a BIV" are false. To get *I know I am not a BIV* one needs disquotational truth-conditions for "I'm not a BIV." Vat-English is not disquotational. Assuming disquotation assumes one speaks English, which is what was to be shown.

**Wright (1992): third person and self-knowledge of content.** Disquotation is the *less* problematic assumption. The third-person version (prove a priori that *Putnam* is not a BIV) is "altogether incredible." Homophony does not yield sameness of meaning. The assessor needs identifying knowledge of content that [[Concepts/Semantic Externalism (Putnam)|externalism itself]] "proscribes."

**Brueckner (2010) on the transcendental reading: epistemic circularity.** The sentence "I'm not a BIV" is true in both languages, of different propositions. If I do not yet know which world I am in, I do not know which contents are mine. Knowing the self-knowledge premise requires knowing the conclusion.

**Stroud (1968, 2000): the general dilemma.** Hedge the mind-to-world thesis to a mind-to-mind thesis (it is enough that I not *seem* to be a BIV); to recross the "bridge of necessity" invoke idealism or verificationism; those doctrines already block skepticism, so the transcendental argument is superfluous. Pritchard and Ranalli: content externalism can cross the bridge without idealism (world-dependent content, mind-independent world). Residual worry about a local collapse into verificationism for the special sentence "I'm not a BIV" is noted and declined.

**Nagel (1986): reductio and remainder.** See [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]]. If the argument works it refutes the theory; if it works only here, ineffable hypotheses remain; and the leftover thought is still skepticism.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the demon is rewritten as a vat; Putnam's rescue is semantic, not theological.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: the "transcendental" label is Putnam's; he refuses independence from empirical assumptions.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: lists Putnam 1981 as a no-illusion predecessor he rejects (strong externalism). The Simulation Riposte: the argument, if it works, hits "brain in a vat," not "computer simulation." Structuralism, not externalism, drives [[Concepts/Simulation Realism (Chalmers)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Robert Nozick]]**: named only as a closure-denier facing the *epistemological* BIV argument Putnam recasts.
- **Davidson** (flagged, not developed): another externalist transcendental anti-skepticism.
