# Three Problemata (Kierkegaard)
> If the ethical is the universal, Abraham is condemned. Hegelians praise Abraham. Therefore the ethical is not the universal — or they must stop talking about faith.

## Summary

Johannes *de silentio* extracts from Genesis 22 "its dialectical element, in the form of *problemata*, in order to see how monstrous a paradox faith is, a paradox capable of making a murder into a holy act well pleasing to God, a paradox which gives Isaac back to Abraham, which no thought can grasp because faith begins precisely where thinking leaves off."

Each *problema* opens the same way. The ethical is defined as the universal. A consequence is drawn. The consequence is also an implication of Hegel's account of ethics. If Hegel is right, Hegelians have no right to talk of faith or to credit Abraham as its father.

Hannay names the form *modus tollens*: if A then B; not-B; therefore not-A. A is the Hegelian definition (ethical life as identification of the individual with the totality of social life). The three B's:

| Problema | Question | Consequence of "ethical = universal" | How Abraham infringes |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Is there a teleological suspension of the ethical? | Moral performance is judged by underlying social intention | He acts as though there were a superior measure that makes social intention irrelevant |
| II | Is there an absolute duty to God? | No duties to God other than duties that are first duties to the universal | He supposes an absolute duty that overrides the ethical |
| III | Was it ethically defensible to conceal his purpose from Sarah, Eleazar, Isaac? | One must not conceal moral projects or reasons for failing them | He cannot reveal the intention; speech would translate him back into the universal |

In each case the refrain: **or else faith has never existed because it has existed always; or else Abraham is done for.**

The argument does not prove the Christian concept of faith. Johannes does not have that concept (Hannay: it is reserved for later pseudonyms, especially *Postscript*). What it proves, minimally, is that *if* you are a Hegelian you cannot talk glibly of having fathomed faith and gone further. Johannes himself still writes as if the ethical-as-universal were the only going principle. He uses it to measure the distance between his sense of Abraham's greatness and his ability to understand him.

## Key Moves

- **Not a theodicy and not a divine-command theory as usually sold.** The faith being tested is not "God exists" and not "obey any command." It is the double movement: resign Isaac and believe him back for this life. See [[Concepts/Knight of Faith and Infinite Resignation (Kierkegaard)]].
- **Tragic heroes are the control group.** Agamemnon, Jephthah, Brutus satisfy A and are intelligible. Abraham fails A and is either murderer or paradox.
- **Do not judge by the outcome.** The ram is not the justification. If Isaac had died, Abraham would not be less justified.
- **Luke 14.26** is enlisted in II as a New Testament parallel that tasteful exegesis has defanged.
- **The merman** in III is the intelligible use of the paradox (guilt → back to the universal on another basis). Abraham is the unintelligible use (already in the universal, he puts the capacity into God's hands).

## How This Sits in the Vault

- Against [[Thinkers/Hegel]]: the first dedicated ethical attack on *Sittlichkeit*. The vault has Hegel on Geist, dialectic, history, and (via Berlin) positive liberty; it does not yet have the *Philosophy of Right*. Johannes is targeting that book. See [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Hegel - Faith vs Sittlichkeit]].
- Against [[Thinkers/Kant]]: same hoop, different metal. Universal law / humanity-as-end cannot underwrite a private command to kill. See [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Kant - Absolute Duty vs Universal Law]].
- Against Sartre's public Abraham: the lecture uses the story as undecidable authority for a legislator. The *problemata* use it to show that Abraham is precisely *not* a legislator.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Whether "not-B" is available: i.e. whether one *must* keep Abraham as father of faith. Johannes's readers are assumed to want to. A reader willing to call Abraham a murderer is outside the argument's grip.
- Hannay thinks Kierkegaard envisages an alternative ethical paradigm that Johannes only outlines by collision. That paradigm is not in this book.
