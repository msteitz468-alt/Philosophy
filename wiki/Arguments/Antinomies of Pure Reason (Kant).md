# Antinomies of Pure Reason (Kant)

> Reason can prove both sides of four cosmological questions with equal necessity — if appearances are taken as things in themselves. Transcendental idealism is the key: the world-whole is not a given object.

## The Four Conflicts

| | Thesis | Antithesis |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Magnitude** | "The world has a beginning in time, and in space it is also enclosed in boundaries." | "The world has no beginning and no bounds in space, but is infinite with regard to both time and space." (A 426–427 / B 454–455) |
| **2. Division** | "Every composite substance in the world consists of simple parts…" | "No composite thing in the world consists of simple parts, and nowhere in it does there exist anything simple." (A 434–435 / B 462–463) |
| **3. Causality** | Besides natural causality it is "necessary to assume another causality through freedom." | "There is no freedom, but everything in the world happens solely in accordance with laws of nature." (A 444–445 / B 472–473) |
| **4. Necessity** | "To the world there belongs something that, either as a part of it or as its cause, is an absolutely necessary being." | "There is no absolutely necessary being existing anywhere, either in the world or outside the world as its cause." (A 452–453 / B 480–481) |

Each side "is not only without contradiction in itself but even meets with conditions of its necessity in the nature of reason itself, only unfortunately the opposite has on its side equally valid and necessary grounds" (A 421 / B 449). The proofs are well grounded *if* appearances are things in themselves (A 507 / B 535).

**Mathematical (1–2) vs dynamical (3–4).** 1–2 demand a homogeneous synthesis of magnitude; every condition is itself an appearance and a member of the series. 3–4 allow a heterogeneous, merely intelligible condition *outside* the series (A 529–532 / B 557–560).

## The Key and the Resolutions

**Transcendental idealism as the key.** Everything intuited in space/time is appearance, "mere representations, which… have outside our thoughts no existence grounded in itself" (A 490–491 / B 518–519). The world-whole is not a given object; it exists only in the empirical regress (A 504–505 / B 532–533). "Because the world does not exist at all (independently of the regressive series of my representations), it exists neither as an in itself infinite whole nor as an in itself finite whole" (A 505 / B 533).

**1 and 2: both false** (dialectical opposition, not analytical contradiction). The world is neither an in-itself finite nor infinite whole. Regress of the world's magnitude is *in indefinitum*; of a given whole's division, *in infinitum* — without the world's consisting of infinitely many parts.

**3: both may be true.** The same action can be empirically necessary in appearance and free in its intelligible character. "Freedom and nature, each in its full significance, would both be found in the same actions, simultaneously and without any contradiction" (A 541 / B 569). "By freedom in the cosmological sense… I understand the faculty of beginning a state from itself, the causality of which does not in turn stand under another cause determining it in time" (A 533 / B 561). Only the *possibility* of non-conflict is shown, not the reality of freedom: "nature at least does not conflict with causality through freedom — that was the one single thing we could accomplish" (A 558 / B 586).

**4: necessary being not in the series.** All members of the sensible series remain empirically contingent; an unconditionally necessary being, if thought at all, is *ens extramundanum*, "entirely outside the series" (A 560–561 / B 588–589). Not a proof of such a being.

The **regulative principle**: "a rule, prescribing a regress… in which regress it is never allowed to stop with an absolutely unconditioned" (A 508–509 / B 536–537).

## How This Lands in the Vault

- **First Antinomy** is the mature form of [[Contradictions/Temporal Creation vs Eternal World]] (al-Ghazali vs Ibn Rushd / Maimonides). Both sides can be "proved"; the question outruns possible experience.
- **Third Antinomy** is the theoretical warrant for [[Contradictions/Free Will - Spinozan Necessity vs Kantian Autonomy]] and the *Groundwork*'s [[Concepts/Two Standpoints: Sensible World and Intelligible World (Kant)|two standpoints]] / [[Concepts/Freedom as the Key to Autonomy (Kant)|freedom as key]].
- **Fourth Antinomy** hands off to the [[Concepts/Existence is Not a Real Predicate (Kant)|Ideal]]: speculative theology cannot place a necessary being *in* the world-series.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The Third Antinomy shows only *non-conflict*, not that we *are* free. The *Groundwork* and later practical philosophy must still do that work — and Kant himself later treats freedom as a "fact of reason" rather than a deduction from this chapter.
- Hegel will refuse the "both false / both may be true" pattern and treat the antinomy as the motor of a single developing whole.
