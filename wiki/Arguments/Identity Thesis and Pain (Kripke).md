# Identity Thesis and Pain (Kripke)

> If pain = C-fiber stimulation, the identity is necessary. The heat / molecular-motion analogy fails, because pain is picked out by "being pain itself, by its immediate phenomenological quality," not by an accidental property. God, having made the C-fiber stimulation, "must do something in addition" to make it a pain.

## Summary

Lecture III of [[Sources/Naming and Necessity - Saul Kripke (1980)]] turns the apparatus of rigid designation and the necessary a posteriori against the mind–body **identity thesis**. The target is not only type-type identity (pain = C-fiber stimulation) but also particular sensation / brain-state identities. Armstrong and Lewis's **causal-role** / topic-neutral analyses are named (n. 75); Smart and Place are not.

**Identity is not a relation between names.** Kripke introduces **schmidentity**, stipulated to hold only between an object and itself (p. 108). A true identity between names is necessary, though not a priori. The identity thesis cannot treat "pain = C-fiber stimulation" as a *contingent* scientific identification on the Franklin/bifocals model. If it is true, it is necessary: "there could not be a C-fiber stimulation which was not a pain nor a pain which was not a C-fiber stimulation" (pp. 148–149).

**Why the heat analogy fails.** Heat is picked out by an *accidental* property — producing sensation *S*. We can imagine a qualitatively identical evidential situation in which something else plays that role; that is not a world in which heat ≠ molecular motion. Pain is not like that. "Can any case of essence be more obvious than the fact that being a pain is a necessary property of each pain?" (p. 147). "To be in the same epistemic situation that would obtain if one had a pain *is* to have a pain… Pain… is not picked out by one of its accidental properties; rather it is picked out by the property of being pain itself, by its immediate phenomenological quality" (p. 152). "For it to exist without being *felt as* pain is for it to exist without there being any pain" (p. 151).

**Both directions look possible.** C-fiber stimulation without pain, and pain without C-fiber stimulation. "To make the C-fiber stimulation correspond to pain, or be felt as pain, God must do something in addition to the mere creation of the C-fiber stimulation" (pp. 153–154). There is no "zombie" slogan and no full-world physical duplicate claim. The stress is the particular psychophysical identification.

**What materialism then requires.** "Materialism… must hold that a physical description of the world is a complete description of it, that any mental facts are 'ontologically dependent' on physical facts in the straightforward sense of following from them by necessity" (p. 155). That is the sentence Chalmers later transcribes as logical supervenience. "Rejection of the identity thesis does not imply acceptance of Cartesian dualism"; necessity of origin (sperm and egg) "implicitly suggests a rejection of the Cartesian picture"; the mind-body problem is left "wide open and extremely confusing" (p. 155 n. 77). The **functional state view** is named and not discussed.

Nagel is cited (n. 73) with Davidson as accepting particular sensation–brain identities while denying types; Nagel's review of Armstrong is cited (n. 75). The essential phenomenological character of pain is Kripke's own wording here.

## How Different Thinkers Use This

- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: 1974 note 11 says the bat essay is "closely related" to *Naming and Necessity* on the essential subjective character of pain. Kripke's "immediate phenomenological quality" is that contact. Nagel refuses to infer that physicalism is *false*; Kripke refuses the *contingent* identity thesis and does not close on Cartesian dualism.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: takes the God-must-do-more image and the "following… by necessity" demand as the engine of [[Concepts/Logical Supervenience and Reductive Explanation (Chalmers)]] and [[Arguments/Zombie Argument (Chalmers)]]. He then claims 2-D semantics defuses Kripke-style a posteriori necessity *except* for consciousness, where "primary and secondary intensions coincide." That last move is Chalmers. Kripke's argument is that pain is *not* a Hesperus/Phosphorus case, because the picking-out property is the essence.
- **[[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]]**: the knowledge argument is epistemic leftover, not this modal argument. Jackson ranked the modal/zombie intuition as *weaker*.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: denies the leftover quality. Not addressed in this book.
- **Armstrong / Lewis** (no pages): causal-role analyses named as the identity theorist's attempt to treat the mental term as non-rigid.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] This is not the zombie argument. No "zombie," no whole-world duplicate. Both pain-without-C-fibers *and* C-fibers-without-pain are pressed.
- > [!warning] Chalmers's 2-D claim that Kripkean cases never rule out a conceivable world is a reception of Hesperus, not an account of what this argument says about *pain*.
- The book does not choose among dualism, monism, and a "wide open" remainder. Nagel 2023 and Chalmers 1996 take opposite next steps.
