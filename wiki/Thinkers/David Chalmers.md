# David Chalmers
> Australian philosopher of mind (b. 1966), coiner of the "hard problem of consciousness" and co-author of the extended-mind thesis, whose *Reality+* (2022) argues that virtual reality is genuine reality.

## Summary

Chalmers entered the vault indirectly — as the co-author of [[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)]] (with [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]], 1998) and as the named target of the deflationists' "hard problem" polemics ([[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]], [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]]). With the ingest of *Reality+* and *The Conscious Mind* he became a primary voice; with the ingest of [[Sources/The Extended Mind - Andy Clark and David Chalmers (1998)]] the co-authored thesis is now primary too. He is listed second, "in order of degree of belief in the central thesis." The paper already marks the split the later books will enlarge: experiences "may be determined internally" while beliefs extend. Footnote 4 (his forthcoming work on content) reads Otto/Twin Otto as a difference in *notional* (primary-intension) content because the cognitive system itself includes the notebook.

By his own later account he began in mathematics, physics, and computer science, turned to philosophy "to go deeper," and spent most of his career on consciousness before virtual reality gave him "a new perspective" on Descartes's problem of the external world.

*Reality+* is a project in what he calls **technophilosophy**: "(1) asking philosophical questions about technology and (2) using technology to help answer traditional philosophical questions" — the name modeled on Patricia Churchland's *Neurophilosophy* (1987). Its central thesis: **"Virtual reality is genuine reality"** — virtual worlds are not illusions ([[Concepts/Simulation Realism (Chalmers)]]), virtual lives can be fully meaningful, and we cannot rule out being in a virtual world now ([[Concepts/Simulation Hypothesis (Chalmers)]]).

With the ingest of *The Conscious Mind* (1996), the consciousness work that made his name is now primary-source material. The book argues that phenomenal consciousness fails to supervene logically on the physical ([[Arguments/Zombie Argument (Chalmers)]], [[Concepts/Logical Supervenience and Reductive Explanation (Chalmers)]]), so materialism is false and [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)]] — property dualism plus fundamental psychophysical laws — takes its place. The constructive side: [[Concepts/Coherence Between Consciousness and Awareness (Chalmers)]], [[Concepts/Organizational Invariance and the Qualia Arguments (Chalmers)]], and the speculative [[Concepts/Double-Aspect Theory of Information (Chalmers)]]. He honestly prices in the [[Concepts/Paradox of Phenomenal Judgment (Chalmers)]], defends machine consciousness against Searle ([[Concepts/Strong AI and Computational Sufficiency (Chalmers)]]), and closes with an Everett reading of QM ([[Concepts/Consciousness and the Everett Interpretation (Chalmers)]]). A self-described "temperamental materialist" driven to dualism: "If this is dualism, then we should learn to love dualism."

## Key Positions

- **Anti-skeptical strategy**: accept the Cartesian skeptic's premise that we can't know we're not simulated, deny that this undermines knowledge — because simulated objects are real, not illusory ([[Concepts/Cartesian Skepticism and the Simulation Riposte (Chalmers)]], [[Concepts/Simulation Realism (Chalmers)]]).
- **Probability of simulation**: refines [[Arguments/Simulation Argument (Bostrom)]] (now from the 2003 paper) via sim blockers; estimates "the chance that we are sims is at least 25 percent or so." Bostrom himself recommends an even split among the three horns and does not quote a 25% floor.
- **The cogito, minimally**: "I am conscious, therefore I am" survives every skeptical scenario.
- **Objective reality**: against post-truth readings — "Reality exists, independently of us. The truth matters... Even in an age of multiple realities, I still believe in objective reality."
- **Consciousness**: *Reality+* holds its hard-problem commitments mostly offstage, but adds a "fourth plank": virtual and augmented minds are genuine minds. The full commitments are in *The Conscious Mind*: [[Concepts/Hard Problem of Consciousness (Chalmers)]], zombies, naturalistic dualism, and the information-based prototheory whose it-from-bit thread resurfaces in *Reality+* ([[Concepts/It-from-Bit Hypothesis (Chalmers)]]). The phrase "something it is like" and the robot-without-experience remark are inherited from [[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]] (1974, now primary). Nagel 2023 uses the same zombie conceivability argument and then *refuses* the step to property dualism, hoping for [[Concepts/Psychophysical Nexus and Neutral Monism (Nagel)|monism]] instead. The knowledge argument (Mary) is now primary in [[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]] (1982): Chalmers files him as Type-C, adopts Mary as argument 4, and treats the Lewis–Nemirow ability analysis as "deeply implausible." Jackson himself ranked the modal/zombie argument as *weaker* than the knowledge argument, and defended epiphenomenalism as possible rather than as a worry to be escaped.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Chalmers's non-deflationism about consciousness conflicts with [[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]], [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]], [[Thinkers/Anil Seth]], and [[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]] — see [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]], [[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]], [[Contradictions/Seth vs Chalmers - Real Problem vs Hard Problem]], and [[Contradictions/Dehaene vs Chalmers - Access Consciousness vs the Hard Problem]].
- > [!warning] Fork with [[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]: both take subjectivity as irreducible and both use zombie conceivability; Nagel 2023 refuses [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)|property dualism]] and hopes for monism. The nearest Chalmers construct is [[Concepts/Double-Aspect Theory of Information (Chalmers)]].
- > [!warning] Near-ally, different cost-accounting, with [[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]]: both reject physicalism via leftover phenomenal facts; Jackson treats [[Concepts/Epiphenomenal Qualia (Jackson)|epiphenomenalism]] as a perfectly possible view, Chalmers as a problem priced in the [[Concepts/Paradox of Phenomenal Judgment (Chalmers)]].
- > [!warning] His simulation realism inverts [[Concepts/Simulation Hypothesis (Virk)]]'s framing: where Virk treats simulation as a revelation that ordinary reality is game-like, Chalmers treats it as no threat to the reality of the ordinary.
- > [!warning] Third pole on [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]]: Putnam (reception) tries to make "I am a BIV" unthinkable; Nagel 1986 treats that as a reductio; Chalmers grants we cannot know we are not simulated and relocates the fight to the Reality Question.
