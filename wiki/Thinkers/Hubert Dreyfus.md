# Hubert Dreyfus
> Hubert Lederer Dreyfus (1929–2017), American philosopher; longtime professor at UC Berkeley. The vault's primary voice for the claim that human intelligence is **embodied skilled coping** in a situation, not the manipulation of facts and formal rules — and that classical AI is the last metaphysics of the Platonic–Leibnizian tradition.

## Summary

Dreyfus entered the vault as a one-line application of [[Concepts/Readiness-to-Hand and Presence-at-Hand (Heidegger)|ready-to-hand]] to expert systems. *What Computers Still Can't Do* (1972 / 1979 / 1992) is the primary. It is not a book about qualia. It is a **critique of artificial reason**: an argument that the research program John Haugeland later named GOFAI is the technological fulfillment of Socrates' demand for an effective procedure, and that phenomenology (Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty, later Wittgenstein, Polanyi) shows why that demand cannot be met.

Career as this source presents it: a 1965 RAND paper, "Alchemy and Artificial Intelligence"; the 1972 book; a 1979 revision adding micro-worlds and frames; a 1986 book with his brother Stuart on expertise (*Mind over Machine*); the 1991 commentary *Being-in-the-World* on *Being and Time* Division I; the 1992 MIT introduction that treats GOFAI as a Lakatosian degenerating research program and extends the critique to CYC and connectionism. Lenat called him the "Black Knight of AI." The 1979 acknowledgments name [[Thinkers/John Searle]] as a helper on the revised edition.

The 1992 introduction is explicit about the logical status of the claim. "This failure does not mean that this sort of AI is impossible; no one has been able to come up with such a negative proof." What failed is the research program based on facts and rules. Connectionism is the right *kind* of successor and still inherits the commonsense and generalization problems unless a net shares our body, architecture, and [[Concepts/Forms of Life (Wittgenstein)|form of life]].

## Key Claims / Positions (from this source)

- Human intelligence is know-how, not knowing-that. Rules play a role in *acquiring* a skill; they need not operate in later *application*. See [[Concepts/Skilled Coping and the Five Stages of Expertise (Dreyfus)]].
- Classical AI rests on four optional assumptions — biological, psychological, epistemological, ontological. See [[Concepts/Four Assumptions of Artificial Reason (Dreyfus)]].
- The commonsense-knowledge problem is not a missing catalog of facts. It is organization, the conversion of know-how into knowing-that, and situational relevance. See [[Concepts/Commonsense Knowledge Problem (Dreyfus)]].
- The body is a condition of intelligence, not an implementation detail. See [[Concepts/Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)]].
- GOFAI is a degenerating research program. What computers *can* do is associationistic and simple-formal work; adult nonformal intelligence (*esprit de finesse*) is not a further increment of the same kind. See [[Concepts/GOFAI as Degenerating Research Program (Dreyfus)]].
- Man "can embody the truth, but he cannot know it" (Yeats, closing the 1979 introduction). Computers begin after the facts have already been produced.

## Relation to Other Vault Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]** (principal source): being-in-the-world, ready-to-hand, and the workshop totality are the positive account of what GOFAI tries to represent as facts. Dreyfus's 1991 *Being-in-the-World* is the commentary behind "Heideggerian AI" (Winograd, Agre–Chapman). On Husserl, Dreyfus emphasises discontinuity; Crowell emphasises continuity — already noted on the Heidegger page.
- **[[Thinkers/Alan Turing]]**: Dreyfus *uses* Turing on discrete-state machines, universality, the imitation game, and the 2000 prediction, then treats the imitation game as the wrong criterion. Behavioral similarity is a goal for builders, not a proof of intelligence. The child-machine programme is left more open than Athena-like adult intelligence, but Piaget-style learning seems to need the same unformalizable capacities.
- **[[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]]** (principal opponent inside AI): "within a generation" optimism; frames as stereotyped situations; the large-data-base estimate (10^5–10^6 elements); free will as arbitrary-or-rule-governed. Dreyfus reads frames as Husserl's noema formalized, still treating the background as one more object.
- **[[Thinkers/John Searle]]** (ally against strong AI, different reason): Searle's Chinese Room says syntax is never sufficient for semantics. Dreyfus says the problem is prior — there are no discrete symbols with fixed significance to manipulate, because significance is situational and bodily. Searle helped with the 1979 revision; the 1992 introduction cites Searle on metaphor as a nonrepresentational capacity.
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]** (partial successor): extended mind and predictive processing try to give a mechanism for the world-involving mind Dreyfus describes. Clark is more optimistic that prediction machines can recruit tools and environments; Dreyfus's 1992 dilemma is that a device must already be enough like us to generalize *our* way.
- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] / [[Thinkers/Evan Thompson]] / [[Thinkers/Eleanor Rosch]]**: *The Embodied Mind* (1991) names Dreyfus the "Heideggerian gadfly" of cognitive science and groups him with Taylor as doubting the science because they equate it with cognitivism. The book is the constructive sequel — [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]], [[Concepts/Natural Drift (Varela)|natural drift]], Brooks's world-as-model — plus a first-person method Dreyfus's 1993 *Mind* review rejects as inward observation. Thompson 2016 accepts that objection; Rosch 2016 does not. See [[Sources/The Embodied Mind - Varela Thompson and Rosch (2016)]], [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]].
- **[[Thinkers/Melanie Mitchell]]**: documents the empirical aftermath — brittleness, missing common sense, hype cycles — without the Heideggerian diagnosis.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: ally against functional reduction, talking past each other. Nagel's datum is what-it-is-like; Dreyfus's is skilled coping. A zombie expert would bother Nagel and not, as such, bother Dreyfus.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]** / **[[Thinkers/Douglas Hofstadter]]**: the computationalist side of [[Contradictions/Dreyfus vs Computationalism - Skilled Coping vs Formal Rules]]. Dennett's heterophenomenology and Hofstadter's strange loops both treat formal structure as enough; Dreyfus treats formal structure as the wrong *kind* of structure.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: [[Concepts/Strong AI and Computational Sufficiency (Chalmers)]] names Dreyfus as a target. Chalmers defends computational sufficiency for consciousness; Dreyfus is not primarily arguing about consciousness.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: first to conceive robots and to note that a finite machine cannot be a universal instrument. Dreyfus inverts the moral: what machines lack is "an involved, situated, material body," not an immaterial soul.
- **[[Thinkers/Wittgenstein]]**: later Wittgenstein on rule-application regress and forms of life; *Tractatus* atomic facts as "purest formulation" of the ontological assumption, then self-critique.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Kuhn]]**: paradigms structure relevance (without a paradigm all facts are "equally relevant"); Dreyfus uses Kuhn against Newell–Simon methodology and against Minsky's misreading of exemplars as frames. Lakatos, not Kuhn, names the 1992 verdict (degenerating program).
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: *phronēsis* and perception of degree as the ancient recognition that application is not a formula. Dreyfus credits the method (describe the structure of perception) and rejects the practical syllogism as one more step toward simulating reckoning alone.
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]**, **[[Thinkers/Hobbes]]**, **[[Thinkers/Leibniz]]**: the genealogy of artificial reason.
- **[[Thinkers/Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]** (named source for the body): figure/ground, synergy, the cane, the hold. *Phenomenology of Perception* is now primary ([[Sources/Phenomenology of Perception - Maurice Merleau-Ponty (1945)]]). "Maximum grasp" is Dreyfus's name; MP writes **hold**, **optimum equilibrium**, **scope of this hold**. See [[Concepts/Motor Intentionality and the Intentional Arc (Merleau-Ponty)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions
