# Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)

> Harvard philosopher whose causal constraints on reference are used, in the 1981 BIV argument, to argue that one cannot truly think "I am a brain in a vat." This page is **reception-first**: the vault has Pritchard and Ranalli's 2016 reconstruction, not *Reason, Truth and History* and not "The Meaning of 'Meaning'."

## Life and Context

The vault's first dedicated Putnam material is Duncan Pritchard and Chris Ranalli, "Putnam on BIVs and radical skepticism," ch. 6 of Goldberg (ed.), *The Brain in a Vat* (Cambridge, 2016). They reconstruct the argument of Putnam 1981b (*Reason, Truth and History*) and record Putnam's 1994 remark that Crispin Wright put "more clearly than I myself did … the premises and the deductive steps involved in my argument."

He had already existed in the vault as a **contrast class** and as a **failed refutation**:

- [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] and [[Thinkers/David Chalmers]] (1998) set [[Concepts/Active Externalism (Clark and Chalmers)|active externalism]] against "Putnam/Burge" historical content (Putnam 1975, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'"; Burge 1979). Twin-Earth differences are distal and passive; Otto's notebook is in the loop.
- [[Sources/Reality+ - David J. Chalmers (2022)]] lists Putnam 1981 as one of three "no-illusion" predecessors Chalmers rejects (the others: Bouwsma via verificationism, Zhai via idealism). The [[Arguments/Cartesian Skepticism and the Simulation Riposte (Chalmers)|Simulation Riposte]] files Putnam's "semantic contradictoriness" argument as working, if at all, against "brain in a vat," not "computer simulation."
- [[Thinkers/Ian Hacking]] discusses Putnam on reference and internal realism at the representation level of *Representing and Intervening*.
- [[Concepts/Structuralism and the Garden of Eden (Chalmers)]] pairs Putnam with [[Thinkers/John Searle]] on computation-triviality arguments Chalmers rebuts.

None of those is a Putnam primary. This ingest does not close that hole.

## Core Positions (as this source states them)

**Content externalism.** To think thoughts with a particular content, causal conditions must be met. A Martian with no encounter with trees, and no linguistic community that refers to trees, cannot think about trees. The same constraint is applied to a lifelong brain in a vat stimulated by supercomputers. See [[Concepts/Semantic Externalism (Putnam)]].

**The BIV argument is semantic, not epistemological.** Putnam's conclusion is that one cannot *truly think* that one is a BIV, not that one can *know* one is not. The two claims "are logically distinct, in both directions" (Pritchard and Ranalli). The stipulated scenario is **permanent** envattment by computers; recent envattment would leave prior causal connections intact. See [[Arguments/Putnam Brain in a Vat Argument]].

**Transcendental self-description.** Putnam (1981b: 16) calls the investigation "transcendental": it concerns "the pre-conditions of reference and hence of thought — preconditions built in to the nature of our minds themselves, though not (as Kant hoped) wholly independent of empirical assumptions."

**What this source does not give.** Twin Earth, multiple realizability, internal realism, the model-theoretic argument, and the later "common-sense realism" are named elsewhere in the Goldberg volume and in the vault's Chalmers/Hacking pages. They are not argued here.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the BIV is the late-twentieth-century rewriting of the evil demon. Putnam tries to block that rewriting on semantic grounds; Descartes's own rescue was theological.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: Putnam claims a transcendental investigation of the preconditions of reference, while refusing Kant's hope that those preconditions are wholly independent of empirical assumptions.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: *The View from Nowhere* (1986) treats the argument as a reductio of semantic externalism and as an exacerbation of skepticism ("Perhaps I can't even think the truth about what I am"). See [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]].
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: rejects Putnam's route (strong externalism) and answers the descendant scenario — the simulation hypothesis — by [[Concepts/Simulation Realism (Chalmers)|structuralism]], not semantics. "Even if I'm in a computer simulation, I can truly say, 'I'm in a computer simulation.'"
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: 1998 active externalism is a *third* option, not a restatement of Putnam. Twin-Earth content is not rejected; it is refused as the identity of mind-externalism.
- **[[Thinkers/Robert Nozick]]**: named by Pritchard and Ranalli as a closure-denier (with Dretske) facing the same skeptical argument Putnam recasts. Nozick 1981 (*Philosophical Explanations*) is not in the vault.
- **[[Thinkers/Saul Kripke]]**: *Naming and Necessity* Lecture III (now primary) treats Putnam as an independent development on natural-kind terms (*"It Ain't Necessarily So"*; cats/automata) and notes that Putnam "does not base his considerations on the apparatus of necessary versus a priori truths." Twin Earth / "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" remains outstanding. Do not write this page as if 1975 had been ingested.
- **Crispin Wright, Anthony Brueckner, Barry Stroud, Donald Davidson**: the chapter's interlocutors. Wright's reconstruction is the one Putnam endorsed. Davidson is flagged as a parallel transcendental externalist anti-skeptic, not developed.
