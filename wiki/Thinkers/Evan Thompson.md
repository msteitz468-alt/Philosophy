# Evan Thompson
> Evan Thompson, Canadian philosopher of mind; co-author of *The Embodied Mind* and writer of the 2016 introduction that recants three of the 1991 book's strategies while keeping its spine.

## Summary

As this volume presents him: first-year Ph.D. student in philosophy at the University of Toronto when he and [[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]] began the book in Paris in 1986; undergraduate Asian Studies at Amherst (Buddhist philosophy; a paper on Heidegger and Nishitani Keiji); dissertation on theories of perception via comparative color vision, written in Varela's lab and later published as *Colour Vision*. His father, William Irwin Thompson, founded the Lindisfarne Association, where Varela became family. Later books named here, not ingested: *Mind in Life* (2007), *Waking, Dreaming, Being*.

The 2016 introduction is the vault's first Thompson primary-voice text. It keeps the 1991 aim — circulation among cognitive science, phenomenology, and Buddhism; cognition as [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]]; [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)|groundlessness]] as "groundless ground" — and retracts three argumentative strategies.

## Three 2016 Recantations

1. **Western phenomenology is not a failed project.** The 1991 portrayal of Husserl, Heidegger, and Merleau-Ponty as bogged down in abstract reflection is withdrawn. *Mind in Life* includes an appendix "correcting and explaining the reasons for our mischaracterization of Husserl." Gallagher and Zahavi are named as showing phenomenology's use for cognitive science. Late Varela's **neurophenomenology** used Western phenomenology as well as mindfulness.

2. **Abhidharma and Nāgārjuna do not simply derive from meditation.** That claim is "simplistic and inaccurate." Classical Indian Buddhist philosophy was shaped by doctrine, scholasticism, and debate, and "was certainly not based on the kind of 'Buddhist modernist' style of meditation that we call 'mindfulness/awareness.'" Buddhist philosophy is "every bit as abstract, theoretical, and technical as Western philosophy."

3. **Mindfulness is not inner observation of an observer-independent stream.** Hubert Dreyfus's 1993 *Mind* review "rightly objected": inward observation alters experience and cannot be read back into world-immersed embodiment. The book's better view: mindfulness is skillful know-how for enacting situated mind–body states. The ch. 4 claim that mindfulness reveals consciousness to *really be* discontinuous is the rejected strategy.

He also disavows writing a "Buddhist cognitive science" or a work of Buddhist modernism: "the parts of the book that I like the least are the ones where we veer into that mode." The aim is "a new kind of wisdom… rooted in a mature cognitive science that is deeply informed by experiential practices of ethical human transformation."

## 2016 Definition of Enaction (keep distinct from 1991 wording)

"The living body is a self-producing and self-maintaining system that enacts or brings forth relevance, and… cognitive processes belong to the relational domain of the living body coupled to its environment." Science itself, applied reflexively, is "a highly refined distillation of our embodied sense-making," not "the exhibition of the nature of reality as it is in itself." 2016 terms such as "grounded cognition," "adaptively autonomous," and "offline" sensorimotor emulation are later jargon — do not write them into the 1991 chapter pages.

## How Thompson Relates to Prior Thinkers in the Vault

- **[[Thinkers/Francisco J. Varela]]**: mentor, co-author, family. Thompson is the 2016 custodian of the joint book.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: accepted critic of the introspectionist reading of mindfulness.
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: 1991 target; 2016 ally via the *Crisis* life-world.
- **[[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]]**, **[[Thinkers/Karl Friston]]**: named in 2016 as the later landscape (consciousness science; free-energy), not as 1991 sources.
- **[[Thinkers/Eleanor Rosch]]**: co-author; her 2016 introduction does *not* accept the Dreyfus objection as Thompson does, and she pushes mind-apart-from-brain evidence he does not endorse here.
