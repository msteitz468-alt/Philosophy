# Frank Jackson

> Frank Jackson (Australian philosopher; at Monash University in 1982). Author of the **knowledge argument** against physicalism — Fred's extra colour, then Mary in the black-and-white room — and of the claim that the leftover properties, **qualia**, may be causally idle with respect to the physical world.

## Summary

Jackson enters the vault as the third pole of its consciousness cluster. [[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]] supplies the datum ("what it is like"), [[Thinkers/David Chalmers]] supplies the modal metaphysics, [[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]] supplies the deflation — and Jackson supplies the argument all three have been arguing *about*. Until the 1986 ingest his Mary case existed only as reported by opponents ([[Concepts/Qualia Disqualified (Dennett)]]) and allies ([[Arguments/Zombie Argument (Chalmers)]] argument 4; [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)]], Type-C). The 1982 paper is now primary as well: it is where Fred, Mary, the contrast with Nagel, and the defence of [[Concepts/Epiphenomenal Qualia (Jackson)|epiphenomenal qualia]] actually live.

He calls himself a "qualia freak": no amount of physical information — including functional role — captures "the hurtfulness of pains, the itchiness of itches, pangs of jealousy," or the look of the sky. The unargued-intuition version of that claim is "weak from a polemical point of view." The [[Arguments/Knowledge Argument (Jackson)|knowledge argument]] is built to have premises "obvious to all, or at least to as many as possible." He treats the modal/zombie argument as genuine but disputed, and he *separates* his argument from Nagel's: he is complaining of a **property of the experience**, not of failure to occupy Fred's point of view or to extrapolate in imagination.

The 1986 sequel is characteristically narrow. It does not re-defend epiphenomenalism. It fixes the meaning of "physicalism" precisely enough that its failure is a well-defined possibility, isolates the one premise doing the work — that Mary learns a fact about *other people* — and shows that each of Churchland's three objections attacks something else. The concession is as characteristic as the argument: the knowledge argument is "a valid argument from highly plausible, though admittedly not demonstrable, premises," and "that, after all, is about as good an objection as one could expect in this area of philosophy."

*(Not established by these sources, but flagged so no vault page overstates his position: Jackson later renounced the argument's conclusion and became a physicalist. The vault holds no source for that reversal; every claim here is the 1982/1986 Jackson.)*

## Key Claims / Positions (from these sources)

- **Physical information includes functional role** (1982) and, more sharply, complete physical knowledge must be complete knowledge *simpliciter* if the world is *entirely* physical (1986).
- **The knowledge argument**: Fred, then Mary. All the physical information is not all the information; Physicalism is false. 1986 display: she knows everything *physical* about other people; she does not know everything about them.
- **Not imagination, not *de se*, not Leibniz's Law**: those are neighbours or misreadings.
- **Modal argument weaker**: the zombie intuition is disputed (Kirk recanted); it can be a consequence of the knowledge argument, not its ground.
- **Distinct from Nagel**: a missing *property*, not a missing point of view. Physicalism "makes no special claims about the imaginative or extrapolative powers of human beings."
- **Epiphenomenalism is possible** (1982 §IV): qualia need not make a physical difference; they may affect other mental states; the subject is aware of them. Obvious causation, Darwin, and other-minds do not refute this. Our failure to understand the how and why is what selection predicts (sea-slugists).

## Relation to Other Vault Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: Jackson is "much indebted" to the 1974 essay and then dissociates. Nagel refuses Jackson's conclusion — Physicalism is unintelligible, not shown false. Nagel 2023 is hostage to consciousness doing causal work, the option Jackson's §IV says we need not take.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: principal opponent. Dennett denies that Mary with genuinely all physical information learns a fact, and treats strong epiphenomenal qualia as incoherent (parity / gremlins). Jackson's actual epiphenomenalism is not Dennett's "undetectable even by introspection" sense. See [[Concepts/Qualia Disqualified (Dennett)]].
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: closest ally on the knowledge argument (one of five; Type-C). Difference of ambition and of cost-accounting: Chalmers builds a positive dualist theory and treats epiphenomenalism as a worry ([[Concepts/Paradox of Phenomenal Judgment (Chalmers)]]); Jackson 1982 treats it as a perfectly possible view and the expected result of selection.
- **David Lewis** (no thinker page yet): thanked in both papers; co-author with Laurence Nemirow of the ability analysis Jackson answers in 1986; discussion partner already in 1982 §III (Nemirow's review of *Mortal Questions*). A Lewis page is an outstanding gap — see [[Outstanding Sources]].
- **Paul Churchland** (no thinker page yet): the 1986 interlocutor. His own paper is not in `raw/`.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the knowledge argument is a modern descendant of the conceivability tradition, but Jackson's preferred route is epistemic (a fact left out) rather than modal (a possible separation).
