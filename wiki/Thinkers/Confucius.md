# Confucius

> Kong Qiu of Lu (551–479 BC), Latinized from Kong *fuzi*, Master Kong. China's earliest named teacher and moral thinker, known here from the *Analects* in Annping Chin's 2014 translation. He "refrained from putting forth theories" and "did not think that he must be right" (9.4). Humaneness (*ren*) is beginning and end; "restrain the self and return to the rites" is the way to it (12.1). Good men have to be tested in politics.

## Profile

- **Identity/dates**: Born 551 BC in Lu (Shandong), late Spring and Autumn. The Kongs were common gentlemen (*shi*) — a notch above common folk, educated in the six arts, without hereditary entitlements. Father a military officer then district steward, already old; died soon after Confucius's birth. Confucius was "poor and from a humble station" and so "skilled in many menial tasks."
- **Career**: Service with the Jisun clan (granaries, livestock, district officer), then Lu minister of works and minister of crime. Effective in law-and-order and diplomacy. In 498 BC a failed attempt to restore the ruler against the hereditary families forced fourteen years of self-exile (Wei, Song, Chen, Cai, back to Wei), seeking a ruler who would "put [him] to use": "How can I be like a bitter gourd that hangs from the end of a string and not be eaten?" Invited home 484; addressed as "elder statesman." Died at seventy-two (479).
- **Disciples**: Wide backgrounds — aristocrats, *shi*, merchants, farmers, artisans, criminals. Earliest named trio on the exile journey: Zigong, Zilu, Yan Hui. After his death, Youzi, Zengzi, Zixia, Zizhang, Ziyou compiled and split (Book 19).
- **Work in the vault**: *The Analects* (*Lunyu*), Chin 2014 (Penguin) — see [[Sources/The Analects - Confucius (Chin trans., 2014)]]. Mencius and Xunzi "took his teachings in different directions"; they are **not** this page.

## Core Doctrine (from Chin's *Analects*)

- **Humaneness (*ren*).** The gentleman does not abandon it "even for the duration of a meal" (4.5). Yan Hui alone "lets his heart abide in humaneness for three months at a time" (6.7). Method: "A humane person wishes to steady himself, and so he helps others to steady themselves" (6.30). Culmination: "Restrain the self and return to the rites [*keji fuli*]. This is the way to be humane. … Humaneness rests with the self" (12.1). He will not certify most disciples as humane (5.8, 5.19) and "dare[s] not call [himself] a sage or a humane man" (7.34). See [[Concepts/Ren - Humaneness (Confucius)]].
- **Rites (*li*).** Without *ren*, rites and music are useless (3.3). "Harmony is what is most prized" in their practice, but harmony without the rules "will not work" (1.12). Sequence: "*Odes* … stimulate. The rites … steady us. Music is the final lesson" (8.8). To his son: without the *Odes* you cannot speak; without the rites you cannot find your balance (16.13). Book Ten is others' record of his embodied deportment. See [[Concepts/Li - Ritual Propriety (Confucius)]].
- **The gentleman (*junzi*).** Not a vessel (2.12). "Right balance of native material and refinement" (6.18). Understands what is morally right; the petty man, what is profitable (4.16). Sometimes still the older rank-word "man of position" (1.8, 12.19). See [[Concepts/Junzi - The Gentleman (Confucius)]].
- **Filiality (*xiao*) as root.** Youzi: filiality and respect for elders are "the roots of one's humanity" (1.2). Not feeding (that is what one does for dogs and horses) but respect and the difficult facial expression (2.7–2.8). Three-year mourning because a child does not leave the parents' arms until three (17.21). See [[Concepts/Xiao - Filiality (Confucius)]].
- **Government by virtue and names.** "To rule by virtue is like the way the North Star rules" (2.1). Statutes plus punishment yield evasion without shame; *de* plus *li* yield shame and self-reform (2.3). First task: **rectifying names** (13.3). "Let the ruler be a ruler, the subject be a subject, a father be a father, a son be a son" (12.11). Food, army, trust — drop army then food; without the people's trust the state cannot survive (12.7). Many people → make them rich → instruct them (13.9). No need to kill; those at the top are wind, those below grass (12.19). See [[Concepts/Zhengming - Rectifying Names (Confucius)]].
- **The one thread.** Zengzi: doing one's best (*zhong*) and treating others as alive with humanity (*shu*) (4.15). Lifelong one-word guide: *shu* — "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not want" (15.24). See [[Concepts/Shu - Reciprocity (Confucius)]].
- **Learning, transmission, silence.** "I transmit but do not innovate. I love antiquity and have faith in it" (7.1). One corner; the student must return three (7.8). Zigong: one can learn his *wenzhang*, "but not his views on human nature and the way of Heaven" (5.13). "People are similar by nature; they become distinct through practice" (17.2) — Chin/Qian Mu: the point is *practice*, not Mencius's good nature. "What does Heaven ever say? Yet the four seasons move in order" (17.19).
- **Staying in the world.** Recluses tell him to keep what he believes to himself and wade according to the water's depth (14.39); Chang Ju and Jie Ni: "Everywhere in the world is the same" — follow those who have run away (18.6). He answers: "We cannot flock with birds and beasts. … Whom can I be with if not with other human beings?" Zilu: a gentleman takes office to understand ruler–subject rightness, "know[ing] all along that it is not possible" to put the Way fully into practice (18.7). "I have no preconceptions about what one can or cannot do" (18.8).

## Relations to other vault thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Laozi]] / [[Schools/Daoism]]**: the named opponent of *Daodejing* chs. 18–19 and 38. Laozi treats humankindness, duty, ritual, sagacity, and filial piety as *symptoms of the course's loss*. Confucius treats them as the work. Shared word *wuwei* at 15.5: Shun "order[ed] the world without taking any [deliberate] action" by holding himself respectfully and facing south — Chin: Confucian scholars insist this is not Laozi's nondoing. See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].
- **[[Thinkers/Sun Tzu]]**: near-contemporary *shi*; different craft. Confucius wants a government that runs on trust and moral capital and prefers not to raise armies (16.1); Sun Tzu calculates war as "a matter of life and death." Overlap: both prefer to win without slaughter; Sun Tzu by deception and *shih*, Confucius by wind-over-grass example.
- **[[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: both form character by practice (17.2 ≈ habituation) and test the good man in the polis. Aristotle's *eudaimonia* is excellent *activity* of the rational soul, crowned by *theōria*. Confucius's fulfillment is *ren* in relationships and office; he does not rank contemplation above politics, and he will not theorize *xing* or *tiandao* (5.13). *Zhongyong* at 6.29 is everyday balance, not Aristotle's *mesotēs* and not the later *Zhongyong* book. See [[Concepts/Eudaimonia]], [[Concepts/Doctrine of the Mean (Aristotle)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: *shu* (15.24) sounds like a formula of reciprocity. Kant's duty is necessity of an action *from respect for the law*; moral worth requires a motive. Confucius's *shu* is analogical method from what is close at hand (6.30), not a formal test of maxims, and 12.1's humaneness "rests with the self" through rites, not through autonomy as self-legislation. See [[Concepts/Duty (Kant)]], [[Concepts/Formula of Humanity as End in Itself (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Socrates]] / [[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]**: both refuse system and teach by conversation. Socrates is Hadot's *philo-sopher* who is not a sage; Confucius likewise "dare[s] not call [himself] a sage" (7.34). Difference: Socrates writes nothing and dies by the city's law; Confucius compiles no book either (disciples do), but he *wants office* and treats withdrawal as a live option he refuses. See [[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Plato]]**: Odes / rites / music as formative (8.8, 16.13) sit next to the *Republic*'s music and gymnastic. Confucius follows Zhou culture (3.14); he does not found a kallipolis or exile the poets.

## Vault / AI / Personal Relevance

- **Evidence before judgment**: the Que boy (Introduction / 14.44-style): sit and walk with elders and you are not eager to learn — you want to grow up fast. Conduct first, then the name.
- **Names that can be carried out** (13.3): if a title cannot hold up in speech and action, punishments become arbitrary. Transfer to roles, job titles, and model claims.
- **Stay in the squalid**: 14.39 / 18.6–18.8 — not because the Way can be realized, but because one cannot flock with birds and beasts.
