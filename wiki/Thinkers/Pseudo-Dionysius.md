# Pseudo-Dionysius

> Unknown Syrian ecclesiastic, writing near the end of the fifth century under the name of St. Paul's Athenian convert, who put Athenian Neoplatonism into Christian dress: God is Super-Essential (beyond being and every name), yet overflows into a world that can be named, then stripped, until the initiate enters the Darkness of Unknowing.

## Authorship and Date

The writings profess to be those of Dionysius the Areopagite (Acts 17:34) and stage a Pauline circle (Timothy as addressee; Hierotheus as teacher "next to Paul"). C. E. Rolt, following the modern consensus already available in 1920, treats the claim as collapsed by anachronism, "by far the chief" of which is later Neoplatonism in almost every paragraph: the texts "appear to reflect, and even to quote, the doctrines of the Pagan philosopher [[Thinkers/Proclus]], who began lecturing at Athens in A.D. 430." Rolt also thinks the Hierotheus who figures so largely is the Syrian mystic Stephen bar Sudaili. Date: near the very end of the fifth century. True name unknown; "probably a monk, possibly a bishop, certainly an ecclesiastic of some sort"; home believed to be Syria.

The first recorded appeal is 533, at a council in Constantinople, when Severus, Patriarch of Antioch, cited the writings for Monophysite teaching. They already had some authority then; they acquired much more. Apostolic identity long shielded them; seventeenth-century criticism produced the standing label "Pseudo-Dionysius."

Four treatises survive: *On the Divine Names*, *On Mystical Theology*, *On the Celestial Hierarchy*, *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*. This vault has ingested the first two, in Rolt's 1920 translation (Dover 2004 reprint), with W. J. Sparrow-Simpson's preface and closing reception essay.

## The Two Treatises

*The Divine Names* is [[Concepts/Cataphatic and Apophatic Theology (Dionysius)|cataphatic]] theology: the scriptural titles of God — Good, Being, Life, Wisdom, Power, Peace, One — are a "Symbolical Revelation" of the Super-Essence's "beneficent Emanations," not a description of the Godhead in itself (DN I.4). All **undifferenced** titles apply to the *entire* Godhead; **differentiated** titles are Father, Son, and Spirit, plus the Incarnation (DN II). The itinerary of names ends at Perfect and One, then prefers the negative method (DN XIII.3).

*The Mystical Theology* is the complementary [[Concepts/Cataphatic and Apophatic Theology (Dionysius)|apophatic]] ascent. Timothy is told to "leave the senses and the activities of the intellect" and strain toward union with "Him whom neither being nor understanding can contain" (MT I). Moses meets not God but "the place wherein He dwells," then plunges into the [[Concepts/Divine Darkness and Unknowing (Dionysius)|Darkness of Unknowing]]. Chapter V denies even "one," "unity," "Godhead," and "Goodness": "nor can any affirmation or negation apply to it."

The two works are one itinerary: procession into names, return by their stripping. Affirmation and negation do not contradict, because the Cause "precedes and surpasses all deprivation" (MT I).

## Key Doctrines

- **[[Concepts/Super-Essential Godhead (Dionysius)]]**: the hidden Godhead is "Universal Cause of existence while Itself existing not, for It is beyond all Being" (DN I.1). Speech and conception of it are confined to Scripture; its nature belongs to Unknowing.
- **[[Concepts/Divine Names (Dionysius)]]**: nameless *and* many-named; titles name "the powers that stream Therefrom to us," not the Mystery (DN II.7).
- **[[Concepts/Ecstasy of Divine Love (Dionysius)]]**: Good is the first name. Yearning "allowed Him not to remain unfruitful in Himself" (DN IV.10). By excess of Goodness the Creator "is transported outside of Himself… whereby He yet stays within Himself" (DN IV.13). Lovejoy's plenitude line lives here.
- **Evil as non-being**: no second principle; evil is "farther removed than the Non-Existent from the Good" (DN IV.19). Matter *qua* matter is not evil (DN IV.28) — a correction of Plotinus's occasional identification of matter with evil.
- **[[Concepts/Hierarchy of Existence, Life, and Intelligence (Dionysius)]]**: one God under more and less universal titles (Existence > Life > Wisdom); creatures rank the other way (intelligences include the lower gifts).
- **Time and eternity**: God is "both Eternity and Time… before Time and above Time… before Eternity and above Eternity" (DN X.3). The universe is "produced… out of Himself" (DN X.1), not stated as a first-instant *creatio ex nihilo* and not as an uncreated co-eternal cosmos.

Rolt's Introduction equates the Super-Essence with Bradley's Absolute ("God is but an Appearance of the Absolute"). That is Rolt, not Dionysius. Sparrow-Simpson already flags the enthusiasm: the bearing of a "Supra-Personal Deity" on the Person of Christ and the Atonement is under-defined.

## Reception (Sparrow-Simpson)

- **East**: Maximus the Confessor (d. 662) is "the first great propagator," notes on all four treatises, clearer than the master. John of Damascus takes incomprehensibility and the cataphatic/apophatic pair "with discrimination."
- **West**: Pope Martin I cites Dionysius at the Lateran Council of 649 (the *theandric* activity: *nova operatio deivirilis*, not *una*). Greek corpus sent by Pope Paul in 757, unread ~a century at St. Denis. [[Thinkers/Thomas Aquinas|Erigena]], at Charles the Bald's request, translates all four and builds *De Divisione Naturae* on them (*plus quam bonitas*; God as *Nihil*; strictly, Deity is not capable of love). Hugh of St. Victor comments the *Heavenly Hierarchy*. Aquinas comments *Divine Names* — "one of St. Thomas's favourite authorities," used critically, averse to pantheism.
- **Critics**: Le Quien (monophysite tendencies); Baur (Trinity "little more than names"); Vacherot ("To know God we must cease to think of Him"); Dorner (family of Monophysitism); Bach (mysticism not founded on the historical Christ or Redemption as a once-in-time fact); Westcott, "surprised that such a scheme… should even be reckoned Christian at all."
- Sparrow-Simpson's own line: strength is philosophy + mysticism; "philosophy prevails" over Christian theology; the "greatest admirers… employed him with discretion" under Catholic presuppositions (Damascene, Aquinas).

Wallis had already placed him as Athenian Neoplatonism in Christian dress, with the risk that God becomes "merely the supreme term in the metaphysical hierarchy." The primary texts confirm the dress and the risk; they also show a Trinitarian and incarnational insistence (undifferenced vs differentiated names; Super-Essential Jesus, DN II.6, II.10) that later readers either rescue (Aquinas) or flatten (Erigena, Baur).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] The itinerary of names vs their final denial: [[Concepts/Cataphatic and Apophatic Theology (Dionysius)]]. Dionysius holds both; Erigena's Absolute and modern critics treat the negative as cancelling the Christian names.
- > [!warning] Matter-as-evil (Plotinus, at times) vs evil as privation not seated in matter (DN IV.28). See [[Thinkers/Plotinus]], [[Thinkers/Augustine]].
- > [!warning] Eternal overflow vs temporal creation: [[Contradictions/Temporal Creation vs Eternal World]]. Dionysius produces the universe "out of Himself" and places God before both Time and Eternity.
- > [!warning] Contemplative stripping (MT) vs hierarchical/sacramental mediation (*Celestial* / *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*, not in this volume): structurally Iamblichean. See [[Contradictions/Plotinus vs Iamblichus - Contemplation vs Theurgy]].
