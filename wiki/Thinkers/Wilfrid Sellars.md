# Wilfrid Sellars

> The American philosopher who killed the **Given** — the empiricist idea of a self-authenticating, non-inferential awareness that could found empirical knowledge — and then, with the **Myth of Jones**, showed that killing it costs us neither inner episodes nor naturalism.

## Biography and Historical Context

Wilfrid Sellars (1912–1989) delivered *Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind* as the University of London Special Lectures on Philosophy for 1955–56, in March 1956, under the title "The Myth of the Given: Three Lectures on Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind." It was published that year in *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science*, vol. 1, and reissued by Harvard in 1997 with an Introduction by [[Thinkers/Richard Rorty|Richard Rorty]] and a Study Guide by [[Thinkers/Robert Brandom|Robert Brandom]].

Rorty places the essay alongside [[Sources/Two Dogmas of Empiricism - W.V.O. Quine (1951)|Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism"]] (1951) and Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* (1953) as one of the three seminal works of the shift from logical-empiricist to "post-positivist" analytic philosophy — a shift that began around 1950 and was complete by around 1970. On Rorty's division of labor: "Whereas Quine's 'Two Dogmas' had helped destroy the distinction between analytic and synthetic truths, 'Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind' helped destroy the empiricist form of foundationalism by attacking the distinction between what is 'given to the mind' and what is 'added by the mind.'"

Sellars was unusual among prominent post-war American philosophers in having a wide and serious knowledge of the history of philosophy; he held "that philosophy without the history of philosophy is, if not blind, at least dumb," a view his analytic contemporaries found "merely perverse." Rorty characterizes his overall project as the attempt "to usher analytic philosophy out of its Humean and into its Kantian phase" — and, through the closing sections, into "its Hegelian stage." Sellars himself described the essay as "incipient *Méditations Hegeliennes*" (§20) and called Hegel "that great foe of 'immediacy'" (§1).

## Core Contributions

### The Myth of the Given
See [[Concepts/Myth of the Given (Sellars)]]. Sellars is careful about what he is *not* denying: "I presume that no philosopher who has attacked the philosophical idea of givenness or, to use the Hegelian term, immediacy, has intended to deny that there is a difference between inferring that something is the case and, for example, seeing it to be the case." Many things have been said to be given — "sense contents, material objects, universals, propositions, real connections, first principles, even givenness itself." The target is the *framework*, of which sense-datum theory is only the first instance attacked. The framework has been common to "both 'dogmatic rationalism' and 'skeptical empiricism.'"

### The inconsistent triad in sense-datum theory
See [[Arguments/Inconsistent Triad in Sense-Datum Theories (Sellars)]]. The classical sense datum is "a mongrel resulting from a crossbreeding of two ideas": inner episodes that occur without any prior concept-learning, and inner episodes that are non-inferential *knowings* serving as the evidence for all other empirical propositions. Nothing can be both.

### The logic of 'looks'
See [[Concepts/The Logic of Looks (Sellars)]]. "Looks" is not a triadic relation among object, person, and quality. "This looks green to me now" reports an experience whose propositional claim the speaker declines to endorse. Crucially, "the concept of *looking* green … presupposes the concept of *being* green" — reversing the empiricist order of analysis.

### The space of reasons
"In characterizing an episode or a state as that of *knowing*, we are not giving an empirical description of that episode or state; we are placing it in the logical space of reasons, of justifying and being able to justify what one says" (§36). See [[Concepts/Space of Reasons (Sellars)]].

### Psychological nominalism
"All awareness of *sorts, resemblances, facts*, etc., in short all awareness of abstract entities — indeed, all awareness even of particulars — is a linguistic affair" (§29). This entails that "Locke, Berkeley, and Hume were wrong in thinking that we are 'aware of certain determinate sorts … simply by virtue of having sensations and images'" (§28). See [[Concepts/Psychological Nominalism (Sellars)]].

### The Myth of Jones
§§48–63. A story explaining "why we can be naturalists without being behaviorists, why we can accept Wittgenstein's doubts about … 'self-authenticating non-verbal episodes' without sharing [[Thinkers/Gilbert Ryle|Ryle's]] doubts about the existence of such mental entities as thoughts and sense-impressions." See [[Concepts/Myth of Jones (Sellars)]].

## Relations to Other Vault Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Bertrand Russell]]** — the classical sense-datum theory Sellars dismantles is Russell's ([[Concepts/Sense-Data vs. Physical Objects (Russell)]], [[Concepts/Knowledge by Acquaintance and Knowledge by Description (Russell)]]). Sellars notes that "knowledge by acquaintance" is "a useful metaphor for this stipulated sense of *know*" that has "congealed into a technical term."
- **[[Thinkers/A.J. Ayer]]** — Ayer is named as the exponent of the "another language" reading of sense-datum talk (§8), and *Language, Truth and Logic* is the canonical statement of the position under attack.
- **[[Thinkers/W.V.O. Quine]]** — parallel demolition of the two dogmas; both replace foundations with a holistic, self-correcting whole. Compare Sellars's "self-correcting enterprise which can put any claim in jeopardy, though not all at once" (§38) with [[Concepts/Confirmation Holism (Duhem-Quine Thesis)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Popper]]** — independent anti-foundationalism; [[Concepts/Basic Statements and the Empirical Basis (Popper)]] and [[Concepts/No Authoritative Sources of Knowledge (Popper)]] reach a similar verdict about incorrigible observation from the philosophy-of-science side.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]** — Sellars follows the *Investigations* in identifying concept possession with mastery of the use of a word ([[Concepts/Concept Possession as Ability (Wittgenstein)]]) and in rejecting self-authenticating non-verbal episodes.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — "intuitions without concepts are blind"; Sellars's project is to move analytic philosophy into its Kantian phase.
- **[[Thinkers/John McDowell]]** — *Mind and World* (1994) takes over the Myth of the Given directly and tries to keep experience rationally constraining by making it already conceptual.
- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]**, **[[Thinkers/George Berkeley]]**, **[[Thinkers/Hume]]** — the classical empiricists whose account of impressions and ideas psychological nominalism denies.
