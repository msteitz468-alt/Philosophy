# Anil Seth

> Anil Seth, British neuroscientist and philosopher of consciousness; professor of cognitive and computational neuroscience at the University of Sussex and co-director of the Sackler (now Sussex) Centre for Consciousness Science. *Being You* (2021) is the vault's primary for the **real problem** of consciousness and the **beast-machine** thesis: we are conscious selves *because* we are living bodies that predict and control their own physiological condition.

## Summary

Seth enters the vault as the closest complement to [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] without duplicating him. They share a campus, a Helmholtz/Friston mechanism, and the slogan "controlled hallucination." Clark's *Experience Machine* is a theory of *how brains work* (and how they extend). Seth's *Being You* is a theory of *what consciousness is for*: not intelligence, not a rational soul, but the regulation of a living body. The distinctive claim is the inversion of Descartes's *bêtes-machines*: "We are conscious selves precisely *because* we are beast machines."

The book is shaped by three biographical facts he puts on the first pages: childhood recognition that "I" would die and therefore depended on "the physical materiality of my body and my brain"; a PhD detour through AI and robotics; and a six-year stint with Gerald Edelman at the Neurosciences Institute in San Diego, which "finally delivered the chance to investigate the brain basis of consciousness directly." The opening set-piece is general anaesthesia as temporary abolition of being — "the art of turning people into objects."

Metaphysically he is a "functionally agnostic flavour of **physicalism**." Functionalism (simulation = instantiation) is left open: is consciousness more like Go or like weather? Panpsychism is untestable; mysterianism "unjustifiably pessimistic"; philosophical zombies are a failure of imagination dressed as a necessity. The method is the [[Concepts/Real Problem of Consciousness (Seth)|real problem]]: accept that experience exists, then explain, predict, and control its *properties* — level, content, self — until the hard-problem intuition "vanish[es] in a puff of metaphysical smoke."

## Core Positions (from *Being You*, 2021)

- **The real problem, not the hard problem.** Consciousness science should neither solve Chalmers's *why-experience-at-all* question first nor sweep experience under the carpet. Primary goals: *explain, predict, and control* phenomenological properties via mechanisms in embodied, embedded brains. See [[Concepts/Real Problem of Consciousness (Seth)]].
- **Level is integration plus information, not Φ-as-identity.** Every experience is simultaneously unified and distinct. Candidate measures (causal density, neural complexity, PCI, observer-relative Φ) capture that pair. [[Concepts/Phi and Integrated Information (Seth)|IIT]] is kept as a research programme and rejected as a theory that consciousness *is* Φ.
- **Perception is inside-out.** "What we actually perceive is a top-down, inside-out neuronal fantasy that is reined in by reality." Predictive processing is the mechanism theory; controlled hallucination is that theory applied to phenomenology. See [[Concepts/Inside-Out Perception (Seth)]], [[Concepts/Controlled Hallucination (Clark)]].
- **The self is another perception.** Six dissociable layers (ground-state / embodied / perspectival / volitional / narrative / social). "We do not perceive ourselves in order to know ourselves, we perceive ourselves in order to control ourselves." See [[Concepts/Self as Controlled Hallucination (Seth)]].
- **Beast-machine theory.** Consciousness happens "*with, through, and because of* our living bodies." Interoceptive inference is control-oriented perception that keeps essential variables viable (allostasis). "We are not cognitive computers, we are feeling machines." See [[Concepts/Beast Machine Theory (Seth)]].
- **Free will is a controlling hallucination.** Compatibilist, not illusionist. Spooky/Cartesian intervention is incoherent; the *experience* of volition and the *capacity* to control many degrees of freedom are real. See [[Concepts/Free Will as Controlled Hallucination (Seth)]].
- **Life before intelligence.** All mammals are conscious by shared mechanism; birds and octopuses have a strong case; nematodes and bacteria do not make the grade. Silicon predictive-processing "beast machines" would "probably not" be conscious: "it is life, rather than information processing, that breathes the fire into the equations." See [[Concepts/Animal Consciousness and the Beast Machine (Seth)]], [[Concepts/Machine Minds (Seth)]].

## How Seth Relates to Prior Thinkers in the Vault

- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: Sussex colleague; Clark blurbed *Being You*. Same PEM family; Seth does *not* credit Clark for "controlled hallucination" (he heard it from Chris Frith; traces it to Ramesh Jain). Seth names *Surfing Uncertainty* as the champion of *action-oriented* PP — now primary. Clark 2016 already uses Seth on presence and interoceptive inference (Ch 7). Clark's distinctive moral is extension; Seth's is biologization. Clark 2023 later deflates the hard problem via the meta-problem; 2016 only creeps up. Seth dissolves by explaining properties (vitalism analogy). They are allies on mechanism, not interchangeable.
- **[[Thinkers/Jakob Hohwy]]**: source for PP history and co-author (Palmer–Hohwy–Seth 2015; Hohwy & Seth 2020 on FEP as "mathematical philosophy"). Hohwy secludes the mind; Clark extends it; Seth biologizes it. Hohwy *declines* the hard problem; Seth goes after it indirectly. Hohwy already used Seth 2012 on interoceptive emotion.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Friston]]**: technical pillar. Coins active inference; FEP is the physical grounding of the beast machine ("I predict myself therefore I am"). Seth treats FEP as a theory *for* consciousness science, not a theory *of* consciousness.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: named foil. The hard problem is quoted at length and refused as the agenda. Real-problem strategy vs. naturalistic dualism is now [[Contradictions/Seth vs Chalmers - Real Problem vs Hard Problem]].
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the *bête-machine* is inverted. Animals were flesh automatons without rational selfhood; Seth's conscious selves *are* beast machines. The two tests themselves are not discussed.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]**: *Consciousness Explained* is named as a turning point; ally on dissolving mysteries, degrees of freedom, and machines as "intelligent tools, not colleagues." Seth keeps experience as the explanandum (real problem, not eliminativism).
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: the 1974 "something it is like" definition is adopted as the mark of consciousness. Nagel is not named in the animal chapter; the bat appears only as echolocating *Umwelt*.
- **[[Thinkers/John Searle]]**: unnamed in the machine chapter, but "life… breathes the fire into the equations" is a predictive-processing cousin of [[Concepts/Biological Naturalism (Searle)]] — aimed at multi-level self-maintenance, not mysterious biological juice.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: noumenon as the unreachable reality perception is designed *not* to deliver; Helmholtz as scientific Kant. Not used for autonomy.
- **Julien Offray de La Mettrie**: named inversion in the Epilogue — a twenty-first-century *l'homme machine* in which life is constitutive of mind.
