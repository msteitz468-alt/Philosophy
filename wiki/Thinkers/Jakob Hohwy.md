# Jakob Hohwy

> Jakob Hohwy, Australian philosopher of mind and cognition (Monash). Author of *The Predictive Mind* (OUP 2013), the vault's systematic statement of **prediction-error minimization**: one reiterated statistical mechanism is supposed to be all the brain ever does. Against [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]] he locates that mechanism *inside* a skull-bound, secluded mind, behind a "veil of sensory input."

## Summary

Hohwy enters the vault as the missing systematic counterpart to Clark's already-ingested popularization. *The Experience Machine* (2023) gave the wiki [[Concepts/Predictive Processing (Clark)|predictive processing]] as a toolkit and a slogan ("controlled hallucination"). Hohwy 2013 is earlier, drier, and more imperial: prediction-error minimization (PEM) is not one useful idea among others but "all the brain ever does," and perception, action, and attention are "three different ways of doing the very same thing" (p. 2). The book is an inference to the best explanation — PEM unifies, is mechanistically simple, and is theoretically fecund — written with "a minimum of technical, formal detail" so the mechanism can be applied to philosophy of mind.

The technical source he names is [[Thinkers/Karl Friston]]. The philosopher he names as taking the same framework "in different directions than what I argue for" is Clark (p. 7). That fork is now a vault contradiction: [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].

Hohwy's own path into the theory, as he tells it, ran through delusion (with Ian Gold), an interdisciplinary group around Andreas Roepstorff in Aarhus, and work with Chris Frith on neuropsychiatry, before broadening to self, visual perception, introspection, emotion, and attention. The book is dedicated to Linda Barclay and to Asker and Lewey, "terrific rubber-hand guinea pigs."

## Core Positions (from *The Predictive Mind*, 2013)

- **PEM as the one mechanism.** The brain is "a sophisticated hypothesis-testing mechanism, which is constantly involved in minimizing the error of its predictions of the sensory input it receives from the world" (p. 1). Sensory input does not shape perception directly; it is "feedback to the queries issued by the brain" (p. 2). See [[Concepts/Prediction Error Minimization (Hohwy)]].
- **Perception is causal inference.** From effects available inside the skull the brain infers hidden worldly causes, by Bayes, hierarchically, Helmholtzian and unconscious. Perceptual content *is* "the predictions of the currently best hypothesis about the world" (p. 48). See [[Concepts/Perception as Causal Inference (Hohwy)]].
- **Expected precision is attention.** "Attention is nothing but optimization of precision expectations in hierarchical predictive coding" (p. 71). This identification is already in Hohwy 2013; Clark later popularizes it. See [[Concepts/Precision-Weighting (Clark)]].
- **Action is the other direction of fit.** Change the model to fit the input, or change the input to fit the model. Action cannot itself select good hypotheses; perception cannot itself reduce surprisal. They must alternate. See [[Concepts/Active Inference and Action (Clark)]].
- **Binding, penetrability, illness.** Binding is shallow common-cause inference. Cognitive penetrability is real but restricted by early PE suppression and grain. Delusions vs autism are opposite expected-precision errors. See [[Concepts/Binding as Inference (Hohwy)]], [[Concepts/Cognitive Penetrability (Hohwy)]].
- **Misrepresentation and the Bayesian room.** Content is what a parameter carries on average; a Searle-style room that minimizes PE *does* perceive if mutual information rises. Rule-following is answered by existing = occupying unsurprising states. See [[Concepts/Misrepresentation and the Bayesian Room (Hohwy)]].
- **Hard problem declined.** He describes which states are conscious (the winning first-order inference) and why they are unified (one active-inference policy). He is "not intending to touch upon… the 'hard problem'" (p. 202). A PEM creature matching us neuron for neuron need not be phenomenally conscious. This is not Clark's later deflationism. Ignition is borrowed from [[Thinkers/Stanislas Dehaene]] (now primary: [[Sources/Consciousness and the Brain - Stanislas Dehaene (2014)]]) and given a PEM job. See [[Concepts/Perceptual Unity and Ignition (Hohwy)]], [[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]].
- **Seclusion.** The mind is "solidly set over against the rest of the world," isolated "behind the veil of sensory input" (pp. 238, 258). Clark-style tools and niches raise the precision of the world-to-senses *channel*; they do not extend the mind. PEM is optimized "relative to just the nervous system" (p. 241). See [[Concepts/Secluded Mind and the Sensory Boundary (Hohwy)]].
- **Self as sensory trajectory.** Emotion is interoceptive inference; introspection is inference on mental causes; privacy is for social Bayes; the self is an "agent-bound sensory trajectory" (p. 256). See [[Concepts/Self as Sensory Trajectory (Hohwy)]].

## How Hohwy Relates to Prior Thinkers in the Vault

- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: same Helmholtz/Friston family; opposite moral about the mind's location. Hohwy names the split at p. 7 (Clark 2012/2013 "taking the framework in different directions"). *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016) is now primary as that direction: Clark reports Hohwy's "evidentiary boundary" and refuses the moral (Ch 6; "Don't Fear the Demon"). 2023 then popularizes "perfect internal organs." See [[Contradictions/Hohwy vs Clark - Secluded Inference vs Extended Mind]].
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Friston]]**: Hohwy's acknowledged technical source (Part I; comments on every chapter). Philosophical packaging — normativity of priors, phenomenology, "world is the truth," seclusion, the Bayesian room — is Hohwy's.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: prior conceptions organize the chaotic sensory manifold; Hohwy quotes A51/B75. Helmholtz is the hinge: how, on a Kantian picture, "we escape from the world of the sensations of our own nervous system into the world of real things."
- **Helmholtz**: "we sense the effects, never the external objects." Unconscious perceptual inference is the core idea Hohwy says the modern formal work still implements.
- **[[Thinkers/Hume]]** and **[[Thinkers/Judea Pearl]]**: causation as regularity *and* intervention (Hume, Lewis, Woodward, Pearl 1988/2000) is what unconscious perceptual inference extracts.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]**: ch. 8 claims a naturalistic answer to the rule-following paradox — existents occupy unsurprising states; "dying becomes the ultimate misperception" (p. 181). See [[Concepts/Rule-Following (Wittgenstein)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]** and **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: Hohwy cites Chalmers 1996 to *decline* the hard problem and keeps conceivability open. Dennett appears in ch. 12 on the self, not as a deflationary ally. Clark later takes the Dennett side; Hohwy does not.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: Hohwy's secluded internal model is a computationalism Dreyfus would still reject, but it is not GOFAI. The interesting fork is with Clark, who tried to keep Dreyfus's world-involving mind.
- **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: collaborator (Palmer–Hohwy–Seth 2015; Hohwy & Seth 2020). Hohwy 2013 already used Seth 2012 on interoceptive emotion. Same family, third geography: Hohwy secludes, Clark extends, Seth biologizes. Hohwy declines the hard problem; Seth's real problem tries to dissolve it. FEP is, for both, "mathematical philosophy" rather than a consciousness theory.
