# Robert Nozick (1938–2002)

> Harvard philosopher whose *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974) is the vault's first primary libertarian treatise: individuals have rights so strong they raise the question of what, if anything, the state may do.

## Life and Context

The 1974 book is the source. Its first nine chapters were written 1971–72 while Nozick was a Fellow at the Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences at Palo Alto (“a minimally structured academic institution bordering on individualist anarchy”). Chapter 10 was first given as an APA symposium paper on “Utopia and Utopianism” in 1969. The whole manuscript was rewritten in summer 1973.

Acknowledgments name the book's interlocutors: Barbara Nozick; Michael Walzer; W. V. Quine, Derek Parfit, and Gilbert Harman (whole manuscript); John Rawls and Frank Michelman (Ch. 7); Alan Dershowitz (Part I); Ronald Dworkin (competing protective agencies); Burton Dreben; the Society for Ethical and Legal Philosophy. A conversation about six years earlier with Murray Rothbard “stimulated [his] interest in individualist anarchist theory.” Still earlier, arguments with Bruce Goldberg “led me to take libertarian views seriously enough to want to refute them, and so to pursue the subject further.”

The preface records a conversion: he first found libertarian conclusions “apparently callous” and wanted to refute them; “with reluctance” he became convinced. He writes in the mode of contemporary epistemology and metaphysics — counterexamples, structural conditions, startling theses — and refuses the “finished, complete, and elegant whole.” He flags what the book does *not* contain: a precise theory of the moral basis of rights, of retributive punishment, or a fully specified statement of the three entitlement principles.

In the vault he had existed only as a reported target: Chalmers answers the experience machine; Clark borrows the title. This ingest is the primary.

## Core Positions (from *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*)

**Rights first.** “Individuals have rights, and there are things no person or group may do to them (without violating their rights).” Political philosophy begins with the anarchist’s question: why any state at all? Moral philosophy sets the boundary of political philosophy. See [[Concepts/Side Constraints (Nozick)]].

**From Locke’s anarchy to the minimal state.** Starting from something close to [[Concepts/State of Nature, Property, and Revolution (Locke)|Locke’s state of nature]], mutual-protection associations and then market agencies arise. A dominant agency appears by an [[Concepts/Invisible-Hand Explanation of the State (Nozick)|invisible-hand]] process. It is not yet a state (no announced monopoly; only paying clients are protected). Once it may prohibit independents’ risky private enforcement, the [[Concepts/Minimal State and Ultraminimal State (Nozick)|principle of compensation]] requires it to protect those it disadvantages. The dominant agency then satisfies the two marks of a state without anyone intending to found one, and without special rights. The individualist anarchist is answered: the transitions violate no side constraints.

**No more extensive state.** [[Concepts/Entitlement Theory (Nozick)|Entitlement theory]] (justice in acquisition, transfer, and rectification) is historical and unpatterned. [[Arguments/Wilt Chamberlain Argument (Nozick)|Liberty upsets patterns]]. Taxation of labor earnings is on a par with forced labor. Rawls’s original position cannot yield historical principles; the difference principle does not take seriously the distinction between persons. Equality, envy, workers’ control, and Marxian exploitation fail as grounds for a larger state. **Demoktesis** is a hypothetical history in which a more-than-minimal democracy is ownership of people by the people. See [[Contradictions/Nozick vs Rawls - Historical Entitlement vs Patterned Justice]].

**Utopia = the framework.** What can be saved from the utopian tradition is a [[Concepts/Framework for Utopia (Nozick)|filter of voluntary communities]], equivalent to the minimal state. The chapter “starts (and stands) independently” of Parts I–II and converges. Last line of the book: “How *dare* any state or group of individuals do more. Or less.”

**Experience machine.** Offered in Ch. 3 as a reason we are not hedonists about what matters (we want to *do*, to *be*, and to contact a deeper-than-man-made reality). Local job in the book: blocking “felt from the inside” as the sole limit on treating animals. Reception (Chalmers, Clark) has used it for other purposes. See [[Concepts/Experience Machine (Nozick)]].

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Locke]]**: explicit starting point (Second Treatise §§4–13, 123–126; money §§46–47, 50; compact §95). Locke was wrong that a compact is needed for civil society, as he was wrong that consent was needed to invent money. Mixing-labor is rejected as stated; the “enough and as good” proviso is restated more weakly. The moral basis of the law of nature is postponed — “we here are following the respectable tradition of Locke.”
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Hobbes]]**: the Hobbesian worst-case is the wrong comparison class. Investigate the *best realistic anarchy*. The state that emerges is night-watchman, not Leviathan, and has no special rights.
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]**: side constraints are the political form of the Formula of Humanity. “Individuals are inviolable.” See [[Concepts/Formula of Humanity as End in Itself (Kant)]].
- **[[Thinkers/John Stuart Mill]]**: anti-paternalism is shared (the state may not prohibit activities for people’s *own* good). Utility-maximization and patterned distribution are rejected; “experiments of living” reappear as the utopian framework.
- **John Rawls** (not a vault primary): the named opponent of Part II. Lavish praise (“unmatched since Mill”); structural critique. The vault’s first sustained treatment of *A Theory of Justice* is one-sided. See the contradiction page.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]**: Ch. 8 attacks the labor theory of value and the nonaccess story of exploitation; class struggle and historical materialism are barely treated.
- **[[Thinkers/James M. Buchanan]]** / [[Thinkers/Gordon Tullock]]: adjacent public-choice machinery (49/2/49 voting; free-riding; consent). Hart/Rawls “principle of fairness” is rejected; unanimous consent remains necessary. Buchanan’s veil of *uncertainty* is not discussed; Rawls’s veil of *ignorance* is.
- **[[Thinkers/W.V.O. Quine]]**: named reader of the whole manuscript; Harvard colleague.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]** / [[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: later reception of the experience machine — VR as reality machine; perception as prediction machine. Different theses.
- **[[Thinkers/Hilary Putnam]]** (named only): Pritchard and Ranalli (2016) list Nozick 1981 (*Philosophical Explanations*, not in the vault) with Dretske as a **closure-denier** facing the epistemological BIV argument Putnam recasts as semantic. Different book from *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*.
- **[[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]]** (reception, 2013): ancestor atmosphere, not a source. Berlin’s lecture was *used* like Friedman/Hayek non-interference; Crowder insists Berlin is a social democrat who builds a welfare-state case from negative liberty (IN, 38–39). Nozick radicalizes the liberty/conditions split: need is not a liberty-claim. Do not identify them. See [[Concepts/Liberty versus the Conditions of Liberty (Berlin)]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Historical entitlement vs patterned / end-state justice — see [[Contradictions/Nozick vs Rawls - Historical Entitlement vs Patterned Justice]]. Rawls is reported, not primary.
- > [!warning] Side constraints vs aggregative utility (including “utilitarianism of rights”) — see [[Contradictions/Utilitarian Consequentialism vs. Kantian Deontology]].
- > [!warning] A third pole on [[Contradictions/Hobbesian Absolutism vs. Lockean Constitutionalism]]: Lockean anarchy that *backs into* a minimal state without a compact and without special sovereign rights.
- Open in the book itself: the moral basis of rights; a theory of original acquisition (form only); rectification (“I do not know of a thorough… treatment”); children and exit in utopia; controls on a central authority.
