# I. A. Richards

> Literary critic and semanticist who, with [[Thinkers/C. K. Ogden]], wrote *The Meaning of Meaning* (1923). His half of the programme is the [[Concepts/Symbolic and Emotive Functions of Language (Ogden-Richards)|emotive function]] of language — poetry, criticism, and education — given the same theoretical foundation the book attempts for the symbolic.

## Profile

- **In the source**: co-author throughout; prefaces signed with Ogden from Cambridge, 1923–1946. Visiting Professor at Peking, 1929–30 (third-edition preface).
- **Companion works named in the prefaces** (not ingested): *Principles of Literary Criticism* (the emotive-function counterpart to this book); *Science and Poetry*; *Practical Criticism* ("an educational application of Chapter X"); *Mencius on the Mind* (translation and multiple definition); *Basic Rules of Reason*; *Coleridge on Imagination* (emotive language revalued); *Interpretation in Teaching*; *How to Read a Page*.
- **In the vault**: first appearance. Supplies the literary half of a distinction the vault previously met only in its 1936 ethical specialization ([[Concepts/Emotive Theory of Ethics (Ayer)]]). Ch. VII's sixteen uses of "beauty" and the claim-to-truth test are his characteristic instruments.

## Core Positions (from *The Meaning of Meaning*, with Ogden)

- **Aesthetics is not one subject.** If people are not talking about the same thing, lack of correlation among theories of Beauty is unsurprising. Multiple definition (sixteen routes) replaces the hunt for a common quality in Art.
- **Symbolic vs emotive is a principled cut, not an empirical separation.** The functions "usually occur together" but are distinct. Test: "Is this true or false in the ordinary strict scientific sense?" If the question is clearly irrelevant, the use is emotive.
- **A poem tells us, or should tell us, nothing.** It induces a fitting attitude. Truth of its constituent statements may be "instrumental only and subsidiary." Parallel vocabularies of Truth/Reality/Universality for the two uses are "totally distinct as symbols."
- **Word-free vs word-dependent speakers** (Ch. X) matter for criticism: some thinkers can dispense with the word; for others the word *is* the reference-context. Psittacism (words without reference) is not the same as word-dependence.
- **Education is the practical side of the science of symbolism** — later *Practical Criticism*.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/C. K. Ogden]]**: shared triangle, canons, and attack on word-magic. Richards owns the literary/educational sequels; Ogden owns notation, Bentham, Basic English.
- **[[Thinkers/A.J. Ayer]]**: takes the Ch. VII test into ethics. Richards had already said `good` "stands for nothing whatever" when used as Moore's indefinable.
- **[[Thinkers/William James]]**: definition IX of meaning is "practical consequences"; they report it, they do not adopt it as *the* theory of meaning.
- **Croce, Bergson, Coleridge**: Ch. VII interlocutors. Croce forces one meaning onto Art + perception; Bergson's "virtual knowledge" is denied the name of knowledge; Coleridge is later re-read in *Coleridge on Imagination*.
