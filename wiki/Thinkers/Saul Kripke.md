# Saul Kripke

> Saul A. Kripke (1940–2022), American logician and philosopher. The vault's primary voice for the claim that ordinary names are rigid designators, that metaphysical necessity comes apart from a priority, and that the Frege–Russell description theory fails both as meaning and as reference-fixing.

## Summary

Kripke enters the vault as the missing primary behind a cluster of reception pages: Chalmers's two-dimensional semantics, Nagel's note 11 on the essential character of pain, and the already-polarized metre-bar debate with Wittgenstein. Those pages cited *Naming and Necessity* (1972/1980) without having read it.

The 1980 Harvard monograph is three Princeton lectures (20, 22, 29 January 1970), first printed in Davidson & Harman, *Semantics of Natural Language* (1972), reissued with a conservative preface. The preface locates the leading ideas in 1963–64 work that grew out of the model theory of modal logic. Leibniz's law plus `(x) □ (x = x)` already made identity an internal relation: if `a` and `b` are rigid designators, `a = b`, if true, is necessary. The "dark doctrine" of contingent identity between *objects* is a confusion — uniquely identifying properties can coincide contingently; objects cannot be contingently identical (Preface, pp. 3–5).

This is *not* the Kripke of *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language*. McDowell's quarrel with a "sceptical paradox" is a different book. Do not merge them.

## Genesis (from the 1980 Preface)

- Formal modal logic first; then the realization that ordinary names *are* rigid, against the received view that contingent identities between names show they cannot be.
- Distinction that opened the work: using a description to **give a meaning** vs using it to **fix a reference**. Description theory rejected first as meaning, later (Lecture II) even as the typical way reference is fixed. The same remarks then extend to natural-kind terms.
- Tribute to the Frege–Russell edifice: "marvelous internal coherence" explains its long appeal; he does not rank it among ideas that never appealed (pp. 5–6).
- Rigidity is a claim about the truth-conditions of *simple* sentences in counterfactual situations ("Aristotle was fond of dogs"), not a scope convention for modal operators. Dummett's reduction of rigidity to wide scope is "a technical error" (pp. 10–12). Homonymy (Aristotle the philosopher vs Onassis) is irrelevant to the rigidity question once a reading is fixed.
- Possible worlds are **possible states or histories**, the school-probability sample space blown large — thirty-six dice states, not thirty-five phantom dice-pairs in a never-never land, and not a problem of "transworld identification." Stipulate (die A, 6; die B, 5); do not inspect a distant planet and ask which die is which (pp. 15–20).
- No official doctrine on what 'propositions' codesignative names express in epistemic contexts. "Hesperus is Phosphorus" can raise an empirical issue while "Hesperus is Hesperus" cannot; that already blocks universal substitutivity. Belief puzzles are left to "A Puzzle About Belief" (1979). Names are rigid *de jure*; the monograph often states only the weaker rigidity claim (p. 21 n. 21).

## Key Claims / Positions

- **Names are rigid designators** (Lecture I, p. 48). Possible worlds are stipulated, not inspected. Rigidity is not a scope convention.
- **A priori ≠ necessary.** Both necessary a posteriori (Hesperus is Phosphorus; gold is atomic number 79; heat is molecular motion) and contingent a priori (the standard metre; 100°C; some baptismal fixings) exist.
- **The Frege–Russell / cluster theory is false** as meaning and, except at rare initial baptisms, as reference-fixing. Gödel/Schmidt: the vote does not determine the referent.
- **A better picture, not a theory**: initial baptism (ostension or description) then a chain of communication; the receiver intends the same reference. The baptismal description fixes reference and is not synonymous. Madagascar-style shifts are named in the addenda.
- **Necessity of origin**: this woman could not have come from a totally different sperm and egg; this table not from another hunk of matter (n. 56 flagged as problematic in the 1980 preface).
- **Natural-kind terms** behave like names. Identifying marks are neither necessary nor sufficient (fool's gold / fool's water). Scientific identifications are necessary a posteriori.
- **Identity thesis**: if pain = C-fiber stimulation, the identity is necessary. The heat analogy fails because pain is picked out by being pain. Materialism then requires mental facts to follow from physical facts by necessity. Dualism is not thereby entailed; the problem is left open.

## Relation to Other Vault Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Bertrand Russell]]**: principal target of the description theory of names. Russell analyzes "Aristotle was fond of dogs" as a non-rigid description ("the last great philosopher of antiquity…"); counterfactually that would make someone else's dog-fondness the truth-condition of the sentence (Preface, pp. 6–7).
- **[[Thinkers/W.V.O. Quine]]**: the book's other pole on modality. Quine treats de re necessity as unintelligible; Kripke treats it as the primitive notion the model theory made clear. Not a synthesis.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]**: the metre bar (*PI* §50) is Kripke's exhibit of the contingent a priori. The rule-following "sceptical paradox" is *not* this book.
- **[[Thinkers/Thomas Nagel]]**: note 11 of the 1974 bat essay says the view is "closely related" to *Naming and Necessity* on the essential subjective character of pain. Lecture III is the contact.
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]**: inherits the a-posteriori-necessity apparatus and then *domesticates* it with two-dimensional semantics. Primary/secondary intension is Chalmers's vocabulary, not Kripke's.
- **Frege** (no thinker page): co-source, with Russell, of the description theory.
- **Mill** (no thinker page): the "purely Millian" picture (only the referent contributes) is closer than Frege–Russell, but Kripke "never intended to go so far" as universal substitutivity (Preface, p. 20).
- **Putnam** (no thinker page): reported ally on natural-kind terms; do not invent a Putnam page from this source.
