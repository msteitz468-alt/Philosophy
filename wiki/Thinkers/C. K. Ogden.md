# C. K. Ogden

> Cambridge linguist and editor who, with [[Thinkers/I. A. Richards]], wrote *The Meaning of Meaning* (1923): a science of symbolism whose central diagram is the [[Concepts/Triangle of Reference (Ogden-Richards)|triangle of reference]] and whose diagnostic is [[Concepts/Word Magic (Ogden-Richards)|word-magic]].

## Profile

- **In the source**: Magdalene College, Cambridge; general editor of the International Library of Psychology, Philosophy and Scientific Method (the series that also published Wittgenstein's *Tractatus*). Prefaces signed from Cambridge, 1923–1946.
- **Companion works named in the prefaces** (not ingested): *Word Magic* (historical/philological expansion of Ch. II); *The Meaning of Psychology*; *Basic English* (English adapted as a universal language; *Psyche* IX–X, 1928–30); *Bentham's Theory of Fictions*; *Opposition* (an aspect of definition). Later notes point to *Psyche* XVI–XVIII.
- **In the vault**: first appearance. Co-author of the missing ancestor of [[Thinkers/A.J. Ayer|Ayer]]'s [[Concepts/Emotive Theory of Ethics (Ayer)|emotive ethics]] and of the vault's first systematic attack on the idea that words stand directly for things. Ogden also sits behind the vault's *Tractatus* as series editor and English translator — a fact this book does not itself advertise, but the International Library list at the front of the volume does.

## Core Positions (from *The Meaning of Meaning*, with Richards)

- **Words mean nothing by themselves.** They stand for anything only when a thinker uses them. The word–thing relation is never direct: only [[Concepts/Triangle of Reference (Ogden-Richards)|Symbol → Reference → Referent]].
- **[[Concepts/Word Magic (Ogden-Richards)|Word-magic]]** is the conservative force of civilization: names treated as souls, Platonic Ideas as name-souls, Russell's world of Being as a verbal construction, disease-names as natural objects.
- **[[Concepts/Contextual Theory of Reference (Ogden-Richards)|Reference is causal-contextual]]**, not a unique acquaintance with propositions. Truth is whether the external context the psychological context points to actually recurs — not a second correspondence of belief to a fact-entity.
- **[[Concepts/Symbolic and Emotive Functions of Language (Ogden-Richards)|Symbolic vs emotive]]**: statement (truth-apt) vs expression/evocation of attitude. Mixing them generates philosophy, theology, and a good deal of aesthetics.
- **Practical programme**: [[Concepts/Canons of Symbolism (Ogden-Richards)|six canons]], a transferable technique of definition, and an educational reform of communication. The later Basic English project is this book's "universal scientific language" desideratum made concrete.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/I. A. Richards]]**: the other half of the collaboration. Richards takes the emotive function into *Principles of Literary Criticism* and *Practical Criticism*; Ogden takes word-magic and notation into *Word Magic*, Bentham, and Basic English.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]**: same series; Ch. V cites *Tractatus* 6.2 / 6.24 (math as pseudo-propositions). Appendix A *rejects* the picture-correspondence of propositional sign to state of affairs. Different cut from saying/showing: O&R keep a causal hook to referents and a second, emotive use.
- **[[Thinkers/Bertrand Russell]]**: Ch. II treats the *Principles of Mathematics* "term" (including chimera) as neo-Platonist word-magic. Ch. III prefers Russell's later psychological account and still denies acquaintance with propositions.
- **[[Thinkers/A.J. Ayer]]**: 1936 emotivism is the ethical specialization of the symbolic/emotive split.
- **C. S. Peirce** ([[Thinkers/C. S. Peirce]]): Appendix D is the vault's first substantial dossier — reproduced, not adopted. O&R keep a triangle, not an interpretant-series.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Direct conflict with [[Concepts/Picture Theory of Language (Wittgenstein)]] on whether symbol–world hooking is structural isomorphism. O&R: "standing for" ≠ representation; isomorphism is "unnecessary and highly improbable" on a causal theory.
- > [!warning] Relocates [[Concepts/Correspondence Theory of Truth (Russell)]]: once reference is contextual, a further belief–fact correspondence "solves the problem twice."
