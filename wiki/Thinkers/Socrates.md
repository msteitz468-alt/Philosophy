# Socrates
> The prototype of philosophy as a way of life: not a sage and not a system, but a *philo-sopher* — a lover of wisdom — whose irony, dialogue, and death teach a conversion of existence.

## Figure, Not Biography

This page is built from [[Thinkers/Pierre Hadot]]’s “The Figure of Socrates” (ch. 5 of *Philosophy as a Way of Life*), read against Plato already in the vault. Hadot is explicit: “I shall not attempt here to uncover or reconstruct the historical Socrates. Instead, what I shall try to set forth is the figure of Socrates, as it has influenced our Western tradition.” The Western figure is Plato’s *Symposium* Socrates plus Kierkegaard and [[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]. Plato and Xenophon “transformed, idealized, and deformed” him. Testimonies “hide him from us more than they reveal him.”

Attested externals Hadot treats as traditional, not as a reconstructed life: physical ugliness (Plato, Xenophon, Aristophanes); he wrote nothing; marketplace talk; the Zopyrus anecdote (via Nietzsche/Cicero). He was tried and executed in 399 BCE. See [[Thinkers/Plato]].

## What the Figure Is

Socrates **mediates** transcendent wisdom and human reality, but **is not a sage**. “It is a paradox of highly Socratic irony that Socrates was not a sage, but a ‘philo-sopher.’” The figure is “ambiguous, troubling, and strangely disconcerting,” not a Golden Mean.

The Silenus-ugliness is a **mask**. Irony is feigned ignorance and self-deprecation. He becomes a *prosopon* for later writers (Plato’s dialogues, Hamann, Kierkegaard’s pseudonyms, Nietzsche’s “semiotics”). Irony’s mechanism is *Spaltung und Verdoppelung*: two Socrates (who knows the end / who walks the path). The interlocutor is split too, identifies with **aporia**, learns nothing, and yet “has been Socrates himself… he is consciousness.”

**Maieutics** inverts master–disciple: the teacher is a learner; “we do not owe him anything.” Justice cannot be defined in words (against Hippias and the sophists); it must be **lived**. Discourse questions the person until he “give[s] an account… of his present and past life” (*Laches*).

“His philosophy was a **spiritual exercise**, an invitation to a new way of life, active reflection, and living consciousness.” The formula of ignorance means he is not a sage; like Kierkegaard “not a Christian,” he is sage only as conscious of **not** being wise. Desire from that lack makes him **Eros**. See [[Concepts/Spiritual Exercises (Hadot)]], [[Concepts/Philosophy as a Way of Life (Hadot)]], [[Sources/Symposium - Plato]].

He is *atopos* / *atopotatos*: unclassifiable, the first Western **individual**. “*They say I am atopotatos, and all I create is aporia*” (*Theaetetus*). Erotic irony parallels dialectic: pretended lover becomes beloved (Alcibiades). The *Symposium* identifies him with Eros, son of Penia and Poros: *daimon*, *atopos*, *philo-sophos*. “There is a great deal more philosophy in spiritual exercises like Socrates’ dialogues than in the construction of a philosophical system.”

## Modern Readers Hadot Uses

- **[[Thinkers/Søren Kierkegaard]]** — cobold; pseudonyms; deceive into the truth; existing thinker / the Individual; not-Christian ≈ not-sage; erotic-seducer reading of Alcibiades. Now also a primary: Johannes *de silentio* makes Socrates' ignorance infinite resignation (a match for human strength, already past cheap "faith = the immediate") and makes him the intellectual tragic hero who must have the last word. He is not the knight of faith. See [[Sources/Fear and Trembling - Kierkegaard (Hannay 1985)]], [[Concepts/Knight of Faith and Infinite Resignation (Kierkegaard)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]** — mask and educator; Silenus of many souls; *amorous hatred*: life-loving Eroticist vs dying Socrates / Dionysos / last words of the *Phaedo*; “The pathways of the most various philosophical modes of life lead back to him…” **Three** primaries, not one, in date order:
  - *The Birth of Tragedy* **§§11–15** (1872). First primary. Cultural-aesthetic type: Euripides is a mask, Socrates the daemon; **aesthetic Socratism** (“Whatever is to be beautiful must also be sensible”); instinct criticises, consciousness creates (*per defectum*); the *theoretical man* / theoretical optimist; science’s faith that thought can know and *correct* being; “Practice music, Socrates!” as the open symbol. Not yet 1888 *décadence*. See [[Sources/The Birth of Tragedy - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]], [[Concepts/Socratism and the Theoretical Man (Nietzsche)]].
  - *The Gay Science* **§340** ("The dying Socrates"). Opens with admiration — "I admire the courage and wisdom of Socrates in everything he did, said — and did not say" — then reads the last word (*Crito*, the cock for Asclepius) as "life is a disease." "Socrates *suffered life*!" "We must overcome even the Greeks." Kaufmann placed it immediately before the recurrence test (§341) as a coda.
  - *Twilight of the Idols*, **"The Problem of Socrates"** (1888). Does not replace 1872 or §340; it diagnoses the same last-words motif as *physiology* and *cites* BT 1872. Consensus of the sages that life is no good is a shared decadence, not a proof. "The value of life cannot be estimated." Socrates and Plato are "symptoms of degeneration… anti-Greek." Dialectics is a plebeian last weapon and may be *ressentiment*. Reason is invented as a "counter-tyrant" when the instincts play the tyrant. "To have to fight the instincts — that is the definition of decadence: as long as life is ascending, happiness equals instinct." He wanted the hemlock: "Socrates is no physician… here death alone is the physician." See [[Sources/Twilight of the Idols - Nietzsche (Kaufmann)]], [[Concepts/Decadence (Nietzsche)]].
  See also [[Sources/The Gay Science - Nietzsche (Kaufmann 1974)]], [[Concepts/Eternal Recurrence (Nietzsche)]].

## Relevance to Vault

Until this ingest the vault had a large Plato cluster and no Socrates page. Hadot forbids filling that hole with a reconstructed biography. The page is a hub for the *figure* that every later way-of-life claim (Stoic sage, Christian philosopher, Foucault’s *parrhesia*, Holiday’s “you’re now a philosopher”) still uses.

[[Thinkers/Epictetus]] already names Socrates (with Diogenes) as the exemplar of [[Concepts/Stoic Freedom (Eleutheria) (Epictetus)]]. [[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]] starts the genealogy of artificial reason with Socrates asking Euthyphro for an effective procedure — a different figure (the demand for definition). Hadot’s Socrates is the one who *refuses* a transmissible definition of justice.
