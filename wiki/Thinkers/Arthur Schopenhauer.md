# Arthur Schopenhauer (1788–1860)

> German philosopher. Best known for his metaphysical voluntarism and his profound **metaphysical pessimism**, characterizing the ultimate reality of the universe as a blind, striving, non-rational force called "Will."

## Life and Context

Born in Danzig (Gdańsk), Poland/Prussia, to a wealthy merchant family. Schopenhauer studied medicine and philosophy in Göttingen and Berlin. He completed his doctoral dissertation, *On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason*, in 1813, which laid the epistemological groundwork for his philosophy.

His masterwork, *The World as Will and Representation* (often translated as *The World as Will and Idea*), was published in late 1818 (dated 1819). He attempted to teach at the University of Berlin, scheduling his lectures at the same time as the famous G.W.F. Hegel, whom he detested. Hegelianism dominated, and Schopenhauer's lectures were poorly attended, prompting him to retire from academia and live as a reclusive scholar in Frankfurt.

His work remained largely ignored for decades until the publication of *Parerga and Paralipomena* in 1851 (Hayn of Berlin, 750 copies, no honorarium after Brockhaus refused), which finally brought him fame in his last decade. He does not call his own philosophy “pessimism”; Oxenford (“ultra-pessimism,” 1853) and Wundt did.

## Major Works

- *On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason* (1813 / **1847 second edition** now primary): propaedeutic to *WWR*. PSR is four a priori laws, not one. See [[Sources/On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2012)]].
- *On Vision and Colours* (1816 / **1854 second edition** now primary): colour is the qualitatively divided activity of the retina. Goethe's data; this theory. Kantian primer. See [[Sources/On Vision and Colours - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2012)]].
- *The World as Will and Representation* (1819 / **1844 Volume 2 now primary**): the four-book system. Volume 1 is already in as Haldane/Kemp *Idea*. Volume 2 (Payne 1958 / Dover 1966) is the supplements — not a second system. See [[Sources/The World as Will and Representation Volume 2 - Schopenhauer (Payne, Dover 2012)]].
- *Parerga and Paralipomena* (1851): *opera mixta* — thoughts that “could find no place in my systematic works,” “incomparably more popular than everything up till now.” Volume 1 (the *parerga*) is primary: [[Sources/Parerga and Paralipomena Volume 1 - Schopenhauer (Cambridge 2014)]]. Volume 2 (the *paralipomena*; Payne 2000) is now primary: [[Sources/Parerga and Paralipomena Volume 2 - Schopenhauer (Payne 2000)]].

## Key Philosophical Positions

* **The World as Representation**: Downstream of Kant, the world we perceive is structured by the **[[Concepts/Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason (Schopenhauer)|fourfold root]]**, not by a single template called "space, time, and causality." Becoming (causality), knowing (truth of judgments), being (space/time), acting (motivation). PSR is the mother of all sciences *inside* representation and does not apply to the thing-in-itself or to the world as a totality. 1847 drops Kant's twelve categories; everyday seeing is already the work of the understanding ([[Concepts/Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)]]).
* **The World as Will**: Schopenhauer identifies the Kantian *thing-in-itself* (noumenon) as **Will** (*Wille*). Unlike human willpower, the metaphysical Will is a single, blind, insatiable, non-rational, force that underlies all matter, life, and physical forces. 
* **Metaphysical Pessimism**: Because the Will is infinite and has no ultimate goal, it is in a state of eternal, unsatisfied striving. In living things, this manifests as desire, which leads to inevitable suffering. Once a desire is satisfied, boredom sets in, followed by new desires. Thus, life is an endless oscillation between pain and boredom.
* **Denial of the Will**: The only escapes from the suffering of the Will are:
  1. **Aesthetic Contemplation**: Temporary release through art (especially music), where we become "disinterested" observers free from the demands of desire.
  2. **Asceticism and Compassion**: Permanent quietening of the Will through recognizing that all individuals are expressions of the same unified Will, leading to universal compassion (*Mitleid*) and the systematic denial of personal desires.

## From *Parerga and Paralipomena*, Volume 1 (1851; now primary)

The first Schopenhauer primary after *WWR* I. Six essays, not one argument.

- **Ideal and real.** Descartes opened the problem; Kant made the cut; “the will alone is left as the real.” Fichte, Schelling, and Hegel are sophists, not philosophers. See [[Concepts/Doctrine of the Ideal and the Real (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Kant, thinking-through-to-the-end.** Transcendent ≠ transcendental. Schulze was right that Kant could not reach the thing-in-itself via causality. The “narrow gateway” is the body as twofold (representation *and* will). Moral theology is a “sop” / “anodyne.” Theism undermines morality; freedom “cannot lie in acting, it must lie in being” (*aseity*). His system is **immanent dogmatism**. See [[Thinkers/Kant]], [[Concepts/Canon of Pure Reason - Moral Faith (Kant)]].
- **University philosophy.** Philosophy for hire versus love of wisdom; Socrates versus sophists; Hegel as apotheosis of philistinism. Teach only logic plus a one-semester history Thales-to-Kant. See [[Concepts/University Philosophy (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Fate and spirits.** Demonstrable fatalism (necessity of acts) versus transcendent fatalism (a life composed — labeled fantasy). Spirit-seeing: facts of animal magnetism, *idealistic* not spiritualistic explanation. See [[Concepts/Transcendent Fatalism (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Spirit-Seeing and Idealistic Explanation (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Aphorisms.** Eudaemonology is an **accommodation** to the ordinary error that we exist to be happy. What one is outweighs what one has and what one represents. See [[Concepts/Eudaemonology as Accommodation (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/What One Is, Has, and Represents (Schopenhauer)]].

Self-position in the university essay: “I have sought truth, not a professorship.”

## From *WWR* Volume 2 (1844; Payne, now primary)

The 1844 supplements. Not a second system. Payne’s **representation** and **will-to-live**.

- **Idealism.** “The world is my representation” is as certain as Euclid. True philosophy must be idealistic; the world is a *phenomenon of the brain*. Berkeley found the ideal; Kant the mode. See [[Concepts/Doctrine of the Ideal and the Real (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Intellect.** Perception is understanding’s work; animals have understanding without reason; laughter is incongruity of concept and percept. Man is the *animal metaphysicum*. See [[Concepts/Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Theory of the Ludicrous (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Man's Need for Metaphysics (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Primacy of the will.** We ourselves are the thing-in-itself, but not quite naked: time still veils. Intellect is a brain-function and tool. Strong blind man / sighted lame man. See [[Concepts/Primacy of the Will in Self-Consciousness (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Aesthetics.** Genius is surplus perceptive intellect beyond service of the will. History is not a science (*Eadem, sed aliter*). Music copies the will itself, not Ideas. See [[Concepts/Genius (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/History as Particular (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Death, sex, denial.** Indestructible = will, not intellect. Sexual love is the species choosing the next generation. Affirmation is procreation; denial is *nolle*. Worst of all possible worlds (Ch. XLVI). The inborn error: we exist in order to be happy (Ch. XLIX). Philosophy is immanent; the world’s act of will is our own (Ch. L). See [[Concepts/Indestructibility of Our True Nature (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Metaphysics of Sexual Love (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Vanity and Suffering of Existence (Schopenhauer)]].

He still does not name his *system* “pessimism.” The word appears as a classification of religions (optimism vs pessimism) and as a charge he answers.

## From *Parerga and Paralipomena*, Volume 2 (1851; Payne, now primary)

The *paralipomena*: thirty-one short essays for a second visit. Not a second system. Payne’s “will-to-live.”

- **Method and intellect.** All explanation bottoms in the inexplicable. The philosopher sees the universal in the particular (will-less subject). Pantheism “states nothing” and is necessarily optimism, hence false. See [[Concepts/Doctrine of the Ideal and the Real (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Ethics and the state.** Compassion (*Mitleid*) is the only genuine moral incentive; *tat tvam asi*. Right is the negation of injury. The state yokes might to right. See [[Concepts/Compassion and Justice (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Death, vanity, denial.** After death you are what you were before birth. Vanity is the form of existence; pain is positive. Suicide is not denial. Denial is *nolle*, not annihilation. “A happy life is impossible.” See [[Concepts/Indestructibility of Our True Nature (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Vanity and Suffering of Existence (Schopenhauer)]], [[Concepts/Affirmation and Denial of the Will-to-Live (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Religion.** True at best *sensu allegorico*. No true religion *sensu proprio*. Optimism vs pessimism is the real cut among religions. See [[Concepts/Religion as Allegory (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Intellectual life.** We know only what we have thought out. Reading is thinking with someone else’s head. See [[Concepts/Thinking for Oneself (Schopenhauer)]].
- **On Women.** Primary rant, not a metaphysics. Record it; do not sanitize; do not present it as vault fact. See [[Concepts/On Women (Schopenhauer)]].

He still does not call his own philosophy “pessimism.”

## Influence and Legacy

Schopenhauer's voluntaristic metaphysics and emphasis on the non-rational had a profound influence on late 19th- and early 20th-century thought. He was the primary philosophical catalyst for **Friedrich Nietzsche** (who later rejected his pessimism but retained his voluntarism as the "Will to Power") and **Richard Wagner**.

**From Nietzsche, *The Birth of Tragedy* (1872), the first *use* of this system as scaffolding:** *WWR* I.1.3 (Maya / *principium individuationis* / rowboat) becomes Apollo; music as “immediate copy of the will itself” is endorsed at §16; *WWR* I.3.51 (lyric as will + contemplation) is already **rejected**. The use is un-Schopenhauerian: art *prevents* Buddhistic denial of the will; existence is *justified* as aesthetic phenomenon. See [[Sources/The Birth of Tragedy - Nietzsche (Kaufmann, Collection 2013)]], [[Concepts/Apollinian and Dionysian (Nietzsche)]], [[Concepts/The Will (Schopenhauer)]].

**From Nietzsche, *The Case of Wagner* §4 (1888), not from Schopenhauer's own text:** the *Ring* sailed as revolutionary optimism (Siegfried vs “old contracts”) and dashed on Schopenhauer as on a reef. Wagner then interpreted the wreck as the goal and made Brünnhilde versify Book IV of *The World as Will and Idea*. “It was the *philosopher of decadence* who allowed the *artist of decadence* to find himself.” Nietzsche includes Schopenhauer among the diseases he had to side against (Preface). This is Nietzsche’s late diagnosis, not Schopenhauer’s self-description. See [[Sources/The Case of Wagner - Nietzsche (1888)]], [[Concepts/Decadence (Nietzsche)]]. 

His concept of the blind Will driving human behavior anticipated **Sigmund Freud**'s theory of the unconscious and the id. In literature and art, his pessimism influenced writers like Leo Tolstoy, Thomas Mann, and Marcel Proust, and his philosophy of science influenced Ludwig Boltzmann and Albert Einstein.

## Relevance to Vault

Schopenhauer represents the voluntaristic, pessimistic limit of German Idealism:
- **Idealism vs. Voluntarism**: Hedges the Kantian epistemological framework in a voluntaristic direction, contrasting with the rationalist teleology of [[Thinkers/Hegel|Hegel]]'s *Geist*.
- **Pessimism vs. Optimism**: Stands in direct, unresolved tension with the rationalist optimism of [[Thinkers/Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz]]'s "best of all possible worlds." See [[Contradictions/Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism|Schopenhauer's Pessimism vs. Leibnizian Optimism]].
- **Bridge to Nietzsche**: The 1872 primary now shows the *first* use: Schopenhauer’s Will, Maya, and music-metaphysics are the scaffolding of *The Birth of Tragedy*, already resisted on lyric theory, and already turned against resignation (aesthetic justification; metaphysical solace). Later primaries then break: *The Gay Science*: **§99** (followers take the One Will, denial of the individual, pity, "dying is really the purpose of existence," and skip the intellectual conscience); **§151** (the "metaphysical need" is not the origin of religion); **§357** (first inexorable German atheist; answer still stuck in Christian-ascetic perspectives); **§370** (romantic sufferer from *impoverishment*, not Dionysian over-fullness). The 1887 Preface's "sick thinkers" typology fits him without naming him. *Twilight*: morality "as it has in the end been formulated once more by Schopenhauer, as ‘negation of the will to life’" is "the very instinct of decadence" ([[Concepts/Morality as Anti-Nature (Nietzsche)]]); Four Errors §6 quotes *WWR* II on pain as desert; Ancients §5: tragedy is a *rebuttal* of Schopenhauerian pessimism, not a proof of it. See [[Sources/The Gay Science - Nietzsche (Kaufmann 1974)]], [[Sources/Twilight of the Idols - Nietzsche (Kaufmann)]], [[Concepts/The Great Health (Nietzsche)]].
- ***Beyond Good and Evil* (1886, now complete).** §16: “I will” is a superstition of immediate certainty, like “I think.” §19: Schopenhauer treated the will as absolutely known; it is “a unity only in name.” §47: his start is “how is the saint possible?” §56: opposite of Schopenhauer/Buddha is the world-approving man calling *da capo*. §186: *neminem laede* is sentimental in a world whose “essence is Will to Power.” See [[Sources/Beyond Good and Evil - Nietzsche (AmazonClassics Edition)]], [[Concepts/Soul as Social Structure (Nietzsche)]].
- ***Nietzsche Contra Wagner* (1888/89).** With Wagner he is an *antipode*: both “deny life, they both slander it.” Nietzsche says he had bestowed *himself* on them. Schopenhauer is more at home in the France of taste than in Germany. See [[Sources/Nietzsche Contra Wagner - Nietzsche (Ludovici, Collection 2013)]].
- ***Human, All Too Human* 1878 (first break, still not Will to Power).** §26: Schopenhauer as *reaction* — a Christian *Weltbetrachtung* resurrected, useful for justice, then to be left for the enlightenment line (Petrarch, Erasmus, Voltaire). §39: he infers *intelligibeln Freiheit* from guilt-depression; Nietzsche: remorse exists only because man *deems* himself free. §110: the reaction overrated religion as allegorical deepest knowledge (Schopenhauer's *sensus allegoricus*); "never has a religion… contained a truth." The 1886 Preface admits he had "shut [his] eyes to Schopenhauer's blind will towards ethic." Do not file 1878 as the later affirmation of Will to Power. See [[Sources/Human, All Too Human - Nietzsche (Zimmern, Collection excerpt)]], [[Concepts/Historical Philosophising and the Chemistry of Concepts (Nietzsche)]].

## From *On the Fourfold Root* (1847)

The vault's first Schopenhauer *epistemological* primary. 1813 ≠ 1847. Wolff's formula kept as abstraction; four roots specified; proof of PSR is circular; motivation is causality seen from within; no ground *per se*. Do not file this book as a denial of sufficient reason.

## From *On Vision and Colours* (1816 / 1854)

Composed 1815; Goethe kept the manuscript on a Rhine tour; printed Easter 1816. 1830 Latin *Theoria colorum physiologica*; 1851 *Parerga* II colour chapter as storehouse; 1854 second edition folds those additions back. Two voices, as in 1847 FR. Self-position: Goethe's student and champion, not epigone — Goethe still called him an opponent for one departure and wrote the student/teacher epigram.

- **Vision.** All intuition is intellectual. Without understanding a vista is palette-blobs on the retina. Stereoscope as proof. Illusion (*Schein*) deceives understanding; error deceives reason. Animals have understanding; plants move from stimuli; inorganic bodies from equal causes. See [[Concepts/Intellectual Nature of Intuition (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Colour.** Qualitatively divided activity of the retina. Complements restore white. Polarity is successive and retinal, not a polarity of light. *Skieron* derived *a priori*. True urphenomenon = the retina's capacity to split. Goethe denied white-from-colours too far; Newton was right only in wording. Do not import Will-as-noumenon. See [[Concepts/Physiological Theory of Colour (Schopenhauer)]].
- **Kantian primer.** "The completely subjective essence of colour" is a primer for the a priori subjective forms of all cognition. The brain stands like a wall before the being of things.
