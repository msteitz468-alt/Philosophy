# Joseph de Maistre

> Savoyard diplomat and Catholic polemicist. This page is **Berlin’s reading** in *The Crooked Timber of Humanity* (essay drafted ~1940s–1960, first published 1990), not a Maistre primary. Berlin’s thesis: Maistre “spoke the language of the past, but the content… presaged the future.” He is not Mussolini, and he did not invent fascism.

## Profile

Textbook Maistre (Sainte-Beuve, Faguet) is a simple Catholic reactionary: “Pope, King and Hangman,” a medieval doctor born late. [[Thinkers/Isaiah Berlin]]’s counter: compared with Constant, Staël, Bentham, he is “ultra-modern, born not after but before his time.” Influence in his lifetime was narrow (ultramontanes, Savoy). The “attitude of mind” waited a century.

Works Berlin uses: *Considérations sur la France* (1797), *Soirées de Saint-Pétersbourg*, *Examen de Bacon*, *Quatre chapitres sur la Russie*.

## Core Positions (as Berlin reads them)

- **No such thing as man.** With Burke he rejects abstract *man*: “I have seen Frenchmen, Italians, Russians… as for *man*, I declare that I have never met him.” Rousseau’s “Man is born free” is “the opposite of the truth.” Savages are “refuse,” not noble prototypes. Rights of Man are chimeras in whose name massacres occur.
- **Nature as slaughterhouse.** “The whole earth, perpetually steeped in blood, is nothing but a vast altar” until “the death of death.” War, not planning, completes the decree when courts cannot shed enough blood.
- **The executioner as keystone.** “All greatness, all power, all subordination rest on the executioner… Remove this mysterious agent… society disappears.” Men are saved only by “the terror of authority.”
- **Sovereignty is papal and indivisible.** No society without State, no State without sovereignty, no sovereignty without infallibility. Government is “a true religion”; individual reason must be “annihilated” in “the reason of the nation.”
- **Only the irrational lasts.** Reason is a “flickering light.” Hereditary monarchy, marriage, family endure *because* they cannot be defended. Prejudice and sacred language store ancestral wisdom; to clarify is to dehydrate.
- ***La secte*.** Protestants, Jews, scientists, democrats, Jacobins, liberals, intellectuals — “the first” precise enemy-list of “the great counter-revolutionary movement that culminated in Fascism.”

## Fascism Claim — and the Hedges

Berlin hears “the paranoiac world of modern Fascism,” “the heart of the totalitarianisms, of both left and right,” “the earliest note of… militant anti-rational Fascism.” Maistre is “the first theorist” of the line that “culminated in Charles Maurras, a precursor of the Fascists.” The visualised society “became actual.”

**Hedges Berlin actually states:**

- Maistre is **not a Romantic**. He “detested the Romantic spirit” and, “like Charles Maurras and T. S. Eliot,” stood for “classicism, monarchy and the Church.”
- Catholic **ultramontane**, not pagan vitalist. He “detested” military government (*bâtonocratie*) even while glorifying war.
- He is **not Mussolini**. Berlin never says he invented fascism.
- Letters (Besançon, Gastaldo, 1990): Maistre would not have been happy with Mussolini/Hitler (“roughnecks”), but attraction to Robespierre/Napoleon, iron discipline, *la secte qui ne dort jamais* still makes him a precursor of totalitarian repression. Italian Fascism and to a degree Nazism compound Maistrean authoritarianism **plus** Herderian populism via Fichte. Origins are “insecure, perilous.”
- The vision is “detestable to those who truly value human freedom.” Berlin praises “depth and accuracy of his insight” into unregarded motives and calls him a “terrifying prophet” — **antidote**, not program.

## How This Relates to Prior Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Jacques Rousseau]]:** type-case of the Enlightenment’s “born free.” Maistre inverts it. Adds a second Berlin–Rousseau front (utopia / nature) beside the 1958 “forced to be free” cut. See [[Contradictions/Berlin vs Rousseau - Negative Liberty vs Forced to Be Free]].
- **Burke:** shared anti-abstraction. Berlin splits them: Maistre more theocratic, no contract/compromise.
- **Bonald:** admired “twin”; Berlin splits them (Bonald “obsolescent” medievalism).
- **[[Thinkers/Hegel]]:** cunning of reason / historicism as a *different* way of making violence intelligible.
- **[[Thinkers/Karl Marx]]:** tough-minded successor in *cast of mind*, not doctrine.
- **[[Thinkers/Johann Gottfried Herder]]:** Gastaldo letter — *Blut und Boden* “does to some degree… descend from Herder” though Herder has no *Blut* and preached tolerance. Do not smear Herder with Maistre.
- **[[Concepts/Counter-Enlightenment (Berlin)]]:** Maistre is the dark pole. Vico/Herder are the healthy fruit.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Reception, not primary. Do not mark Maistre’s books ingested.
- > [!warning] “Origins of Fascism” is Berlin’s title. Record affinity + first enemy-catalogue + Maurras line. Do not upgrade to “Maistre invented fascism.”
- Berlin’s admiration for the psychology vs rejection of the politics is the load-bearing ambivalence. Do not flatten either way.
