# Eleanor Rosch
> Eleanor Rosch, Berkeley psychologist; co-author of *The Embodied Mind* and writer of the 2016 introduction that insists the book "is about something real" and splits enaction into a samsaric phase and a groundless one.

## Summary

As this volume presents her: professor of psychology at Berkeley; friend of Varela for many years; joined the book in 1989 when Thompson was a visiting postdoctoral scholar there. Inside the 1991 text she is also an *object* of the argument: Berlin & Kay / Rosch–Dani color categories and basic-level categorization are evidence that color and categories are "experiential, consensual, and embodied," not recoveries of a pregiven world (ch. 8).

The 2016 introduction is a different voice from Thompson's. It opens: "This book is about something real. For that reason it does not fit easily into any of the usual academic disciplines. It is not science, it is not philosophy, it is not phenomenology, and it is certainly not Buddhism, although it touches on all of these."

## 2016 Positions Distinctive to Rosch

- **Two phases of enaction.** Phase 1: the self-organizing living body bringing forth a world — matching the five *skandhas*, and *samsara*. Phase 2: "another mode of knowing not based on an observer and observed" — [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)|groundlessness]]. Kabat-Zinn quotes this as the book's core.
- **Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty romanticize samsara.** Their engaged, pre-reflective agent is still the samsaric world-maker. Phase 2 requires later Buddhism.
- **Dreyfus's "looking changes experience" objection confuses path with result.** Thompson accepts Dreyfus; Rosch does not give the objection the same force.
- **"Buddhist modernism" as academic delegitimation is rejected.** Living sanghas use doctrine as meditation and path. (Thompson uses the term descriptively and critically of the 1991 book's own assumptions.)
- **Mind = brain is metaphysics, not science.** She cites yogic subtle body, *tummo*, mind-to-mind transmission, and death samadhi as paradigm-challenging. Enaction risks "body-based reductionist materialism." Where present science meets experience, "the science simply takes over like a colonial ruler. This is body imperialism, not dialogue."
- **Mindfulness-based research** often cannot say what is being practiced (see Appendix A). MBSR and laboratory "mindfulness" are not interchangeable with the book's method.

These 2016 frames (*samsara* as phase label, *tummo*, "mentons") are apparatus, not 1991 chapter claims.
