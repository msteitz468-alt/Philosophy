# Robert Brandom

> Sellars's successor at Pittsburgh and his most systematic interpreter; author of the Study Guide to the Harvard edition of [[Sources/Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind - Wilfrid Sellars (1956)|EPM]] and of the **inferentialist** program that makes good on what Sellars called his "promissory notes."

## Biography and Historical Context

Robert Brandom (b. 1950) teaches at the University of Pittsburgh. His Study Guide to EPM (1997) grew, as he explains, out of "notes that follow… developed over the years to help my graduate and advanced undergraduate students at the University of Pittsburgh see their way through the textual trees to the Sellarsian forest." He describes them as "meant to provide only a first take on the material, to indicate the most general outlines of the structure of the essay and of the thought behind it," and is candid that "many philosophically interesting issues and discussions have been brushed past." He cites section numbers of EPM in single brackets, his own in double.

Rorty's Introduction places him as the one who "begins, so to speak, where Sellars's essay leaves off": *Making It Explicit* (1994) "makes good on a lot of what Sellars called his 'promissory notes,'" ending with "the complete and explicit interpretive equilibrium exhibited by a community whose members adopt the explicit discursive stance toward each other" — an equilibrium Brandom identifies with "social self-consciousness," and a vision of "one great Community comprising members of all particular communities — the Community of those who say 'we' with and to someone."

## Core Contributions (as they appear in the Study Guide)

### Inferentialism
Brandom reads Sellars as an **inferentialist** about conceptual content, against **representationalism**. Rorty adopts the contrast: representationalists (Descartes, Locke) "take concepts to be representations (or putative representations) of reality," whereas inferentialists (Leibniz, Kant, Frege, the later Wittgenstein, Sellars) take them, as Kant did, as *rules* specifying how something is to be done.

His statement of the position in the Guide: "the conceptual content expressed by a sentence (what is believed) essentially involves its potential as a premise and as a conclusion of inferences. Unless one can employ noninferentially acquired beliefs as the premises of inferences leading to further beliefs, their acquisition does not qualify as acquiring beliefs… at all. On this inferentialist picture of conceptual content, one cannot have one concept without having many inferentially interrelated ones."

Crucially this does *not* abolish observation: "the concepts of observables are concepts that have noninferential, reporting, uses. The requirement is only that the concepts that can be used to make noninferential reports must also be available to be applied inferentially." This is Brandom's gloss on [[Concepts/The Logic of Looks (Sellars)|Sellars's "whole battery of concepts"]] holism.

### The Two-Ply Account of Observation
Brandom's reconstruction of Sellars §35 separates two independent components of an observation report:

1. A **reliable differential responsive disposition** — "it must be the expression of a differential responsive disposition. But photocells and parrots could satisfy this condition, which shows that so far only the responsive dispositions part has been specified."
2. The **epistemic/normative dimension** — "of endorsement, of undertaking inferentially articulated commitments; of producing a performance with a distinctive kind of authority."

Merely "classifying the stimuli according to the repeatable responses" is not enough: "conceptual awareness requires something beyond… classifying by differential response."

He applies this to a memorable case. A physicist reports a mu meson; challenged, he cites "the distinctively hooked vapor trail in the bubble chamber," from which the mu meson can be inferred. "But to say that is not to say that the original report was the product of an inference after all. It was the exercise of a reliable differential responsive disposition keyed to a whole chain of reliably covarying events, which includes mu mesons, hooked vapor trails, and retinal images. What makes it a report *of* mu mesons, and not of hooked vapor trails or retinal images, is the **inferential role** of the concept the physicist noninferentially applies." Hence: "if mu meson is the concept the physicist applies noninferentially, then if he is sufficiently reliable, that is what he *sees*."

He extends the point strikingly: someone trained to apply normative vocabulary noninferentially "would be directly observing normative facts. It is in this sense that we might be said to be able to *hear* not just the noises someone else makes, but their words, and indeed, what they are saying — their meanings."

### The Priority of Practice
On Sellars's demand that the reporter must *know* his reports are reliable, Brandom notes the "'ing/ed' ambiguity" in "justification" — "a practical activity, or being justified" — and reads Sellars as claiming "that one cannot have the… unless it is possible to redeem that claim… by engaging in the activity." He labels this "the priority of practice over… a species of pragmatism, to which Sellars adheres."

### The Tortoise
Brandom's recurring image for the Given, taken from Sellars §38: preconceptual awareness would be "the tortoise underneath the elephant." His summary of the essay's achievement is that we can have "direct awareness of mental episodes (the application of inferentially articulated concepts of thoughts and sense impressions, elicited noninferentially by thoughts and sense impressions), including the limited but very real privileged access each of us has to such inner episodes, without committing ourselves to the Myth of the Given."

## Relations to Other Vault Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Wilfrid Sellars]]** — the direct inheritance; Brandom's Guide is the standard scholarly entry to EPM.
- **[[Thinkers/John McDowell]]** — the other half of the "Pittsburgh School," and a rival heir: McDowell keeps experience conceptual so the world can rationally constrain belief, where Brandom builds outward from social-inferential practice. Both accept the Myth of the Given.
- **[[Thinkers/Richard Rorty]]** — reads Brandom as the completion of Sellars's program and as a bridge to Hegel, Gadamer, and Habermas.
- **[[Thinkers/Ludwig Wittgenstein]]** — the "giving and asking for reasons" framing descends from the *Investigations* and from [[Concepts/Rule-Following (Wittgenstein)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]]** — the source of the "concepts as rules" reading Brandom uses against representationalism.
