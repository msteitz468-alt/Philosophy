# Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855)

> Danish writer who made faith a task for a lifetime and the single individual higher than the universal — and who signed the work that says so Johannes *de silentio*.

## Summary

Søren Aabye Kierkegaard was born in Copenhagen on 5 May 1813 and died there on 11 November 1855. This page is built from Hannay's introduction to *Fear and Trembling* plus the 1843 text itself. It is a first-primary page, not a survey of the authorship. *Either/Or*, *Repetition*, *The Concept of Anxiety*, *Philosophical Fragments*, *Stages on Life's Way*, *Concluding Unscientific Postscript*, *The Sickness unto Death*, and *Practice in Christianity* are named, not ingested.

Hannay's biographical sketch (placed at the *end* of his introduction so as not to dissolve the book's compositional integrity into Regine-allegory): youngest of seven; father a wealthy Jutland-born wholesaler; mother the father's second wife, formerly maid to the first. Deaths of siblings and mother before he was twenty-one; he expected not to live past thirty-three. Seven years as a theology student, also liberal arts and science; reputation for brilliance and extravagance. Met Regine Olsen in 1837 (she was fourteen); engaged 10 September 1840; returned the ring in August 1841; break final that November, after defending the doctoral thesis *The Concept of Irony with Constant Reference to Socrates*. Left for Berlin and Schelling's lectures. The flood of pseudonymous and signed works followed, beginning with *Either/Or* (February 1843). *Fear and Trembling* and *Repetition* appeared together on 16 October 1843. He predicted that *Fear and Trembling* alone, with its "frightful pathos," would immortalize his name. Late feud with the *Corsair*; late attack on the Danish State Church (*The Instant*). On his sickbed he told Emil Boesen his life had been a "great and to others unknown and incomprehensible suffering" that looked like pride and vanity but "wasn't"; he regretted not having married or taken office.

The aesthetic works (*Either/Or* through *Practice in Christianity*) were written under *noms de plume*. Kierkegaard's posthumous *Point of View* (quoted by Hannay) says the method is to deceive: not "I am a Christian, you are not" but "You are a Christian, I am not"; "Let's talk about the aesthetic" in order to come to the religious. Johannes *de silentio* is that device in one book: he writes from the life of immediacy, does not have the Christian concept of faith, and bangs his head on the Hegelian paradigm so the reader can feel the gap.

## Key Claims / Positions (from *Fear and Trembling*)

- **Faith is not a beginning.** Contemporaries treat it as a children's disease one hopes to get over. Johannes: it was a task for a whole lifetime. Even rendering its content into concepts would not be grasping how one came to it.
- **Two movements.** Infinite resignation: by one's own strength one renounces the finite and wins eternal consciousness. Faith: on the strength of the absurd one gets the finite back, for *this* life. Resignation does not require faith; faith presupposes resignation. See [[Concepts/Knight of Faith and Infinite Resignation (Kierkegaard)]].
- **The paradox.** The single individual as the particular is higher than the universal and stands in an absolute relation to the absolute. This cannot be mediated. See [[Concepts/The Single Individual (Kierkegaard)]], [[Concepts/Teleological Suspension of the Ethical (Kierkegaard)]].
- **Abraham or murder.** If the ethical is the universal, Abraham is a murderer and Hegelians have no right to praise him. The three *problemata* are one *modus tollens*. See [[Arguments/Three Problemata (Kierkegaard)]], [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Hegel - Faith vs Sittlichkeit]].
- **Silence.** Speech translates into the universal. Abraham *cannot* speak; "it is a trial" is what no one can understand. The true knight is a witness, never a teacher; the false knight is a sectarian.
- **Passion, not progress.** No generation learns the genuinely human factor from another. Faith is the highest passion; none comes further.

Johannes does not claim these as Kierkegaard's doctrines. He says he does not have faith and cannot perform the last movement.

## How This Compares to Other Thinkers in the Wiki

- **vs [[Thinkers/Hegel]]**: the load-bearing opponent. Ethical life = identification of the individual with the social whole; faith as immediacy to be gone beyond. Johannes: if that is right, stop talking about Abraham. See [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Hegel - Faith vs Sittlichkeit]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Kant]]**: an absolute duty to God can give love of neighbour the opposite expression to ethical duty. Luke 14.26 is not to be softened. See [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Kant - Absolute Duty vs Universal Law]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Socrates]]**: Socrates' ignorance is infinite resignation, "a match for human strength"; he went further than philosophy's cheap "faith = the immediate." He is the intellectual tragic hero who must have the last word. He is not the knight of faith. Hadot already had Kierkegaard as a modern reader of the Socratic figure (pseudonyms; deceive into the truth; the Individual; not-Christian ≈ not-sage). This book is one such *prosopon*.
- **vs [[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: Sartre takes "the anguish of Abraham" as the legislator who must decide whether the voice is an angel's, and drops the theological frame. Johannes's Abraham already believes God exists; the trial is getting Isaac back for this life while drawing the knife. Sartre's legislator is closer to Johannes's *tragic hero* (who has the universal) than to Abraham. See [[Concepts/Anguish, Abandonment and Despair (Sartre)]].
- **vs [[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: *Angst* pages already name Kierkegaard as ancestor (dizziness of freedom before possibility). That formula is from *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844), not this book. *Fear and Trembling*'s anguish is the contradiction between murder and sacrifice, and the solitude of not being able to speak.
- **vs [[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]**: Deleuze already contrasts them on repetition — Kierkegaard's theater of faith (the leap) vs Nietzsche's of cruelty and dance. This book is the leap-side primary: double movement, not eternal return.
- **vs Descartes (as Johannes cites him)**: Descartes did not cry "Fire!" and make doubt everyone's duty; he would submit to revelation against the natural light. Martensen's "go further" from that doubt to Hegel is the Preface's joke.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Conflicts with [[Thinkers/Hegel]] on whether the ethical as the universal is the highest — see [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Hegel - Faith vs Sittlichkeit]].
- > [!warning] Conflicts with [[Thinkers/Kant]] on whether there can be a duty to God that suspends universal moral law — see [[Contradictions/Kierkegaard vs Kant - Absolute Duty vs Universal Law]].
- The relation of Johannes to Kierkegaard is itself an open question the book forces: Hannay thinks Kierkegaard envisages an alternative ethical paradigm that Johannes only charts from the outside. *Postscript* and the signed discourses are the hole.
- *The Concept of Anxiety* is the missing ancestor-text for the Heidegger *Angst* page.
