# Francisco J. Varela
> Francisco J. Varela (1946–2001), Chilean biologist and cognitive scientist. First author of *The Embodied Mind*: cognition is not the representation of a pregiven world by a pregiven subject, but the enactment of a world through a history of embodied action.

## Summary

The 2016 revised edition's CIP gives the dates 1946–2001. Evan Thompson's introduction supplies the career as this volume presents it: Varela came to a 1977 Lindisfarne conference ("Mind and Nature") with Gregory Bateson, was scholar in residence there in 1978, and became family to Thompson. He moved to Paris from Chile by way of the Max Planck Institute in Frankfurt (a year with Wolf Singer) to set up a laboratory on the neurophysiology of vision at the École Polytechnique and the Institut des Neurosciences. He and Thompson began the book in Paris in 1986; Eleanor Rosch joined in 1989 at Berkeley; the three finished it in 1989–1990. The working title was *Worlds Without Ground*.

Earlier work named in the 2016 apparatus, not ingested here: *Autopoiesis and Cognition* and *The Tree of Knowledge* with Humberto Maturana (self-producing living systems); *Principles of Biological Autonomy* (operational closure). After this book, and before his death in 2001, he helped found *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences*, joined the "naturalizing phenomenology" movement, and formulated **neurophenomenology** — first-person phenomenology plus mindfulness used to study large-scale brain dynamics of conscious experience (Lutz, Lachaux, Martinerie, and Varela 2002). That later program is reported, not primary.

Inside *The Embodied Mind* the voice is joint. The load-bearing inventions the three assign to Part IV are [[Concepts/Enaction (Varela)|enaction]] and [[Concepts/Natural Drift (Varela)|natural drift]]. The method they pair with those inventions is [[Concepts/Mindfulness-Awareness as Method (Varela)|mindfulness/awareness]]. The philosophical close is Madhyamaka [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)|groundlessness]].

## Key Claims / Positions (from this source)

- Embodiment is double: the body as lived, experiential structure *and* as the milieu of cognitive mechanisms (Merleau-Ponty continued, not as scholarship).
- Cognitivism (cognition = computation over symbolic representations of a pregiven world) discovers a nonunified self and then ignores the discovery in life. See [[Concepts/Selfless Minds (Varela)]].
- Connectionism explains emergence without a homunculus and still treats successful cognition as correspondence to a pregiven capacity.
- Autonomous systems have **operational closure**: "the results of [their] processes are those processes themselves." They do not recover an independent world; they "enact a world as a domain of distinctions."
- Evolution as [[Concepts/Natural Drift (Varela)|natural drift]] is the biological counterpart of cognition as embodied action: proscriptive viability, not optimal fitness.
- Objectivism and nihilism share a root in grasping. The middle way is not a third foundation. See [[Concepts/Cartesian Anxiety (Varela)]], [[Concepts/Groundlessness and the Middle Way (Varela)]].

## How Varela Relates to Prior Thinkers in the Vault

- **Merleau-Ponty** (no thinker page yet): named ancestor. Double embodiment; *Structure of Behavior* and *Phenomenology of Perception*. Outstanding Sources still lists the latter.
- **[[Thinkers/Hubert Dreyfus]]**: 1991 introduction calls him the "Heideggerian gadfly" of cognitive science, grouped with Charles Taylor as doubting cognitive science *because they equate it with cognitivism*. 1993 *Mind* review objects to mindfulness as inward observation; Thompson 2016 accepts the objection. Dreyfus describes the involved body; Varela adds a method and an evolutionary/biological mechanism. See [[Concepts/Embodied Intelligence and the Role of the Body (Dreyfus)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: 1991 text treats the epoché as "twentieth-century Cartesianism" that studies contents "purely internally." Thompson 2016 recants that portrayal and points to the *Crisis* life-world as the background of science.
- **[[Thinkers/Martin Heidegger]]**: background / being-in-the-world as source; ch. 11 prefers Heidegger's "planetary thinking" to Rorty's "conversation of the West."
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: the name of the anxiety — the craving for an absolute inner or outer ground.
- **[[Thinkers/Marvin Minsky]]**: society-of-mind architecture used, then charged with leaving a theoretical no-self that everyday grasping cannot inhabit.
- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]** / **[[Thinkers/Douglas Hofstadter]]**: *The Mind's I* is the popular selfless-mind book they find procedurally inadequate (no disciplined first-person method).
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: the 1998 [[Concepts/Extended Mind (Clark and Chalmers)|extended mind]] paper names this book as kin. Clark mechanizes coupling; he does not take the Madhyamaka close or the mindfulness method. Do not read later predictive-processing slogans back into 1991.
- **[[Thinkers/Anil Seth]]**: later neighbor via Maturana-style autopoiesis / life-first consciousness. Seth's source is *Being You*, not this book.
- **[[Thinkers/Richard Rorty]]**: edifying conversation of the West refused.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Representational recovery of a pregiven world vs enactment of a perceiver-dependent world — see [[Contradictions/Representational Cognitivism vs Enaction]].
- Autopoiesis is the earlier Maturana–Varela theory Thompson 2016 treats as a source of enaction. The 1991 body uses **operational closure** and **structural coupling**, not the word *autopoiesis*. Do not collapse the later term into this book.
- Neurophenomenology is post-1991. A concept page from this volume would be interpolation.
