# Thomas Nagel

> Thomas Nagel (b. 1937), American philosopher; University Professor of Philosophy and Law, Emeritus, at New York University. The vault's primary voice for the claim that consciousness has an irreducibly **subjective character** — there is something it is like to be an organism — that no third-person physical or functional description captures. Not to be confused with [[Thinkers/Ernest Nagel]] (philosopher of science; *Gödel’s Proof*).

## Summary

Nagel entered the vault as the missing pole of [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]]: Dennett (1991), Chalmers (1996), and Clark (2023) all argue with "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974), but until this ingest the page was written from their side. The 2024 Oxford reprint supplies the 1974 essay, a 2024 preface, and the 2023 lecture "Further Thoughts: The Psychophysical Nexus."

The 1974 thesis: an organism is conscious iff there is something it is like *for* that organism. Bat sonar makes the point vivid because imagination cannot carry a human into that point of view, but the same structure holds between humans and would hold for Martians trying to conceive us. Reduction in the physical sciences works by leaving a species-specific appearance behind; experience *is* the appearance, so the same move takes us farther from the phenomenon. Nagel does not infer that physicalism is false — only that we have no conception of how it could be true. The 2024 preface insists this is a call to *expand* science, not to exile consciousness from it.

The 2023 lecture adds a constructive program Nagel had already sketched at length in "The Psychophysical Nexus" (2000/2002). Natural language is conceptually dualist; zombie conceivability is therefore genuine; every behaviorist or functionalist analysis fails to entail inner consciousness. He still refuses both Cartesian substance dualism and Chalmers-style property dualism, and hopes for a **monism** in which mental and neurophysiological descriptions are incomplete aspects of one more basic kind (Spinoza, neutral monism, dual-aspect theory). The view stands only if consciousness is essential to the explanation of behavior.

Works listed in the 2024 volume but not yet in the vault: *The Possibility of Altruism*; *Mortal Questions* (the original home of the bat essay); *The View from Nowhere*; *What Does It All Mean?*; *Equality and Partiality*; *Other Minds*; *The Last Word*; *The Myth of Ownership* (with Liam Murphy); *Concealment and Exposure*; *Secular Philosophy and the Religious Temperament*; *Mind and Cosmos*; *Analytic Philosophy and Human Life*; *Moral Feelings, Moral Reality, and Moral Progress*.

## Key Claims / Positions (from this source)

- **Subjective character as the mark of the mental**: conscious iff there is something it is like for the organism. Functional and intentional ascriptions can hold of unconscious automata.
- **Point of view is a type**, not a private atom: phenomenological facts can be shared among sufficiently similar subjects; they remain subjective because even third-person ascription requires first-person uptake.
- **Facts beyond human concepts**: bat and Martian phenomenology can be real without being representable in any human conceptual scheme.
- **Physicalism presently unintelligible**, not refuted. The identification "mental event = physical event" lacks the theoretical background that makes other scientific identifications intelligible.
- **Objective phenomenology** as the next method: describe subjective character in terms usable by beings who cannot have the experiences.
- **Conceptual dualism, metaphysical monism** (2023): resist property dualism; look for unmediated inner/outer aspects of a single central state, beginning with affect.

## Relation to Other Vault Thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Daniel Dennett]]** (principal opponent): Dennett's *Content and Consciousness* (1969) is already a named target in the 1974 notes. Heterophenomenology and [[Concepts/Qualia Disqualified (Dennett)]] are built to dissolve Nagel's residue. Nagel's reply, implicit in the essay and explicit in the preface, is that a method that abandons the point of view has changed the subject. See [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]].
- **[[Thinkers/David Chalmers]]** (ally on the datum, opponent on the metaphysics): the [[Concepts/Hard Problem of Consciousness (Chalmers)]] is Nagel's "what it is like" renamed and sharpened. Chalmers takes zombie conceivability all the way to [[Concepts/Naturalistic Dualism (Chalmers)|property dualism]]; Nagel 2023 uses the same conceivability argument and then *refuses* that step. The nearest Chalmers construct is [[Concepts/Double-Aspect Theory of Information (Chalmers)]].
- **[[Thinkers/Frank Jackson]]** (near-ally on the leftover, opponent on the inference; now primary, 2026-08-12): Jackson is "much indebted" to the 1974 essay and then *separates* his [[Arguments/Knowledge Argument (Jackson)|knowledge argument]] from it (1982 §III). He is complaining of a *property of the experience*, not of failure to occupy a point of view or to extrapolate in imagination. He draws the conclusion Nagel refuses (Physicalism is false) and defends the option Nagel's 2023 monism is hostage to avoiding ([[Concepts/Epiphenomenal Qualia (Jackson)]]).
- **[[Thinkers/Andy Clark]]**: treats the "what it is like" puzzle as a conceptual artifact of self-opaque self-models ([[Concepts/Predictive Processing and the Hard Problem (Clark)]]). Clark's page had conflated Thomas with [[Thinkers/Ernest Nagel]]; they are different people.
- **[[Thinkers/Edmund Husserl]]**: Nagel's insistence that the essence of the inner world *is* the point of view aligns with Husserl against naturalization — see [[Contradictions/Husserl vs Naturalism — Phenomenal Consciousness and the Hard Problem]]. Nagel's "objective phenomenology" is a different project from Husserl's: it tries to describe subjectivity in a form usable *without* empathy.
- **[[Thinkers/John Searle]]**: shares the intuition that formal/functional description misses something essential; Searle locates it in biological causal powers, Nagel in the subjective point of view. Neither is a property dualist.
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]**: named precedent for the 2023 monism — mental and physical as aspects of one thing, not two properties glued by contingent laws.
- **[[Thinkers/Descartes]]**: Nagel accepts the conceivability argument as evidence of *conceptual* dualism and denies that it settles the metaphysics.
- **[[Thinkers/Jean-Paul Sartre]]**: the 1974 essay flags the *pour-soi* / *en-soi* contrast ([[Concepts/Being-for-itself and Being-in-itself (Sartre)]]) as the older vocabulary for subjective vs objective, then sets it aside as too coarse.
- **[[Thinkers/Saul Kripke]]** (now primary, 2026-08-12): Nagel's note 11 says the 1974 view is "closely related" to *Naming and Necessity* on the essential subjective character of pain. Lecture III is the contact: "being a pain is a necessary property of each pain"; pain is picked out by "its immediate phenomenological quality," not by an accidental role, so the heat / molecular-motion analogy fails ([[Arguments/Identity Thesis and Pain (Kripke)]]). Kripke rejects the *contingent* identity thesis and refuses to infer Cartesian dualism; Nagel refuses to infer that physicalism is false. Allied on the leftover quality; forked on the next step.
- **[[Thinkers/Hilary Putnam]]** (1986, *The View from Nowhere*, **reported** from Pritchard and Ranalli 2016, not a Nagel primary): the intelligibility of [[Concepts/Brain in a Vat|BIV]] skepticism is a reductio of causal theories of reference; Putnam's argument leaves the worse thought "Perhaps I can’t even think the truth about what I am." See [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]]. *The View from Nowhere* remains outstanding.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- > [!warning] Irreducible subjectivity vs third-person sufficiency: [[Contradictions/Dennett vs Nagel - Multiple Drafts vs Subjective Experience]].
- > [!warning] Non-deflationist fork: Nagel vs Chalmers on whether conceptual dualism licenses property dualism. Nagel's monism, if it can be made sense of, would undercut Chalmers's "further facts"; if it cannot, Chalmers's dualism is the remaining non-deflationist option.
- > [!warning] Semantic anti-skepticism vs the intelligibility of skepticism — see [[Contradictions/Putnam vs Nagel - Semantic Anti-Skepticism vs Intelligible Skepticism]]. Nagel 1986 is reported, not primary.
- The 2023 monism is hostage to consciousness doing causal work. That is the same pressure that produces Chalmers's paradox of phenomenal judgment and Dennett's parity argument against epiphenomenal qualia. Jackson's 1982 §IV is now the primary statement of the option the hostage clause names.
