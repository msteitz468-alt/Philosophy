# Laozi

> Traditional name ("Old Master") for the position of the *Daodejing* — 81 chapters of aphoristic Chinese, here in Brook Ziporyn's 2023 translation. Not a securely historical author. The corpus ironically reverses purposive *dao* into nonpurposive "course," acquired *de* into unlearned "virtuosity," and the valued A-pole (strength, name, action, high) into the ignored B-background (softness, namelessness, nondoing, low) that generates it.

## Profile

- **Identity/dates**: Traditional story (Ziporyn's Introduction, then discarded): Li Er / Li Boyang / Lao Dan, Zhou royal archivist, rebukes the young Confucius, resigns as the empire decays, rides a water buffalo to the Western Pass, and under duress dashes off ~5,000 characters for the border guard Guanyin — then disappears westward. "Modern scholars generally regard none of this as true." Late attestation of the man; the text is likely composite.
- **Textual history**: Guodian bamboo slips (4th c. BCE) hold less than half the received material, shuffled. Mawangdui silk manuscripts (early 2nd c. BCE) reverse the two halves. Ziporyn treats the corpus as "Classic Passages on the Course and Virtuosity from the Old Masters" — a curated commonplace book of a defunct genre, with a consistent tone despite many hands.
- **Work in the vault**: *Daodejing*, Ziporyn 2023 (Liveright) — see [[Sources/Daodejing - Laozi (Ziporyn trans., 2023)]]. The name "Laozi" on this page is a convenience for the minimally discernible position of that corpus, not a biography.

## Core Doctrine (from the Ziporyn *Daodejing*)

- **The course that cannot be taken as the right course.** "Any course can be taken as the right course to take, but no course like that can be the course taken always" (ch. 1). A *dao* in the older sense is a purposive how-to; the ironic course is nonpurposive process — and *thereby* the real source of values. Nicknamed "the course," forced name "the vastness"; it "conforms to how everything is when left to itself" (ch. 25). See [[Concepts/Dao - The Course (Laozi)]].
- **Ironic virtuosity.** Highest *de* "is not grasped as virtuosity" and "*does* nothing, for it harbors no motives or purposes" (ch. 38). Virtuosity appears when the course is lost; then humankindness, duty, ritual — ritual is "the beginning of disorder." Course generates, virtuosity rears, "producing them but not possessing them" (ch. 51). See [[Concepts/De - Virtuosity and Potency (Laozi)]].
- **Nondoing.** The sage "abides in the work of nondoing" (ch. 2). "Doing nondoing, all is ordered" (ch. 3). "The constancy of the course lies in doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone" (ch. 37). Learning adds daily; the course *subtracts* until nondoing (ch. 48). To do something with a thing "is to ruin it"; the sage "helps each thing be as it is of its own accord" (ch. 64). See [[Concepts/Wu Wei - Nondoing (Laozi)]].
- **A/B reversal and the unhewn.** Once all know the beautiful, the ugly is already there (ch. 2). Reversal is the activity of the course; weakness is its functioning; what is there is born from what is not there (ch. 40). The unhewn (*pu*), nameless and undyed, is the B-background that names, carving, and ritual shatter into "vessels and tools" (chs. 19, 28, 32). See [[Concepts/Unhewn Background and the A-B Reversal (Laozi)]].
- **Water, softness, the low place.** "The highest good is like water" — it benefits by never competing, going where none want to go (ch. 8). Function is in the empty hub (ch. 11). Rivers reign by getting below (ch. 66). Alive = soft; dead = hard (ch. 76). Nothing softer than water, yet none outdo it against the hard (ch. 78). See [[Concepts/Water, Softness, and the Low Place (Laozi)]].
- **Politics of the unseen.** Highest rulers "are not even known to exist"; the people say they did it "of ourselves, all on our own" (ch. 17). More laws, more thieves; "I take no action and the people transform themselves" (ch. 57). Three treasures: loving care, frugality, never putting ourselves first (ch. 67). Small state, unused weapons, neighbors heard but never visited (ch. 80).

## Relations to other vault thinkers

- **[[Thinkers/Sun Tzu]]** (the vault's other classical Chinese voice): shared water, formlessness, and preference for victory without engagement (ch. 68 ≈ "winning without fighting"). But Sun Tzu's *tao* is a calculable strategic asset and his water is a tactic for imposing will; Laozi's course is nonpurposive, and "to do something with it is to ruin it."
- **[[Thinkers/Heraclitus]]**: both teach process and the unity of opposites. Heraclitus's Logos is the rational rule of strife ("war is the father of all"); Laozi's course *inverts* the valued pole and treats the nameless background, not the law of conflict, as what is constant.
- **[[Thinkers/Nietzsche]]**: both invert received value-pairs. Nietzsche revalues toward strength and the pathos of distance; Laozi revalues toward softness, lowliness, and not being first. Same formal gesture, opposite content.
- **[[Thinkers/Maimonides]] / negative theology**: both unsay the named highest. Maimonides' God is a simple essence known only by negation; Laozi's nameless is a *mother* and a gushing source — generative process, not a simple One.
- **[[Thinkers/Spinoza]]**: both refuse a transcendent lawgiver. Spinoza's *Deus sive Natura* is a unique substance known through attributes; Laozi's course "conforms to how everything is when left to itself" and has no lordship over what it clothes and feeds (ch. 34).
- **[[Thinkers/Kant]] / [[Thinkers/Aristotle]]**: nondoing without motives is not duty and not excellent activity. Highest virtuosity "harbors no motives"; the work of the course is subtraction, not *energeia*.
- **[[Thinkers/Sextus Empiricus]]**: both quiet the drive to determine. Pyrrhonian *epoché* suspends on the non-evident and lets ataraxia follow by chance; Laozi has a position (the B is the real source) and a political program (unhewn simplicity), not a chronicler's report of appearances.
- **[[Thinkers/Confucius]]** (now primary, Chin 2014): the named opponent of chs. 18–19 and 38. Humankindness, duty, ritual, sagacity, and filial piety are treated here as decay-markers; the *Analects* treats them as the work (*ren* as beginning and end; *keji fuli* as the way). Shared word *wuwei* (Analects 15.5: Shun faces south) is not this book's nondoing. See [[Contradictions/Confucius vs Laozi - Humaneness and Rites vs Course Lost]].

## Vault / AI / Personal Relevance

- **Subtraction as method**: "the work of the course is daily subtraction" is a charter against adding frameworks, metrics, and names that shatter the unhewn. Ingest and planning can over-carve.
- **Nondoing is not idleness**: "doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone" — handle the difficult while easy (ch. 63), the journey starts underfoot (ch. 64), then stop. Close to Sun Tzu's "win without fighting," farther from Stoic endurance.
- **Low place / non-contention**: rivers reign by getting below; "because he does not contend, none in all the world can contend with him" (ch. 66). Transfer to negotiation and leadership without the Art of War's deception doctrine.
